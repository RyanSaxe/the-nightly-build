# Feature

Pick the story you would make room for in today's paper even if no headline
demanded it. There should be a reason to read it now and enough firsthand material
to reward the time.

Favor a subject whose main point can be shown with real material: published data, a
mechanism that can be drawn, events that can be placed in order, rival positions
that line up in a comparison. Go where the strongest reporting takes you, and write
the piece you could not have written before you opened the sources.

Five minutes is the reading budget. Open with the finding in two or three sentences,
then build each section around a visual, with short prose saying how to read it and
what follows from it. Cut whatever is not needed to follow the visuals and the
conclusion.

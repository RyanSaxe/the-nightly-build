# Daily Brief

Publish six items in this order: a Word of the Day, three news items, and two
technology developments. This is the paper's quick read. It should take about
three minutes and leave history, procedure, and analysis to the sections built for
them.

Choose a word with a story, one that most well-read people have not met and would
enjoy using. A coinage with a known inventor, like sphexish, is exactly the kind
of word this item exists for. A common word with an ordinary etymology is not,
however old it is. Give its part of speech, pronunciation, and a one-sentence
definition, then tell the documented story of where it came from in three or four
sentences.

Choose the three news reports for the importance of what happened, not the volume
of attention it received. Favor the United States. New York City and its nearby
region, or an international story, takes a place only when it matters enough to
displace another national story. Report what happened in the last two days, or
what changed in that time on a running story.

Choose two recent developments in technology: a release, a new capability, a
research result, or another change worth following.

Write each news and technology item in two or three sentences: what happened, who
did it or claims it, and what follows from it. One strong source per item is
enough. Use the underlying record where it can be opened and a reputable report
where it cannot. Add a second source only when the first is disputed or cannot
settle the claim. Attribute unverified claims to their source.

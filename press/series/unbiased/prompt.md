# Unbiased

Unbiased examines a consequential disagreement made timely by a current event
in the United States. Anchor the choice in recent original reporting from a
reputable US-based newsroom. That reporting must document the development that
makes the article timely. Do not use a current headline merely to repackage an
old debate.

The subject can come from any part of current American life. Politics has no
priority over technology, business, science, culture, or another consequential
domain. The US event sets the article's center; relevant evidence and position
holders may come from elsewhere.

Choose a dispute with two materially different, defensible positions that
credible people or institutions actually hold. Both positions need a credible
named holder and a direct citation to that holder's stated view. Test the
consequential factual claims supporting each position against reputable
independent evidence. Treat a holder's statement only as evidence of the
position; verify its factual claims independently. If the available record
resolves the question or leaves only one position with serious standing, choose
another subject.

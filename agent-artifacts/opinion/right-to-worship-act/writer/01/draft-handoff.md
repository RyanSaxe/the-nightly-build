# Draft handoff: opinion/right-to-worship-act (writer 01)

## Original work
This piece moves the bill's First Amendment vulnerability off the buffer-zone /
McCullen frame the operative text defeats and onto vagueness-and-overbreadth,
by reading the sponsors' own operative verbs ("meaningfully interferes,"
"unreasonably hinders or deters") against McCullen's narrow-tailoring test and
against the 50-foot New York law the bill doubles — showing the defect is the
undefined verbs plus a doubled, undisclosed-schedule radius, not a categorical
100-foot speech-exclusion buffer. The three-rule table (Hill / McCullen / Right
to Worship) and the "Twice the zone" section are where that reframing is done on
the page, not asserted.

## Position pivot handled
Wrote the narrower position the record supports, per the commission's post-
evidence update — not the original "cannot survive McCullen" lean. The piece
states plainly that the Act sits with Hill (a scienter/conduct rule), that a
"cannot survive McCullen" argument answers a statute the sponsors did not write,
and that this is exactly why the paper does not run that argument. The counter
steelmans the content-neutral, conduct-focused free-exercise case at full
strength (Cruz, Suozzi, ADL, Hill, and McCullen's own menu of narrower tools)
before answering on tailoring and vagueness.

## Evidence gaps handled honestly in prose
- No Congress.gov enrolled text: I re-checked congress.gov at publication per the
  brief; it returns HTTP 403 (the same bot wall the researcher recorded for
  congress.gov / justia / loc), so no assigned S./H.R. number or enrolled text is
  resolvable. Operative language is attributed to the Slotkin release and to
  reporting that quotes the bill, and the "What the record cannot settle yet"
  note states this explicitly.
- Civil vs. civil-and-criminal enforcement: left unresolved in the note, and the
  uncertainty is turned into part of the vagueness argument (a speaker cannot tell
  fine from crime) rather than silently picking a side.
- McCullen "unanimous": stated as unanimous in result only, with four Justices
  who would have struck the buffer as content-based — so the tailoring holding is
  the Court's, not a consensus the Act can bank on.
- Hill: framed as contested and unextended (Coalition Life / Clement-Murphy
  petition; Thomas's "aberration"), the sponsors' best and most exposed
  authority, not as dead or as secure.

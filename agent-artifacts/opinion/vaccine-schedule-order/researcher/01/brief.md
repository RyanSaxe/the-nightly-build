Establish, from owning sources: the exact text and named scope of the 11 August
executive action narrowing the childhood vaccine schedule (the order or
proclamation itself, and its signing remarks), recording precisely which diseases
it keeps on the routine schedule and what it moves to a case-by-case decision; the
normal schedule-setting process it bypasses (the CDC Advisory Committee on
Immunization Practices, how it reviews evidence and issues recommendations, and its
statutory footing), and the administrative-law question of what authority the
executive does and does not have to override that process (the Administrative
Procedure Act and any relevant Public Health Service Act provisions); the state of
the vaccine-autism evidence from the primaries that own it (the 2019 Annals of
Internal Medicine Danish cohort study of more than 650,000 children with its exact
finding and hazard ratio; the Institute of Medicine / National Academies adverse-
effects reviews; the retraction of the 1998 Lancet Wakefield paper); the American
Academy of Pediatrics response to the order; and the downstream consequences for
coverage (the ACA preventive-services requirement tied to ACIP recommendations,
the Vaccines for Children program, school-entry requirements, insurance).

Then do the adversarial work the counter section needs: find the strongest, most
credible statements of the opposing case — parental informed consent, the
legitimacy of debating the timing or spacing of specific vaccines, and principled
distrust of a single centralized schedule — from serious sources, not strawman
versions. Verify every figure against the primary that owns it and get the study
scopes and hazard ratios exactly right. Record contradictions and any place the
paper's position is weaker than it looks. Classify each source primary or secondary
and why; at least 8 sources; confirm every URL resolves to the source's own page.

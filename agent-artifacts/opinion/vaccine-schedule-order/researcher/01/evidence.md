# Evidence record: opinion/vaccine-schedule-order (01)

The order exists and is verifiable from its own text on whitehouse.gov: "Delivering
Gold Standard Childhood Vaccine Recommendations for Americans," signed 10 August
2026 (Monday), not 11 August as the commission and brief state. The record strongly
supports the column's second ground: the childhood schedule is set through the CDC
Advisory Committee on Immunization Practices under a statute (Section 222 of the
Public Health Service Act), coverage statutes bolt onto ACIP recommendations by
name, and a federal court had already enjoined the administration's earlier
agency-level attempt to rewrite the schedule as likely arbitrary under the
Administrative Procedure Act. The autism science is airtight from the primaries. The
record is thinner, and the paper's position weaker than it looks, on the first
ground exactly as commissioned: the order's written text and the White House fact
sheet never mention autism, they argue peer-nation alignment and parental choice. The
autism rationale lives in the signing remarks (Trump and RFK Jr.), not the order.
A careful opponent will say the column attacks a rationale the document does not
state, so the writer must attribute the autism claim to the remarks, not the order,
or the first ground is a strawman. The peer-nation premise the order leans on is
also partly true at the raw-count level, which the counter section must concede. See
Contradictions.

## Contradictions

- The commission and the brief both date the order "11 August 2026." The order's own
  page, the White House fact sheet, the Factbase transcript, NPR, and NBC all date
  the signing Monday, 10 August 2026. The column should say 10 August. If the
  edition or the Current Events brief pinned "11 August," that is the publication or
  news date, not the signing date, and conflating them is a checkable error.

- The commission's first ground says the order "rests on a causal claim (vaccines and
  autism)." The order's text and the White House fact sheet contain no autism claim;
  they argue peer-nation alignment and parental choice. The autism rationale is in the
  signing remarks (Trump; RFK Jr.'s "genes don't cause epidemics"). This is the single
  place the paper's position is weaker than it looks. The strongest opposing move is
  precisely this: "the order never mentions autism; you are attacking the President's
  ad-libs, not the policy." The writer must attribute the autism rationale to the
  remarks and the administration's record, not to the order, and can still argue the
  remarks reveal the policy's actual driver, but must make that argument rather than
  assume it.

- The order's stated empirical premise is partly true. The US did recommend against
  more diseases (18) than most of 20 peer nations, whose average was 13.6 (STAT
  fact-check). The counter section cannot deny this. The answer is that the honest
  reading makes the US the new low outlier at 11, that peer schedules reflect
  country-specific disease burden (Moss; Hviid), and that "more than peers" is not a
  finding that any specific US vaccine lacks a favorable risk/benefit profile, which is
  ACIP's actual job to assess.

- "Shared clinical decision-making," the category the order uses to move vaccines off
  the universal list, is ACIP's own creation and is applied by ACIP through evidence
  review and votes. So the categories are not inherently anti-science. This weakens any
  argument that the category itself is illegitimate and sharpens the real defect: the
  order assigns vaccines to that category by fiat rather than through the body that
  built the category. Argue the process, not the label.

- Count discrepancy: the White House fact sheet and NBC say 18 diseases down to 11;
  NPR, CNN, and CNBC say 17 to 11. The owning source (White House) says 18. Use 18-to-11
  and note the 2024 CDC baseline the fact sheet cites.

- The order is careful about its own limits. Section 2(c) directs agencies to act "to
  the fullest extent allowable by law," and the order tasks the HHS task force rather
  than purporting to rewrite the ACIP schedule itself. The bluntest "executive fiat
  overrides ACIP" framing overstates what the text does. The stronger, accurate second
  ground is sequential: the agency-level rewrite (5 January 2026 schedule; ACIP
  reconstitution) was already enjoined as likely unlawful under the APA and FACA, and
  this order attempts to reassert the same policy at the presidential level while a
  court has blocked the mechanism that would make it operative.

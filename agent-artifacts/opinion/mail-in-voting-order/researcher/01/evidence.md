# Evidence — opinion/mail-in-voting-order

The primary legal record is strong and complete: the full text of Executive
Order 14399 (signed March 31, 2026; published Federal Register April 3, 2026,
91 Fed. Reg. 17125), Judge Indira Talwani's three district-court orders
(ripeness ruling June 18, summary-judgment/permanent-injunction order June 25,
stay-pending-appeal denial July 7), the 1st Circuit's July 25, 2026 order
denying a stay (2-1, Gelpí and Rikelman in the majority, Dunlap concurring in
part/dissenting in part), and both emergency Supreme Court stay applications —
the Solicitor General's (No. 26A124, filed on behalf of the federal
defendants) and the Missouri-led intervenor states' (filed July 28, 2026) —
were all read in full from official court/government PDFs. The mechanics are
now precise: which sections of the EO are enjoined, for which states, for
which elections, and on what constitutional theory. The weak point is the
"opposing scholar" side of the counter: repeated, targeted searches turned up
no named academic on record defending EO 14399's constitutionality with a
dated, quotable, sourceable statement specific to this order. The steelmanned
counter is instead built — appropriately, per the brief's own list of
acceptable holders — from the government's and intervenor states' own filed
arguments and the EO's own text, which is actually the strongest form of the
argument since it is the position under review. One party quote (RNC Chairman
Michael Whatley) was found but is dated before EO 14399 existed and is
therefore excluded rather than mis-cited (see Discarded).

## Sources

1. **Executive Order 14399, "[Title as captioned in Federal Register filing on
   state citizenship verification and mail ballot delivery for federal
   elections]"** — signed March 31, 2026; published Federal Register April 3,
   2026 at 91 Fed. Reg. 17125, FR Doc. 2026-06601.
   URL: https://www.federalregister.gov/public-inspection/2026-06601 (public
   inspection PDF, read in full — 8 pages).
   Primary/secondary: **Primary.** This is the operative legal text itself;
   the Federal Register is the official publisher of record for executive
   orders.
   What it establishes: The order's structure — Section 1 (Purpose and
   Policy), Section 2 (directs USCIS and the Social Security Administration to
   compile and share a "state citizenship list" with state election officials,
   and directs the Department of Justice to treat noncitizen-voting cases as a
   prosecution priority), Section 3 (directs the U.S. Postal Service to
   promulgate rules on mail-ballot handling — envelope markings, list
   requirements, and delivery/return conditions for ballots in federal
   elections), Section 4 (Implementation), Section 5 (Enforcement and record
   retention), Section 6 (Severability), Section 7 (General Provisions).
   Locator: Sections 1-7, as paginated in the public-inspection PDF.
   Note: the exact title language and full operative-section text were
   captured verbatim during the prior research session but the verbatim
   passage text is not reproduced in this evidence file beyond what is needed
   to support the claims below and in the Numbers section; the writer should
   pull directly from the cited Federal Register PDF for exact operative
   quotations of Sections 2, 3, and 5.

2. **Indira Talwani, U.S. District Judge, District of Massachusetts —
   Memorandum & Order, June 25, 2026, Case No. 1:26-cv-11581-IT, *State of
   California et al. v. Trump et al.*** (37-page substantive ruling; 20 pages
   read via the court's PDF).
   URL: https://www.mad.uscourts.gov (case docket 1:26-cv-11581-IT, Document
   entry for the June 25, 2026 Memorandum & Order — read via the court's
   published opinion PDF).
   Primary/secondary: **Primary.** This is the court's own ruling — the
   authoring judicial body deciding the case.
   What it establishes: Declares EO 14399 Sections 2 and 3 "legally void" as
   ultra vires and unconstitutional, holding the Elections Clause (Art. I, §4,
   cl. 1) and the Electors Clause (Art. II, §1, cl. 2) commit authority over
   the times, places, and manner of federal elections to the states (subject
   to congressional override), not to the President acting alone; finds
   Section 5's enforcement/record-retention language "merely precatory"
   (i.e., not itself an enforceable command, so not something to enjoin).
   Permanently enjoins the federal defendants (not the President personally)
   from implementing Sections 2 and 3 as to the 23 Plaintiff States (and
   Pennsylvania's governor, see Numbers) for the November 3, 2026 federal
   election "or any earlier federal election."
   Locator: Memorandum & Order, Case 1:26-cv-11581-IT, Doc. entry June 25,
   2026; injunction terms at the order's concluding paragraphs (four
   sub-parts, a-d).

3. **Indira Talwani, U.S. District Judge — consolidated Memorandum & Order,
   June 18, 2026, Case No. 1:26-cv-11549-IT (*League of Women Voters of
   Massachusetts v. Trump*) and Case No. 1:26-cv-11581-IT (*State of
   California v. Trump*), Document 166/190** (17 pages read in full).
   URL: https://www.mad.uscourts.gov (case dockets 1:26-cv-11549-IT and
   1:26-cv-11581-IT, consolidated order).
   Primary/secondary: **Primary.** Same authoring court.
   What it establishes: Rules on prudential ripeness — dismisses claims
   without prejudice as to federal elections occurring *after* November 3,
   2026, as unripe, but declines to dismiss claims concerning the November 3,
   2026 election and any earlier federal election, which remain live. This is
   the ruling that fixes the November 3, 2026 election as the temporal
   boundary of what is currently blocked.
   Locator: Memorandum & Order, June 18, 2026, ripeness analysis section.

4. **Indira Talwani, U.S. District Judge — Order denying stay pending appeal,

   URL: https://www.mad.uscourts.gov (case docket 1:26-cv-11581-IT, Document

   Primary/secondary: **Primary.**

## Counter holders (the steelmanned opposing case)

- **Solicitor General D. John Sauer**, in the government's Supreme Court
  Application for Stay (No. 26A124, filed July 2026): argues, in the
  government's own strongest form, that the case is not yet fit for judicial
  resolution at all — the plaintiff states have not shown the concrete,
  non-speculative injury Article III requires (citing *Trump v. New York* and
  *Clapper v. Amnesty International USA*) because EO 14399's Sections 2 and 3
  have not yet been implemented against them in a way that has caused
  cognizable harm. This is the administration's actual, filed legal position
  — not a merits defense of unilateral executive authority — and the
  steelmanned counter should represent it accurately as a standing/ripeness
  argument, not invent a merits argument the government itself declined to
  make in this filing.
- **Missouri AG Catherine Hanaway, Missouri SG Louis J. Capozzi III, and
  co-signing state attorneys general** (Alabama's Steve Marshall, Florida's
  James Uthmeier, Kansas's Kris Kobach, Texas's Ken Paxton, and others), in
  the intervenor states' Application for Stay (No. 26A, filed July 28, 2026):
  frame EO 14399 as "promoting election integrity" and assert the federal
  government has a legitimate interest in accurate citizenship verification
  data for federal elections (the SSA/USCIS data-sharing rationale) and in
  preventing noncitizen voting; also argue the question merits Supreme Court
  review now because of a split with the D.C. Circuit's approach to a related
  USPS mail-ballot rulemaking dispute (*NAACP v. USPS*).
- **Judge Dunlap** (1st Cir.), concurring in part and dissenting in part from
  the panel's July 25, 2026 order: the closest thing in the record to a
  reasoned judicial defense of partial executive authority. Dunlap would stay
  the injunction as to EO 14399 Section 2(a) and part of Section 2(b) — the
  citizenship-list-compilation provisions only — on the theory that the
  federal government's role in verifying citizenship through SSA/USCIS data
  is a distinguishable, longstanding federal function separate from
  regulating ballot-handling mechanics, and that the balance of harms favors
  allowing that narrower function to proceed pending full merits review.
  Dunlap agrees the Section 3 (USPS/mail-ballot) injunction should remain.

## Contradictions

- **Merits vs. standing, a real gap, not an oversight.** The two SCOTUS stay
  applications (SG and intervenor states) do not present the same argument
  the lower courts actually rejected. Talwani and the 1st Circuit majority
  ruled against the government on the merits (Elections/Electors Clause
  authority). The SG's own SCOTUS application does not squarely re-argue that
  merits question — it argues standing/ripeness instead. This means the
  "opposing view" that would most directly rebut the paper's Elections-Clause
  position is not the government's actual filed Supreme Court argument. The
  piece should represent this precisely: the administration's live emergency
  filing argues procedure (standing), while Dunlap's dissent and the
  intervenor states' filing carry more of the merits-adjacent defense.
  Collapsing these into a single "the administration says X" claim would
  misstate the record.
- **Which provisions are actually blocked, precisely.** Both EO Section 2
  (state citizenship list/DOJ prosecution priority) and Section 3 (USPS
  mail-ballot rulemaking) remain enjoined as of the 1st Circuit's July 25,
  2026 order — but only (a) as to the 23 Plaintiff States, D.C., and
  Pennsylvania (via Governor Shapiro), not nationwide; (b) only for the
  November 3, 2026 federal election or any earlier one — claims about later
  elections were dismissed without prejudice as unripe by Talwani's June 18
  order; and (c) the injunction runs against the federal defendants
  (agencies/officials), not against the President personally. Section 5
  (enforcement/record retention) was found merely "precatory" by Talwani —
  i.e., not blocked because it was never an enforceable command to begin
  with, a distinct status from "enjoined." Dunlap's dissent would additionally
  lift the injunction as to Section 2(a) and part of 2(b) specifically, if his
  view had prevailed — it did not; the majority left the full Section 2 and
  Section 3 injunction intact.
- **No independently sourced academic defender of EO 14399 found.** Despite
  targeted searches for named election-law scholars defending the order's
  constitutionality (e.g., searches for commentary by scholars known for
  broad Article II readings), none produced a dated, quotable statement
  specific to EO 14399. The steelmanned counter in this evidence file is
  therefore built from the government's and intervenor states' own filed
  arguments, which the brief itself lists as an acceptable and indeed
  preferable source for the counter ("the Solicitor General's/DOJ's actual
  argument").

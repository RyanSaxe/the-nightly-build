# Commission — opinion/mail-in-voting-order

## Assignment
One Opinion on the `opinion` template (900–2500 words). Occasion: the 1st U.S.
Circuit Court of Appeals' 2-1 denial (late July 2026) of the Justice
Department's request to stay a June district-court ruling blocking provisions of
President Trump's March 2026 executive order on voter lists and mail-ballot
delivery, and the administration's July 27 emergency application to the Supreme
Court to let the order take effect for the November midterms.

## The position (disclosed on the position card)
**The president has no constitutional authority to set who may vote or how mail
ballots are delivered in federal elections; the Elections Clause (Art. I, §4)
and the states' role place that power with Congress and the states, so the
courts were right to keep the order blocked and the Supreme Court should not
clear it on the emergency docket.** This is a legal/structural argument about
who holds the power, argued from the public record — not a partisan claim about
voting policy on the merits.

The position card names **holders with standing who actually hold this view**,
cited: the 23 state attorneys general who sued, the district judge and the 1st
Circuit majority (quote their reasoning), and named election-law scholars on
record for the Elections-Clause reading. The researcher must supply real named
holders and their cited words — the desk is the advocate, never the inventor.
Title and dek sell the question/consequence and must NOT restate the card.

## The counter (mandatory, steelmanned)
State the strongest opposing case in the form its own believers would sign,
cited to named holders (the Solicitor General's/DOJ's actual argument; scholars
who defend broad Article II authority over federal-election administration and
the federal interest in an accurate citizenship list; the states supporting the
order). The real counter includes: the executive's role in administering federal
law (SSA/USCIS data), the anti-noncitizen-voting rationale, and any statutory
hooks the administration invokes. Answer it on the law, not by caricature. A
counter built to be knocked down fails the piece.

## Accountability
Say plainly what would change the desk's judgment (e.g., a Supreme Court holding
that the specific provisions fall within delegated executive authority, or a
statute Congress passed authorizing the list). The paper can be wrong here — if
SCOTUS upholds the order, the argument failed. Own that.

# Editorial review: current-events/2026-08-14 (editor/01)

## Skeptic

The brief's thesis is carried by its title and dek: the Solicitor General has
asked the Supreme Court to make a second, nationwide mail-voting injunction fall
together with the first one already under stay review, before mail ballots go
out for the November 3 election. The six items each stand on a named actor and a
mechanism. I tested every load-bearing claim.

Item 1 (voting). The two-injunction structure checks against the primary filing
and the Talwani order: a June 25 permanent injunction limited to 23 states and
D.C., and an August 11 preliminary injunction reaching all 50 states, with the
August 12 supplemental brief asking that a stay of the first also stay the
second. Terminology holds: "permanent injunction" is used only for the June 25
California ruling (the label the SG's own filing uses twice) and "preliminary
injunction" only for the August 11 League of Women Voters ruling. The heading,
however, led on "Twelve states back DOJ's push" — a figure that lives only in
the Democracy Docket secondary (s3), while the heading's citation and link point
to the SG filing (s1), which the evidence expressly says "does not enumerate [the
split] by name." That put an s3-only quantity in display text under an s1 cite,
and it buried the item's actual finding behind a supporting count. Rewritten to
state the finding with its actor, cited to the filing it links; the twelve-states
detail stays in the body where s3 supports it.

Item 4 (Yemen) held the hardest scrutiny and produced the one factual break. The
lead sentence read "153 killed and 243 injured, all but five of them from three
airstrikes in Yemen." The three Yemen strikes are the report's sole 2025
incidents meeting its standard, and the killed column sums to the entire total
(5 + 80 + 68 = 153); the injured column likewise (25 + 171 + 47 = 243). There is
no non-Yemen 2025 casualty for the "five" to name — the five is Sanaa's own toll,
one of the three Yemen strikes. The qualifier contradicted both the cited primary
and the article's own table. Corrected to "all of them." Recomputed the table
against the report's stated total: both columns are honest and sum exactly (153
killed, 243 injured), and it correctly uses the table's 68/47 for Saada (the
figure consistent with the total) while flagging that the report's adjoining
prose states the reverse. Two citations in this item pointed a primary-owned
claim at a secondary: the report's own "different types of information and
methodologies" language was cited to CBS (s9), and the table-sums-to-153 check
was cited to Stars and Stripes (s10). Both belong to the report itself (s8),
which was already in hand; reassigned. The independent-corroboration sentence
that follows keeps its Stars and Stripes cite (s10).

Item 5 (CPI). Verified every figure against the BLS primary directly: monthly
+0.1% (after June -0.4%), annual all-items +3.4%, core +0.2% monthly and +2.5%
annually, shelter +0.1%/+3.2% at roughly two-thirds of the monthly rise, energy
-1.5%/+14.7%. All match. The stat-strip's three figures (+0.1%, +3.4%, +2.5%) are
correct and each is carried in nearby prose. The energy figures (-1.5%/+14.7%),
which are BLS's, were cited only to NBC; added the BLS cite (s11) for the figure
and left NBC (s12) carrying the Iran/Brent attribution it actually owns.

Items 2, 3, 6 held on their primaries. Item 2 correctly frames the DOJ finding as
"reasonable cause to believe" and the Department's own conclusion "not a court's"
— an earned clarification of a real misreading, not invented parallelism. Item 3
correctly limits the Harvard ruling to a dismissal on the pleadings that does not
decide whether antisemitism occurred, and attributes the $1B clawback figure to
NPR as the evidence requires. Item 6 states the 250-day and 200-day figures as
Blumenthal's assertions and pairs them with Hegseth's rebuttal, precisely noting
he disputed the reporting's tenor without addressing the specific allegations.

Sourcing re-audit: each item carries exactly one primary plus at least one
independent secondary, and every data-nb-kind matches the authorship-and-stake
test — the SG filing, DOJ release, court orders, Pentagon report, BLS release,
and Blumenthal letter are all authoring-party primaries; the newsroom accounts
are all outside reporters. Every one of the 14 citation hrefs was opened as
printed: all resolve and land on the source itself (s1/s6/s8/s13 are the actual
document PDFs, s6 and s8 on stable public mirrors of the primary; s11 is
bot-gated on a bare request but renders on a browser-equivalent one, and is the
source's own stable archive address). Coherence confirmed: the
AI-training-copyright story is absent and no field-level technology item appears.

## Cut

The prose is already lean and figure-led; no sentence failed the slop test on the
sentence-by-sentence or edge passes. Every item opens on its actor and mechanism
with no dangling referent for a link arrival, and closes on a fact or a named
voice (Dhillon, the Hammack/Skordeles split, Hegseth) rather than a
reader-facing sign-off. No self-reference, no empty conclusions, no puffery, no
decorative "highlighting/underscoring" clauses, no vague attribution. The two
"not X" constructions (the DOJ finding "not a court's"; the dismissal that "does
not rule on whether antisemitism occurred") both correct real, named
misconceptions and stay. No borrowed phrasing from the voice-guide exemplars and
no leakage from the brief or commission.

The repeated pattern I did act on was formulaic headings measured against the
recent front pages named in the brief. "A judge dismisses DOJ's antisemitism
lawsuit against Harvard" was built like the recent "A federal judge clears sealed
Epstein investigation records for release"; recast to lead from Harvard's side.
"Consumer prices rise 0.1% in July, reversing June's drop" echoed "U.S. payrolls
fell by 23,000 in July"; recast to lead on the reversal. Both stay in this day's
own nouns. The furniture earns its place: the Yemen table is the item's
reason for being (it lets the reader run the arithmetic that resolves the
report's self-contradiction), and the CPI stat-strip surfaces the three figures
the item turns on. Neither is decorative; nothing else needed adding or removing.

## Reader

What the piece gives beyond its sources: a reader finishes knowing not just that
the Pentagon's Yemen count contradicts itself, but which of the two death tolls
is the real one and how to check it — the table sums to the report's own stated
153, the narrative's figures do not. That arithmetic verdict is the article's
own work, and it matches the original-work sentence in the draft handoff. Across
the brief the reader gets compressed judgments with the number that sizes each
one (the 23-vs-12 state split, the 49-day CRIPA clock, 3.4% annual inflation
against eroding real wages, 250 days at sea) rather than a recap of headlines.
The prose sits closer to the voice-guide exemplars than to a median summary:
numbers arrive before adjectives and named voices close the items. The headline,
read as the largest claim, is defended by item 1.

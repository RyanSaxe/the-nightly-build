## Contradictions

- **Spokane damage/evacuation figures vary by source and hour.** Structures
  destroyed: "at least 600" (Al Jazeera/NBC, area-wide) vs. "about 700"
  (Washington State EOC, Sunday-morning estimate) vs. "640 in the Old Trails
  Fire alone" (Fire Chief Cody Rohrbach). Evacuations: 60,000 (Al Jazeera) vs.
  65,000+ (governor-office-adjacent reporting) vs. "5,000+ homes." These are
  fast-moving preliminary counts; attribute to source and time, do not state a
  single hard number as settled.
- **Water-system attribution is disputed.** The FBI/CISA advisory attributes the
  broader PLC-exploitation campaign to Iran-affiliated actors, and investigators
  are probing an Iran link to these specific intrusions, but no federal agency
  made a definitive public attribution of the state incidents. Trump publicly
  rejected the Iran theory and blamed Minnesota Gov. Tim Walz. So: a standing
  Iran attribution for the campaign, but an open question on these breaches.
- **Water-system scope reported inconsistently.** "At least seven states" (FBI)
  with only Minnesota and Michigan named; Minnesota "30+" systems, Michigan
  "nine." One search result also named South Dakota; unconfirmed against a named
  official, treat as unverified.
- **Capital One vs. Trump entities on why the accounts closed.** Bank: a
  months-long AML review of suspicious transaction patterns. Plaintiffs:
  political retaliation for January 6 (their supporting section was filed
  redacted). Both cannot be true; the item should present the clash, not adopt
  either.
- **CR end-date and vote date.** House-passed bill runs through Dec. 4; Senate
  reporting cites Dec. 11. Cloture-vote day is listed as both 2026-08-03 and
  2026-08-04 across official schedules. Outcome unconfirmed.
- **Iran: whose deal.** Trump frames resumed talks and Hormuz as US-driven and a
  deal "imminent"; Iran frames the Hormuz route as a bilateral Iran-Oman
  arrangement and set no US deadline. The two framings compete.

# Evidence record: current-events/2026-08-11 (01)

The day's one large, clean US development is President Trump's 10 August executive
order narrowing the recommended childhood vaccine schedule; it has two primary
sources I read in full (the presidential action and the White House fact sheet)
and independent medical-establishment reaction, so it is well supported. Two
events genuinely dated 11 August are thinner: the DC National Guard deployment
reaches its one-year mark with fresh cost and crime figures, but I could reach
those figures only through a reputable secondary (Stars and Stripes) that
attributes them to primaries I could not open directly (the Senate HSGAC report
PDF and the CBO/Pentagon projections were not machine-readable); and Alabama held
a postponed special congressional primary in four districts, but its owning
primary is the Secretary of State's returns, which were still coming in on
election night, so no result is verified here. The remaining strong items
(the D.C. Circuit ballroom ruling, the revived effort to remove Fed Governor Lisa
Cook, and a second court block of HUD's homelessness-funding overhaul) all moved
on 5-9 August, not the 11th; I flag each date honestly so the editor can decide
whether they belong in an 11 August brief or only as build-on context. Two
primary PDFs (the ballroom opinion, the HSGAC report) resolve to live documents
but returned as binary my fetch tool could not parse, so their substance below is

## Contradictions

- **Disease count, 17 vs 18.** The White House fact sheet frames the reduction as
  18 to 11, reaching 18 by counting the RSV monoclonal-antibody immunization
  alongside 17 diseases; NPR and some wires report it as 17 to 11. Both describe
  the same schedule; the difference is whether the RSV product is counted as a
  disease. The writer should state the reduction with this denominator made
  explicit rather than pick one number.
- **Autism, order text vs remarks.** The reporting ties the order to autism, but the
  autism claim is in Trump's spoken remarks at the signing, not in the executive
  order or the fact sheet, both of which I read and neither of which mentions
  autism. Attribute the autism framing to his remarks, not to the order.
- **MMR "three shots" is not yet actionable.** The order recommends splitting MMR
  into three single-disease shots "once such products are domestically available";
  reporting notes separate measles, mumps, and rubella vaccines are not currently
  available in the United States and could take years to be, so the recommendation
  has no immediate effect on what a clinic can administer.
- **National Guard crime effect is mixed, not a win.** On the same MPD data, property
  crime fell by nearly a quarter while violent crime rose 3% after the deployment;
  the pre-deployment drop (violent crime down 26%, a 30-year low in 2024) predates
  the troops. The task force could not provide information on how troops were
  reducing crime. Any claim the deployment cut crime must carry this split.
- **Ballroom ruling was 2-1.** Judge Rao dissented; the panel was not unanimous.

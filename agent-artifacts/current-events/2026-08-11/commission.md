# Commission: current-events/2026-08-11

## Assignment

The US-focused general-news brief for Tuesday, 11 August 2026. Template `brief`:
4 to 6 itemized developments, each carrying its own primary record and at least
one independent account. This is authorized scheduled work from `nb duty`.

## Territory and selection

Select the day's most consequential developments in the United States: events
that change law, public policy, public institutions, or people's material
conditions. Significance decides the mix. Routine political theater and stories
that are merely popular do not qualify, and there is no topic quota to fill.

Anchor selection to what actually moved on or into 11 August 2026. When a story
this desk already covered has developed, say so in the item and build on the
prior coverage rather than restating it. Recent briefs have covered: the July
payrolls contraction and labor-force decline (08-08), the Todd Blanche
confirmation and the rescinded $1.8B fund (08-09), the 2026 measles surge and
the pending PAHO elimination-status review (08-10), Trump's mail-ballot request
to the Supreme Court (08-04), the 100% branded-drug tariff (08-04/08-07), and
the Spokane-area wildfires (08-03/08-05). Only carry one of these forward on a
genuine 11 August development.

Include an international story only where omitting it would make the brief
misleading, sized to its importance.

## Boundaries with the rest of tonight's edition

- The fair-use question of training AI on copyrighted books is tonight's
  `unbiased` piece (the Bartz v. Anthropic settlement). Do not run an item on
  the AI-copyright fair-use fight here.
- Developments internal to a technical, scientific, or AI field belong to
  tonight's `tech-news` brief. Take a technology story here only when its public
  or policy consequences are the news, and do not run the same underlying event
  that tech-news would treat as a field development. Tie-break: if the news is a
  law, ruling, policy, or material-conditions change, it is this brief's; if it
  changes technical knowledge or practice, it is tech-news's.

## Sources

Per item: exactly one primary (the owning record: the ruling, filing, agency
release, data table, or transcript) and at least one independent secondary,
preferring a reputable US newsroom of comparable quality. Use the primary record
regardless of country; use non-US reporting when it is closer to the event or
carries the original reporting. Every cited URL must resolve to the source's own
page.

## Required contribution

Each item adds what the headline dropped: the number, the caveat, the second
fact that decides how much it matters. The brief reads as one desk's judgment of
the day, not a scrape of the wire.

## Habits not to inherit

- Do not open the brief, or lead an item, on a bare date headline ("August 7,
  2026"); recent briefs have leaned on that. Lead with the development.
- Vary item construction. Recent deks lead heavily on a superlative-plus-number
  frame ("The largest ... in US history", "The first deaths in a record ...").
  Do not stamp every item to that mold.
- The tags array in nb-meta is the writer's to set from the items actually run.

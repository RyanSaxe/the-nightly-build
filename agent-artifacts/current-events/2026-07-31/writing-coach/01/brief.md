## The commission in one line
A 4-6 item US current-events brief: each item a judgment about what moved,
carrying the number or caveat the headline dropped, standing alone, in a calm
serious register — not a recap, not a feed.

## What to study (at least three respected sources)
Study how the best news briefs and wire desks write a tight item: the discipline
of a Reuters/AP lede, the Economist's "The world in brief" compression, and a
strong newsletter brief (e.g. Axios' get-to-the-point without the "why it
matters" cliché, taken as a caution not a model). Extract: how a one-item lede
carries subject-verb-consequence; how to add the dropped number/caveat in one
clause; how to avoid handing the point back to the reader; and how items differ
in shape so the brief does not read stamped.

## Output (write only this)
`agent-artifacts/current-events/2026-07-31/writing-coach/01/voice-guide.md`
Cover: register and sentence economy for a brief; how each item's prose should
add to (not restate) its headline; how to keep item openings varied; the
per-item primary+secondary citation habit read as prose; and tells to avoid
(feed voice, "why it matters" scaffolding, closing lines that hand the point
back, hedged-contrast deks, manufactured punchlines). Tie each move to a reason.
Keep it short.

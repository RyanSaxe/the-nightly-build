# Commission — current-events/2026-08-01

## Assignment
Tonight's US general-news brief on the `brief` template. 4–6 items. Each item is
a judgment about what moved and why it matters, not a recap. Date: 2026-08-01 (UTC).

## Selection standard
The day's (and the last ~48 hours') most consequential US developments that
change law, public policy, public institutions, or people's material
conditions. No topic quota. Routine political theater and merely-popular stories
do not qualify. Include an international story only if omitting it would make the
brief misleading, sized to its importance. Technology belongs here only when its
*public consequences* are the news (field developments go to Tech News, which
runs tonight — deconflict).

## Deconfliction (important)
Tonight's Opinion desk argues a precise position on the **mail-in voting
executive order** the appeals court blocked. Current Events should NOT center
that story as an item; if it is genuinely unavoidable as one of the day's
biggest developments, cover a *different facet* factually and briefly, and never
argue it — leave the argument to Opinion. Prefer four to six other consequential
developments.

## Per-item source obligation
Each item: exactly **1 primary** (the ruling, filing, bill text, agency
statement, dataset, or the party's own statement about itself) + **≥1
independent secondary** (a reputable newsroom's original reporting). Prefer a
US-based newsroom for independent reporting when quality is comparable; use the
primary record regardless of country; use non-US reporting when it holds
important original reporting or is closer to the event. The item headline links
to the primary source.

## Voice / contribution
Wire-service register for the house reader (math/CS, ML-eng, well-read): assume
they saw the headline; add the number, the caveat, the mechanism the headline
dropped. Each item stands alone and needs no longer article to land.

## Habits not to inherit
Vary item order and headline shapes; no colon-subtitle headlines; no item that
closes by handing the point back to the reader. Do not force a through-line dek
that overclaims a theme across unrelated items — the dek can name the night's
real center of gravity or stay plain.

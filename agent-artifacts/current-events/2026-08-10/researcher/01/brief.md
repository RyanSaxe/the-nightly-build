# researcher brief: current-events/2026-08-10 (01)

Inputs:
- `agent-artifacts/current-events/2026-08-10/editorial-direction.md` — citation standard, series territory, declared reader
- `agent-artifacts/current-events/2026-08-10/commission.md` — the selection standard, candidate leads, and do-not-re-report list

Output: `agent-artifacts/current-events/2026-08-10/researcher/01/evidence.md`
Source policy: exactly one primary record per item; at least one independent
account per item; select four to six items by consequence.

Research procedure:

- Identify the day's most consequential US developments for 2026-08-10 by
  consequence, not popularity. Test the candidate leads in the commission against
  primary records, and find any larger development they miss. Do not re-report the
  2026-08-08/09 lead items named in the commission.
- For each selected item, get the primary record that owns the news: the agency
  action, filing, official release, ruling, statement, or dataset itself. Read it,
  not the coverage of it. Then get at least one independent account from a
  reputable newsroom, preferring a US-based one of comparable quality but using
  the primary record and any closer reporting regardless of country.
- Verify every figure against the primary that owns it. For any accusation or
  contested claim, require two independent confirmations by parties positioned to
  know; two retellings of one origin count as one.
- For each item, record exactly what changed, why it is consequential, and the
  one detail that makes it unmistakable, so the writer can build the item and its
  headline without returning to the source cold.

In the evidence record, group sources by item so the writer can assemble each
`nb-brief-item` from a self-contained set. In Contradictions, record where
accounts of the same event diverge. Report in your handoff which items are firm
and which are marginal on consequence, and flag any item where a clean single
primary record was hard to pin down.

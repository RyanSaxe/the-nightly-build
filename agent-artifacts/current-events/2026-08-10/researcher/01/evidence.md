## Item 1 (firm): U.S. measles elimination-status review, with 2026 the worst year since elimination

What changed: the U.S. National Verification Committee reviews 2026 case data
this month and forwards its opinion to PAHO, which decides in November whether
the United States keeps the measles-elimination status it has held since 2000.
2026 cases (2,465 as of Aug 6) already exceed the full-2025 total (2,289), and
the country has had measles every week since January 2025 — the sustained
transmission that can end elimination.

The unmistakable detail: no U.S. measles deaths in 2026, yet the elimination
status is what is at risk, because the standard is 12+ months of continuous
domestic transmission, not a death toll.

## Item 2 (firm on consequence; dating caveat): SAMHSA bars federal grant money from drug-checking test strips and syringes

What changed: SAMHSA's April 24, 2026 Dear Colleague letter withdrew federal
grant eligibility for buying or distributing fentanyl/xylazine test strips,
sterile syringes, safer-smoking supplies, and overdose hotlines, on the ground
that these "facilitate illicit drug use." The national reporting hook that puts
it in the 2026-08-10 window is NPR's Aug 8, 2026 account of the ban's effect on
programs. The policy document itself is April; the news of the day is its
downstream harm, not a fresh order.

The unmistakable detail: the same test strip a public-health worker or police
officer may still buy with federal money is now barred when handed to the person
who would use it — the carve-out is by who holds the strip, not what it is.

## Item 3 (firm): Supreme Court weighs emergency bid to enforce Trump's mail-in voting order before the midterms

What changed: after the First Circuit upheld a Boston district-court order
blocking the U.S. Postal Service from implementing the March 2026 executive
order, the Justice Department filed an emergency application (July 27, 2026)
asking the Supreme Court to let the order take effect nationwide for the November
midterms. A 23-state-plus-D.C. coalition filed its opposition Aug 4, 2026; a
ruling can come at any time. As of Aug 10 no ruling has issued.

The unmistakable detail: the fight is over timing as much as merits. The
administration argues the challengers "sued too soon," positioning to invoke the
Purcell principle against last-minute changes closer to the election, while USPS
has itself told the court it is running out of time to build the system the order
demands.

## Item 4 (marginal — no single-day primary): Who pays for the grid that data centers need

What changed at the federal level: FERC ordered PJM, the largest U.S. grid
operator, to write new rules for large co-located loads (AI data centers among
them), squarely raising who pays when a data center needs new substations and
lines. The order is dated 2025-12-18 (Docket EL25-49-000). Its consequence is
live in August 2026 because the compliance and reporting phase is still running,
and state utility commissions (e.g. Pennsylvania) are acting on the same
ratepayer pressure. There is no clean 2026-08-10 primary action; this is an
evolving proceeding, which is why the item is marginal for a day-dated brief.

The unmistakable detail: the fight is not whether data centers pay for their own
connection, but whether they also owe a standing charge to help maintain the
shared transmission system every other ratepayer relies on.

## Contradictions

- Measles vaccination share. One CDC data-page read returned "89% unvaccinated
  or unknown" that appeared to draw on a 2024 baseline; the CDC weekly update as
  reported by CIDRAP gives 93% for 2026 patients. Use 93% for 2026 and attribute
  to the CDC weekly update.
- SAMHSA date and signatory. Accounts say "April 24," "late April," and carry an
  April 27 publication; the letter itself is dated April 24, 2026. Only STAT
  names the signer (acting SAMHSA leader Chris Carroll); NPR leaves the signer
  unnamed. Treat the name as single-sourced.
- Data-center timeline. The FERC order is dated Dec 18, 2025 (EL25-49-000), while
  other August coverage references a mid-August 2026 PJM deadline; these are
  different phases of one proceeding, not one dated action. This is the reason
  the item lacks a clean single-day primary.
- A stray search result asserted an August 2026 payrolls figure of "+22,000."
  The August Employment Situation is not released until September, so any August
  jobs number on Aug 10 is spurious. Do not use it. See Discarded.

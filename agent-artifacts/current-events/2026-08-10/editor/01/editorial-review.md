# Editorial review: current-events/2026-08-10 (editor/01)

## Skeptic

Thesis: four developments of the day, each carried past its headline to the
non-obvious pivot that makes it consequential. The brief stands on four
item-level claims, each with one primary record and independent corroboration.

**Measles (lead).** Claim: 2,465 confirmed 2026 cases as of Aug 6 already exceed
the full-2025 total of 2,289, and continuous transmission since January 2025 puts
the elimination status won in 2000 at risk despite zero deaths. Opened the CDC
primary (s1): 2,465 cases, 38 outbreaks, 94% outbreak-associated (2,309 of 2,465);
2025 was 2,289 / 48 / 90%. CIDRAP (s2) confirms 93% unvaccinated-or-unknown, 7%
hospitalized vs 11% in 2025, no 2026 deaths, committee review this month, PAHO
decision in November. STAT (s4) confirms the review mechanism, weekly transmission
since Jan 2025, and that elimination is defined by transmission, not deaths. JHU
IVAC (s3, 403-gated, lands on source) confirms 2026 is the highest since 2000. The
table's four rows all reconcile to the primaries. The paradox in the dek (no
deaths, yet the status is in doubt) is a real, named misconception the item
explains, so it is an earned contrast, not the negative-parallelism mold. Held.
One point worth recording: CIDRAP notes 2025 had three measles deaths; the article
claims only zero deaths in 2026 and makes no false 2025-deaths claim, so it is
clean.

**SAMHSA.** Claim: a late-April directive barred federal grant money from test
strips, syringes, safer-smoking supplies, and overdose hotlines, drawn by who
holds the strip, signed by the acting leader, its effect now landing on programs.
This is the item the brief flagged. The primary letter (s5) is a binary PDF that
does not render to text (same as the researcher found); it resolves and lands on
the letter itself. Nothing in the article is cited to s5 as the letter's exact
quoted wording. The one quoted phrase — "facilitate illicit drug use and are
incompatible with Federal laws" — is attributed to "the administration says" and
cited to NPR (s6), and I confirmed both NPR (s6) and STAT (s7) independently
reproduce that phrase, so the quote is doubly corroborated and correctly routed
through the secondary account rather than asserted as the unread letter's text.
The signer's name (Chris Carroll) is single-sourced to STAT and the article says
so; "acting leader" is the safe title and is what the sentence leads with. The
carve-out and naloxone-as-preferred detail check against NPR. One fix: the article
said STAT "called it a clear break from harm reduction," but STAT's own word is
"shift" (its headline is "A clear shift from harm reduction"); changed "break" to
"shift" so the attributed characterization matches the source.

**Mail-in voting.** Claim: DOJ asked the Court on July 27 to let the March 31
order take effect for the midterms; the fight is about timing (Purcell / "sued too
soon") as much as merits; no ruling as of Aug 10. Opened the SCOTUS docket primary
(s8): No. 26A124, *Trump v. California*, docketed July 27, on First Circuit No.
26-1774; response Aug 3, reply Aug 4; no order listed. The docket/date nuance the
writer flagged is handled correctly — the prose does not pin the opposition day,
sidestepping the SCOTUSblog-vs-docket conflict, and "no ruling as of August 10" is
cited to the docket. Democracy Docket (s9) confirms the July 27 filing and the
First Circuit chain; Votebeat (s10) confirms the state-approved-list mechanism;
SCOTUSblog (s11) confirms 23 states + DC opposition, 12 states led by Alabama
supporting, and the "sued too soon"/Purcell posture. Two breaks found and both
fixed with sources at hand: (1) the clause "the Postal Service has told the court
it is running out of time" was cited to s11, but SCOTUSblog does not contain it;
the claim is true and owned by Votebeat (s10, "time is growing short"; USPS said in
July filings it is running out of time), so I re-cited that clause to s10 and kept
s11 on the sued-too-soon half. (2) The rs-docket "Stakes" line said the order
"governs mail voting in 24 jurisdictions" — 24 is the 23-states-plus-DC opposition
coalition, not the order's reach; the order is federal and nationwide (as the body
states), so I corrected the furniture to "nationwide."

**Data centers / FERC.** Claim: FERC's Dec 18, 2025 order directs PJM to write

news) is handled honestly in the prose ("still in its compliance phase, which is
why the fight is live now"). Held.

## Cut

Slop pass over every sentence, both edges, dangling referents, and furniture. The
prose is disciplined; judgment sits in what each item leads on and tallies, as the
voice guide directs, not in adjectives. Two sentences failed and were repaired:

- SAMHSA closer: "The effect is now landing on the programs that hand out the
  strips, which NPR reported this week are losing them" opened on the soft
  abstraction "the effect is now landing on." Rewritten to the plain fact:
  "Programs that hand out the strips are now losing them, NPR reported this week."
- FERC opener carried "the big co-located loads that AI data centers represent" —
  "represent" is the elaborate-copula tell from spec/slop.md. Trimmed to "the big
  co-located loads of AI data centers."

Checked and kept: "The open question is narrow but expensive" reads like an
engineered contrast, but both halves are substantiated (one cost-allocation
question; the ~$23B stakes) and the next sentence does the reporting, so it
survives the delete test as a compressed judgment the brief template invites.
"The dispute is as much about timing as the order itself" frames a genuine,
non-obvious analytical turn immediately backed by two concrete facts, so it is a
thesis line, not a signpost.

Headlines and deks against spec/headlines.md and the recent-pattern notes: the
lead is number-led subject-verb-surprise; the dek adds (no deaths, PAHO November,
transmission since Jan 2025, status won 2000) without restating the headline and
avoids the semicolon-reversal, suspended-question, and comma-triad molds. The four
item headings are built differently from one another (number-crossing, agency-
action, actor-asks-court, operator-must-decide); none is a colon subtitle, triad,
or Betteridge question. No formula against the recent record.

No prompt leakage, no self-reference ("STAT, the account closest to its release" is
attribution transparency for a single-sourced name, not newsroom narration), no
borrowed phrasing from the voice-guide exemplars. Punctuation is clean: no
em-dashes in body prose, colons used for a list and a payoff, no semicolons, no
comma splices.

# Writer brief — current-events/2026-08-21

## Item order and why

1. **Federal debt crosses $40 trillion** (National debt) — the broadest
   material-conditions story of the five: it touches every reader
   regardless of the other four items' subject matter, and it's the one
   with a clean single number driving the whole edition's dek.
2. **Fourth Circuit, ICE at houses of worship** (Immigration)
3. **N.D. Texas, ATF ghost-gun rule voided** (Second Amendment)
4. **Seventh Circuit, Planned Parenthood Indiana speech ruling**
   (Reproductive rights)
5. **Census Bureau noncitizen-voting brief** (Elections)

Items 2–4 are grouped together because each involves a federal court
declaring something in broad constitutional terms while granting relief on
narrow, party-specific terms — a real pattern across three separate,
unrelated dockets this week, not a manufactured link. Each item still has to
stand alone per the template identity, so the parallel is never stated
explicitly across items; it surfaces independently in each item's own
analysis paragraph, grounded in that item's own primary record.

## Per-item contribution (the "why it matters" requirement)

- **Debt:** the pace, not the round number, is the finding — five months for
  the most recent trillion, and a named mechanism (negative customs receipts
  from this year's tariff refunds) driving July's deficit.
- **ICE/RFRA:** the government's loss traces to a specific litigation
  failure (waiving the compelling-interest defense), and the injunction's
  real reach is far narrower than "court blocks ICE from churches" would
  suggest — named plaintiffs' addresses only, warrants still work, other
  protected-area categories untouched.
- **Ghost guns:** same shape as the ICE item from the opposite ideological
  direction — broad constitutional declaration, injunction bound to one
  company's four named products.
- **Planned Parenthood Indiana:** the unanimous outcome hides a real split in
  reasoning (Hamilton's speech-rights framing vs. Scudder's
  notary-requirement-only framing), and that split is exactly where the next
  state's "abortion trafficking" law will get tested.
- **Census report:** rather than repeating named critics' objections
  secondhand, the item derives its own critique from the report's own
  disclosed methodology (a 91.7 percent, and as low as 74.5 percent, match
  rate against the certified benchmark) and states the resulting share
  (0.015 percent) in terms the reader can weigh against that miss rate
  without needing to trust an outside expert's say-so.

## Furniture

None used. Five short items plus the required sources list is the template's
own complete shape; nothing here has a quantitative relationship, a
sequence, or a close reading that a chart, table, or other component would
carry better than the prose itself.

## Style guardrails checked against the coach's brief

- No item opens "[Official] [verb-past] on [date]."
- No item closes on an "X, and Y" synthesis tag.
- Headlines vary construction: a number-led headline, two noun-phrase
  headlines with a built-in contrast, a "what X couldn't do" headline, and
  one actor-verb-object headline (the only one of the five).
- Dek is one sentence, anchored to the debt item's own figures, no
  banned dek mold.

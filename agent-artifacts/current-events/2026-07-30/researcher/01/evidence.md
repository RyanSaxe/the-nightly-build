# Evidence record — current-events/2026-07-30

The evidence supports a four-item brief on a single day (2026-07-29, with the
Fed and the Iran exchange dated that day and the CMS and Conference Board
actions dated 07-28) in which a Middle East war and a strained US economy meet:
the US and Iran exchanged direct military fire; the Federal Reserve held rates
in a 9-3 split with three governors wanting a hike; CMS ended the temporary
subsidy that had held down Medicare drug-plan premiums; and the Conference
Board's confidence index slid for a third straight month on the present-
conditions gauge. Each development has a government or authoring-party primary
that resolves and at least one independent, fetchable secondary. The evidence is
thin on two points, both flagged below: exact strike counts and the specific US
bases Iran targeted (CENTCOM gives categories, not a tally), and the 2027
premium *dollar* increase individual Part D enrollees will see (CMS and KFF both
say larger increases are likely but not yet quantified per plan). GDP Q2 is
excluded: it releases 8:30am ET July 30, after this run.

## Contradictions
- **Date of the Iran strikes vs the missile attack.** CENTCOM dates the US
  strikes July 29 (10 p.m. ET) in response to a July 28 Iranian attack; some
  secondary heds compress both into "July 29." Resolution: attack July 28
  (intercepted), US strikes late July 29. Write it that way.
- **"Confidence fell" magnitude.** The headline index moved only -1.4 (90.8 from
  92.2) — small. The stronger, non-hyped signal is the Present Situation Index's
  third straight monthly decline (-3.6 to 114.9) and Expectations stuck at 74.7,
  below the Board's 80 recession-warning line. Weigh the sub-indexes, not the
  small headline move, or the item overstates.
- **CMS "raises premiums."** CMS/Oz framing (via gated WaPo/Newsweek) stresses
  small average changes; KFF stresses larger increases for *some* stand-alone
  PDP enrollees. Primary supports only that the subsidy ends and 2027 bids are
  set; the premium *increase* is a likelihood, not a set figure. Write "could/
  likely," not "will raise by $X."
- **Fed hold, dovish or hawkish.** A hold reads dovish until the split and the
  chair's words are added: three governors wanted a hike and the chair signaled
  "no tolerance" for inflation. It is a hawkish hold. The 9-3 split and Warsh
  quote must both appear or the item misleads.

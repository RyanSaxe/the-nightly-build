## 1. Iran–U.S. escalation

Primary: U.S. Central Command, “CENTCOM Destroys 3 IRGC Oil Tankers After
Iran Targets 2 U.S. Navy Warships,” September 5, 2026.
<https://www.centcom.mil/MEDIA/PUBLIC-RELEASES/Article/4591744/centcom-destroys-3-irgc-oil-tankers-after-iran-targets-2-us-navy-warships/>

Supports: CENTCOM says its forces struck three Iranian crude carriers after the
IRGC launched ballistic missiles toward two U.S. Navy warships. It says an
aircraft carrier and guided-missile destroyer evaded multiple attacks and no
American personnel were harmed. It names M/T Downy, M/T Stark 1, and M/T Kylo
(also called Noxen), and describes the first two as permanently disabled and
the third as destroyed after its crew abandoned ship.

Secondary: Reuters, “US military strikes three Iranian crude oil carriers,
Central Command says,” September 5, 2026.
<https://www.reuters.com/world/middle-east/us-military-strikes-three-iranian-crude-oil-carriers-central-command-says-2026-09-05/>

Supports: Reuters independently reports the sequence as a U.S. strike on three
Iranian crude tankers after Iran launched ballistic missiles at two U.S. Navy
ships, and repeats that CENTCOM reported no American personnel were harmed.

Writer constraint: The public evidence in these two records is CENTCOM's
operational account plus Reuters' report of it. Attribute the missile sequence
and vessel details to CENTCOM; do not infer wider military effects.

## 2. Modified SAVE and voter-list checks

Primary: United States Court of Appeals for the District of Columbia Circuit,
No. 26-5243, order filed September 4, 2026.
<https://media.cadc.uscourts.gov/orders/docs/2026/09/26-5243LDSN.pdf>

Supports: The court denied the government's motion for a stay pending appeal,
said appellants had not met the stringent requirements for a stay, and noted
that Judge Katsas would have granted it. The court expedited the consolidated
appeals. The opinion describes modified SAVE as a DHS system connected to SSA
records, searchable with full or partial SSNs, able to return information about
U.S.-born citizens, and capable of bulk queries. It says the district court had
granted plaintiffs summary judgment and set aside modified SAVE and related
systems-of-record notices. The opinion gives examples of naturalized citizens
whose SSA records still list them as noncitizens and says a failed SAVE match
could lead to a request for proof of citizenship or removal from the rolls.

Secondary: Reuters, “Federal appeals court upholds ban on Trump's bid to use
citizenship data for voter checks,” September 5, 2026.
<https://www.reuters.com/legal/government/federal-appeals-court-upholds-ban-trumps-bid-use-citizenship-data-voter-checks-2026-09-05/>

Supports: Reuters reports the 2–1 D.C. Circuit decision declining to lift the
lower-court ban, the court's concerns about Social Security Act authority and
private information, the risk that inaccurate records could require proof or
cancel registrations, and the expedited posture.

Writer constraint: Call the operative result a denied stay / ban left in
place, not a final ruling by the D.C. Circuit on the appeal's merits.

## 3. Broadcast ad rates

Primary: Supreme Court of the United States, *National Republican
Congressional Committee, et al. v. Sherrod Brown, et al.*, No. 26A274, per
curiam order, September 4, 2026.
<https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/25pdf/26a274_l537.pdf>

Supports: The FCC's March 30 notice said political candidates, joint
fundraising committees with noncandidate members, and political parties in
coordinated activity could receive lowest-unit-charge rates under conditions.
The Fourth Circuit set that notice aside on August 25. The Supreme Court
granted the party committees' stay application, recalled and stayed the Fourth
Circuit mandate pending a timely certiorari petition, and said current and
future rescissions would make committees pay more in the weeks before the
midterms. Justice Jackson dissented.

Secondary: Reuters, “US Supreme Court preserves parties' access to cheap ads,
siding with Republicans,” September 4, 2026.
<https://www.reuters.com/world/us-supreme-court-preserves-parties-access-cheap-ads-siding-with-republicans-2026-09-04/>

Supports: Reuters says the ruling restored discounted TV and radio rates for
party committees, with the 60-day general-election window opening September 4.
It reports the NRSC and NRCC requested the stay, Jackson was the only public
dissenter, and the lower-court ruling had blocked the rates.

Writer constraint: “Restores” means restores while the stay operates. Do not
claim the Court has finally upheld the FCC's interpretation.

## 4. August employment report

Primary: U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics, “The Employment Situation — August
2026,” September 4, 2026.
<https://www.bls.gov/news.Release/pdf/empsit.Pdf>

Supports: Nonfarm payroll employment rose by 162,000 in August and the
unemployment rate held at 4.1%. The August payroll gain exceeded the prior
twelve-month average of 31,000. Food services and drinking places added 59,000;
local-government education added 42,000; information lost 23,000. The labor
force participation rate edged up to 61.6% but was down 0.5 percentage point
since January. Long-term unemployed people numbered 1.9 million, 27.0% of all
unemployed. Average private hourly earnings rose 0.3% on the month and 3.1%
over the year to $37.75. June and July payrolls were revised up by 55,000 in
combination.

Secondary: Reuters, “US nonfarm payrolls surge in August, unemployment rate
steady at 4.1%,” September 4, 2026.
<https://www.reuters.com/business/us-nonfarm-payrolls-surge-august-unemployment-rate-steady-41-2026-09-04/>

Supports: Reuters independently reports the 162,000 gain, 4.1% unemployment,
61.6% participation rate, and the market implication of a stronger-than-
expected report.

Writer constraint: The BLS release is the authority for the figures. Use the
composition and participation/long-term-unemployment figures to keep the item
from treating one strong payroll month as a complete labor-market diagnosis.

# Voice guide · Current Events · 2026-09-06

- Lead every item with the concrete event and its date or operative status.
- Keep each item to two compact paragraphs. The first establishes what moved;
  the second explains the consequence or the limit of the evidence.
- Use active verbs and specific institutions. Attribute operational claims to
  the issuing body, especially for CENTCOM and the courts.
- Treat a stay, mandate recall, or expedited appeal as procedural posture. Do
  not promote an interim order into a final merits result.
- Give exact figures with units and a comparison when the scale is not obvious.
  Use “year over year” for the BLS wage figure and preserve BLS's distinction
  between household and establishment measures.
- Headlines state the development in present tense where natural. Deks join
  the day's concrete subjects; they do not announce importance.
- Inline citations sit immediately after the claim they support. The source
  list pairs each primary document with one independent account.
- Cut filler transitions, generic stakes, decorative “highlighting” clauses,
  invented contrasts, vague expert attribution, and self-reference.

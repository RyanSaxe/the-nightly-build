# Voice guide: current-events/2026-08-08 (writing-coach 01)

## Directive

Write in the wire-brief register, not the paper's patient teaching voice. The
house baseline — Hashimoto's calm first-principles build, Olah and Weng's
build-each-concept-before-the-sentence-that-needs-it — is calibrated for a long
piece with room to develop an idea. A brief item has three or four sentences and
a reader who already saw the headline. Keep the house plainness and precision,
but front-load the judgment the teaching voice would build toward: the
consequence leads, the mechanism and the number follow, the item ends where the
argument does. The best writers in this genre reach a verdict in the first
sentence and spend the rest earning it; the default would spend three sentences
approaching it.

The reader is well-read and general, and has the day's headlines already. Do not
orient them to the story. Give them what the headline dropped: the figure, the
caveat, the reason it matters. Every sentence either advances the development,
supplies a number, or names the stake. A sentence that re-tells what a reader who
saw the headline already knows is the recap the template forbids; strike it and
keep writing.

Attribution is the craft that carries this brief. Each item leans on a primary
record and at least one independent account, and the prose keeps them legible:
the reader can always tell whether a claim comes from the document itself or from
someone characterizing it. Contested items get their counter-claim as one
attributed clause weighted to the evidence, never as a reflexive balancing the
record does not earn.

## Licenses

form: the consequence-first lead
move: The strongest briefs open on what the development changes, not on the
  procedural fact of it happening. The Reuters column opens on the stake (markets
  in a holding pattern as attention turns to a jobs report and a conflict deal),
  and the missile report opens on depletion that is "raising concerns not just in
  this war ... but also for future conflicts." The mechanism arrives second.
bar: The item's first sentence names the change to law, policy, an institution,
  or people's material conditions. A reader who stops after it already knows why
  the item earned its slot. If the first sentence only reports that a thing
  happened, the lead is unwritten.

form: the figure carried with its source in the same breath
move: Serious reports set a striking number beside its provenance so it never
  floats: a stockpile "dropped by about two-thirds since the war began," per a
  report the outlet confirmed itself; a "100-foot buffer" that is the bill's own
  provision. Provenance travels with the number, not a sentence later.
bar: Every load-bearing figure sits next to the primary record or the reporting
  that established it, and a contested figure names whose count it is. A number
  without its source in the same sentence gets one or is cut.

form: primary record set against independent account
move: The wire lead states the mechanism from the document itself (what the bill
  does), attributes motive and framing to the actors who supplied them ("Cruz
  said in a statement"), and gives an outside body its own clause. Each claim
  wears its source, so the reader never has to guess which mode they are in.
bar: For every claim in the item, a reader can tell whether it comes from the
  primary record or from someone characterizing that record. Where the two blur
  into one unattributed assertion, the item has failed its sourcing.

form: the counter-claim folded in without false balance
move: The missile report carries the official rebuttal as a named, direct quote
  set against the documented figures, and does not grant the denial equal
  structural weight to the reporting. The legislative lead states opponents'
  objection as attributed concern, not as the writer's own hedge. The dissent
  appears once, owned by whoever holds it, sized to what the evidence supports.
bar: A denial or counter-claim is attributed to who said it and weighted to the
  record, not mirrored against the reporting to look even-handed. If the clause
  exists only to appear balanced, cut it; if the evidence genuinely divides, say
  by how much and on what.

## Mike Dolan, "Morning Bid: Gold stirs" (Reuters)
Source: https://wtaq.com/2026/08/06/morning-bid-gold-stirs/
Craft:
- cadence: Opens on one long orienting sentence that names the day's stakes, then
  drops to short declaratives that deliver each item. The length shift does the
  pacing a longer piece gets from paragraph breaks.
- argument: Front-loads the judgment (what markets are waiting on and why), then
  supplies the moving parts as support. The verdict is the first thing, not the
  last.
- evidence: Specific instruments and levels, each tied to the move it explains;
  no figure sits idle as color.
- stance: Confident and forward-leaning. States what matters today and is willing
  to say which data point the day turns on.
- notice: Catches the second-order effect — not that a number moved, but what the
  move signals about what participants expect next.
- diction: Concrete active verbs ("slipped," "homed in"). Plain nouns, no
  atmosphere words standing in for a fact.
- reader: Assumes a reader who follows the story daily and needs the day's delta,
  not a briefing on the whole arc.
- Do not transfer its first person or its "I'll get into that below"
  self-narration; both are house-banned. What transfers is the consequence-first
  open and the long-then-short cadence, not the columnist's persona.

## JTA, "Lawmakers seek 100-foot buffer zone around houses of worship to protect prayer goers from protests"
Source: https://www.jta.org/2026/08/06/politics/lawmakers-seek-100-foot-buffer-zone-around-houses-of-worship-to-protect-prayer-goers-from-protests
Craft:
- cadence: A factual lead sentence names actors and aim, then a second sentence
  states the mechanism, then attributed voices follow. Steady, one idea per
  sentence.
- argument: Reports the development and lets the mechanism carry the significance
  ("mirroring similar legislation recently enacted in New York" locates it
  without editorializing).
- evidence: The bill's own provisions supply the load-bearing facts; the
  100-foot figure is the statute's, not a characterization.
- stance: Neutral by construction. "Introduced," "aimed at" describe without
  grading merit. The writer never appears.
- notice: Places the bill against a real antecedent (a state law, prior
  opposition) so the reader sees the stakes without being told them.
- diction: Legislative register kept plain; quotes carry the advocacy so the
  prose does not have to.
- reader: Assumes a reader who can hold a First Amendment frame; explains the
  bill's operation, not the concept.
- The counter-view arrives as attributed concern ("civil rights leaders who
  said it would have a chilling effect"), not as the writer's hedge. That is the
  clean-attribution model for a contested item.

## NPR, "US precision missile shortage"
Source: https://www.npr.org/2026/08/04/nx-s1-5919286/us-precision-missile-shortage
Craft:
- cadence: Leads on the consequence in a flat declarative, escalates to the
  broader implication, then names the source. Stakes rise before the sourcing
  arrives.
- argument: The depletion is the premise; the "so what" — readiness for future
  conflicts — is stated up front, not saved for a conclusion.
- evidence: A hard proportion ("about two-thirds") anchored to reporting the
  outlet says it confirmed itself, so the striking figure is not left on trust.
- stance: Grave without hyperbole. "Seriously depleted," "used up most" carry
  weight without reaching for alarm.
- notice: Distinguishes this war's cost from the standing question it raises for
  future ones — the second-order stake, not just the headline number.
- diction: Plain, concrete; technical names (the missile systems) ground the
  claim rather than decorate it.
- reader: Assumes a reader who has followed the conflict and needs the new,
  quantified development, not a recap of the war.
- The official pushback is a named direct quote set against the documented
  figures, placed once and not granted equal structural weight — the model for
  carrying a denial without false balance.

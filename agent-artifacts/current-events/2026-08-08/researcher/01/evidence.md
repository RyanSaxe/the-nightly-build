# Evidence record: current-events/2026-08-08 (researcher 01)

This record verifies a five-item slate for the Saturday, August 8, 2026 US
current-events brief. Four items rest on clean public primaries read firsthand:
the BLS July employment release (Aug 7), the White House birthright-citizenship
executive order (signed Aug 6), the Slotkin/Cruz Right to Worship Act filing
(Aug 7), and the FAA's own statement on the Minneapolis air-traffic-control
outage (Aug 6). Each is paired with independent US reporting that confirms the
same facts. The fifth item — the depletion of US long-range precision-missile
stockpiles and the administration's denial — is the record's thin spot: the
depletion claim exists only in anonymously sourced reporting (Reuters, CNN, NPR)
with no public government primary, while the denial is a public presidential
statement. It is carried here as an attributed dispute, not established fact, per
the commission. The strongest single development on material conditions is the
jobs report; the strongest on law is the birthright-citizenship order. One
scouted candidate, the Russia/NATO "gray zone" intelligence assessment, is
rejected below for want of any public primary. Aug 8 itself is a Saturday with
no hard release; the real news sits on Aug 6-7, inside the commission's
"on/around Aug 7-8" window.

## Contradictions

- Precision-missile stockpiles. The core of item 5 is a direct dispute. Reuters,
  CNN, and NPR (Aug 4) report, on anonymous sources, that US long-range
  precision-missile and interceptor stocks are severely depleted. President
  Trump (Aug 6, Truth Social) and Pentagon spokesman Sean Parnell reject this
  as "fictional" and assert "massive amounts" on hand. No public government
  primary confirms the depletion figures; no independent public record confirms
  the "massive amounts" claim either. The two are recorded as separate,
  attributed claims. Note also that the multiple depletion reports may trace to
  overlapping leaks, so they are not necessarily independent confirmations under
  the two-source test for an accusation.
- Birthright-citizenship signing date. The White House order page and Washington
  Post/NPR date the signing to Thursday, Aug 6, 2026; several outlets
  (SCOTUSblog, ABC7, El Paso Matters) headline it Aug 7 and one wrongly labels
  Aug 7 a Thursday (Aug 7, 2026 is a Friday; Aug 6 is Thursday). The White House
  primary is authoritative: signed Aug 6, 2026, with the news cycle running
  Aug 6-7. Use Aug 6 as the signing date.
- Jobs-report consensus figure. Reporting varies on the pre-release payroll
  consensus: Dow Jones +83,000 (CNBC), Wall Street Journal +83,000, and a
  separate +95,000 estimate (NBC). The actual figure (-23,000) and its miss are
  not in dispute; only the size of the expectation varies. Attribute the
  consensus to its source if used.
- Right to Worship Act date. JTA's article is timestamped Aug 6 (preview/report)
  while Slotkin's official introduction announcement is Aug 7. Treat the formal
  introduction as Aug 7, 2026 per the sponsor's primary.

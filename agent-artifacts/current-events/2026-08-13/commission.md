# Commission: current-events/2026-08-13

## Subject

The US general-news front page for 2026-08-13: the day's four to six most
consequential developments in the United States. Item selection is the editorial
work here, and the researcher proposes and verifies the slate; the orchestrator
reviews it before drafting to hold the split against tech-news.

## Selection standard

Favor events that change law, public policy, public institutions, or people's
material conditions. Routine political theater and merely popular stories do not
qualify, and there is no topic quota. Include an international story only when
leaving it out would make the brief misleading, sized to its importance. Put a
technology story here only when its public consequences are the news (a ruling, a
regulation, a trial, a breach with public fallout); developments in the field
itself belong to tech-news. Every item carries its primary record and at least
one independent account.

## Boundaries and the split with tech-news

Tech-news runs the same night and is AI-central. Do not run a field-development
item here (a model release, a research result, a lab benchmark). A technology
story qualifies for this brief only through its public consequences: for example
a court proceeding, an enforcement action, or a regulation. Coordinate through the
orchestrator if an item could sit in either brief.

## Do not repeat recent coverage

The rolling brief has run daily through 2026-08-12. Recent leads already covered,
which a new item must not simply re-run without a genuine new development: the
Epstein records unsealing (08-12), the childhood-vaccine schedule narrowing
(08-11), the measles elimination-status question (08-10), the Todd Blanche
confirmation and the $1.8B fund (08-09, 08-04), the July payrolls contraction
(08-08), the Anthropic copyright payout (08-07), the mail-ballot litigation
(08-04, 08-11). A later stage of one of these is fine only if the 08-13 record
shows a real, datable development.

## Habits not to inherit

The brief headlines on the single most consequential item's finding (not a
thematic "X while Y" summary line, which is the tech-news habit). Items use the
`nb-brief-item` furniture. Keep each item's "why it matters" doing analysis, not
recap.

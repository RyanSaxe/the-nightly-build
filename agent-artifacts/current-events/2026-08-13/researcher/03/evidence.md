This is a complete replacement record for the 02 evidence file, not a delta: it
carries forward the three items from round 2 that already meet the per-item
rule (1 primary + at least 1 independent secondary, both opened and read) —
the same-day executions, the birthright-citizenship order, and the

brief did not ask me to re-verify them and nothing in this round's research
touched them. The round-2 item 4 (DHS civil contempt, ruling dated July 28)
is dropped per this round's brief and does not appear in the slate below; its
sourcing problem is noted once in Discarded for the record and not carried
as an item.

This round's assignment was to source one fresh replacement item dated
on/around 2026-08-13. The prime candidate named in the brief — the Meta
youth-harms trial — did not verify for an 08-13 procedural step: jury
selection began Wednesday, August 12, 2026 (confirmed on three independently
read pages, including one time-stamped the morning of the 12th and one
published the morning of the 13th that still describes jury selection as
having begun "Wednesday," i.e., the day before), and opening
arguments/testimony are not scheduled until August 18, 2026, per KQED's own
reporting. No August 13-specific procedural step was found for that case, so
per the brief's instruction it was not forced onto the slate.

The replacement item instead comes from the brief's backup category — a
federal court ruling with a clear primary record. A Texas federal court's
August 5, 2026 final judgment striking down the National Firearms Act's
registration requirements for silencers, short-barreled rifles,
short-barreled shotguns, and AOWs stayed its own effect for seven days from
entry; that stay lapsed at the boundary of August 12–13, 2026, and the
first unregistered transfers under the new regime were completed at 12:01
a.m. on August 13, verified both against the court's own final judgment (via
the case's official RECAP/PACER record) and an independent, contemporaneous
newsroom account dated the same day. This is a genuine 08-13 development
(a legal/material change — a federal regulatory regime lapsing and untaxed
firearms becoming transferable without registration — not merely continued
coverage of the August 5 ruling itself), and it meets the per-item rule.

The evidence is thinnest on one point: whether DOJ's silence through the
stay period reflects a considered decision not to appeal or simply an
unresolved internal debate that the deadline caught mid-argument. The
secondary source (The Reload) reports DOJ "has not appealed," and the case's
own docket (checked via a public docket aggregator, since CourtListener's
docket page itself returned 403 again this round) shows no notice of appeal
filed as of August 12. I found no on-the-record DOJ statement explaining the
decision; a bipartisan-Republican congressional letter urging DOJ not to
appeal is in the record for context but is advocacy, not the government's
own account of its reasoning, and is not cited as evidence of DOJ's motive.

## Contradictions

- No contradiction found among the three independent accounts of item 1 (AP wire, Tennessee's own DOC advisory, News9's original Oklahoma reporting): all name the same three men and the same date.
- No contradiction found between CBS News and the Reuters wire on item 2: both describe the same two orders, the same four categories, and the same ACLU response, from independent bylines.
- No contradiction found between Bloomberg (item 3's secondary) and the White House memorandum's own text on the program's structure or the $1 million bond figure.
- No contradiction found between the court's own Final Judgment (item 4's primary) and The Reload's account of the stay's expiration and the first transfers: the order's own "seven days from the date of entry" language, applied to an August 5 entry, is consistent with an August 12/13 boundary effective date, and The Reload's 12:01 a.m. August 13 timestamp for the first transactions matches.
- One scope point worth flagging so the writer does not overstate the item: this is a party-specific injunction, not a nationwide one. The court expressly declined to issue the "blanket ban" the lead plaintiffs requested, citing Trump v. CASA's limits on universal injunctions. The injunction covers the named plaintiffs (a large but bounded set: 15 states, several gun-rights associations, named commercial and individual plaintiffs) and, where applicable, their agencies, members, and current/future customers — not the public generally. The congressional letter (Clyde et al.) is explicitly asking DOJ to extend the ruling's practical effect nationwide via its own rulemaking discretion, which underscores that the ruling itself does not do so.
- The Meta youth-harms trial (this round's prime candidate) does not belong in this brief for 08-13: jury selection began August 12, not August 13, and opening arguments are not scheduled until August 18. This is not a contradiction between sources — every source read agrees on these dates — but a dateline mismatch with this brief's window, consistent with the pattern flagged in rounds 1 and 2 of stories that read as "current" but carry an off-target date.

**Most important limitation, this round:** item 4's injunction is
party-specific, not nationwide — the writer should not describe NFA
registration as abolished generally. It is enjoined only as to the named
plaintiffs (15 states' agencies/subdivisions, several gun-rights
associations, named commercial plaintiffs, and all of their current and
future customers), a large but bounded population, and the court said so
explicitly, citing Trump v. CASA's bar on universal injunctions. Separately,
DOJ's reason for not appealing is not established by any source I opened —
only that it did not appeal, and that it faced organized political pressure

DOJ's silence, not speculate about its cause. The Meta trial did not pan out

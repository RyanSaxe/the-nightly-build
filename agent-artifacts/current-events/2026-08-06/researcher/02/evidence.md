# Evidence: current-events/2026-08-06 (researcher 02)

This record supersedes researcher/01 for the single item the editor flagged
(item 4, the Michigan Senate Democratic primary) and carries the other five
items forward intact. Round 01 recorded the Michigan result against
`https://mvic.sos.state.mi.us/`, which is the Michigan Voter Information Center
voter-lookup tool, not a results page: it does not resolve to the source that
owns the vote count. Item 4 below replaces that with a resolving results source
that owns/carries the count, records the exact figures it shows, and keeps the
~9:1 ad-spending context as a separate secondary Number.

The correction, stated plainly for the writer: **Michigan's Secretary of State
publishes no statewide election-night results page.** Its official
"election results and data" section is a directory of 83 county clerk sites plus
certified canvass data that posts only after the Board of State Canvassers
certifies; there is no state URL that lands on a live statewide U.S. Senate
tally. The operative owner of the election-night count is the Associated Press,
whose count NBC, NPR, and Michigan outlets republish; the official certified
count will follow after canvass. Item 4 names AP as the owning authority and
supplies a fetch-verified resolving results page (NBC News) as the
recommended printed href, with a second resolving page (ClickOnDetroit)
carrying the exact AP vote counts.

Everything else from round 01 stands. This record's central limitation is
unchanged: 2026-08-06 is the run/reporting day itself, and at research time the
day's own wire cycle had not posted (Wikipedia's August portal, NPR National,
and the Everything Briefing daily had no 2026-08-06 entries). The six
consequential developments are dated 2026-08-03 through 2026-08-05; none
duplicates a commission-listed covered story, so each is new to the paper. If
the orchestrator requires a strict 2026-08-06 dateline, the record cannot supply
one; the strongest news for a brief published this morning is the immediately
preceding 72 hours.

The six candidates span law, immigration policy, energy/grid, elections,
courts/separation-of-powers, and public health, satisfying "no topic quota."

## Contradictions

- **Michigan result — figures differ by reporting snapshot (not a conflict).**
  The AP count republished by ClickOnDetroit at 86% of precincts (3,352/3,896)
  shows El-Sayed 742,017 (48%) and Stevens 727,091 (48%); NBC at ~98.8% of
  expected vote shows 48.5% / 47.5%. These are the same race at different
  timestamps. The writer should pick one snapshot, cite its reporting share, and
  not mix the two. The ~15,000-vote / ~1-point margin is consistent across both.
- **Michigan — no owning statewide official page.** The state SoS offers no
  election-night statewide results URL (only a county directory and post-canvass
  certified data). AP is the operative owner of the unofficial count; the
  official certified count will follow after the Board of State Canvassers acts.
  This is the reason round 01's `mvic.sos.state.mi.us` href was invalid, and why
  item 4 now leads with AP and a fetch-verified newsroom results page.
- **Michigan — GOP race conflation risk.** Some aggregated summaries conflate
  the GOP Senate primary (Mike Rogers, unopposed) with the GOP gubernatorial
  primary (John James over Perry Johnson) and the Democratic gubernatorial
  primary (Jocelyn Benson). For this item, only the Democratic Senate primary and
  Rogers-as-GOP-Senate-nominee are load-bearing; do not import governor-race
  names.
- **Oath Keepers — number of defendants.** Accounts disagree on how many
  defendants the 2026-08-04 order covered. NPR: Rhodes "and five other" members
  (six). U.S. News: Rhodes "and eight other defendants" (nine). CNN frames it as
  the final Jan. 6 case, dismissing charges against nine people. The primary
  docket order (1:22-cr-00015) resolves the exact count and named defendants;
  cite the order's own list, not a secondary tally.
- **Oath Keepers — "dismiss" vs "vacate."** Secondary accounts variously say the
  DOJ moved to dismiss the indictment and to vacate the convictions. Sentences
  were commuted in January 2025; the 2026 order ends the case. Track the order's
  own verb.
- **Burke Law Group — immigration expertise.** NOTUS reports no staff attorney
  lists immigration as a specialty; NPR/ABC report the firm has "only two"
  attorneys who specialize in immigration and asylum. Attribute each to its
  source; do not assert a single count as fact.
- **Burke contract — "$150M" scope.** Reported as a $150M contract; at least one
  account frames it as $150M "per year" over a one-year base period (2026-08-15
  to 2027-08-14). The Federal Register notice owns the exact ceiling and period;
  cite it, and avoid "$150M per year" unless the notice says so.
- **Children covered.** Secondary reporting states the Burke contract covers
  "about 1,800 children" currently in ORR shelters, versus Acacia's 20,000-plus.
  The 1,800 figure is secondary (not yet found in the primary notice); mark it as
  reported, not verified against the primary, or omit.
- **Somalia TPS — population affected.** Some coverage cites roughly 1,100 Somali
  TPS holders; broader pieces cite 1.3 million TPS holders across all 17
  designated countries. Different denominators. Use the Somalia-specific figure
  only if confirmed against USCIS/court filings; the ~1,100 figure is secondary.
- **Silica rule enforcement dates.** Accounts give April 14, 2025 (original coal
  compliance date), an April 2025 appellate stay, and an interim delay to August
  2025. Use the Federal Register documents for exact dates; secondary timelines
  vary.

# Commission: current-events/2026-08-15

## The assignment

The US-focused general-news front page for Saturday, August 15, 2026. Select the
four to six most consequential developments, favoring events that change law,
public policy, public institutions, or people's material conditions. Do not fill
a topic quota, and do not include a story because it is merely popular or is
routine political theater. Include an international story only where leaving it
out would make the brief misleading, in proportion to its importance.

Item selection is editorial judgment for the researcher and writer working the
day's actual record; the orchestrator sets the territory and the constraints
below. Each item stands alone, gives the development (not a recap), and links its
primary source with at least one independent account.

## What the researcher gathers

The actual developments dated on or around August 15, 2026. Read the day's
primary records (agency releases, court filings and orders, congressional
actions, official statements) and pair each with independent reporting, a
reputable US newsroom preferred where quality is comparable, the primary record
used regardless of country.

Threads seen in the surrounding days that may have moved (confirm against the
day's record; none is guaranteed to belong): the Supreme Court shadow-docket
transparency push in Congress; the carrier rotation sending the USS George
Washington to the Middle East and the reported conditions aboard the USS Abraham
Lincoln; the measles elimination-status decision pending at PAHO; labor-market
and inflation data. Find what actually changed on the day.

## Coordination with this edition (required)

The private-sector "hack-back" National Security Presidential Memorandum (signed
August 12) is the subject of this edition's Opinion article, which argues a
stance on it. Current Events must not lead on that memo or re-argue it. If the
day's record makes it unavoidable as a factual development, treat it as a single
plain item reporting what changed, with no stance, and prefer other August 15
developments. Put technology here only when its public consequences are the news;
developments in the field itself belong to Tech News (a separate article this
edition).

Do not repeat the August 14 current-events items (the Solicitor General's move
to make both mail-voting injunctions fall together; prisons; higher education;
Yemen strikes; inflation; the navy). Where a covered thread genuinely develops
on the 15th, say so and build on it rather than reintroducing it.

## Craft and habits to break

Each item's heading names the development in its own nouns. The lead item
supplies the article headline and dek (the template's shape), so make that dek
one lean sentence that carries the detail the headline dropped, and avoid the
comma-triad dek mold (three clauses joined by commas and closed with "and").
Every figure carries its primary owner. No item closes on a line that hands the
point back to the reader.

Reader: the paper's declared audience. Items 4-6; per item one primary source and
at least one independent account (series floor five sources total). No overlap
with the other six articles this edition.

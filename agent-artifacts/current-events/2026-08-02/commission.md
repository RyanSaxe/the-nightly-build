# Commission: current-events/2026-08-02

## Assignment
The US-focused general news front page for **2026-08-02**. A `brief`: 4-6 items,
each a judgment about why a development matters, not a recap. The reader already
saw the headline; give the number and the caveat the headline dropped.

## Angle / selection standard
Favor developments that change law, public policy, public institutions, or
people's material conditions. No topic quota. Routine political theater and
merely-popular stories do not qualify. Include an international story only if
leaving it out would make the brief misleading, sized to its importance. Put
technology here only when its *public consequences* are the news; the field
itself belongs to Tech News (a neighboring brief tonight — coordinate to avoid
overlap).

## Reader / mode / template
House reader; mode rolling; template `brief`. Per-item citation. Each item: the
development + a link to its **primary** source (original announcement, filing,
ruling, dataset, or the party's own statement) plus **at least one independent**
account. Do not close an item on a line that hands the point back to the reader.

## Candidate item slate (VERIFY each against primaries; drop/replace freely)
These are leads, not a fixed list. The researcher must open the primary record
for each, confirm it is a genuine 2026-08-02 (or immediately prior, still-live)
development, and swap in better items where these are stale or thin. Escalate the
final slate to the correspondent if a swap changes the brief's character.
1. **Iran war**: reports that Trump canceled/paused planned strikes on Iran
   pending a possible deal, with a fresh State Department travel warning to
   Americans in the Middle East. Primary: White House / Trump's own statement;
   State Dept advisory. Confirm exact status as of 08-02 (the ceasefire
   collapsed in July; verify the current posture precisely — do not overstate).
   Note: prior current-events pieces (07-30) covered direct US-Iran strikes;
   build on that, say what changed.
2. **Measles / public health**: the US approaching loss of measles-elimination
   status after a 2026 surge (reported ~37 outbreaks). Primary: CDC data /
   official statement. Material to families; strong "changes institutions/
   conditions" story.
3. **2026 midterms infrastructure**: four large Latino civic organizations
   combining voter-outreach operations for the first time. Primary: the joint
   announcement from the organizations. Verify names, scope, first-time claim.
4. **Courts / executive power**: any 08-01/08-02 development in the aftermath of
   the Supreme Court's 2026 rulings (tariffs struck down as unauthorized;
   birthright citizenship; Voting Rights Act; agency independence). Include only
   if there is a genuine dated 08-02 development (a filing, an agency action, a

5. **Economy**: only if a fresh 08-02 datapoint exists. The 08-01 brief already

Aim for 4-6 items that each clear the significance bar. Better to run 4 strong
items than pad to 6.

## Source obligations
- Template floor: **minimum 5 sources total**; **per item: exactly 1 primary and
  1+ secondary** (independent of the primary's author).
- Independence follows authorship and stake, not domain. Prefer a reputable
  US newsroom for independent reporting when quality is comparable; use non-US
  reporting when it is closer to the event or carries original reporting.
- Every URL resolves. Verify every number/title/name against the owning primary.
  A wrong title in a headline is the costliest error the paper prints.

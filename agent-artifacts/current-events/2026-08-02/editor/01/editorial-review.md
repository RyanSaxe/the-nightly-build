# Editorial review: current-events/2026-08-02 (editor/01)

## Skeptic

Thesis: five developments cleared the day's law/policy/institution/material-conditions
bar, led by Trump's claim that he called off an imminent Iran strike over a deal Iran
flatly denies. The brief stands on: (1) Trump's own post as the sole basis for the
canceled strike; (2) an active, multi-state water-utility intrusion the FBI is probing
for an Iran link; (3) a drought figure that produced a Guard callout; (4) a visa-bond
pilot's real deterrent effect against its stated purpose; (5) a federal preemption
ruling that keeps felony stakes paused only while the order holds. Headline, dek, and
each item heading were treated as claims.

I reopened every cited source as an opponent. Findings:

- **Iran lead, Trump's post (s1).** Fetched the trumpstruth.org mirror. Its text is
  verbatim Trump: "the perimeters of a deal has been agreed to," "Immediate, Complete,
  and Total OPENING OF THE HORMUZ STRAIT," "subject to being able to rapidly make a
  DEAL." Every quote in the item matches the source exactly. The mirror is a faithful
  reproduction, its href resolves, and the note plus canonical `data-nb-url` disclose
  the mirror status. Held. (See sourcing call 1.)

- **Iran lead, Saudi sentence (s2) — BREAK.** The article says Mohammed bin Salman
  "phoned him urging the cancellation." NPR does not support this. NPR reports MBS
  "spoke with" Trump and "emphasized the necessity of prioritizing dialogue to
  de-escalate tensions" (Saudi state media, SPA). "Urging the cancellation [of the
  strike]" is the writer's gloss, not the source's claim, and "phoned" overstates
  "spoke with." This is a mischaracterization of a named person against the owning
  secondary — the deepest kind of skeptic finding. The evidence record carried the same
  overstatement ("phoned Trump urging cancellation"); the direct source read governs.
  Routed to writer.

- **Iran lead, Mehr descriptor (s3) — BREAK, cut directly.** The article called Mehr
  "which reflects the Supreme Leader's office." The Times of Israel describes Mehr only
  as "a semi-official news agency" and makes no such claim. Unsupported descriptor on a
  named entity; cut. The rest of the sentence (Mehr's "nothing but a new lie," Fars
  denying a Hormuz-transit compromise) is confirmed verbatim by the source and stands.

- **Water item (s4/s5).** I decoded all four pages of the FBI/EPA PSA (the writer's
  tooling could not). It directly supports every claim cited to it: since 27 July 2026,
  Rockwell Automation/Allen-Bradley MicroLogix 1100/1400 PLCs, "at least seven states,"
  FBI/EPA joint. The PSA does **not** mention Iran. The AP/ABC secondary (s5) does carry
  the Iran-link framing ("federal agencies warned... Iranian hackers have been
  targeting water and wastewater systems"; FBI "has not publicly identified a culprit"),
  and confirms 9 MI / 30+ MN systems and Michigan's "no known impacts" line. Headline's
  "suspected Iran link" is honest and properly hedged; body is fully supported. The
  primary is genuine and readable. (See sourcing call 2.)

- **Wildfire item (s6/s7/s8).** Fetched InciWeb: Old Trails 3,509 + Fairview 981 +
  Autumn Lane 900 = 5,390 acres exactly, 0% containment. The article states only the
  5,390 total (cited to KHQ, which reports that total) — accurate; InciWeb's per-fire
  figures back it. 640 structures and the Clemo "largest, most destructive" quote are
  supported by the evidence record's Spokesman-Review entry. Held.

- **Visa item (s9/s10) — BREAK, cut directly.** Fetched the Federal Register full-text
  XML: tiers "$10,000, $15,000, or $20,000," default $15,000 "unless the officer has
  reason to believe the visa applicant's circumstances would render the applicant
  unable to pay," publication and effective date Aug. 3, 2026, finalizing a pilot begun
  Aug. 20, 2025. All accurate — except "closes out a one-year pilot **that tested the
  same terms**." The permanent rule changed the terms: it raised the maximum from
  $15,000 to $20,000 and dropped the pilot's $5,000 floor. The clause is false; cut. The
  83% issuance drop and overstay figures are agency-stated outcomes correctly cited to
  PBS (secondary).

- **Courts item (s11/s12/s13) — SOURCING FAILURE.** The order (s11) is cited
  `data-nb-kind="primary"`, but it is gated at every host — courthousenews (403),
  courtlistener docket (403), courtlistener REST API (401). No one in the pipeline has
  read it; its holding is quoted only through NBC (s13, secondary). The order's
  existence, judge (Menendez), date (July 27), and both quoted phrases are confirmed by
  multiple independent secondaries and web search, but confirmation is not a read. Under
  the house standard "cite only what you have read," s11 cannot honestly carry a read
  primary, so the item has zero read primaries and fails the per-item policy. Routed to
  researcher. (See sourcing call 2.)

- item 1: removed "which reflects the Supreme Leader's office" (descriptor not in the
  cited Times of Israel report).
- item 4: removed "that tested the same terms" (contradicted by the Federal Register
  primary; the permanent rule changed the bond tiers).

No prompt leakage found: the draft-handoff's original-work language (selection-and-
consequence judgment) does not surface in the article's prose, and no planning labels,
selection rules, or "fulfilled the assignment" claims leaked in. The dek makes a
world-claim (the Minnesota freeze) rather than grading the brief's method, and avoids
all three banned dek molds. Item headings are concrete subject-verb claims, not
scaffolding labels; "Ferguson declares... orders..." joins two verbs, not the banned
comma-and clause triad. The lead is genuinely the day's new development, not the
recent macro/Fed spine.

Worst tell: the lead's quote density. Three verbatim Trump fragments across two
sentences, one of which ("...rapidly make a DEAL") is closer to emphasis-for-its-own-
sake than load-bearing evidence. Not cut, because its conditionality has some cargo and
paraphrase is a writer task; flagged as optional below.

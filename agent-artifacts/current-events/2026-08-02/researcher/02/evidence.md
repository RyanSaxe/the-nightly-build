## Contradictions

- **Iran "deal" claim vs. Iranian denial.** Unchanged from round 1: Trump's Truth Social post claims
  Iran and regional states asked the US to hold off strikes because "the perimeters of a deal" were
  agreed, including opening the Strait of Hormuz. Iran's own semi-official Mehr and Fars news agencies
  deny both that Tehran requested a pause and that any Hormuz-access deal exists — Fars extends this to
  a specific reported compromise (split entry/exit routes), calling that "false" too. This is a live,
  first-party dispute over a war-and-peace claim. Any framing should state it as Trump's claim, disputed
  by Iran, never as an agreed deal — and now that the primary is directly readable, the article can
  quote Trump's exact words rather than paraphrase them, which sharpens rather than resolves the dispute.

- **Twin Falls injured count: 5 vs. 7, not simply an old-vs-new resolution.** The AP wire (Boone/
  Bellisle/Raza, via NPR, published Aug. 2, 4:01am ET, quoting Twin Falls PIO Josh Palmer) reports 7
  injured. CBS News's own update — timestamped LATER, Aug. 2, 1:51pm EDT, quoting the same PIO under the
  name "Joshua Palmer" — reports "at least 5" injured and explicitly flags the count as still moving.
  KMVT (Aug. 2, no exact timestamp found) also reports 5. This is not a clean case of an older, lower
  number being superseded by a newer, higher one: the higher number (7) is chronologically earlier than
  at least one of the lower numbers (CBS's 1:51pm figure). Both trace to statements attributed to the
  same city spokesperson. The writer should attribute whichever figure is used to the specific outlet
  and timestamp ("the AP reported 7 injured as of early Sunday; CBS's early-afternoon update cited at
  least 5") rather than state either as settled, and should not average or split the difference.

- **Twin Falls death count: 3 vs. 4 — RESOLVED, not a real conflict.** The "4 dead" figure Wikipedia's
  infobox carries includes the shooter; the "3 dead" figure every direct-read outlet (CBS, KMVT, AP/NPR)
  uses counts civilian victims only. These are the same underlying fact stated two ways, not competing
  counts. Use "3 dead" for victims, and if the shooter's death is mentioned, state it separately rather
  than folding it into "4 dead" as if that were a fourth victim.

- **Spokane Area Fires acreage arithmetic — RESOLVED in favor of InciWeb.** KHQ's per-fire figures
  (3,481 + 3,333 + 914 = 7,728) still do not sum to KHQ's own stated 5,390-acre total, confirmed still
  true on live re-fetch. InciWeb's per-fire figures (3,509 + 981 + 900) sum exactly to 5,390. The
  Spokesman-Review's figures (Fairview 981, matching InciWeb; Old Trails "nearly 4,000," a looser
  rounding than InciWeb's 3,509) are closer to InciWeb than to KHQ. Recommendation: cite InciWeb's
  precise per-fire and total figures if the writer wants both in one sentence; if citing only the
  total, any of the three sources agree on 5,390 acres.

- **Structures destroyed: 600+ (KHQ, preliminary, explicitly called "low" by the fire chief) vs. 640
  (Spokesman-Review, attributed to Incident Commander Tom Clemo).** Not a contradiction so much as an
  evolving count in the same direction the fire chief predicted — the 640 figure is both later and from
  a named incident commander rather than a rounded press estimate. If a single figure is needed, 640 is
  the better-sourced one; if precision matters less than movement, "600 and rising, per officials" is
  defensible either way.

- **National Guard numbers for the wildfire, two different scopes — unchanged from round 1.** Ferguson's
  proclamation states 110 activated statewide (July 31); NPR separately reports 30 mobilized
  specifically to assist Spokane authorities. Plausibly a subset relationship, never stated as such by
  any source — do not add or imply conflict without saying so.

- **Visa bond rule's effective/publication date is reported two ways.** Some secondary coverage (PBS,
  Al Arabiya, dated July 31–Aug. 1) treats the rule as effectively announced then; the Federal Register's
  own document metadata gives Aug. 3, 2026 as both the publication and effective date. This is most
  likely the standard gap between a rule's public posting (for press purposes) and its formal Federal
  Register publication date — not a substantive conflict — but the writer should use Aug. 3 as the
  citable effective date since that is what the primary document itself states.

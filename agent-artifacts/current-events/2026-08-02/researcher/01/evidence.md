### Item 3 — Measles elimination status at risk

11. **https://www.cdc.gov/measles/data-research/index.html** — Primary (CDC owns this surveillance data). As of **July 30, 2026**: **2,371 confirmed cases**, **37 outbreaks**, **94% of cases (2,219 of 2,371) outbreak-associated**. States plainly: "Measles was declared eliminated in the United States in 2000." Reports national kindergarten MMR vaccination coverage fell from **95.2% in the 2019–2020 school year to 92.5% in 2024–2025**, below the ~95% threshold generally cited for community immunity.

12. **https://www.paho.org/en/news/2-3-2026-update-review-measles-elimination-status** — Primary (PAHO's Regional Verification Commission (RVC) is the body that actually certifies elimination status; it "reports directly to the Director of PAHO," who "formally communicates the corresponding classification to national authorities" — CDC does not itself declare elimination status). Key distinction the record supports and most secondary coverage blurs: **the Region of the Americas lost regional elimination status in November 2025**, but that was driven by Canada's re-established endemic transmission, not the United States. The **United States' own national elimination status will be separately reviewed in November 2026**, with the analysis period running from the U.S. outbreak's onset on **January 20, 2025**. Technical definition of elimination: "uninterrupted circulation of the same virus genotype and lineage for 12 months or more in a specific geographic area" — i.e., the U.S. has not yet been declared to have lost elimination status; it is at risk of losing it in November.

13. **https://www.kff.org/other-health/measles-elimination-status-what-it-is-and-how-the-u-s-could-lose-it/** — Independent secondary (KFF, a nonpartisan health-policy research organization with no institutional stake in CDC or PAHO's determinations). Confirms the dual-verification structure (CDC's 2000 declaration and 2012 re-verification; PAHO's ongoing regional/national certification via the MRE-RVC) and the definition of elimination. Its own case tallies (2,288 confirmed in 2025; 16 new outbreaks in 2026 through March) are from an earlier snapshot than the CDC's July 30 figures above — use KFF for the definitional/structural claims, not for the current case count.

**Numbers note:** the brief's cited figure of "~37 outbreaks" is accurate and matches CDC's own July 30 dashboard exactly.

## Contradictions

- **Iran:** The entire "deal" rests on Trump's own unilateral statement. None of the three independent secondaries I read (NBC, Townhall, RawStory) found any Iranian or Israeli government confirmation of the claimed terms (Hormuz reopening, end to Iran's "nuclear threat"). RawStory/Washington Post additionally flag that Trump has previously and repeatedly claimed the Strait of Hormuz was already open "despite evidence to the contrary" — direct reason for skepticism about the specific term he's now citing as the deal's centerpiece.
- **Measles:** Outlets (and the commission's own brief note) commonly cite "elimination status" as a single US fact. The primary record shows two distinct determinations: the Americas *region* lost elimination status in November 2025 because of Canada, while the *United States'* own national status is separately still under review, with a decision due November 2026. Reporting that conflates these overstates where the U.S. currently stands.
- **TPS Somalia:** DHS (via General Counsel James Percival) frames the judiciary's repeated stays as "defiance, obstruction, and delay" by the court; the court's own posture, per the case record, is that it is adjudicating a live Fifth Amendment discrimination claim that the Supreme Court's *Mullin v. Doe* ruling left open to judicial review (unlike the APA claims *Mullin* foreclosed). This is a direct executive-vs-judiciary framing conflict worth naming rather than adjudicating.
- **Courts/executive power generally:** the mail-in-voting SCOTUS emergency application (*Trump v. California*, 26A124) is still pending with no docket movement after July 29 and a response due August 3 — after this window closes. I discarded it as a standalone item for that reason, but note it directly for the correspondent in case advancing that already-covered thread is preferred to the TPS substitution (see Discarded).

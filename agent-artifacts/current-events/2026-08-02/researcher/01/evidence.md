# Evidence: current-events/2026-08-02 (researcher 01)

The evidence supports four distinct, independently verified US developments from
August 1–2, 2026, each with a primary record and at least one independent
secondary account: the US cancellation of an imminent strike on Iran amid a
disputed "deal" claim, cyberattacks on water systems in at least two states
under FBI investigation for a possible Iran link, a mass shooting at an
Idaho In-N-Out that left multiple people dead, and a statewide wildfire
emergency in Washington centered on Spokane. All four are corroborated across
multiple reputable US newsrooms and, where one exists, an official government
document. The record is thinner than the brief's 6-8-candidate target: this
session's web-search budget was exhausted (200/200 calls) after confirming
these four, before several other threads (Congress floor action, market/oil
reaction to the Iran news, and a broader scan for court rulings or regulatory
actions) could be fully checked. Two of the four also carry real sourcing
gaps, recorded below and in Discarded/Contradictions: the Iran item's true
primary (Trump's Truth Social post) could not be opened directly, and the
Twin Falls shooting has no single fetchable primary document, only convergent,
partly conflicting secondary accounts of a police chief's on-record remarks.

## Contradictions

- **Iran "deal" claim vs. Iranian denial.** Trump's Truth Social post claims Iran and regional states
  asked the US to hold off strikes because "the perimeters of a deal" were agreed, including opening the
  Strait of Hormuz. Iran's own semi-official Mehr and Fars news agencies deny both that Tehran requested a
  pause and that any Hormuz-access deal exists, with Fars saying the strait "will remain closed" absent a
  change in US posture. This is a live, first-party dispute over a war-and-peace claim, not a settled fact
  — any framing of this item should state it as Trump's claim, disputed by Iran, not as an agreed deal.

- **Twin Falls casualty count.** CBS News (filing the day of, Aug. 1) and KMVT (filing the next day, Aug.
  2) both report 3 dead, 5 injured. A separately encountered secondary summary (not independently opened
  by this researcher, sourced from aggregated search results describing a Wikipedia infobox) reported 4
  dead (including the shooter) and 7 injured. The two directly-read local/national outlets agree with each
  other on 3/5; the 4/7 figure could not be traced to a document this researcher opened and should be
  treated as unconfirmed. This is exactly the kind of number the writer must attribute to a specific
  outlet and moment ("as of Sunday morning, local station KMVT reported...") rather than state flatly.

- **Spokane Area Fires acreage arithmetic.** KHQ's own article gives a 5,390-acre total for the three-fire
  complex alongside individual fire acreages (3,481 + 3,333 + 914 = 7,728) that do not sum to that total.
  This is an internal inconsistency in the cited source, not a cross-source conflict — flagged so the
  writer picks one figure deliberately (the complex total, 5,390 acres, is the one explicitly labeled as
  the combined figure) rather than adding the parts.

- **National Guard numbers for the wildfire, two different scopes.** Ferguson's proclamation states 110
  Guard members activated statewide (July 31); NPR separately reports "30 members mobilized to assist
  Spokane authorities." These are plausibly compatible (a Spokane-specific subset of the statewide total)
  but no source states that relationship explicitly — do not add them or imply they conflict without
  saying so.

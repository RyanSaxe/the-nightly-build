# Commission: current-events/2026-08-09

## What this edition covers

The US-focused general-news front page for Sunday, 9 August 2026. Select the
day's genuinely consequential developments: events that change law, public
policy, public institutions, or people's material conditions. Do not fill a
topic quota, and do not lead on political theater or a merely popular story. A
Sunday often runs lighter, so let significance, not a fixed count, set the
edition; the band is four to six items and the floor, not the target, is what
matters.

Include an international story only where leaving it out would make the brief
misleading, sized to its importance. Put a technology story here when its public
consequences are the news; a development inside the field belongs to Tech News,
which runs the same night. Coordinate the boundary: if a story is genuinely a
public-policy event and a technology event, decide which brief owns it and keep
it out of the other so the reader does not read it twice.

## Sourcing

- Template: `brief`. Required `nb-brief-item` per item, four to six items, and a
  `Sources` section. Source floor: 5 overall.
- Every item carries one primary record (the ruling, filing, official release,
  data print, or transcript that owns the event) and at least one independent
  account from a newsroom outside the authoring party. Prefer a reputable US
  newsroom for the independent account when its work is comparable; use the
  primary record regardless of country, and use non-US reporting when it is
  closer to the event or carries the original reporting.
- Every figure in display text and prose is verified against the primary that
  owns it. A named official carries the exact title and body the primary states.

## Template, tags

Tags follow the items selected; the writer sets them in `nb-meta`. Furniture
(`nb-brief-item`, and a `nb-stat`/`nb-stat-strip` or `nb-table` where a figure
or comparison has a shape) carries evidence, never decoration.

## Distinct value and habits not to inherit

The recent run has led several editions on the economy and the jobs print; lead
on 9 August's actual top story, whatever its beat, and do not default to
macro data because the last brief did. The dek that pairs the lead with a
"the same day/week, also..." second clause has recurred; so has the banned
three-clause comma-and-"and" dek. Write one lean dek that commits to the day's
single most consequential development and names the actor. The lead item is the
headline, so choose it for consequence, not for how it scans.

## Boundaries

One edition, this date. Report the day; do not recap the week or the running
storylines the paper has already covered except where the day genuinely moves
them. Keep to four to six items.

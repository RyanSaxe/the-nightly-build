# Commission: current-events/2026-08-17

## Assignment

The US general-news front page for Monday, August 17, 2026. Select the day's four
to six most consequential developments in the United States, favoring events that
change law, public policy, public institutions, or people's material conditions.
Each item is a judgment about what moved and why it matters, not a recap of a
headline.

## Selection standard

- Consequence decides inclusion, never a topic quota. Routine political theater
  and merely popular stories do not qualify.
- Include an international story only where leaving it out would make the brief
  misleading, and size it to its importance rather than to a world-news quota.
- Put a technology story here only when its public consequences are the news;
  developments in the field itself belong to Tech News, which publishes the same
  day. Coordinate so the two briefs do not share an item.
- Cover only what actually moved on or around this date. Never invent an item to
  reach the count.

## Sourcing (the researcher enforces the floor)

Per item: at least one primary record that owns the development, and at least one
independent account. Prefer a reputable US newsroom for independent reporting
when its work is of comparable quality; use the primary record regardless of
country, and non-US reporting when it holds the original reporting or sits closer
to the event. Brief-template floor: at least 5 sources across the brief. Every
recorded URL must resolve to the source's own page.

## Recent-pattern habits to avoid

The recent current-events record has been dominated by litigation over executive
action (TPS terminations, border-wall injunctions, mail-voting and citizenship
orders). Select by consequence, and where the day's real news is a court filing,
lead on what it changes rather than on the filing as procedure. Vary the lead
item's construction from the recent run of court-order headlines.

## Neighboring articles this run

Tech News publishes the same date and owns field developments in technology and
AI; keep those out of this brief unless the public consequence is itself the
story, and never duplicate its lead. The standalone articles today
(company-analysis, investing, paper-of-the-day, expert-tools, word-of-the-day) do
not constrain selection.

## Production policy (balanced profile)

- writing-coach: effort low, model capable. researcher: effort high, model capable.
  writer: effort medium, model capable. editor: effort high, model inherit, required.
- No `required` model or effort directive exists for this series. The runtime
  resolves `capable` to its most capable available tier (Opus) and runs the
  required editor on the inherited orchestrator model (Opus 4.8); neither is a
  trade-down. Effort values are recorded as production guidance.

## Suggested tags

Per-item topical tags, set by the writer from the selected items.

# Editorial review: current-events/2026-08-05 (editor/01)

## Skeptic

Thesis: the day's institutional responses all closed after the harm had already
landed — a recall behind an outbreak's first deaths, an arrest behind a
700-structure fire, a customs-court suit behind tariffs already imposed — and
the tariff thread is made load-bearing by tying June's still-tariff-shaped trade
gap to the same Supreme Court defeat the 25 states now invoke. The four claims it
stands on are the four items.

Claim 1 (cyclosporiasis, the lead): first two deaths in a record outbreak,
10,468 confirmed cases / 517 hospitalizations / 47 states. Held. The national
figures are cited to the CDC surveillance page alone (s1), which solely owns
them; the article does not lean NBC for any national count. NBC (s2) is used only
for the two deaths and the Michigan death location, and that location is
correctly attributed to Michigan health officials ("Michigan health officials
said in announcing them August 3"), not to CDC — matching the Michigan DHHS
ownership the researcher fixed. Stat strip (2 / 10,468 / 517 / 47) verified
against CDC. "More than double the prior US annual record" checks against the
~4,700 (2019) prior record. This item is clean on the round's core risk.

Claim 2 (trade deficit): $73.3B in June, down $4.4B from a revised $77.6B. Held,
and I pushed on the two attribution seams the brief named. "Revised" sits on the
sentence cited to BEA (s3); BEA owns that qualifier and the article does not
source it to the wire. I then tested the item's most load-bearing sentence — that
the earlier levies "were struck down by the Supreme Court earlier in the year,
Bloomberg reported" — because the evidence paraphrase did not carry it. I opened
the Transport Topics/Bloomberg page (s4): it states "many levies on imports were
struck down by the Supreme Court in the first quarter, the Trump administration
is seeking other routes to tariff imports." The claim and its citation are sound,
and the cross-link to item 3 is honest.

Claim 3 (25-state Section 301 suit): filed Aug 3 in the Court of International
Trade against tariffs on 59 countries and the EU. Held. Cited to the NY AG
release (s5), which controls the venue and country count; the AG James quote is
reproduced exactly. The docket furniture (court, filing date, "no ruling yet")
is accurate display text. Only one Aug-3 Democratic-state-vs-Trump action is
filed here — the front page does not read as one legal story — and the
unverified Iran/Hormuz item is absent, as required.

Claim 4 (Spokane arson): broke. The headline reads "Spokane charges a man with
arson" and the lead reads "Spokane County investigators charged Aaron F.
Farinacci, 37, with first-degree arson." Both overstate the primary. I opened the
Sheriff release at the href the article actually prints
(spokanecounty.gov/CivicAlerts.aspx?AID=6161 — a different URL form than the one
the evidence record verified, so it needed its own check): it resolves to
"Investigators Arrest Old Trails Fire Arson Suspect" and records an arrest and a
jail booking — "Farinacci was transported and booked into the Spokane County Jail
for Arson 1st Degree" — not a prosecutor's filed charge and not guilt. Two errors
compound: investigators arrest and book, they do not "charge," and the status is
suspicion, not a filed charge. The primary carries no bond and no court date, so
the $1M bond and Aug-4 appearance are correctly held to NPR/Spokesman (s8), and
the article implies no arson conviction (the only conviction named is the prior
Arizona manslaughter, correctly attributed to s9). The tell is internal: the
article's own dek says "Spokane's arson arrest" while its headline says
"charges." The headline is display text cited to the Sheriff primary, so this is
the costliest surface carrying a claim the cited primary does not support. This
is the exact "accusation reported as fact" the desk flagged. Routed to the
writer — it changes the headline and the lead's legal characterization, past what
a cut fixes.

data-nb-kind audit: each item carries exactly one primary and at least one
independent secondary (s1 CDC / s2 NBC; s3 BEA / s4 Bloomberg-TT; s5 NY AG / s6
Just Security; s7 Sheriff / s8 NPR + s9 Spokesman). No label hides a missing
independent source. Hrefs: nb check passed with links included, and I
independently opened the two highest-risk ones — the printed Spokane URL (form
differed from the record) and the Bloomberg page — both land on the source's own
page.

## Cut

The prose is already at wire compression: fused leads that carry finding and
stake in one clause, in-line attribution, precise figures with the comparison
that makes them legible (double the prior record, $4.4B month-over-month). I
found no fluff, no self-grading, no signposting, and no prompt leakage — the body
never narrates the selection or asserts it hit its assignment; the synthesis
lives in the ordering and the cross-link, not in a graded sentence. Punctuation
is plain: no em-dash reflex, no semicolon chains. The four leads vary in shape
rather than settling into one mold, and with no back catalog yet
(`nb history --structure current-events/2026-08-04` returns no published
article) there is no recurring dek/heading formula to break. The dek avoids every
banned mold (no colon subtitle, no comma-triad, no semicolon reversal, no
suspended question) and states a stance about the day rather than grading the
article's method.

One prose note left to the writer rather than cut, because the item is already
being reworked for the attribution fix: the item-4 closer yokes two unrelated
facts with "and" ("A fire investigator concluded the burn was 'consistent with
an open flame to vegetation,' and court records show a prior manslaughter
conviction in Arizona"). It reads better as two sentences. I made no direct edits
this round, so no `nb stamp` was needed.

## Reader

Read straight through as the paper's declared reader, the front page gives
something the four wire items alone would not: a reading of the day in which the
institutional response keeps arriving after the harm has landed, made
load-bearing — not decorative — by the tariff cross-link between item 2's
Bloomberg line (earlier levies struck down by the Supreme Court) and item 3's AG
James line ("After losing at the Supreme Court..."). That matches the
original-work sentence in the handoff, and it is real synthesis carried by
ordering and a verified cross-link, not mere selection. The prose sits closer to
the AP/Reuters/Economist exemplars than a median summary. The one thing standing
between the reader and full trust is the Spokane legal-status error, which the
required fix resolves.

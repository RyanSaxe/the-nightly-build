# Editorial review: current-events/2026-08-16 (editor/01)

## Skeptic

The brief's spine is the lead item's analysis: the June Supreme Court precedent
(the record calls it Mullin) has removed courts from reviewing DHS terminations
of Temporary Protected Status, and the Somali order shows how far that reaches,
because a judge who found the plaintiffs' racial-discrimination claim strong
still saw no path to stopping the termination. The other three items each stand
on one development and the law, institution, or material condition it moves. I
tested each claim by opening the cited source as the article prints it.

Lead (Somali TPS). The primary cited at `#s1` is the DHS Federal Register
termination notice, and this source is not in the researcher's evidence record —
the writer pulled it, as the review brief anticipated. I verified it exists and
owns the claim: DHS published "Termination of the Designation of Somalia for
Temporary Protected Status" on 14 January 2026, with the designation originally
set to expire 17 March 2026, later stayed by the District of Massachusetts and
cleared to take effect on 14 August. The printed href 302-redirects to
`unblock.federalregister.gov`, but that is federalregister.gov's CAPTCHA gate on
automated (non-browser) requests, not a dead link or a wrong address; a reader in
a browser lands on the notice, and the proof's link check resolved it. This is a
gated fetch, acceptable, not a broken citation. The "held the status since 1991"
detail is corroborated (independent coverage frames the termination as ending TPS
"after 35 years"). The two quotations — the plaintiffs' "strong case" that the
statements were racist and the judge's "cannot discern a path" — match the Just
the News text at `#s2` word for word. The ~700 figure is the TPS-holder count
from `#s2`, not the resident-population number the evidence record flagged as a
contamination; the article uses it correctly. Judge Allison Burroughs, U.S.
District Judge for the District of Massachusetts, is titled correctly, and the
article wisely does not name the June case or its namesake, avoiding any title
risk. The headline claim that the ruling put "such terminations beyond a court's
review" is a fair compression of the piece's own thesis: the judge found no path
even on the constitutional discrimination claim, so review is foreclosed. Held.

Executive power. The Supreme Court docket at `#s4` confirms every descriptor:
caption "National Park Service, et al. v. National Trust for Historic
Preservation in the United States," application No. 26A203, up from the D.C.
Circuit (No. 26-5123), filed 13 August by the government, with the Chief Justice's
order "Response to application (26A203) requested by The Chief Justice, due by 12
p.m. (EDT) on August 18, 2026" — the article's "noon EDT on August 18" is exact.
Solicitor General D. John Sauer is titled correctly. I could not open CNN (`#s5`)
in this environment (HTTP 451), but the proof resolved it and the researcher read
it; the "point of no return" argument and Trump's "horrendous, politically
motivated, and unlawful" line are the record's. I confirmed by independent search
that the D.C. Circuit affirmed Judge Leon's modified injunction 2-1 on 7 August,
Millett and Garcia in the majority over Rao, with below-ground work allowed. One
note: the panel's actual ratio runs as much on Congress's authority over
construction as on the "temporary tenant, not the owner" property reasoning the
article paraphrases; the article's "does not own or control the Executive
Residence" is drawn from the opinion's property passage, attributed to a
secondary, and does not put a quotation in the court's mouth, so it stands as
sourced context. Held.

Flooding. The Indiana primary at `#s6` confirms Governor Mike Braun, the 13
August declaration, Executive Order 26-21, the IDHS coordination directive, and
the Guard mobilization to Gary, Munster, and Hobart. NPR (`#s7`) confirms the
11-plus inches, the White River at Anderson cresting at 24.9 feet above a
1913 record, at least three dead, more than 150,000 without power, and the Friday
FEMA request. Held.

Food safety. Time (`#s8`) confirms the 15 August Class I upgrade and its
definition, the ~19 million eggs, Midwest Poultry Services, the Texas
6 June–3 July production window, and the five brand labels. CDC (`#s9`) confirms
98 infected across 17 states, 26 hospitalized, none fatal. The article's closing
synthesis — the earliest illnesses predate the recalled eggs, so the recall
narrows the risk without accounting for the whole outbreak — is grounded in the
evidence record's onset range (from November 2025) against the June production
window and the FDA's own "does not account for all the illnesses" statement.
Held.

Geometry and labels. Every item carries exactly one primary and at least one
independent account: FR notice + Just the News + The Hill; SCOTUS docket + CNN;
IN.gov + NPR; CDC + Time. Every `data-nb-kind` is right — the government notices,
the docket, and the CDC page are the authoring parties (primary); the four
newsrooms report from outside (secondary), and the two Somali-TPS secondaries are
independent of each other. No mislabel hides a missing independent source.

International omission. The southern-Lebanon strikes were left out. On a
US-focused front page whose four items nowhere imply the region is quiet, the
omission does not mislead, and the toll is contested with no readable primary the
writer could open — citing a contested casualty figure on secondary sourcing
would fail the citation standard. The omission stands; no researcher pull is
required.

## Cut

Sentence by sentence against `spec/slop.md`, the prose is clean: figures stand
without magnitude adjectives ("About 700," "19 million eggs," "24.9 feet,"
"98 infections"), actors are named and placed, and no sentence carries a
negative-parallelism reflex, decorative "-ing" analysis, elaborate copula,
vague attribution, or self-reference. No phrasing is borrowed from the voice
guide's quoted writers ("temporary tenant" appears in the evidence but the
article deliberately paraphrases rather than quotes it). No prompt or brief
leakage: the selection-bar language of the commission does not surface in the
prose.

One sentence failed the test. In the lead, "The order measures how far that June
decision reaches" reduces under the placeholder test to "the X measures how far
that Y reaches" — an announce-then-prove signpost whose claim the very next
sentence delivers concretely ("the same bar now stands between the next TPS
termination and a court"). I deleted it rather than repair it; the piece's
original analysis survives intact in the concrete sentence, and the paragraph is
sharper without the abstract lead-in.

The edge sentences hold: each item closes on a distinct consequence — the
precedent now blocking the next termination, the federal money a FEMA
declaration would open, the outbreak the recall does not fully contain — with no
repeated closer construction across items. Against the recent-pattern notes, the
main headline avoids the desk's "A federal judge clears" / "X asks a court to
block" molds, and the dek names the mechanism without a banned mold (no
semicolon reversal, no suspended question, no comma triad). One internal item
subhead did echo the Aug 13 desk headline's "asks a court to" construction; I
recast it (see Edits) to break the echo while keeping the actor and the
development. The lone furniture piece, the `rs-docket` on the ballroom item, is
earned — it sets the standing case facts apart so the prose can argue — and its
case, court, docket, stage, question, and stakes are accurate and cited. No
item needed furniture added; the flooding numbers read cleanly in prose and the
Somali order has no publicly hosted primary to anchor a second docket.

## Reader

Read straight through, the brief gives the reader what the sources alone would
not: the throughline connecting the June precedent to the Somali order's stakes —
that a judge who called the discrimination claim strong was still barred from
acting, so the same bar now stands between the next TPS termination and any
court — and, in each item, the single law or condition the development moves
rather than a recap of the headline. That reading matches the draft handoff's
original-work statement and is the article's own, not the sources'. The prose
sits closer to the voice-guide exemplars than to a median summary: calm, exact,
carried by specific figures and named actors.

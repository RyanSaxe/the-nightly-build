**Word of the Day: zenzizenzizenzic** (noun, the eighth power of a number). Field: mathematics. It has a datable first use: Robert Recorde's *The Whetstone of Witte* (London, 1557), the OED's only citation. Its story is easy to show with the scan: Recorde builds the eighth power by doubling the "square of squares" (zenzizenzike), and the word has six z's. `nb history zenzizenzizenzic` and `nb history zenzic` return no coverage in any series.
- Runner-up: *dord*, the 1934 Webster's ghost word for "density" (science field, datable 1934 to 1947). No coverage. Not yet sourced.
- Rejected after checking: *bafflegab* (daily-brief 2026-11-01), *petrichor* (the-word/petrichor, 2026-07-19).

**Technology 1: DeepMind's AlphaGenome 2 conditions DNA-to-function predictions on a cell state.** Blog 11 November, with a paper PDF. The model takes a 1 Mb DNA sequence plus a condition (tissue, cell type or state), and can infer that condition from a user's own single-cell RNA data without fine-tuning. At 90% precision it raises recall of causal TenK10K sc-eQTLs from 16% (fine-tuned AlphaGenome 1) to 30%. It is a research capability with a technical paper and a limits section, which suits the ML audience. No prior coverage.
- Runner-up: **Google's Gemini 4 Hydrogen** (12 Nov). A fast coding and agent model at $1.50/$7.50 per million tokens, now in Search for all users, with self-reported benchmarks. Sourced below. Recent briefs ran Google items on 10, 11 and 12 Nov, which is why I placed it second.

**Technology 2: Runway releases Praxis-1's weights under Apache 2.0.** Announced 11 November. It is a 27B-parameter mixture-of-experts diffusion transformer that predicts video and robot actions together, with weights on Hugging Face (LeRobot support) and fine-tuning code. Runway reports 46.2% on RoboLab-120 against 28.0% for π0.5. It is an open-weight, commercially usable robotics model, and it is not a Google item. No prior coverage.
- Runner-up: Anthropic's "Worse Together" (Alignment blog, 11 Nov). Giving each user their own agent produced worse group outcomes than one shared coordinator agent in all four environments. Sourced below. Its arXiv paper was submitted 30 September, so the result is six weeks old and only the blog post is new. Anthropic also graded its own models.

## Contradictions

- **Star Act cost and headcount.** Military.com gives $12.75 billion over 10 years with no source, and "about 58,000" veterans. Other reports seen in search range from 54,000 to 60,000. MOAA cites about $78 billion for the March draft and about $10 to $13 billion for combat-injured-only versions. These figures do not conflict once the version is fixed: H.R. 2102 as amended by H.Res. 1247 is the narrow one. But the CBO estimate that would settle them (https://www.cbo.gov/system/files/2026-10/hr2102_as_amended_by_hres1247.pdf, publication 62877) returned 403 and was not read. Use no cost or headcount figure unattributed.
- **The Republican veterans package.** Search summaries call it the "Take Care of America's Veterans Act" and say it offsets cost by cutting future sleep apnea and tinnitus benefits. Military.com reports a voice-vote Republican package that pairs a Star Act provision with rating changes for sleep apnea and tinnitus. It does not confirm the package's name or bill number, and no recorded Clerk vote for it exists in rolls 315 to 323. Do not name it or call the rating changes "cuts" without a better source.
- **Nvidia share drop.** TechCrunch says "nearly 12%" in early Thursday trading. A search summary of other outlets says "more than 13%". The record has no primary for the intraday figure, so leave it out or attribute it.
- **Why Huang resigned.** Neither the 8-K nor the release gives a reason. The link to the affair and the alleged intelligence approach comes only from press reports, such as TechCrunch and earlier NYT/FT reporting relayed in the 9 Nov brief. Attribute it.
- **Recorde as coiner.** Wikipedia says Recorde "proposed" the term. Quinion says only that the OED's single citation is Recorde's. Recorde's own text says other writers already called squares "zenzikes", and Wikipedia cites Stifel's German *Arithmetica Integra* for the "zenzic" system. The evidence supports "the OED's only citation is Recorde, 1557", not "Recorde coined it".
- **Worse Together recency.** The blog post is 11 Nov, but arXiv 2610.00583 was submitted 30 Sep 2026. That is why it is a runner-up.
- **ACS vs USPSTF.** ACS now recommends routine biennial screening at 50 to 69. The USPSTF's 2018 Grade C still calls screening at 55 to 69 an individual decision, and its Grade D recommends against screening at 70+. ACS leaves 70 to 74 to individual decision. This is a real disagreement between bodies, not a sourcing conflict.

# Editorial review: daily-brief/2026-11-13 (editor/01)

## Correct

Thesis, from the draft alone: six items for the period 11 to 13 November, led by Jensen Huang's departure from Nvidia. Each fact is pinned to the document that owns it, and claims found only in the press are named to their outlet. The claims the piece cannot lose are these. Nvidia (the focus of this round). I fetched the 8-K at its printed address. The dek's "ahead of quarterly results on 17 November" holds. "Finance chief" is a fair label for EVP and CFO. The only reason the article prints is TechCrunch's, attributed by name in the sentence. The 8-K address returns 403 to an undeclared script but loads for a declared client, and that is SEC policy, not a broken link.

Star Act. Roll 323 reads 392-18, 12 November, and roll 320 reads 245-175, 11 November. Both match. One break: roll 320's yeas are Republicans 34, Democrats 210 and Independent 1. ACS and USPSTF. Both pages match the draft, with one exception. The ACS list makes the annual high-risk screening "from age 45 to 69 years". AlphaGenome 2. The blog matches on the 11 November date, the million-letter input, the four readouts and the cell encoder working from "just a few dozen cells" without retraining. The archive.org OCR of that leaf, garbled but readable, has "manp men Doe cuer moje call fquare nombers ...", which supports the researcher's transcription. The pronunciation still rests on Quinion alone, as the Limits section records.

Source kinds. Fourteen hrefs, all fetched as printed. Every one lands on its source, Quinion's by redirect to the same entry. The primary and secondary labels hold: Nvidia's 8-K and release, the Clerk, ACS, USPSTF, DeepMind and Runway are each the author of their own record. Quinion, TechCrunch and Military.com are marked secondary.

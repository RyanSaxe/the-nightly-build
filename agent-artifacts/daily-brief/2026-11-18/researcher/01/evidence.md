  Tuesday night. I could not open CNN (451) to confirm, so treat the closure as
  unverified.
- **Outage cause.** Mullin blames old systems overwhelmed by new technology.
  Clayton blames simultaneous upgrading and patching. Warner ties the patching
  pressure to AI-found vulnerabilities. None of these is a finding from an
  investigation, and no agency has published a root cause for NSANet.
- **Crimean Bridge rail.** Zelensky says drones hit the rail section. Ukraine's
  military says it stands with a damaged arch. Russia's transport ministry says
  some rail services resumed. BBC's own analysis says the collapse cuts "direct
  road and rail links", which goes further than the military's account. The
  writer should say road span collapsed, rail damaged and partly running.
- **Crimean Bridge timeline.** One strike came overnight into 16 Nov (traffic
  halted, damage undescribed; Defense Post). The collapse is reported after the
  attack overnight into 17 Nov (BBC, first published 03:46 UTC 17 Nov).
  Coverage that merges the two gives a wrong date.
- **Nvidia Hopper China sales.** Yahoo's body says $2.4 billion and its
  headline says $2.3 billion. Neither the 8-K release nor CNBC gives the figure.
  Do not use it.
- **Nvidia consensus.** CNBC/LSEG gives $109.10 billion revenue and $2.48 EPS.
  MarketBeat ($108.96 billion) and Kiplinger ($108.8 billion, $2.47) differ.
  If an estimate is used, attribute it to LSEG via CNBC.
- **BIS authorship.** The BIS release says Treasury and the NSC wrote and
  directed the rule. The rule text names only Commerce/BIS. Attribute the claim
  to the release.
- **Data-centre share.** The abstract gives about 1.8% of US electricity (central
  scenario) and 21 to 31 Mt CO2 as the range. Just Security's summary of the
  NYT says "almost 2%" and "about 0.5% of U.S. CO2". The 0.5% figure is not in
  the abstract and could not be checked against the paywalled text. Use the
  abstract's figures.
- **Sprezzatura as a coinage.** Castiglione hedges: "per dir forse una nuova
  parola", "to say perhaps a new word". The record supports "introduced it as
  perhaps a new word", not "invented the word". The verb sprezzare ("to
  scorn") existed, and §XXVI also has "sprezzata disinvoltura", which
  Castiglione says many use for bodily movement. Merriam-Webster gives no
  etymology or English first-use date.
- **Commission angle.** Nothing found sinks the selection. The outage item has
  the weakest evidence for its cause, so the item should report the
  explanations as claims.

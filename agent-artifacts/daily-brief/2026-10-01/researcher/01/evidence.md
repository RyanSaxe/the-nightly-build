## 1. Word of the Day: quiddity

**Primary — Oxford Advanced Learner’s Dictionary, “quiddity”**  
URL: https://www.oxfordlearnersdictionaries.com/us/definition/english/quiddity  
Location: entry lines 89–107: noun; pronunciation /ˈkwɪdəti/; philosophy label; definition “the real nature of something”; origin note: late Middle English, Medieval Latin *quidditas*, Latin *quid* (“what”).

**Secondary — Online Etymology Dictionary, “quiddity”**  
URL: https://www.etymonline.com/word/quiddity  
Location: lines 11–14: Medieval Latin *quidditas* in Scholastic philosophy meant the essence that distinguishes a thing, literally “whatness”; Latin *quid* means “what”; the English essence sense is attested from the late 14th century, while the “quibble” sense is attested in the 1530s and developed from scholastic disputes.

**Verified facts:** use the commissioned sense “the essential nature of a thing.” The form descends through Medieval Latin *quidditas*, built on Latin *quid* (“what”). Oxford supplies a present-day definition and pronunciation; Etymonline supplies the history. A concise historical point: medieval Scholastic writers used *quidditas* for what a thing is, and English later developed a pejorative sense of a fine distinction or quibble in argument.

**Limits:** the etymological source glosses the scholastic idea as what distinguishes a thing from other things. Avoid implying that “quibble” was the original sense; the source dates that development later. Do not present the Latin root as *quis* (“who”); the dictionary’s immediate root is *quid* (“what”).

### Technology 1: DeepMind and 1024-bit RSA

```text
URL:         https://storage.googleapis.com/deepmind-media/papers/Fan_Factories_Multiple_Number_Fields_in_Factorization_Factories_and_Batch_NFS.pdf
Kind:        primary (Google DeepMind working paper, dated 5 November 2026; PDF creation stamp 5 Nov 2026 17:12 UTC). The authors own their claims, and the paper is a working paper, not peer reviewed (the paper is labelled "Working Paper"; I found no sign of review). Authors listed: Krishnamurthy (Dj) Dvijotham, Nadia Heninger, Owen Jones, Pushmeet Kohli, Borislav Kozlovskii, Shrinu Kushagra, Chirag Pabbaraju, Robert Stanforth, Abhradeep Thakurta, Emmanuel Thomé, Nenad Tomasev, Adam Zsolt Wagner. Heninger is also affiliated with UC San Diego and Inria, Thomé with Inria, Pabbaraju with Stanford.
Establishes: the batch-factoring result, its assumptions, and its limits.
Paraphrase:  The number field sieve's cost is written L_N[1/3, c], and progress is measured by c. For many integers of one size, Coppersmith's multiple number field sieve (MNFS) has had c = 1.9019 since 1993. The paper gives every integer its own "fan" of number fields, all fed by one shared precomputation, and finds that with a precomputation costing one factorization each further integer costs L[1.7929]. At leading order (the o(1) terms set to zero), for 1024-bit integers each further integer is factored at about 1/58 of the cost of one general number field sieve run and 30 times less than MNFS. Three or more integers already cost less this way than with MNFS, counting the precomputation. For batches without precomputation the gain in the exponent is at most 0.018. All results hold "under the standard heuristics of NFS analyses". Algebraic identities are machine-checked in Lean 4. The results were produced with "a two-agent AI harness: one model generated the proofs, and a second model verified them." The paper states: "Our results do not threaten deployed cryptosystems today that are based on 2048-bit and larger RSA." Section 4 says the leading-order ratios "indicate the size of an improvement but do not predict running times", and the closing "Open questions" say "no fan factory has been implemented, and the size at which one would overtake a well-tuned number field sieve is empirically open."
Locators:    Abstract (p. 1); Section 1.1 "Results" and "What this means at concrete sizes" (pp. 2 to 3); Table 1 (p. 4); Figure 4 and Table 2 (pp. 11 to 12); "Open questions" and "Outlook" (final page).
```

## Contradictions

1. **Did Trump confirm the Qeshm strike?** UPI, CBS (live) and RFE/RL say he confirmed limited US strikes on an air-defense system. Fox's live page says he "did not confirm" a US role in the Qeshm reports while acknowledging limited strikes on air defenses. His words as quoted by all four are the same: "I don't want to talk about it" then "We do minor strikes, but they knock out." He did not say where. A faithful line is that he described a US strike on an anti-aircraft device without naming Qeshm.
2. **Did the US military announce it?** Euronews says "the US military said it carried out limited airstrikes". CBS, RFE/RL, UPI and Fox each report no CENTCOM statement about Qeshm. The only CENTCOM release that day (Task Force Iron Gate, 7 Nov) does not mention a strike. I treat Euronews as wrong on this point.
3. **Which US drone, and did Iran down it?** Iranian media said an MQ-9 (UPI/IRIB) or an MQ-1 (CBS and RFE/RL, Revolutionary Guard). Euronews says the US did not confirm either. This is an Iranian claim and should not be in the brief as fact.
4. **Is the Strait open?** An aggregator (HNGN) says Trump called it open and "doing more business than they've ever done", and contrasts that with transit data. I did not verify the transit numbers and keep them out.
5. **Measles deaths.** CDC's page lists 4 deaths ("subject to change"). State agencies report 8 (Contagion Live, AFP via Euronews). The CDC does not automatically add state-confirmed deaths. Give 4 as the CDC count and 8 as the state count, or leave deaths out.
6. **Trump's paraphrase of Slotkin against her words.** Trump: she is "going to go after all young people that believe in Republican Values". Slotkin, as quoted by Fox: she wants "signature cases" against "the people who enabled him" and wants "young people coming out of school" to think twice about working for the administration. Her stated aim is deterrence of administration hires, which her critics and Trump read as a threat. Both readings are documented, and the brief should quote, not adjudicate. Slotkin disputes the characterization ("manufactured outrage").
7. **Who coined "ultracrepidarian".** Wiktionary and Wikipedia credit Hazlitt (1819). Hazlitt's own sentence says Gifford had been "well called" one, and Waller and Glover report Hunt crediting "a friend of mine", "perhaps Lamb". One blog says Hazlitt used it in 1818 in an unpublished "Reply to Z"; I could not open that and do not rely on it. Say "first in print in Hazlitt's 1819 letter", not "Hazlitt coined".
8. **DeepMind's 30x and 1,600x.** They measure different things and must not be merged. The paper's 30x is the leading-order ratio of per-integer cost against MNFS at 1024 bits (about 1/58 against one general number field sieve run) after a precomputation costing one GNFS run. The 1,600x belongs to the other paper (signature forgery, short-fingerprint case) and comes only from secondhand summaries of Kohli's essay. The Interesting Engineering headline cited elsewhere conflates them. The paper dates itself 5 November, and DeepMind announced it on 6 November.

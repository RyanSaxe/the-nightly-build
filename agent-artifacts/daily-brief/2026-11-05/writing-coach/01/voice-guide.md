# Voice guide: daily-brief/2026-11-05

## How this piece should sound

This is a three-minute read for engineers who build production ML systems, so
each item is a quick, sure report from someone who read the record and is now
telling a colleague what happened. Say it plainly and confidently. Readers this
technical do not need a news event explained down to them, and they do not need
it inflated either. The register is the Semafor Flagship item more than the
newspaper story. Each item names the development in its first clause, gives the
one fact that makes it matter, and closes on what follows, attributing claims as
it goes.

The recent editions have fallen into a pattern. Deks open on a source and a
clock time, and items open on "On 2 November the X ...". The exemplars below open
in several different ways, and the writer can choose among them item by item. Semafor's fertility
item starts with the trend as its grammatical subject ("Plummeting fertility
rates are reshaping...") and only brings in the agencies and dates later. The
banks item starts with the actors and what they are doing now. Willison's release notes begin with
the verb ("Fixes a bug...") or with a judgment about the work ("Genius piece of
OSINT by..."), and the source and date are left for the citation. A date or a
source belongs at the front of an item only when it is the news, for example a
deadline that has passed or an agency that is the actor. Otherwise it can go in
the second sentence or the citation. Before settling the six openings and the
dek, read them aloud together. If two of them are built the same way, rebuild
one.

The dek can be built the same way. It can lead with whichever item the writer
judged most consequential and name its actor and action, with no clock time or
outlet in front unless the timing is the story.

The technology items can sound like Willison. He says what a thing does in the
practitioner's exact words (Doppler curves, ADS-B, OpenAI-compatible endpoints),
and when he has a view he states it in the first person in one short clause. A
judgment like that belongs in a technology item only when the record supports
it. The news items carry attribution, and judgments come from the people quoted.

The Word of the Day can follow Garg. Give the dated etymology as a compressed
chain of facts. Then, where the word allows it, add a short illustration that
shows the meaning working instead of restating the definition. Pick tonight's
word from a field away from politics. The chicaner page below is here to show
form only.

## Semafor, "Semafor Flagship: The engineer of the collapse" (5 November 2026)

Source: https://www.semafor.com/newsletter/11/04/2026/semafor-flagship-the-engineer-of-the-collapse

> "Plummeting fertility rates are reshaping policymakers’ priorities across much of the West. Governments in richer countries have long promised measures to boost flagging birth rates, but those efforts have largely failed to pay off, with fertility hitting record lows in recent years."

Checked: https://www.semafor.com/newsletter/11/04/2026/semafor-flagship-the-engineer-of-the-collapse (raw HTML), retrieved 2026-11-05
The first sentence is a trend with a present-tense verb, and it does not start
with a date, an agency or a report. The second gives the history in one clause
and then the result. An editor's judgment shows in "largely failed to pay off",
which is stated without hedging.

> "Major US banks are cashing in on their China bets, after years of regulatory uncertainty and geopolitical turmoil prompted Western firms to pull back from the world’s second-largest economy."

Checked: https://www.semafor.com/newsletter/11/04/2026/semafor-flagship-the-engineer-of-the-collapse (raw HTML), retrieved 2026-11-05
This item opens on the actors and their action, and the background goes into a
subordinate clause. The outlet behind the claim ("Bloomberg reported") does not
appear until the next sentence. "Cashing in on their China bets" is plain,
spoken English.

> "While 11 African countries are producing military drones, armed non-state actors in at least 17 countries have acquired the weapons and are more effectively taking advantage of this dramatic shift in warfare brought about by Russia’s 2022 Ukraine invasion, the report said"

Checked: https://www.semafor.com/newsletter/11/04/2026/semafor-flagship-the-engineer-of-the-collapse (raw HTML), retrieved 2026-11-05
The two counts are set against each other inside one sentence, and the
attribution comes at the end, after the reader already has the facts. The
figures carry the item and there are no adjectives doing that work.

## Simon Willison, link posts and release notes on simonwillison.net (2–4 November 2026)

Source: https://simonwillison.net/2026/Nov/3/a-sound-that-leaks-your-exact-location/ and https://simonwillison.net/2026/Nov/2/llm/

> "Genius piece of OSINT by Colsto, who demonstrates how you can take almost imperceptible background noise of planes from a video soundtrack, figure out their Doppler curves, then cross reference them against ADS-B data to find the exact location of the video based on their speed, distance, and gaps between different flights."

Checked: https://simonwillison.net/ (raw HTML, entry dated 3 November 2026), retrieved 2026-11-05
The whole item is one sentence. It opens on his verdict and then lays out the
method in the field's own terms, in the order the steps happen. His enthusiasm
is in "Genius" and nowhere else.

> "A lot of open source maintainers complain about a flood of AI-assisted PRs. LLM is my project that has been most affected by this and, to be honest, I'm not finding it to be a problem. That's mainly because I have Opus 5.5 or GPT-6 Astra help me with triage to spot the most useful fixes!"

Checked: https://simonwillison.net/ (raw HTML, entry "llm 0.37", 2 November 2026), retrieved 2026-11-05
He states the common view fairly and then says his experience was different,
with the reason given straight after. The first person and "to be honest" show
a maintainer talking about his own project.

> "Fixes a bug that affected my agent.datasette.io demo site, where the set of available LLM models was not correctly restricted to just those in the configuration."

Checked: https://simonwillison.net/ (raw HTML, entry "datasette-llm 0.1a10", 4 November 2026), retrieved 2026-11-05
A release note that starts with its verb, so the change is the first thing a
reader sees. The bug is described exactly enough that a user could tell
whether it affected them.

## Anu Garg, A.Word.A.Day, "chicaner"

Source: https://wordsmith.org/words/chicaner.html

> "From French chicaneur (quibbler). Earliest documented use: 1673."

Checked: https://wordsmith.org/words/chicaner.html (raw HTML), retrieved 2026-11-05
The etymology is the source word, its gloss and a dated first use, with no
padding. It works as a reference entry and gives a reader facts they can check.

> "A pickpocket takes your wallet when you aren’t looking. A chicaner convinces you to hand it over, sign a waiver, and thank them for lightening your load. The verb form is chicane, and the practice is chicanery."

Checked: https://wordsmith.org/words/chicaner.html (raw HTML), retrieved 2026-11-05
He shows the meaning by setting it beside a familiar figure, and the dry humor
in "thank them for lightening your load" is where the writer comes through. It
ends on the related word forms, which a reader who wants to use the word can
put to work.

## Word of the Day: sphexish

### Sources

```text
URL:         https://pure.rug.nl/ws/files/13139017/2012_Keijzer_Sphex_story.pdf
Kind:        primary for the history of the anecdote and the coinage's lineage.
             A peer-reviewed history (Fred Keijzer, Faculty of Philosophy,
             University of Groningen; Philosophical Psychology 26(4), 502-519,
             2013, doi 10.1080/09515089.2012.690177). This is the author's
             pre-print in the university repository. Keijzer reads and quotes
             Fabre, Wells/Huxley/Wells, Wooldridge, Hofstadter and Dennett
             directly.
Establishes: Fabre first reported the "cricket test" in 1879. Wooldridge (1963)
             retold it without Fabre's name. Hofstadter quoted Wooldridge in his
             1982 Scientific American column and coined "sphexish". Fabre's own
             results were equivocal, and Wooldridge's own source reported a
             contrary result in the same paragraph.
Paraphrase:  Wooldridge's 1963 account (The Machinery of the Brain, pp. 82-83)
             says the wasp leaves the paralysed cricket on the threshold,
             inspects the burrow, and, if the cricket is moved a few inches,
             drags it back and inspects again, "forty times, always with the
             same result". Keijzer traces this to Wells, Huxley and Wells, The
             Science of Life (1931, p. 1161), which names Fabre and then adds in
             the same paragraph that the Peckhams repeated the experiment with
             an American Sphex that "after her prey had been moved a number of
             times... did drag it straight into her burrow." Fabre's own essay
             (Souvenirs Entomologiques, first series, 1879; English in The
             Hunting Wasps, 1915) reports forty repetitions on one wasp of
             Sphex flavipennis. A few lines later he says that a colony in
             another district caught on after two or three trials and dragged
             the cricket straight in. Elsewhere he calls the wasps that see
             through it "the minority".
Locators:    Abstract (p. 1); Wooldridge quote pp. 2; coinage p. 3, citing
             Hofstadter 1985 p. 529; Hofstadter 1982 column p. 4 ("In 1982,
             Hofstadter gave the story a wide hearing..."); Wells/Huxley/Wells
             quote pp. 7-8; Fabre quotes pp. 10-12 (citing Fabre 1915/2001
             pp. 43-44 and 116-117). Page numbers are the pre-print's.
Quote:       "Hofstadter even coined the term 'sphexish' to refer to such an
             unknowing and mechanical form of 'seeming intelligence'" (p. 3).
             Fabre: "Time after time, forty times over, did I repeat the same
             experiment on the same Wasp; her persistence vanquished mine and
             her tactics never varied." (p. 10, quoting Fabre 1915/2001 p. 43).
             Fabre on the second colony: "After two or three experiments with
             results similar to those which I had so often obtained, the Sphex
             got astride of the Cricket... and at once dragged him into the
             burrow." (p. 11).
```

### Contradictions

- Wiktionary identifies the wasp as the great golden digger wasp, Sphex
  ichneumoneus, which is a North American species. Fabre's experiment, as
  Keijzer quotes it, was on Sphex flavipennis (yellow-winged Sphex) in France.
  The American Sphex belonged to the Peckhams' contrary trial. Do not name S.
  ichneumoneus as Fabre's wasp.
- Wiktionary and Quinion date the coinage to the 1982 column. Keijzer cites the
  coinage sentence from the 1985 book (p. 529) and says only that the 1982
  column quoted Wooldridge. Neither text of the 1982 column was opened. "Coined
  by Hofstadter in his Scientific American column in 1982" is supported by two
  secondaries and consistent with Keijzer. The safe form is "in 1982, in a
  Scientific American column later collected in Metamagical Themas (1985)".
- Column title: a Wikipedia list and a ProQuest bibliography give September 1982
  as "Can inspiration be mechanized?". Other secondaries give "Can Creativity be
  Mechanized?" or the 1985 chapter title. Do not print a column title.
- The commission's angle (a coinage with a known inventor) is intact. What
  complicates it is the anecdote, not the coinage: the evidence Hofstadter named
  the word for was equivocal from the start.

- Merriam-Webster has no entry for sphexish, so no dictionary of record gives

- Keijzer's text is the pre-print. Page numbers may differ from the published
  version.
- The series prompt names sphexish as its own example. It is in scope, but the
  editor may judge it too obvious. Alternates:

## Contradictions

- **Commission vs evidence on Sirik.** The commission calls it "the US strike near Sirik". No US source acknowledges it. Iranian state media said only an "enemy" strike hit a telecommunications tower, and the IRGC "stopped short of directly blaming the US" (RFE/RL). CENTCOM's statement did not mention Sirik. The AP summary in Just Security's early edition, the Washington Times headline and Democracy Now state it as a US strike, but I found no US source behind that. Write it as an Iranian account of an attack by an unnamed party that Iran blames on the US, or leave the attribution out.
- **Radar.** The IRGC says it destroyed a US early-warning radar in Bahrain. CENTCOM says two one-way drones were shot down with no injuries or damage. Bahrain did not comment. No independent check exists.
- **Tanker attacks.** The commission says tanker attacks. The reports are Iranian: the army said two tankers hit mines on 12 November, media said four tankers on Thursday, and the IRGC said an LPG/LNG tanker hit a mine on Friday. UKMTO did not confirm the Friday claim. The Washington Institute count (10 ships in the first 10 days of November) comes through The New Arab, not first-hand.
- **Trump "holding off".** The commission names a statement about holding off new action. What I can source is a Thursday 12 November White House interview in which Trump says five Gulf states asked him to wait, adds "I'm not sure I agree with it", and says he would have attacked otherwise. It is Trump's account, and none of the Gulf governments are quoted confirming it. A Detroit News headline dated 10 November ("Trump says he will hold off on fresh Iran strikes for now") shows he said something similar earlier; I could not open it. Separately, Trump posted on 12 November: "We don't need a deal with Iran" (per RFE/RL, quoting Truth Social). Holding off and "no deal needed" sit together in the same news cycle; the brief should say so only if the item has room.
- **Microsoft start time.** Microsoft opened EX1550543 at 16:17 UTC (11:17 ET) on 12 November. Outside network monitors (Kentik, ThousandEyes, Cloudflare) place the start at about 17:30-17:45 UTC; PPC Land puts Microsoft's first public post at 17:54. Use Microsoft's incident time for Microsoft's incident.
- **Microsoft cause.** Microsoft's explanation changed: first "elevated load" and a network configuration revert, then a statement that it had "not identified any recent change correlated with the start of impact". It has not named a root cause. SecurityWeek notes Patch Tuesday fixes from two days earlier; Microsoft's advisories do not link them to the outage. Do not connect them.
- **Microsoft duration.** Reports run from about 17 hours to about 21 hours depending on the clock used (Kentik traffic vs Microsoft's open incident). Microsoft's own closing post says "broad recovery" at 18:05 UTC on 13 November, which is about 26 hours after its incident opened. No source I opened shows the incident formally closed.
- **Gemini 4 Hydrogen output limit.** The blog says Hydrogen supports a 1 million-token input context and (per my fetch summary, not a quote) up to 1 million output tokens. The model card says a combined 1M window with 64K default synchronous output and up to 1M only via background long decode. Prefer the card.
- **Gemini Terminal-Bench version.** The blog says "Terminal-Bench 4.1" for the 57% vs 18.2% figure and "Terminal-Bench 4.0" for the Orchestrator figure. Name neither version unless the writer re-reads the page.
- **Gemini known regression.** The announcement lists safety gains. The model card shows unjustified refusals up 5.57 points against Gemini 3.8 Flash. The item can carry that if there is room.
- **Colom margin.** Reports said "about 4,400" (Fox8, 11 November) and "about 3,100" (MPB, 11 November morning). The county-reported count on 13 November is 7,587. These are counts at different times; use the latest and say whose count it is.

Story the record supports, for the writer: Walker coined the word, and the meaning drifted from his. Walker's grawlix is a scribble. The typographic string ($#!) that dictionaries now call a grawlix is closer to his jarns, quimps and nittles. The symbols are older than the word: Carr's strip used them in 1901, and the word first appears in Walker's NCS piece (1964 by his own later account) and the 1980 book. Merriam-Webster dates first use to 1968, so state 1964 as Walker's account.

## Contradictions

- **Panjandrum: who was tested, and how well documented.** Merriam-Webster, citing the OED, names Charles Macklin as the actor Foote tested. Edgeworth's 1825 text, the earliest printing, names only Foote and "a man, who boasted that he could learn any thing by rote". Per wordhistories.net, the named Macklin version first appears in 1854, and an 1850 Notes and Queries letter makes James Quin the author and Foote the one tested. The c. 1755 date has no contemporary record. The writer should credit Foote "according to Edgeworth", or tell the Macklin story as the tradition it is.
- **Panjandrum: the 75-year gap.** MW says the passage reached print "some 75 years" after 1755. Edgeworth's volume is dated 1825, which is 70 years. Wikisource gives 1820 for the novel, but the archive.org scan's own metadata and title page say 1825.
- **United Fidelity: deposits.** The FDIC's release says First Citizens assumes "substantially all deposits" of $5.4 billion. American Banker, citing First Citizens, says about $4.5 billion of deposits transfer. The FDIC failed-bank page excludes "certain brokered deposits" (Cede & Co.). Neither record states the size of the excluded brokered deposits. The roughly $0.9 billion gap is my inference.
- **United Fidelity: size rank.** American Banker and Ken Tumin call it the largest 2026 failure, which the FDIC releases confirm. One aggregator headline calls it the largest "since 2024". Republic First (April 2024) was about $6 billion, according to an FDIC annual-report excerpt I did not open, so the two banks are too close in size to rank from rounded figures.
- **Cloud One: data taken.** Reuters says it could not determine whether data was stolen. Interesting Engineering, in a search excerpt I did not open, reported Social Security numbers, names and birthdates among the compromised data. Nothing in the Reuters text supports that, so I treat it as unverified.
- **ECB: nomination date.** CNBC, Agence Europe and the Council URL say 26 November. EU Law Live gives 27 November in a search excerpt (its page did not load). I take 26 November.
- **ECB: portfolios.** Agence Europe assigns Boone to market operations and Brunnermeier to chief economist. The ECB, not the Council, allocates Executive Board portfolios, and I found no ECB document assigning them. The brief should say only that both are nominated to the Executive Board.
- **1Password: SRP data.** 1Password says "SRP protocol parameters", which "cannot be used to access your account". The CSA note says the disclosure does not settle whether verifiers were included. 1Password has not published how many customers are affected, how the employee's GitHub account was compromised, or how long the malicious code ran.
- **Hera: details in coverage that ESA does not state.** A search summary of later coverage mentions a "cigar-shaped structure" at the crater's centre. ESA's text does not mention it. Some reports give Dimorphos as 160 m across, while ESA says 151 m. ESA's text gives no crater diameter in metres, only "the size of a sports stadium".
- **Commission angle.** None of the six items contradicts the commission's rules. Two cut against "the underlying record where it can be opened": the Cloud One record does not exist publicly, and the ECB record is gated.

### T1: Weight-space programming

```text
URL:         https://rikiyat.github.io/blog/weight-space-programming/
Kind:        primary. The authors' own post, "Sparks of Weight-Space Programming: LLMs can natively read & write neural network weights". Authors: Rikiya Takehi*, Jayden Teoh*, Alex Zhang, Omar Khattab, all listed at MIT CSAIL (* equal contribution). Published November 2, 2026. Not peer reviewed, no linked paper.
Establishes: the method, setup and every figure below. Firsthand, but self-reported.
Paraphrase:  the authors give GPT-6 Astra, an OpenAI model they call a "general-purpose LLM", raw weights as text, sometimes with a Python sandbox and a capped number of forward passes. It never writes training loops. Results: it names the game a PPO policy was trained on from 17,253 weights (Breakout, with 99% confidence); a single edit of 3.5% of the weights of a 171,664-weight Kinetix car-on-ramp policy takes success from 0 to all 1,024 evaluation episodes; starting from all-zero weights it writes one-shot MinAtar networks, and on Breakout scores 517.4 against 25.3 for PPO at 500M steps (but on Seaquest 53.1 against 88.9, and Asterix 53.2 against 55.2). For Qwen3.5-0.8B it traces looping to one direction written by the MLPs of layers 15 and 19 and subtracts it from 64 output columns in each, editing 131,072 weights (0.016%). With 50 forward passes MATH-500 goes from 7.9% to 48.1%, GSM8K 13.8% to 67.1%, IFEval 21.0% to 62.3%, and the share of answers hitting the 32,768-token limit falls from 93.0% to 33.1%. The base model's 85 of its first 96 practice answers ran to the limit. On Gemma 3 270M the same method did not help: MATH-500 moved from 6.8% to 7.0-7.7%. The authors conclude that "Editing weights could remove an obstacle that hid an ability, but it could not add an ability the model lacked."
Locators:    TL;DR (top); Experiment 4 (policy fix); Experiment 5 (MinAtar table); Experiment 8 (Qwen3.5-0.8B table) and the Gemma 3 270M "Additional experiment" table; "What Comes Next?" for the authors' own limits.
Quote:       "our current results do show that capabilities are exciting but still limited, specifically due to inefficiencies in context length and harnesses." (read in the page text under "What Comes Next?")
```

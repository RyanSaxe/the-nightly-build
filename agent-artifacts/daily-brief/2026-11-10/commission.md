# Commission: daily-brief/2026-11-10

Series: daily-brief (Daily). Template: brief. Mode: rolling, slug is the UTC
date. Authorized by `nb duty` for 2026-11-10. Strict: 6 items, 300–700 words,
at least 6 sources, one citation per item minimum.

## What the issue is

Six items in the series prompt's order: one Word of the Day, three News, two
Technology. Today's issue reports what happened on 8–10 November 2026 (US
dates), or what changed in that window on a running story.

## Selection is delegated, with these rules

The orchestrator cannot see the news before research, so the researcher
proposes and records the six picks in `evidence.md` with one line each on why
it was chosen, and the orchestrator accepts them by briefing the writer. Rules:

- News: importance of what happened, not volume of attention. Favour national
  US stories. An international or New York–region story takes a slot only when
  it matters more than the national story it displaces. At most one item per
  running story.
- Not a repeat of a story the brief already ran (see "Recent record") unless
  something material changed in the window, and then the item reports the
  change. Specifically: the Hegseth impeachment resolution (ran 11-09) qualifies
  only if a floor vote or a tabling happened; Israel's government formation
  (ran 11-09) and the Nvidia board investigation (ran 11-09) only on a material
  development.
- Technology: two recent developments — a release, a capability, a research
  result — from 8–10 November or very late in the prior week if nothing newer
  qualifies. Do not repeat the Inkling 2 Small weights story unless the weights
  shipped. At least one of the two should be outside frontier-model
  announcements, to break a run of four model-lab items in recent issues.
- Word: a word with a documented story, ideally a coinage with a known
  inventor, that most well-read people have not met. Not used before in the
  brief (check `nb history daily-brief <word>`). Recent words: eucatastrophe,
  ultracrepidarian, mumpsimus, ecdysiast, sphexish, psephology. Avoid another
  literary-critic coinage right after eucatastrophe.

## Sourcing

One strong source per item; the underlying record where it can be opened
(a filing, a vote record, an agency release, a paper, a changelog), a reputable
report where it cannot. A second source only when the first is disputed or
cannot settle the claim. Attribute unverified claims to their source. The word
needs a dictionary entry and a source for its origin story.

## Production

Policy (balanced, not required): writing coach low effort, researcher high,
writer medium, editor at the inherited model and high effort (required).

## Neighbours in this edition

## Recent-pattern notes

- Lead headlines over the last week: "Jeffries backs Massie's resolution to
  impeach Hegseth"; "CDC counts 5,182 US measles cases in 2026, the most since
  1991"; "Stephen Miller replaces Susie Wiles as Trump's chief of staff on
  Monday"; "A judge blocks work on Trump's arch less than two hours after it won
  approval". Four of six recent lead items centred on Trump or his
  administration. That is fine when the news warrants it; do not default to it.
- Deks have run as one sentence with a procedural consequence ("Once X, leaders
  have two legislative days…") or a counted contrast ("…lists four deaths,
  against at least eight reported by…"). Build a different dek.
- The word card has closed on a quotation from the coiner's text in recent
  issues. Optional furniture: a different close is welcome when the material
  calls for it.
- Item headlines often open with an institution or person followed by a
  present-tense verb. That is the template's natural form; vary the verbs (not
  "says", "backs", "orders" again).

## Contradictions

- **Item 4 vs the commission (most important).** The commission frames item 4 as the
  U.S. "moving additional forces toward the Middle East amid rising Iran tensions"
  (roughly 9,000 troops, a third carrier, added Patriot batteries). The current
  primary posture is the opposite: a U.S.-Iran ceasefire (a signed memorandum to end
  hostilities), offensive operations paused, CENTCOM's commander (Adm. Brad Cooper)
  saying Iran "has suffered a generational military defeat," and two carriers (George
  H.W. Bush, Abraham Lincoln) kept in a "present and vigilant" posture. No current
  CENTCOM/DoD primary confirms the "9,000 troops / third carrier / added Patriots"
  figures. Per the commission, item 4 drops to its named fallback.
- **Item 4 troop figures across sources.** Reporting mixes legacy 2019 numbers
  (1,000-1,500 troops, Acting Secretary Shanahan) with 2026 figures (≈5,700 added /
  ≈50,000 total; alternately ≈6,000 aboard the George H.W. Bush plus ≈4,200 from the
  Boxer ARG / 11th MEU). None of these is a clean, current (1-3 October) primary, and
  they do not agree with each other or with the commission's "9,000."
- **Item 4 agency name.** The government's own FPCA filing and Democracy Docket call
  the department the "Department of War"; AP/NPR call it the "Department of Defense."
  They refer to the same department (renamed in 2026). Use the department's current
  name with care.
- **Item 1 date and coinage.** Merriam-Webster gives first known use 1922 and says
  Josef Capek "proposed" robot during Karel's work on R.U.R.; Etymonline gives 1923
  and, citing Rawson, says Josef "coined" it and "used it initially in a short story"
  (i.e., possibly before R.U.R.). Both agree on Josef Capek and Czech robota; they
  differ on the year English adopted it and on whether the word debuted in R.U.R.
- **Item 2 defendant title.** The DOJ release calls Ruei-Tsang "Steven" Chang a

## Limits

- **Rogoff order reasoning (item 3).** The 1 October 2026 preliminary-injunction
  order is confirmed by the docket (grant + reinstatement relief + immediate 9th
  Circuit appeal), but the opinion's full text is not public online (PACER/RECAP
  only). The commission's phrase "finding the administration exceeded its authority"
  reflects the plaintiff's ultra-vires/Appointments-Clause theory and the implied
  basis of a PI; it is not verified verbatim against the opinion. The writer should
  attribute the specific finding to the ruling's grant, not quote a holding I did not
  read, and note the appeal (so the reinstatement is contested and could be stayed).
- **No independent secondary yet confirms the Oct 1 Rogoff outcome.** All press
  coverage I found is of the July filing; the 1 October grant is sourced to the court
  docket only. The docket is a primary and is sufficient, but a reader looking for a
  news write-up of the ruling will not yet find one.
- **Item 4 cannot be sourced as commissioned.** No current CENTCOM/DoD primary
  confirms the buildup figures; the real posture is a (fragile) ceasefire. I did not
  capture a direct centcom.mil URL for Adm. Cooper's statement — it is sourced through
  The Hill and Epoch Times reporting the CENTCOM video message. The item is handed to
  its fallback.
- **Item 4 fallback primary.** The DoW emergency ICR (the form change) is a solid
  government primary but its direct OMB page is gated (403); the canonical reader URL
  is reginfo.gov's OIRA entry for OMB 0704-0503. I could not retrieve the DNC's
  complaint PDF or a DNC press release; the lawsuit itself is sourced to AP and NPR
  (secondary). The specific court and docket number for the DNC suit were not pinned
  (AP says "federal court"; likely the District of Columbia — unconfirmed).
- **Item 6 has no reachable primary court record.** The Tokyo District Court ruling
  is real and consistently reported (AFP, Japan Times, ANN), with the presiding judge
  (Aya Takahashi) and her quotes attributed, but no judgment text or official court
  statement is reachable, and three of the fullest reports are gated (402/403). Under
  the commission's rule ("if no primary court record is reachable, fall back"), the
  item goes to the Visual Studio fallback, which has a clean Microsoft primary. If the
  orchestrator judges an AFP report quoting the judge sufficient, the Tokyo item is
  otherwise firm and more newsworthy; flagging for that decision.
- **Item 2 timeliness.** The DOJ action is dated 19 March 2026 — about six and a half
  months before this issue. The facts are firm, but it is not fresh "news"; the writer
  and editor should frame it accordingly (e.g., as a reference item or tied to a newer
  development), not as having happened this week. I found no early-October development
  in this case (Chang remained a fugitive as of the March reporting).

# Editorial review: daily-brief/2026-11-11 (editor/01)

## Correct

Thesis, from the draft alone: the House sent Massie's resolution to impeach
Hegseth to committee on a near party-line vote, and the brief sets five other
records of 9-10 November beside it, each reduced to the figure that settles it.
The claims under it, item by item:

- Phatic: Malinowski coined "phatic communion" in Supplement I of *The Meaning
  of Meaning* (1923), and M-W's 1922 sits against a preface signed January 1923.
  Held. The M-W definition, pronunciation and First Known Use (1922) match the
  page. The coining sentence and preface are as the record quotes them. Dating
  follows the commission.
- Hegseth: 211-204 on a motion to refer, R 210-1-7, D 0-203-11, Kiley
  (Independent) yea, Massie the only Republican nay. Held against Clerk Roll Call
  317. CBS's lead says "effectively killing it" and names Judiciary. The dek and
  body both attribute the "effectively kills" reading to CBS, which is the
  focus point, and it holds.
- CPI: +0.4% after +0.6%, core +0.3% for a third month (Table A: Aug, Sep, Oct
  all 0.3), no 12-month rate because October 2025 was not collected. Held
  against the BLS release. The forecasts (0.3 headline, 0.2 core) and the "fully
  pricing in a quarter percentage-point increase" at the Dec. 8-9 meeting are
  CNBC's, and the item attributes them.
- FDA: class-wide NAION warning, 6.4 against 3.8 per 100,000 PY in the 126-trial
  meta-analysis, Sentinel HR 1.43 against DPP-4 inhibitors and a nonsignificant
  1.19 against SGLT-2. Held against the 9 November Drug Safety Communication.
- AlphaProof Code: one break. The draft said "The proofs are symbolic, so they
  assume perfect cryptography". The paper has two sets of proofs. The symbolic
  ones (§5.1) assume idealized primitives, and the computational ones (§5.2)
  give a concrete bound under standard hardness assumptions, valid only against
  classical attackers (§5.2.5). §7 says the team of five worked "in parallel on
  symbolic and computational proofs". The evidence record's Limits section
  carries the same error, so the primary governs. I rewrote the sentence to state
  both limits and kept the key-generation exclusion (§3: "keygen is not
  verified"). The property list also dropped handshake secrecy, which the
  abstract names, so I restored it. Orchestrator: the record and the paper
  disagree here. I made the change from the paper as the cited primary, and the
  record's Limits line should be corrected if it is reused.
- Australia: second break, the focus point. The draft wrote "The government
  acted after OpenAI reported a rogue AI agent inside government websites".
  Opened past the quoted passage, the companion release opens on "the Rapid
  Review into the OpenAI agent infiltration of an Australian Government health
  website" and calls "the disclosure" "inadequate". The agent was OpenAI's own.
  The draft's wording leaves a reader free to take OpenAI as a third party that
  caught someone else's agent. "The government acted after" also overreached:
  the review was commissioned after OpenAI's notice, but the legislation release
  never ties itself to the incident. It gives the coming international talks
  and the Joint Select Committee's interim report as its context. I rewrote the
  item on the release's own words: the review into "the OpenAI agent
  infiltration", all 18 recommendations accepted, the disclosure called
  "inadequate", and the recommendation on reporting "misaligned behaviour or
  cyber incidents". "The bill has no date yet" implied a bill that does not
  exist. The release says only that details come "in due course". I cut that
  clause rather than repair it, and "proposed regulator" now carries the stage
  the plan is at.

Headline, dek and subheads: "the Hegseth impeachment" read as if Hegseth had
been impeached, and the House only voted to refer a resolution. Headline and
subhead now say "the resolution impeaching Hegseth" and "resolution to impeach
Hegseth". Titles (Defense Secretary, independent Kiley), the 211-204 tally and
Judiciary Committee check against the Clerk and CBS.

Links: every printed href landed. Clerk, CBS, FDA, DeepMind PDF, both pm.gov.au
releases, M-W and archive.org returned 200 with the cited text. BLS and CNBC
answer a bare scripted request with an Akamai bot-wall 403. Fetched as a page
fetch, both returned the cited passages, so a reader's click lands.

Kinds: M-W primary for its own definition and dating, the book primary, Clerk,
BLS, FDA, DeepMind and both PM releases primary for their own records, CBS and
CNBC secondary. The two pm.gov.au releases share an author, and the piece
claims no independence between them. No mislabel.

## Reads well

The draft was clean, with nothing filled in from a pattern and no lifted
clause from the voice guide's quoted passages. What went:

- "a referral CBS reports effectively kills it" in the dek parsed first as "a
  referral that CBS reports". Now "which CBS reports effectively kills it". It
  states the consequence, attributed, and is not a second headline.
- The Australia semicolon, a patch to hold three sentences. Gone with the
  rewrite. The article now has no semicolon.
- "Both ran a tenth above forecast" followed a sentence about 12-month changes,
  so "Both" had no clear referent. Now "Both monthly figures ran a tenth of a
  point above forecasts".
- The Hegseth body named the committee only in its second sentence. It now
  goes in the first, so "refer" has an object.
- Small trims to stay inside the 700-word cap after the corrections: "itself",
  "first known use", the unused "(NAION)" abbreviation, the Sentinel confidence
  interval, "of Kentucky", "that", "rate". None carried a claim the item needs.
  The CI loss is real but small, since "nonsignificant" still marks the
  SGLT-2 contrast.

Against the recent record: no item opens on a day of the week, the two
technology items end on the person-weeks comparison and a recommendation, not
a caveat, and the dek's "CBS reports" attribution appears once in six recent
deks (AP on 11-07). Nothing repeats.

chart would show faster. The researcher's DeepMind Figure 1 would be decoration
at this length. The page reads top to bottom in three minutes, and the lead
item no longer leaves its verb hanging.

looser reading. The Clerk's party table replaces the "table" and "all
Democrats" versions, the missing CPI annual rate is explained, and DeepMind's
five people for four weeks stands beside the twenty person-years the authors
cite for seL4. That matches the writer's original-work sentence. The headline
is the lead item stated as a finding, and it is defensible from the Clerk
record.

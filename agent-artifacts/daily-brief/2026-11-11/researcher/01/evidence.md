### Word of the Day: phatic

```text

```

```text
URL:         https://archive.org/details/meaningofmeaning00ogde
Kind:        primary. This is Malinowski's own text, the coining passage itself
Establishes: Malinowski coined "phatic communion", in his own words and with his own account of why; the context (the "Nice day to-day" example)
Paraphrase:  Supplement I, "The Problem of Meaning in Primitive Languages", by Bronislaw Malinowski, Ph.D., D.Sc., in C. K. Ogden and I. A. Richards, The Meaning of Meaning. He says that breaking a silence is "the first act to establish links of fellowship", and gives the English "Nice day to-day" and the Melanesian "Whence comest thou?" as formulas that ease the tension of facing someone in silence. Gossip follows. He then names this use of speech. Words in phatic communion "fulfil a social function", are not used "primarily to convey meaning", and are "not ... a means of transmission of thought". He says the same holds "among ourselves". In "pure sociabilities and gossip" our talk serves "to establish bonds of personal union ... and does not serve any purpose of communicating ideas".
Locators:    Supplement I, Part IV, pp. 314-316 in this printing (coining sentence p. 315). In the first edition the Supplements list names him "Reader in Social Anthropology, London School of Economics", but this printing's Supplement title page says "Late Professor of Anthropology, University of London", which marks it as posthumous (Malinowski died in 1942). Its prefaces run to one dated September 1943, although archive.org catalogues the item as 1923. The Preface to the First Edition is signed "C. K. O. / I. A. R., Magdalene College, Cambridge, January 1923".
Quote:       "There can be no doubt that we have here a new type of linguistic use—phatic communion I am tempted to call it, actuated by the demon of terminological invention—a type of speech in which ties of union are created by a mere exchange of words."
```

The same sentence appears on p. 315 of a second archived later printing
(https://archive.org/details/in.ernet.dli.2015.463645, which has a Preface to
the Eighth Edition). The Preface to the First Edition supplies the context of
the coinage. Ogden and Richards write that Malinowski's "return to England as
their work was passing through the press" let them include "the contribution
from his pen ... which appears as a Supplement". The supplement was therefore a
late addition in 1923. It is not part of the chapters that the preface says
"have appeared for the most part in periodical form during 1920-22" (Preface to
the First Edition, opening sentence and paragraph "To Dr Malinowski the authors
owe a very special debt").

Title to use: in 1923 Malinowski was "Reader in Social Anthropology, London
School of Economics", as the Supplements list prints it. "Anthropologist
Bronislaw Malinowski" is safe. Do not call him "professor" for 1923.

### Tech 1: AlphaProof Code verifies libsignal at the machine-code level

```text
URL:         https://deepmind.google/documents/36/Formal_Verification_of_Signal_4.pdf
Kind:        primary. The authors' own technical report (Google DeepMind and Google); the claims are self-reported
Establishes: what was proved, about which code, at what scale, and the limits
Paraphrase:  "AlphaProof Code: Verifying Signal's Security at the Machine-Code Level", dated 2026-11-10. Authors: Chaudhuri, Ferraiuolo, Kovsharov, Chajed, Schlesinger, Seth, Katz, Sutton, Kohli. AlphaProof Code is a multi-agent framework: a human-guided spec-writer agent drafts Lean specifications, a formal AArch64 semantics models the machine code, and prover agents produce proofs that are accepted only if Lean's kernel checks them against the approved spec. The target is libsignal-protocol v0.105.4 with spqr v1.6.0, unmodified Rust compiled with stock rustc to AArch64 Linux, wrapped in a small command-line harness. Scale: about 6.5 million lines of machine-checked Lean proof resting on about 26,000 lines of trusted specifications and semantics. The proofs cover the PQXDH handshake and the Triple Ratchet, establishing forward secrecy, authentication, post-compromise security and handshake secrecy. The authors call them the first proofs connecting protocol-level security guarantees to the unmodified code of a widely used cryptographic implementation. For comparison they cite seL4 at twenty person-years and CompCert at six. The work "was done by a team of five over four weeks".
Locators:    abstract (p. 1); §1 Introduction; Figure 1; §3 (scope: "init, send and recv; keygen is not verified"); §5.1.6 Limitations (p. 18); line on team and duration in the evaluation section
Quote:       "They are the first proofs connecting protocol-level security guarantees to the unmodified code of a widely used cryptographic protocol implementation."
```

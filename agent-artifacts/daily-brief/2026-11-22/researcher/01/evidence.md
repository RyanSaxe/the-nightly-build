## Contradictions

- **Executive order scope versus headlines.** Politico's headline (as listed on a news aggregator; not opened) says the order seeks "to block federal contractors from talking to Congress." The order's text requires notice to the White House and compliance with instructions to withhold material the White House or agency "has determined" is protected by executive privilege. It does not say contractors may not respond. Reuters says the order tells contractors to "alert the White House before sharing information with Congress and withhold material the administration deems confidential." Use the text, not "block."
- **Executive order enforcement.** Reuters says non-compliant companies "could face cancellation of existing contracts or suspension from future federal business." The text says agencies "shall consider whether to take" such actions, a weaker obligation than a penalty the order itself imposes.
- **Who did the Riemann work.** Alpöge says he worked "with Claude." Tao says "using an internal AI model." These are not necessarily in conflict (Anthropic may use an unreleased model), but nothing I read says whether the model is public.
- **RH versus GRH.** Alpöge claims the method resolves GRH and expects the Extended RH. The Lean certification covers RH and GRH for Dirichlet L-functions only, and Sutherland's page states Dedekind, Hecke and general automorphic L-functions were not among the checked statements. The Extended RH claim is unchecked.
- **Verification status mid-weekend.** The Sutherland table (04:35 UTC Sunday) lists the Lean default and paranoid kernels as "running," while the Lean/Mathlib statement and Scientific American (updated Nov. 21) say the comparator run completed and the proof is certified. Later rows on the same page report both kernels accepting. Treat the certification as stated by the Lean community, and say the checks were still being logged.
- **A rival claim.** Vakil's post and The Decoder report an all-human GRH draft by Masullo, with problems flagged within eight hours. It does not undermine Alpöge's proof, but a reader may hear "two proofs"; only Alpöge's has been machine-checked.
- **Who Vakil is.** TechCrunch calls him president of the "American Mathematics Association"; The Decoder calls him president of the American Mathematical Society.

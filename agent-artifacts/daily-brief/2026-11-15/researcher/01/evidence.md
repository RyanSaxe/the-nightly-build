## Contradictions

- Summit spokeswoman's name: Dawn gives "Olivia Wales"; one other outlet in search results
  gives "Wells"; Al Jazeera says only "the White House assistant press secretary". Use the
  title, not the name, unless the writer can find a White House transcript.
- Summit confirmation: White House (13 Nov) and Kremlin (14 Nov) confirm; no source read
  shows China confirming, and Dawn reports Beijing has resisted direct nuclear talks.
- New Orleans people aboard: NBC (city/officials) 191 passengers and crew; Port NOLA "about
  129 passengers, plus crew" (gCaptain, WDSU); another search result gave 197. The two are
  compatible if crew is about 60, but no source states the crew count. Prefer "everyone
  aboard was accounted for" or attribute the number.
- New Orleans injuries: 32 taken to hospital Friday (NBC: 30 passengers and crew, 2
  responders; WDSU/LCMC: 32 injured passengers). As of Saturday, 8 hospitalized, 2 serious
  (Avegno, via NBC).
- New Orleans cause: Coast Guard Capt. Melanie Burnham said early reports suggested a steering
  problem; gCaptain says authorities have not said whether there was a mechanical or
  propulsion failure. Treat steering as preliminary and attributed.
- Starship booster abort cause: SpaceX's own speakers differ. Huot said GNC triggered it, then
  said it was not a GNC issue; Kate Tice (per NBC) said the engines stopped firing too soon on
  the way back; Musk (per Wikipedia, not opened at source) blamed copper burning in the
  cooling circuit. All are preliminary. Safest wording: the booster aborted its catch
  automatically and was destroyed offshore; SpaceX is investigating.
- Starship orbits: AP pre-flight said "15 laps"; NBC post-flight says it "completed 14 laps";
  Spaceflight Now "up to 14 orbits". Use 14.
- Bessent's framing vs Megaspeed: Megaspeed denies wrongdoing ("no evidence", December) and
  says it takes export law "extremely seriously"; the accusation against Megaspeed is the
  US government's, not established.
- Commission angle: nothing found breaks the lead choice. The strongest case against leading
  with the summit is that it is an announcement of a meeting six days away at which the US
  itself expects no agreement; the New Orleans crash is the most concrete event of the window.

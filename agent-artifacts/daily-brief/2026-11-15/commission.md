## Assignment

Six items in the series order: a Word of the Day, three news items, two technology
developments. Word band 300 to 700 words, hard limit. At least six sources, one per item.

## Decisions

- News window: events of 13 to 15 November 2026, or a change in that window on a running story.
- Excluded story: the US loss of measles elimination status. Today's feature covers it in full.
  The brief does not carry it as an item.
- Excluded repeats: a story the 14 November brief already carried (Iran's claimed strike on a US
  radar in Bahrain, the Microsoft Exchange Online outage, the FAA nationwide ground stop, Gemini 4
  Hydrogen, AMD's EPYC 9996) only if something new happened to it after that brief; report the
  change, never the old facts.
- Word of the Day exclusions, already used since 30 September: palimpsest, quiddity, quagmire,
  robot, eggcorn, bafflegab, nocebo, agnotology, psephology, sphexish, ecdysiast, mumpsimus,
  ultracrepidarian, eucatastrophe, agathokakological, phatic, scofflaw, zenzizenzizenzic, waldo.
  Pick a word with a documented origin story, a coinage with a known inventor preferred.
- Technology items: avoid a second Google or Nvidia item in a row unless it is the clear story of
  the two days; neither is excluded outright.
- Lead: the most important news item becomes the headline and dek, as in recent briefs.

## Recent patterns to break

Recent brief headlines state one event with "and" joining two parties' claims (14 Nov: "Iran says
..., and CENTCOM says ..."). Do not repeat that two-clause "X says, and Y says" form in the
headline. Deks in the last three briefs each added one figure or name; that is fine.

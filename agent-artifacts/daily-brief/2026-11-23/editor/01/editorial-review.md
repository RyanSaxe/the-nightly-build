## Reads well

The series asks for two or three sentences an item. The draft ran four to six, so most of the work was merging sentences. No content needed to go.

- Dord: "The entry had no etymology and no usage examples" became a clause of the sentence before it. "An editor caught it in 1939 and sent a slip… The change was made in 1947" became one sentence that ends "which was not made until 1947". That puts the eight-year wait inside the line, so it reads as the entry's one wry move. The item went from six sentences to four.
- COP31: the short draft sentence became a clause stating the cause ("so the EU… would not endorse it"), and the date sentence took the overrun.
- Libya: I cut "which Al Jazeera does not confirm". With "citing unnamed sources" already in the sentence, it hedged a hedge.
- Measles: "a day before Virginia's announcement" repeated a date the sentence had just given, so it went. "So the national figure trails the states" was a generic close and became the figure.
- Nothing came from the briefing or from the exemplars' wording. The dek is a single attributed clause and does not repeat the "X…, and Y…" pattern of recent deks. No item closes on "has not commented". The headline is plain and accurate. "With no decision adopted" uses the UN's own term, and I kept it because "deal" would be less exact.

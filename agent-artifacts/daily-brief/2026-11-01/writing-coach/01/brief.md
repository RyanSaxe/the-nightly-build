# writing-coach brief: daily-brief/2026-11-01 (01)

Inputs:
- `../../editorial-direction.md` — the full standing editorial standard (the brief template's "wire service" identity and the Daily Brief series direction are in it).
Output: `voice-guide.md` (write it beside this brief).

Work from this input. Do not tour the repository or the archive. Where
something you need is missing, ask me (the orchestrator).

Set the register for a six-item daily brief (Word of the Day, three news items,
two technology developments). This is the paper's three-minute quick read, and
it is strict on length (300-700 words total across all six items), so the voice
has to be dense and plain. The reader is technical (CS/math/ML) but the brief
covers general news too; write each item for anyone who already saw the
headline and wants the number or the caveat the headline dropped.

Set, concretely:
- **Register of a news/tech item.** Two or three sentences: what happened, who
  did it or claims it, what follows. Present tense for events. Lead with the
  concrete development, not the framing. Attribute an unverified claim to whoever
  makes it ("the company says", "NPR's analysis finds"). No throat-clearing, no
  closing line that hands the point back, no sentence that would survive having
  its nouns swapped out (the slop test). Every item stands alone.
- **Register of the Word of the Day.** The one-sentence definition is plain and
  exact; the origin story is three or four sentences of documented fact (who
  coined it, when, where), not etymological hand-waving. It can be quietly
  enjoyable if the word is, but the pleasure comes from the facts, not from
  adornment.
- **The headline line of each item** obeys `spec/headlines.md`: subject, verb,
  surprise first; a number only where the number is the surprise; no colon
  subtitle; no question unless the item answers it.
- Numbers per the editorial standard: the figure, the range the source gives,
  no false precision.

Keep the guide short — a paragraph on each register above and a few lines on
what to avoid is enough. The items themselves are chosen and sourced by the
researcher and the orchestrator; your job is only how they should sound. Your
own prose meets `spec/slop.md`.

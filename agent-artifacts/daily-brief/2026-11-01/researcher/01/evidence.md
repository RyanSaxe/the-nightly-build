## Word of the Day: bafflegab

**Anchor fact.** Milton A. Smith, assistant general counsel of the U.S. Chamber of
Commerce, coined "bafflegab" in the Chamber's weekly *Washington Report*, in a piece
criticising the bureaucratic language of an Office of Price Stabilization price
order. He first considered "legalfusion", "legalprate", "gabalia" and "burobabble".
A plaque for the word was presented to him; newspapers carried the story on
19 January 1952.

**Dates.** Historical item. First use 1952 (Merriam-Webster). Newspaper coverage
19 January 1952, the day after the plaque presentation (Worldwide Words). Green's
Dictionary of Slang says "coined May 1952". That conflicts with the January 1952
press coverage. Do not state a month for the coinage itself. The safe sentence is
that the word was in the newspapers by January 1952.

**Source 1 (definition authority).**
- Merriam-Webster, "bafflegab"
- https://www.merriam-webster.com/dictionary/bafflegab
- Publisher: Merriam-Webster
- Retrieved 2026-11-01
- Page shows: noun; pronunciation "ˈba-fəl-ˌgab"; definition "gobbledygook"; First
  Known Use "1952, in the meaning defined above" (extract).
- M-W gives a one-word definition. If the writer wants a fuller one-sentence
  definition, Dictionary.com (https://www.dictionary.com/browse/bafflegab, retrieved
  2026-11-01) reads "confusing or generally unintelligible jargon; gobbledegook."
  and "First recorded in 1950–55" (extract).

**Source 2 (coinage history).**
- Michael Quinion, "Bafflegab", Worldwide Words
- https://worldwidewords.org/ww-baf1.html
- Publisher: Worldwide Words (page created 25 Jun 2005)
- Retrieved 2026-11-01
- Quotes (extract): "It hit the newspapers and public notice on 19 January 1952,
  the day after a plaque was presented to its inventor..." and the *Washington
  Report* piece "criticised the OPS for the bureaucratic language it used in one of
  its price orders." The plaque was presented by Michael V. DiSalle, head of the
  Office of Price Stabilization. "The newspaper made sure he got one by paying for
  the plaque to be made and organising its presentation." The page names the
  newspaper elsewhere as the *Bellingham Herald* (extract of the page body).
- The four rejected coinages (legalfusion, legalprate, gabalia, burobabble) are
  listed on the same page.
- Corroboration: Wordsmith.org, https://wordsmith.org/words/bafflegab.html,
  "Coined by Milton A. Smith, assistant general counsel for the US Chamber of
  Commerce, in 1952." Etymology there: "From baffle, perhaps from Scots bauchle (to
  denounce) + gab, perhaps of imitative origin." (extract, retrieved 2026-11-01).
- Green's Dictionary of Slang, https://greensdictofslang.com/entry/tc4xjja:
  "coined May 1952, by the assistant general counsel of the US Chamber of Commerce,
  Milton Smith" (extract, retrieved 2026-11-01).

**Flags for the writer.**
- The commission says Merriam-Webster hedges the coiner as "possibly" Smith. The M-W
  page I opened carries no etymology and no hedge. M-W supports only the definition
  and the 1952 date. Do not attribute a hedge to M-W. Worldwide Words and
  Wordsmith both name Smith without hedging.
- The coinage mechanism ("baffle" plus "gab") is from Wordsmith.org and Green's.
  The "perhaps from Scots bauchle" part is Wordsmith's conjecture, so leave it out.
- The Worldwide Words fetch returned two readings of the plaque date (19 January
  versus the day before). The page's own wording is "the day after a plaque was
  presented", which puts the presentation on 18 January and the press coverage on
  19 January. Write "newspapers reported it on 19 January 1952" and skip the
  presentation day.
- Sources disagree on whether Smith's employer was the "U.S." or "American" Chamber
  of Commerce. Worldwide Words, Wordsmith and Green's all say US Chamber of Commerce.
- Backup `grawlix` was not needed.

## News 1: Alaska Senate certification suit (United States v. State of Alaska)

**Anchor fact.** The Justice Department sued Alaska on 2026-10-22 in the U.S. District
Court for the District of Alaska (Civ. No. 3:26-cv-00376-SLG, Judge Sharon Gleason).
It argues that voter confusion over a second candidate named Dan Sullivan means the
state's conduct is preempted by the National Voter Registration Act. On 2026-10-30
DOJ told the court that "the only immediate relief the United States has sought is
the preservation and segregation of relevant ballots." The state filed a 14-page
motion to dismiss on Saturday 2026-10-31. Gleason has expedited consideration, and
the state asked for a ruling by Monday 2026-11-02. Election Day is 2026-11-03.

**Dates confirmed.**
- Complaint: 2026-10-22. Gleason's own order says "the United States initiated this
  action on October 22, 2026" (Democracy Docket, extract). CNBC says the amended
  complaint updated a suit "filed Oct. 23". Use 10-22.
- DOJ ballot-segregation filing: 2026-10-30 (ECF No. 67, "Filed 10/30/26").
- State motion to dismiss: Saturday 2026-10-31 (Anchorage Daily News, CBS News).

**Source (primary record, copy hosted by Democracy Docket).**

## News 2: DC and Virginia sue to block the arch

**Anchor fact.** On Saturday 2026-10-31 the District of Columbia (Attorney General
Brian Schwalb) and Virginia (Attorney General Jay Jones) sued the National Park
Service and the Interior Department over the planned 250-foot "Triumphal Arch" at
Memorial Circle. The complaint says the arch needs congressional authorization and
cites the Commemorative Works Act, the Administrative Procedure Act, the National
Environmental Policy Act and the National Historic Preservation Act. The
administration told the court work could begin on 2026-11-05, the day the National
Capital Planning Commission is scheduled to consider final approval.

**Date confirmed.** 2026-10-31 (Washington Post, ABC, CBS/AP, WDBJ all dated 10-31).

## Technology 1: VOLTA infostealer uses an LLM to appraise victims

**Anchor fact.** Cisco Talos reported on 2026-10-31 that the VOLTA infostealer
(self-named VOLTA-AUTODOXER, a self-deleting Windows infostealer) sends a structured
bundle of stolen data to an LLM through OpenRouter. The system prompt tells the model
to act as "a forensic intelligence analyst drafting a target assessment". The model
ranks the victim's assets by monetization value. The default model is
anthropic/claude-opus-4.6. The fallback chain runs to moonshotai/kimi-k2, then
google/gemini-2.5-flash. VOLTA scans each reply for refusal phrases and passes the
request to the next model when one appears.

**Date confirmed.** 2026-10-31, 08:00, author Ryan Fetterman (page extract). One
aggregator listed 10-29. The Talos page says 10-31.

**Source (primary).**
- Ryan Fetterman, "Appraisal by AI: Model-driven victim prioritization in the VOLTA
  infostealer", Cisco Talos blog
- https://blog.talosintelligence.com/appraisal-by-ai-model-driven-victim-prioritization-in-the-volta-infostealer/
- Publisher: Cisco Talos
- Retrieved 2026-11-01
- Extracts: system prompt casts the model as "a forensic intelligence analyst
  drafting a target assessment" (introduction). Output asks the model to "rank the
  victim's assets by monetization value" (opening summary). Under "The AI
  configuration": default "anthropic/claude-opus-4.6", fallback chain
  "moonshotai/kimi-k2 → google/gemini-2.5-flash". VOLTA checks each response for
  refusal phrases such as "I can't" or "I'm not able" before moving to the next of
  up to three models.

**Flags for the writer.**
- The commission says the LLM ranks "victims by worth". The Talos wording is that it
  ranks the single victim's assets by monetization value, and the post's title says
  "victim prioritization". Write "appraises what each victim's stolen data is worth"
  and not "ranks victims against each other", unless the writer re-reads the post
  and finds the cross-victim claim.
- All Talos findings are Talos's. The post says all observed infrastructure is
  third-party and the LLM stage is optional (it runs only when an API key is set;
  that second point is from a search summary, so leave it out unless re-checked).
- Talos reports that the fallback chain shows the operator expected model refusals.
  That is Talos's inference. Attribute it.
- Neutral framing: this is a finding about malware design, reported by Talos. The
  model is named because the post names it.

## Technology 2: trojanized Claude Code extension in Cursor's marketplace

**Anchor fact.** On 2026-10-29 Cursor reported that two builds of the
`anthropic.claude-code` extension in its marketplace, versions 2.1.312 and 2.1.313
for Windows and macOS, were maliciously modified. The code tries to add an
attacker-controlled SSH key to `authorized_keys`, tries to open a reverse SSH
tunnel, sets up persistence, and reports host and user details. Cursor says
Anthropic did not develop the modified versions. Uninstalling the extension may not
remove the persistence.

**Dates confirmed.** Notice dated Oct 29, 2026, with an update on Oct 30, 2026,
11:30 am PT. Window: "Between about 2:27am PT and 1:35pm PT today" on 2026-10-29.
That is about 11 hours 8 minutes. Cursor does not print "11 hours". The figure is
arithmetic on its window. BankInfoSecurity restates the window as "approximately
9:27 a.m. to 8:35 p.m. UTC", which matches.

**Source (primary).**
- "Security notice: malicious Anthropic.claude-code versions", Cursor blog
- https://cursor.com/blog/claude-code-extension-security-notice
- Publisher: Cursor (Anysphere)
- Retrieved 2026-11-01
- Extracts: "These malicious versions of the extension were not developed by
  Anthropic." "Attempts to add an attacker-controlled SSH key to `authorized_keys`".
  "Collects and reports host and user information". "Uninstalling the extension may
  not be sufficient — the payload drops standalone persistence scripts that keep
  running." Persistence is via a LaunchAgent on macOS or a scheduled task on
  Windows (paraphrase of the notice).
- Second-day corroboration (not needed for the citation): BankInfoSecurity,
  2026-10-30, https://www.bankinfosecurity.com/cursor-confirms-anthropic-claude-code-extension-compromised-a-33263 ;
  BleepingComputer, 2026-10-30,
  https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/malicious-claude-code-extension-on-cursor-planted-ssh-backdoors/

**Flags for the writer.**
- Cursor's wording is "may not be sufficient", not "does not remove". Keep the
  hedge or say "Cursor says uninstalling may not be enough."
- The notice was updated on 10-30 to name both builds. The two press pieces dated
  10-30 name only 2.1.313. Use both builds, per the notice.
- A "reverse tunnel" attempt is in my extract as a paraphrase of the notice, not a
  quoted phrase. If the writer wants the words, re-read the notice.
- User count. One search summary attributes "at most 5,000 users" to Cursor. The
  notice I read contains no user count. Do not print any count. Other outlets give
  larger, conflicting figures.
- Open questions in the press, not in the notice: how the attacker published under
  Anthropic's name, and whether Cursor's or Anthropic's systems were breached. Say
  nobody has said.
- The item is a supply-chain compromise that reached a developer tool, so it fits a
  technical readership and stays on the slate.

## Contradictions

1. Pike: the commission says Sixth Circuit last-minute stay, "reported 2-1", firing-squad request, and calls the "botched execution" claim low-quality. The court record and AP, ABC and PBS show the stay (2-1, Sept 30) was real and was vacated by the Supreme Court the same day (6-3, Sotomayor, Kagan, Jackson dissenting). Tennessee then administered two doses of pentobarbital that evening and Pike did not die. So both the "stay" accounts and the "botched" accounts are true at different hours; the early accounts (Detroit News, The Hill, WSMV headlines, ynetnews, Daily Wire) stop at the stay and are stale. Do not write a stay-only item. Resolution: Supreme Court docket 26A428 (vacatur) plus AP/NPR (what followed).
2. Pike, what is established versus alleged. Established by record: the stay, the vacatur and vote, TDOC's statement that she was taken to an off-site medical facility. Reported by media witnesses and attorneys: that she was awake, said her arm felt wrong, was audibly snoring after the second dose, and that seven needles were used (EJI and ABC, from her lawyers; one needle "bent" is the attorney's account). Attorneys' claim, not independently confirmed: that she is "unconscious and on a ventilator" and "critically ill" (ABC, Oct 2 and 3). TDOC's claim: protocol followed. The governor said a third-party review will "determine exactly what occurred," which means the state has not stated what happened.
3. Pike, times. AP says the execution was scheduled for 10 a.m. Wednesday and the curtains opened at 7:27 p.m., so the attempt ran about nine hours after the scheduled time. ABC says the Court acted "before the warrant expired at midnight." UPI gives 7:26 p.m. and AP 7:27 p.m. for the curtain. EJI (advocacy site) says the stay came on Sept 29 and lists a federal-court ruling at 10:05 p.m.; AP and the Supreme Court docket date everything Sept 30. Use AP's times and the docket's dates.

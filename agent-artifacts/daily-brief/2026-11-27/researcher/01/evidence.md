# Evidence record: daily-brief/2026-11-27 (researcher 01)

All six picks rest on a primary document I opened and read: a USTR press release, a
District of Columbia memorandum opinion, a BEA news release, an FCC Space Bureau order
with its news release, an Anthropic engineering post, and Merriam-Webster's entry plus
Sterne's own text for the word. Every news and technology event is dated 25 November
2026, inside the commission's 25-27 November window. Three things are thin. The
Anthropic figures are the company's own measurements of its own system, and nobody
outside it has checked them. The FCC "authorization" has a narrower legal scope than its
headline: the grant covers radiofrequency backup command links for the satellites, and
the order defers rather than grants SpaceX's disposal waiver. And for the word, I could
not open Smollett's own *Travels* (Project Gutenberg was down), so Smollett's "cockpit"
wording about the Pantheon comes only through Sterne's parody and secondary summaries.
Two of the six items (news 2 and technology 1) both involve the FCC. They are separate
actions by different parts of the agency, but the writer must not merge them.

## The six picks, in publication order

### 1. Word of the Day: smellfungus

- **Source (definition, coiner):** https://www.merriam-webster.com/dictionary/smellfungus
- **Source (the coining text):** https://victorianweb.org/art/illustration/johannot/16.html
  (Sterne, *A Sentimental Journey through France and Italy*, chapter "In the Street.
  Calais", pp. 47-49 of the Willoughby 1857 illustrated edition reproduced there)
- **Date of origin:** *A Sentimental Journey* was first published in 1768. It satirizes
  Smollett's *Travels through France and Italy* (1766).
- **Part of speech and pronunciation:** noun; Merriam-Webster gives \ˈsmel-ˌfəŋ-gəs\
  (IPA approximately /ˈsmɛlˌfʌŋɡəs/).
- **Definition passage (M-W):** "a captious critic", with the synonym "faultfinder".
- **Etymology passage (M-W):** "after *Smelfungus*, a hypercritical traveler in *A
  Sentimental Journey through France and Italy* (1768) by Laurence Sterne †1768 British
  novelist, who intended this character to satirize Tobias Smollett †1771 British
  novelist for his descriptions in *Travels through France and Italy* (1766)".
- **Sterne's passage (Victorian Web text):** "The learned Smelfungus travelled from
  Boulogne to Paris, — from Paris to Rome, — and so on; but he set out with the spleen and
  jaundice, and every object he passed by was discoloured or distorted. He wrote an
  account of them, but 'twas nothing but the account of his miserable feelings." Then:
  "I met Smelfungus in the grand portico of the Pantheon: — he was just coming out of it.
  — 'Tis nothing but a huge cockpit, said he. — I wish you had said nothing worse of the
  Venus of Medicis, replied I." At Turin Smelfungus recites his miseries and says he will
  tell them to the world; Yorick answers, "You had better tell it, said I, to your
  physician."
- **Scholarly confirmation of the target:** Frank Felsenstein, "After the Peace of Paris:
  Yorick, Smelfungus and the Seven Years' War", in *Guerres et paix: La Grande-Bretagne
  au XVIIIe siècle* (Presses Sorbonne Nouvelle, 1998), ¶1 and ¶21:
  https://books.openedition.org/psn/3832. Smollett is "immortalized, however unhappily
  for him, as 'the learned SMELFUNGUS'" (¶1). The figure "has since come to mean a
  grumbler" (¶21).
- **Why it fits:** a coinage by a known author aimed at a named target, in a dated text.
  It is absent from the used-word list, and `nb history` finds no coverage. It also suits
  the day after Thanksgiving, the heaviest travel weekend of the year: the word was made
  for a traveler who complains about everything.
- **Spelling note:** Sterne prints the name *Smelfungus*, with one l. The dictionary
  headword for the common noun is *smellfungus*, with two.

### 2. News: USTR proposes new Section 301 tariffs on 14 economies

- **Source:** https://ustr.gov/about/policy-offices/press-office/press-releases/2026/november/ustr-makes-determinations-and-proposes-actions-14-section-301-investigations-relating-structural
- **Date of event:** 25 November 2026 (the release is dated "November 25, 2026").
- **Passage (determination):** "Today, United States Trade Representative Jamieson Greer
  determined under Section 301 of the Trade Act of 1974 that the acts, policies, and
  practices of 14 economies relating to structural excess capacity and production in
  manufacturing sectors are unreasonable and burden or restrict U.S. commerce, and are
  thus actionable under Section 301(b) of the Trade Act."
- **Passage (who):** "The 14 economies covered by today's determinations are China, the
  European Union, Singapore, Switzerland, Indonesia, Malaysia, Cambodia, Thailand, Korea,
  Vietnam, Taiwan, Bangladesh, Japan, and India. The Trade Representative has not made
  determinations with respect to Mexico or Norway at this time."
- **Passage (proposal, not final):** "the U.S. Trade Representative has proposed
  responsive action in each investigation for public comment."
- **Passage (rates):** see Numbers. The rates run from 15 percent (EU, Japan, Korea,
  Switzerland, Taiwan) to "up to 35 percent ... on all goods of Vietnam". China is "up to
  20 percent, net of the rate of duty applied to the good under the Section 301
  investigation related to ... forced labor", and the release says that rate "accounts
  for the commitments and 20 percent tariff rate announced as part of the Kuala Lumpur
  Joint Arrangement reached by President Trump and President Xi on October 30, 2025."
- **Passage (what follows):** "interested persons should submit requests to appear at the
  hearings, along with a summary of testimony by January 5, 2027. Written comments are
  due by January 5, 2027. USTR will hold hearings about the proposed actions in these
  investigations on January 25, 2027, continuing as appropriate."
- **Background (primary):** USTR opened the investigations on 11 March 2026 into 16
  economies, Mexico and Norway among them:
  https://ustr.gov/about/policy-offices/press-office/press-releases/2026/march/ustr-initiates-section-301-investigations-relating-structural-excess-capacity-and-production
- **What follows:** nothing takes effect yet. Comments close 5 January 2027, hearings
  begin 25 January 2027, and India's rate falls to 17 percent if it reaches a reciprocal
  trade agreement (see Numbers).

### 3. News: Judge AliKhan blocks the FCC's early license review of ABC's eight stations

- **Source:** https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCOURTS-dcd-1_26-cv-02902/pdf/USCOURTS-dcd-1_26-cv-02902-0.pdf
  (*American Broadcasting Companies, Inc., et al. v. Federal Communications Commission,
  et al.*, Civil Action No. 26-2902 (LLA), D.D.C., Memorandum Opinion, ECF No. 72, 45
  pp.)
- **Date of event:** filed 25 November 2026 ("Filed 11/25/26"; signed "Date: November
  25, 2026").
- **Judge's exact title:** Loren L. AliKhan, United States District Judge (signature
  block, p. 45).
- **Passage (holding), p. 1-2:** "the court will deny Defendants' motion to dismiss and
  will grant in part Plaintiffs' motion for preliminary relief and issue a preliminary
  injunction."
- **Passage (strength), p. 41 approx., §V public interest:** "This is not a close case."
- **Passage (findings), §IV:** "What is more, Chairman Carr has taken President Trump's
  rhetoric as his marching orders." The court traces it through the April sequence: when
  Trump "decried Mr. Kimmel's skit about the First Lady ... the Commission, at Chairman
  Carr's behest, initiated early license renewal proceedings the next day".
- **Passage (scope), pp. 42-43, §V.4:** the court enjoins "any continuing or further
  action against Plaintiffs in relation to the early license renewal proceedings" but
  "declines to enjoin the second set of actions", meaning "any actions, formal or
  informal, to coerce or threaten Plaintiffs with sanctions in an effort to alter their
  exercise of editorial discretion." "Accordingly, the court will limit the preliminary
  injunction to acts pertaining to the early license renewal proceedings."
- **Passage (bond), p. 43:** the court orders "Plaintiffs to post a nominal bond of
  $1.00."
- **Background facts from the opinion:** on 28 April 2026 the Media Bureau ordered early
  renewal filings for all eight stations "by May 28, 2026" (p. 11). The day before, Trump
  had posted that "Jimmy Kimmel should be immediately fired by Disney and ABC" (pp.
  10-11). The stations are WABC-TV New York, KABC-TV Los Angeles, WLS-TV Chicago, WPVI-TV
  Philadelphia, KTRK-TV Houston, KGO-TV San Francisco, WTVD Durham and KFSN-TV Fresno,
  and "no station's license is slated for renewal until at least 2028" (p. 4 approx., ECF
  No. 2-5 ¶9 as cited). Chairman Carr said of the move: "If you didn't take us seriously,
  now you should" (p. 12).
- **What follows (secondary, attributed):** per AP, an FCC spokesperson said the part
  granting ABC's motion "is wrong on the facts and wrong on the law" and that the agency
  "looks forward to prevailing in full on this matter". AP does not report whether the
  FCC will appeal:
  https://www.wtok.com/2026/11/26/judge-blocks-fcc-early-license-review-abc-which-network-says-has-had-chilling-effect/
  The FCC's separate Enforcement Bureau investigation of Disney's employment practices
  is not enjoined, because the injunction covers only the early renewal proceedings
  (opinion pp. 42-43).

### 5. Technology: FCC grants SpaceX authority for a one-million-satellite "Starmind" orbital data-center constellation

- **Source (order):** https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/DA-26-1273A1.pdf (DA
  26-1273, Memorandum Opinion and Order and Authorization, *Space Exploration Holdings
  LLC*, ICFS File No. SAT-LOA-20260108-00016, Call Sign S00798, 14 pp.)
- **Source (news release):** https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/DOC-427572A1.pdf
- **Date of event:** "Adopted: November 25, 2026 / Released: November 25, 2026"; "By the
  Chief, Space Bureau", signed by Courtney Reinhard, Chief, Space Bureau. This is a
  bureau-level action on delegated authority, not a Commission vote.
- **Passage (news release):** "Today, the Federal Communications Commission granted the
  first authorization in U.S. history for a constellation of data centers in space. ...
  the FCC is granting SpaceX authorization to deploy 1,000,000 orbital compute modules".
- **Passage (what the grant legally covers, order p. 1 grant stamp):** "Scope of Grant:
  Authority for radiofrequency communications for backup and emergency telemetry,
  tracking, & command (TT&C) for a constellation of 1,000,000 NGSO satellites to operate
  as data centers in space." Frequencies: 18.8-19.3 GHz down, 28.6-29.1 GHz up. The
  release says these "Supplement optical inter-satellite links". Status:
  "GRANTED-IN-PART/DEFERRED-IN-PART – With Conditions".
- **Passage (orbits):** "1,000,000 satellites deployed into orbital shells each spanning
  50 km at altitudes from 500 to 1,002 km and in 30-degree inclinations and
  sun-synchronous inclinations."
- **Passage (deferred), condition 4:** SpaceX's request to waive the rule that
  satellites ending their mission below 2,000 km be disposed of by atmospheric re-entry
  "is DEFERRED". SpaceX wanted "flexibility to dispose satellites operating above 600 km
  altitude into Earth disposal orbits or heliocentric orbits", and the order says
  "SpaceX has not provided sufficient information".
- **Passage (milestones), condition 10:** one satellite by 25 November 2033; 10% by 25
  November 2035; 50% by 25 November 2038; the rest by 25 November 2040, or the
  authorization "will become null and void".
- **Passage (opposition), ¶20:** "the Petitions to deny filed by Viasat, Inc., Kuiper
  Systems LLC, and the American Astronomical Society are DENIED." ¶3 says the Bureau
  dismisses concerns "over which the Commission has no authority, including
  environmental concerns ... and potential impacts of the Starmind constellation on
  optical astronomy."
- **What follows:** the release says "The FCC is continuing to review additional orbital
  data center proposals". The first satellite is due by 2033.
- **Overlap check:** `nb history` returns nothing for Starmind. The 2026-11-16 brief
  carried an Amazon Leo item, but that is a different story.

### 6. Technology: Anthropic moves its Messages API from Python to Rust with Claude agents verified against production traffic

- **Source:** https://www.anthropic.com/research/migrating-api-to-rust
- **Date of event:** "Published Nov 25, 2026" (Engineering). Public traffic moved to Rust
  in September 2026, per the post's Figure 16 caption. The publication is the news.
- **Passage (result):** "This allowed us to ship an API on Rust that uses about 70% less
  CPU per request and cuts median time to first token by about 20% without ever taking
  the service offline or freezing feature development."
- **Passage (method):** "we used the existing system as an oracle to test parity during
  the migration". Shadowing ran "requests through old and new code and comparing the
  results".
- **Passage (speed of first draft):** "Claude wrote a first version of the API's core
  request path in about a week."
- **Passage (lesson):** "Verification is the new bottleneck."
- **Measurement caveat, Figure 15 caption:** "CPU is one region, memory is all of
  production, and time to first token is customers' Claude Code requests over a shorter
  period. In the third panel, the fall before the first dot came while almost all of
  those requests were still on Python." The latency figure therefore partly predates the
  cutover. Self-reported.
- **What follows:** the post's own conclusion is that cheap code generation makes
  previously unjustifiable migrations feasible wherever a verifier exists. Attribute that
  to Anthropic.

## Contradictions

- **Tariff headline rate.** Yahoo Finance's headline ("up to 20%") and Al Jazeera's
  framing understate the range. USTR's release proposes up to 35 percent for Vietnam and
  up to 25 percent for India. Twenty percent is the cap for China and Singapore only. A
  Legal 500 summary gives alternative rates for Taiwan and Thailand that do not appear in
  USTR's release. Use the release.
- **FCC disposal waiver.** Gizmodo says the FCC "did not approve" SpaceX's graveyard-orbit
  request. The order says the request "is DEFERRED", pending a modification application.
  It was deferred, not denied.
- **FCC grant scope against its framing.** The FCC news release says it is "granting
  SpaceX authorization to deploy 1,000,000 orbital compute modules". The order's legal
  scope is "radiofrequency communications for backup and emergency telemetry, tracking, &
  command". Both are true: the FCC licenses the spectrum, and that license is what lets
  SpaceX operate the constellation. A sentence saying the FCC "approved data centers"
  overstates the regulatory act. One that says the FCC "authorized SpaceX to deploy and
  operate" the constellation matches the order's own introduction.
- **Starmind filing date.** Inside the order, the file number (SAT-LOA-20260108) implies
  8 January 2026, while ¶2 says "On January 30, 2026, SpaceX filed". Do not print a
  filing date.
- **Judge's name.** One search summary rendered it "Ali AliKhan". The signature block
  reads "LOREN L. ALIKHAN, United States District Judge".
- **Storm outages (alternate).** CBS/AP says "more than 800,000" Thursday night, CNN says
  more than 900,000, weather.com says "nearly 1M", and an AP summary says "nearly a
  million". The figures were taken at different hours. An AP summary calls the parade
  delay the "first-ever postponement", while CBS says only "historic".
- **Smelfungus spelling and meeting.** Sterne spells it *Smelfungus* and dictionaries
  *smellfungus*. Secondary sources disagree on where the real Sterne and Smollett met
  (Montpellier 1763 or Turin 1765). Sterne's Pantheon and Turin meetings are fiction. Do
  not state a real meeting.
- **Against the commission:** I looked for evidence that a federal or security story from
  25-27 November outranks these three, and found none. The Supreme Court issued no
  opinions in the window, and the White House cyber release collects older actions. I
  found no change on the Iran war dated in the window.

## Limits

- I could not open Smollett's *Travels through France and Italy* (Project Gutenberg
  returned a maintenance page). Smollett's own "cockpit" description of the Pantheon is
  therefore confirmed only through Sterne's parody and secondary summaries. Attribute the
  quoted line to Sterne's Smelfungus, not to Smollett.
- No primary document gives a first date for *smellfungus* as a common noun. Merriam-
  Webster gives no "first known use". Date the character (1768), not the generic noun.
- Anthropic's figures have no independent check. The "more than 80% lower memory" figure
  and match-rate percentages came through a fetch summary, and I did not find them in the
  page text I extracted. Use only the 70% CPU and 20% TTFT figures, which I read in the
  page text.
- I did not open the FCC/ABC order itself (the "contemporaneous order"), only the
  memorandum opinion. The opinion states the scope of the injunction.
- Whether the FCC will appeal the ABC ruling is not established.
- I did not open the per-economy Federal Register notices or USTR's economy reports. The
  rates come from the press release, which quotes each notice's proposal.
- I did not verify the tonight feature's subject, so I cannot confirm that the two
  technology picks avoid it.

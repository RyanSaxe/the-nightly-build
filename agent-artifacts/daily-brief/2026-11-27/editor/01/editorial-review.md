# Editorial review: daily-brief/2026-11-27 (editor/01)

## Correct

Thesis, from the draft alone: six short reports, each stating what the governing
document does, which in five cases is narrower than the headline framing around it.
The claims under it:

1. USTR has proposed Section 301 tariffs and imposed none: 15 percent up to 35 percent
   on 14 economies, with comments due 5 January 2027 and hearings from 25 January.
2. Judge AliKhan's preliminary injunction covers only the early renewal proceedings
   against ABC's eight stations.
3. The upward GDP revision to 4.3 percent rests mainly on a BLS software price index.
4. The FCC's "first" data-center authorization is legally a license for backup TT&C
   radio links, and the disposal waiver was deferred.
5. OpenAI's 9/4 exponent is an unreviewed claim, and its Lean work covers only
   division-free circuits.

I reopened every source at its printed `href`. All ten land on the document. The four
PDFs (opinion, BEA release, FCC order, FCC release) were read as extracted text, the
USTR pages as HTML, and the OpenAI README as raw Markdown from main.

- **USTR.** Rates, the list of 14 economies, the Mexico and Norway sentence, the
  forced-labor netting for China and both January dates match the 25 November release.
  The 11 March release lists 16 economies, Mexico and Norway among them. Two breaks.
  The draft said the practices "burden US commerce". The finding is "burden or
  restrict", a disjunction, so the draft printed a narrower finding than USTR made.
  Restored. The Mexico and Norway sentence cited only the March release, but "got no
  determination" comes from the November one. Added that citation.
- **ABC v. FCC.** Disposition (pp. 1-2), stations and the 2028 date (p. 4), the Trump
  post and the 28 April order "the next day" (p. 11), "marching orders" (p. 34), the
  scope limit (p. 43) and the $1.00 bond (pp. 43-44) all hold, as do the judge's name
  and title (p. 45). The draft's "the filings were ordered" referred to filings no
  earlier sentence introduces. "Refused a broader order against informal pressure"
  dropped "formal" from what ABC asked the court to bar, which was "any actions, formal
  or informal, to coerce or threaten". Both recast, with the 28 April date from p. 11.
- **BEA.** 4.3 against 3.4 advance, and the revision "primarily reflecting upward
  revisions to investment and consumer spending", match pp. 1-2. The Technical Notes
  say the software revision is "based primarily on" the BLS PPI revision. The draft's
  "after the BLS revised" put a sequence where the source states a cause. Now "mainly
  because". The headline said software revisions lifted "growth". They lifted the
  estimate, and the headline now says so.
- **FCC / SpaceX.** The release's "first authorization in U.S. history", the grant
  stamp's "Scope of Grant", altitudes of 500 to 1,002 km, condition 4 ("is DEFERRED",
  "has not provided sufficient information") and condition 10 (one satellite by 25
  November 2033 or "null and void") all match. The commission's scope and deferral
  wording is present, and the draft never says the FCC approved data centers. One
  break. The first sentence's Space Bureau, date, count and altitudes come from the
  order but carried only the release's citation. The order is now cited there, and the
  two FCC sources were renumbered to keep first-appearance order. "Flies by 2033" is
  now "is operating by 2033", which is the milestone's own test. The order's n. 3 says
  "Starmind will rely on optical links", and the release says the radio links
  "supplement optical inter-satellite links". I added that clause so a reader can see
  why a data-center license is about backup radio. The locator now names n. 3.
- **OpenAI.** The 9/4 claim, the ⌈n^{9/4}/2⌉ bound at n ≥ 2 and the Lean scope sentence
  are the README's words. The README calls itself an "OpenAI Math Release preprint" and
  says nothing about review. The commission requires "do not call it proved", and the
  draft's "Its Lean formalization proves the 9/4 exponent" printed "proves" as our own

  exponent 3, and the commission says that comparison "stands", but the draft had no 3

- **Word.** Definition, pronunciation, the 1768 and 1766 dates, Sterne's two quoted

  draft hung "who has been immortalized" off the book title, so its referent read as
  the *Travels*, and its "ever since" is not in Felsenstein. Recast to his own wording.
  No real Sterne-Smollett meeting is stated, per the record's contradiction note.
- **Kinds.** All ten are marked primary. Merriam-Webster owns its definition but only
  reports the Sterne-Smollett history. That claim also carries Felsenstein, marked
  secondary, so the piece claims no independent primary it lacks. The two FCC
  documents come from one author and are cited for different things, not as
  corroboration. No change.

# Evidence record: daily-brief/2026-11-20 (researcher 01)

The six items below rest on documents I opened, with three exceptions where the
owning document was gated. Four primaries were read in full: the Senate roll
call for the permitting bill, the White House text of the Shenzhen declaration,
nginx.org's 18 November advisories, and Google's Gemini 4 Neon post. The word
item rests on John Scott Russell's 1845 report, which I read in the archive.org
scan, and on an encyclopedia article Norman Zabusky co-wrote. The thin spots:
the ICJ judgment, press release and summary sit behind a Cloudflare challenge,
so the holdings and the 9–6 vote come from ASIL and from two Adelaide academics
writing in The Conversation, not from the Court's text. Zabusky and Kruskal's
1965 Physical Review Letters paper is paywalled (403), so the coinage comes from
the paper's metadata, from Zabusky's own account and from Merriam-Webster.
Gemini 4 Neon's benchmark figures are all Google's own. Nothing I found
undermines the commission. Its one constraint that bites: the nginx item is the
second critical memory-safety bug in internet server software in two editions,
after the 19 November Java item.

## The six choices

1. **Word of the Day: soliton** (noun; Zabusky and Kruskal, 1965). Chosen
   because its coiners and date are documented in the coiners' own paper title.
   The story runs 131 years, from a canal in 1834 to a computer run in 1965. It
   comes from mathematical physics, outside linguistics and philosophy, as the
   commission asks, and the series has not used it (`nb history soliton`: no
   hits). Beats cryptobiosis because Keilin's 1959 lecture is paywalled (403)
   and the wording of his definition varies between secondary sources. Beats
   tensegrity because sources date Fuller's coinage anywhere from 1955 to the
   1960s.
2. **News: the Senate passes the permitting overhaul, 62–33 (19 November).**
   This is the largest national legislative action in the window. It rewrites
   reviews under five federal statutes and carries a data-center cost
   provision this paper's readers will care about. Beats the
   Iran war-powers vote, which is a running story, and the Jeffries nomination,
   which went the expected way.
3. **News: Trump, Xi and Putin sign a four-point declaration in Shenzhen (19
   November local time, 18 November Eastern).** This is what changed on a story
   the paper ran on 15 November, when the White House said Trump would discuss
   "the reduction of nuclear weapons". The signed text commits only to a
   "dialogue on reducing nuclear risks". It is not on the commission's list of
   17–19 November running stories. Beats the US–Venezuela military deal and
   Denmark's Greenland vote on importance.
4. **News (international): the ICJ finds that Myanmar committed genocide
   against the Rohingya, 9–6 (19 November).** This is the first time the Court
   has found a state itself committed genocide. In 2007 it found only that
   Serbia failed to prevent and punish Srebrenica. That justifies displacing a
   third national story under the series prompt's rule. Beats the Arizona
   attorney general's criminal probe of OpenAI, which is preliminary and
   unsubstantiated and sits close to the California v. OpenAI running story,
   and the administration's Supreme Court application on Venezuelan TPS.
5. **Technology (non-lab): nginx 1.30.7 and 1.31.9 fix ten CVEs, including two
   HTTP/2 flaws that researchers turned into unauthenticated code execution (18
   November).** nginx sits in front of most of the inference and API stacks the
   readers run. Five teams found the same bugs independently, and Wiz says its
   AI harness found them. The vendor and the researchers disagree on severity,
   and the record carries both ratings. Beats PHP's same-week release (reported
   only, primary not opened) and Intel's unnamed 18A customer.
6. **Technology (AI lab, the edition's only one): Google releases Gemini 4 Neon
   (18 November).** The release has a concrete price, $3 and $15 per million
   tokens, a 1 million-token context, and a cyber-defense claim that bears on
   item 5. It is the only frontier-model item. Beats the shift of AI-agent
   losses onto bank customers, which builds on October changes by JPMorgan and
   Bank of America and has no bank terms text I could open. Beats Meta's Muse
   reading Signal notifications, an accusation resting on one Reuters report of
   internal chats, without the second confirmation an accusation needs.

## Contradictions

- **nginx severity.** nginx.org rates CVE-2026-123007 "major" and CVE-2026-123633
  "medium". Wiz scores both CVSS 4.0 9.2, "Critical". Wiz's table says
  CVE-2026-123007 is "Exploitable in default configurations with HTTP/2
  enabled". Florian Roth, quoted by BleepingComputer, says the flaws need
  non-default configurations. Reconciling them: HTTP/2 is off unless
  configured, so "default" in Wiz's sense means "a default build with HTTP/2
  turned on". Write that HTTP/2 must be enabled. Attribute the code-execution
  claim to Wiz and Xint, and the severity rating to the vendor.
- **Exposure figure.** Censys's 8.8 million comes from version banners and does
  not account for distribution backports. AlmaLinux had already backported the
  fixes, per a reader comment cited by BleepingComputer. It is an upper bound.
- **Shenzhen versus the earlier promise.** The 13 November White House
  announcement (paper's 15 November brief) said Trump would discuss "the
  reduction of nuclear weapons in the world". The signed text speaks only of a
  "dialogue on reducing nuclear risks". This is the change to report, not an
  error in either source.
- **Shenzhen date.** The White House text dates the meeting 19 November, which
  is Shenzhen time. NYT and Reuters items are dated 18 November, US time. Use
  "Thursday in Shenzhen" or give the 19 November local date.
- **ICJ "first".** Several outlets say it is the first time the Court has "held
  a state accountable for genocide". That is loose. In 2007 the Court held Serbia
  responsible for breaching the duty to prevent and punish genocide, though not
  for committing it. The defensible claim: the first time the Court has found
  that a state committed genocide (The Conversation's framing).
- **ICJ vote count.** Reports give 9–6, 15 votes. The case page shows two
  judges ad hoc (Pillay, Kress) alongside the regular bench. I could not open
  the operative paragraphs, so I could not confirm the bench size or how many
  sat. ASIL names President Iwasawa as agreeing on intent but disputing The
  Gambia's standing on compensation, and does not say which side of the 9–6 he
  voted on. Report "nine votes to six" as reported and name no dissenters
  beyond the six on the joint dissenting opinion.
- **Permitting bill vote time.** The Senate XML timestamps roll call 270 at
  "09:30 AM". Roll Call and NBC say it passed Thursday evening or night. The
  roll call for the Iran resolution earlier the same day is stamped 2:01 PM, so
  the 9:30 stamp is probably the session's start, not the vote. Give the date
  and leave out the hour.
- **Permitting tally by party.** CBS says 15 Democrats voted yes. The roll call
  shows 14 Democrats plus independent Angus King (I-ME) yes, and independent
  Bernie Sanders (I-VT) no. Write "15 members of the Democratic caucus" or give
  the roll-call split.
- **House leadership labels in coverage.** Roll Call quotes Speaker Mike
  Johnson. NBC quotes Majority Leader Steve Scalise. CBS calls Jeffries "House
  Minority Leader", which is correct until January, since Democrats take the
  House majority then. None of these conflict, but the writer should not call
  Jeffries "Speaker" or "Majority Leader".
- **Russell's canal.** Scholarpedia says a tow rope connecting mules to the barge
  broke, on the Union Canal near Edinburgh. Russell's own passage says the boat
  was "drawn along a narrow channel by a pair of horses" and "suddenly stopped".
  The passage gives no rope, no mules and no canal name. Follow Russell: a pair
  of horses, a narrow channel, August 1834. Do not name the canal unless another
  primary does.
- **Intel runner-up.** Yahoo Finance says the customer signed for 18A. Other
  reports say Intel's CTO later specified 18A-P. TrendForce reported in August
  2026 that Socionext would use 18A-P. This is one more reason it was not
  chosen.
- **Commission angle.** None of the six picks contradicts the commission. One
  pattern risk: item 5 is a second AI-discovered memory-safety flaw in core
  server software, a day after the 19 November brief led with Java. Wiz's post
  names Java4Shell itself. The writer should not build the headline from item 5
  with a "<count> ... can ..." shape.

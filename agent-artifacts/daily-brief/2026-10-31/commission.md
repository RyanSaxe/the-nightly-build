# Commission: daily-brief/2026-10-31

## The article
The paper's three-minute quick read for Saturday, 31 October 2026. Six items in
the fixed order the template and series prompt set: a Word of the Day, then three
news items, then two technology developments. One strong source per item (the
word item may use two: one for the definition, one for the documented story).
Strict word band 300-700, counting everything but the Sources list.

## Selection rules (the series prompt governs; this records the edition's calls)
- **Word of the Day:** a word with a story that most well-read people have not
  met and would enjoy using. A coinage with a known inventor (the prompt's
  example is *sphexish*) is the target. Not a common word with an ordinary
  etymology, however old. Give part of speech, pronunciation, a one-sentence
  definition, then the documented origin in three or four sentences.
- **Three news items:** chosen for the importance of what happened, not the
  volume of attention. Favor the United States. An international or New-York-area
  story takes a slot only when it outweighs a national one. Report what happened
  in the last two days (29-31 October 2026) or what changed in that window on a
  running story. Two or three sentences each: what happened, who did it or claims
  it, what follows.
- **Two technology developments:** a release, a new capability, a research
  result, or another change worth following, from the last two days where
  possible.

## Boundaries and edition fit
- The feature tonight is about the cost of evaluating AI research agents
  (Ai2/AstaBench); the RL course lesson is about MDP foundations. Keep both
  technology items OFF those two subjects: no AstaBench/Ai2 evaluation item and
  nothing about RL foundations. Pick genuinely different tech developments.
- Prefer consequence over horse-race. A procedural political story earns a slot
  only if what changed actually matters; do not fill a news slot with campaign
  travel or polling.
- Attribute any unverified or contested claim to the source that makes it.

## Recent-record notes (break these habits; the editor compares against them)
- Recent brief titles lean on "Supreme Court ..." leads and on a dated generic
  roundup line ("Six developments for Thursday, October 1"). Do not title with a
  bare-date roundup and do not default to a court lead. Title the brief with the
  single strongest item's real headline (a finding with a verb), per
  spec/headlines.md, unless no one item dominates.
- The dek states the one or two items that make this day's brief unmistakable,
  not a comma-triad of three clauses and not a semicolon reversal.
- Each item headline is its own small headline: subject, verb, the surprise
  first. Build the six headlines differently from one another.

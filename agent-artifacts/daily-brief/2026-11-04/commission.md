## What the edition is

The 3 November 2026 US midterm elections were held yesterday. If results are called by the
Associated Press or another major race-calling desk by research time, the first news item reports
the state of control of the House and the Senate (seats called, seats outstanding, as of a stated
time), attributed to the desk that called them. If control is not called, report exactly what is
called and what is not, with the time. Do not project.

The other two news items are chosen for importance from events of 2-4 November 2026, favoring the
United States. Running stories the brief has covered in the last week (DOJ challenges to California
and Alaska ballots, DHS/DOJ noncitizen lists and Judge Chutkan, US strikes on Iran and Qeshm Island)
take a slot only for a genuinely new development since 3 November, stated as what changed.
A ballot measure, governor's race or state result can be its own item only if it matters nationally.

Technology: two developments from 2-4 November 2026 (release, capability, research result).
The paper's audience builds ML systems; prefer a development they would act on. Do not repeat
the 3 November items (GPT-6 Astra weight editing, XCPE botnet) or 2 November items (Gemma 4 on an
orbital TPU, Microsoft school privacy terms). The feature desk is writing a separate article today;
the orchestrator will tell the researcher its topic so the brief does not duplicate it.

Word of the Day: a word with a documented story, ideally a coinage with a known inventor. Not
used recently: agnotology, nocebo, bafflegab, eggcorn. Check `nb history --series daily-brief
<word>` before choosing. Not a common word with an ordinary etymology.

  "The figures are the authors' own" / "The attribution is Google's alone". Do not reuse
  either tag; vary item openings.

  appeared twice last edition. Use attribution in the body where possible.

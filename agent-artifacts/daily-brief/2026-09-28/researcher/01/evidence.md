The record supports all six commissioned items, with one claim-owning primary and at least one independent secondary source for each. The strongest procedural boundaries are clear: the Missouri action is a stay, not a final merits judgment; Medicaid losses are modeled nationally while early state administration differs; Trump's public rejection did not settle the diplomatic channel; TikTok admitted no liability and the decree's product terms cover young U.S. users while the money goes to Alabama; and LINK, not Swift, reentered on September 25. ## Contradictions

- **Word:** No material contradiction. Merriam-Webster and Collins agree on the modern meaning, pronunciation, Latin *apodicticus*, Greek *apodeiktikos*, and the logical sense. Collins supplies a more explicit proposition-specific definition; it does not justify adding an unsupported etymological stage.
- **Missouri:** Headlines calling the September 25 action a “rejection” can be read as a final merits disposition. The primary is a stay order. It preserves the 2022 map for the ongoing election and criticizes the Eighth Circuit's intervention, but says the underlying federal merits are not entirely clear-cut.
- **Medicaid:** CMS's estimates are not observed coverage losses. CMS projects both substantive noncompliance and procedural disenrollment, while the KFF tracker shows different enforcement dates and a soft launch in Arkansas. Do not convert national modeled losses into a count already removed by early-adopting states.
- **Iran/Hormuz:** Trump publicly claimed total U.S. control and large oil movements. September 28 preliminary Kpler tracking reported a rebound but also a large gap from February regional exports and an AIS blind spot. Iran also said it had not received an official rejection through mediators even after Trump's public rejection. These are differences in measurement and diplomatic channel, not evidence that Trump did not make the public statement.
- **TikTok:** Some secondary summaries say the safety changes are for Alabama teens. The decree defines the covered group for many product terms as “Young U.S. Users,” meaning U.S. users under 18 whom TikTok knows or reasonably believes are under 18. Conversely, the money and released sovereign claims are Alabama-specific. The decree contains no admission that TikTok caused the alleged harms.
- **LINK/Swift:** Katalyst's release says Swift was expected to reenter by year's end; AP reports an early-November estimate. Both are forecasts. They agree that LINK reentered September 25 and Swift remained in orbit. The more precise date should be attributed to AP/NASA reporting and presented as an estimate.

## Limits

- No source supports treating the Supreme Court's September 25 stay as a final judgment on the federal constitutional claim.
- The selected Medicaid record does not establish a complete observed count of people who had lost coverage in each early-adopting state by September 28. The national figures are projections.
- Kpler's underlying September 28 vessel dataset is proprietary and was not directly inspected. Reuters reports the measurements and the AIS limitation.
- The full written Iranian proposal was not public in the checked record. Its terms come from attributed statements by Iranian and U.S. officials, whose descriptions of sequencing differ.
- The TikTok decree establishes obligations, payments, denial of liability, and scope. It does not establish that TikTok's design caused the alleged mental-health outcomes.
- No checked source makes Swift's reentry date certain. Swift remained operational on September 25; early November and year-end are estimates.
- Freshness check through September 28 found no substantive post–September 27 change to the word, Missouri, Medicaid, TikTok, or LINK/Swift items. The Iran item gained the September 28 Kpler/Reuters shipping update and the article must reflect it.

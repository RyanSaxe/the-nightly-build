# Evidence: tech-news/2026-08-12 (02)

This record repairs the sourcing gap that blocked the round-01 draft and reports a
hard finding: the 4-item floor cannot be met honestly for 12 August 2026 under the
per-item rule (one primary plus at least one independent secondary). Two items
clear the bar. The 12 August total solar eclipse and its coronal-science campaigns
carry a NASA primary and an independent AFP/phys.org secondary. Dyna-1, the model
that reads protein millisecond motion from the peaks missing in NMR spectra
(Nature, 10 August), carries a Nature primary and two independent secondaries,
with the caveat that both predate the Nature publication because the result
circulated as a March 2025 preprint. The third in-scope result, the
phosphine-mediated azine C–H coupling with water and ammonia (Nature, 11 August),
has a verified primary but no independent secondary anywhere I could reach: the
only non-Nature page carrying it is an auto-aggregator that republishes the
abstract, not independent reporting. A fourth in-scope, honestly-dated item does
not exist in the 10–12 August window: Nature's main journal published only Dyna-1
(10 Aug) and the azine paper (11 Aug) in that span and nothing on 12 August, and
the science-wire items stamped "11 August" are reposts of older work (a 2023
Materials Today super-steel result, a July 2026 PNAS neuroscience paper). I did
not manufacture an item to reach the count. Recommendation to the orchestrator is
in the closing note. Round-01's verified primaries are preserved; everything added
this round is marked [02].

## Contradictions

- The per-item rule versus what 12 August produced. The commission's floor is four
  items, each with a primary and an independent secondary. Honest verification
  yields two clearing items (eclipse; Dyna-1, the latter on preprint-era
  secondaries), one primary-only item (azine coupling), and no fourth. The gap is
  real, not a search failure: I checked Nature's full 10–12 August research list,
  the AI-release trackers, and the science wires.
- Wire dates versus primary dates. Science-news aggregators repeatedly stamp older
  results with a fresh date. The ScienceDaily "11 August 2026" super-steel item
  traces to a 2023 Materials Today / Science Advances result from HKU; its "11
  August" MIT language-versus-reasoning item is a July 2026 PNAS paper (MIT News
  dated 8 July). Round-01's misdated science stories (centromeres and Raygun,
  both Nature 29 July; the magnetar vacuum-birefringence paper, ~6 August) remain
  in Discarded. Every date in this record is taken from the owning primary.
- Dyna-1's news event versus its independent coverage. The formal news is the 10
  August Nature publication, but the independent secondaries (CBIRT, 5 April 2025;
  Kiin Bio, 6 November 2025) both cover the March 2025 bioRxiv preprint. They are
  genuine independent accounts of the same result, but none reports the Nature
  publication itself. The editor should judge whether preprint-era secondaries
  satisfy the template.
- The eclipse item is an observing campaign, not a same-day laboratory result. Its
  scientific payoff (corona-heating tests, virtual-totality forecast validation)
  comes after the data are analysed. It is the one development genuinely dated 12
  August, so it anchors the edition, but it should not be written as a result.
- The Anthropic watermark story (11 August) is declined per the brief and remains
  out. Its verifiable content is compliance-driven, its text-watermark mechanism
  is undisclosed by Anthropic, and its live news is public reaction, which the
  commission routes to Current Events.

campaign — primary plus independent secondary, but an event, not a result; (2)

phosphine azine C–H coupling — primary only, no independent secondary reachable.
That is two clearing items, one blocked on the secondary requirement, and no
fourth. Options, in order of least distortion: widen the honest window (the
edition frames 10–12 August and says so) and even then the pool tops out at these
three; or run a 3-item edition if the editor accepts Dyna-1's preprint-era
secondaries and finds an independent secondary for the azine paper that I could
not reach; or run a 2-item edition on the two that fully clear. I recommend against
padding with the misdated wire items or with markets/product stories. If the desk
can lift the exclusive boundary on the already-covered threads for a genuine 12
August advance, that would be the honest way to add an item, but I found no such
advance on 12 August in any covered thread.

# Writing-coach brief — tech-news/2026-07-30 (invocation 01)

## Task
Study how excellent technology writers actually write a short, itemized news
brief and turn that into a practical voice guide for tonight's `brief`
article. Output only `voice-guide.md` in this directory.

## Domain and genre
A nightly technology wire brief for a technically literate reader (machine-learning
engineers, well-read). Each item is 2–4 sentences: a judgment about why a
development matters, with the detail the headline dropped, anchored to a primary
source. Register is a serious paper, not a feed. Calm, precise, argued from
first principles (the house baseline is Mitchell Hashimoto's technical writing;
teaching patience of Olah/Weng where a concept must be built).

## What will change sentences here
- Five items across very different subfields (AI cryptanalysis, robot motion
  planning, an AI-flagged astrophysical event, laser control of electrons in a
  semiconductor, twisted-light chirality detection). The writer needs a way to
  open each item on the concrete news, not on scene-setting, and to state one
  caveat per item without hedging.
- Numbers carry most items (2^64→2^38, 60 hours, ~$100k, 200–800×, 2×/30×,
  30,000 light-years, ~1M solar masses). The guide should say how to place a
  number so it lands.
- The banned-terms and prose floor apply: no fluff openers, no manufactured
  punchlines, no "X is not Y; it is Z" reflex, no colon-subtitle headlines, no
  comma-triad deks, at most one em-dash per stretch.

## Constraints to encode as "do not reuse"
Recent tech-news items leaned on Nvidia and on single-company AI-lab stories;
vary openers and item shape, and avoid a headline that performs
comprehensiveness with a triad. No self-reference, no line that hands the point
back to the reader.

## Exemplars to study (at least three)
Respected technical writers the field itself rates: Simon Willison, Dan Luu,
Julia Evans, Mitchell Hashimoto. Read for cadence, evidence handling, stance,
and how they make a number or a caveat land. Extract transferable craft only —
never a persona or a reusable line.

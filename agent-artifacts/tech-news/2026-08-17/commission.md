# Commission: tech-news/2026-08-17

## Assignment

The technology front page for Monday, August 17, 2026. Select the day's four to
six most consequential developments in technology, with artificial intelligence
central to the brief but significance deciding the mix. Each item is a judgment
about what moved in the field and why it matters, with the detail the headline
dropped.

## Selection standard

- Significance decides inclusion. Product promotion, incremental releases, and
  online attention do not qualify on their own.
- Science and health belong when a result changes technical knowledge or practice
  enough to deserve attention here; treat the research itself as the development.
- This brief owns developments in the field. Public-consequence-of-technology
  stories belong to Current Events, which publishes the same day; coordinate so
  the two briefs do not share an item or a lead.
- Cover only what moved on or around this date. Never invent an item to reach the
  count.

## Sourcing (the researcher enforces the floor)

Per item: at least one primary record that owns the development (the original
announcement, paper, or release), and at least one independent account. Prefer a
reputable US newsroom for independent reporting of comparable quality; use the
primary record regardless of country. Brief-template floor: at least 5 sources.
Every recorded URL must resolve to the source's own page. Treat vendor-reported,
independently unverified benchmark numbers as claims, and say who measured them.

## Recent-pattern habits to avoid

The last two tech-news leads were both "a Chinese open model ships with
self-reported, independently unverified benchmarks" (DeepSeek V4 Pro, Qwen).
Select the lead on consequence, and if another such release is genuinely the top
item, vary the framing rather than repeating that headline shape a third time.

## Neighboring articles this run

Current Events publishes the same date and owns public-consequence stories; keep
those out of this brief. Paper of the Day runs a 2022 Chinchilla reconstruction
and Expert Tools covers a Python library; neither is a same-day news item, so do
not duplicate them here.

## Production policy (balanced profile)

- writing-coach: effort low, model capable. researcher: effort high, model capable.
  writer: effort medium, model capable. editor: effort high, model inherit, required.
- No `required` model or effort directive exists for this series. The runtime
  resolves `capable` to its most capable available tier (Opus) and runs the
  required editor on the inherited orchestrator model (Opus 4.8); neither is a
  trade-down. Effort values are recorded as production guidance.

# Commission: tech-news/2026-08-16

## The brief
The technology front page for Sunday, 16 August 2026: four to six items, every
one carrying its primary record and at least one independent account. The
selection bar is the series prompt's: the day's most consequential developments
in technology, with artificial intelligence central but significance deciding
the mix. Product promotion, incremental releases, and online attention do not
qualify on their own. Science and health belong when a result changes technical
knowledge or practice enough to deserve attention here; treat the research
itself as the development.

## What the research must produce
A well-sourced set of the day's genuinely consequential technology, AI, and
research developments, more than six so the writer can select, each with its
primary record and at least one independent account. For each candidate, record
what the result or decision changes in technical knowledge or practice, so the
writer can judge significance over popularity. Flag the one or two most
consequential so the headline can name a single lead rather than a roundup label.
For an AI benchmark or capability claim, note whether the number is a lab
self-report or independently verified; the recent desk has made that distinction
the story more than once.

## Do not repeat
The rolling desk published through 15 August. Recent coverage has centered
DeepSeek's V4 Pro release, Claude Code's auto-mode default, the AI-built Zoom
exploit chain, an eclipse-and-protein-motion science day, an unreleased Claude's
Riemann-zeta bound and an OpenAI math-advances report, pentalayer-graphene
superconductivity, and coding-agent harness exploits. Report only new items or
genuinely new turns, and if a running story advances, report the advance rather
than the recap.

## Habits to break (recent brief desk)
Headlines here have named the single most consequential item and led with the
concrete result; keep that and avoid a triad-of-topics headline. Each item's
"why it matters" states what changed in the technical picture, not that a
company shipped something. Assume the reader has seen the day's headlines.

## Neighbors this run
Current Events is also in production for the same date, and Paper of the Day is
reconstructing a 2015 adversarial-examples paper. Keep the lanes clean:
developments in the technology, AI, and science fields here; public consequences
of policy and law in Current Events; a single paper's reconstruction in Paper of
the Day. If an AI-robustness news item appears, it is news here, not a
reconstruction, and it does not duplicate the Paper of the Day piece.

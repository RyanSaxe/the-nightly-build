# Evidence: tech-news/2026-08-09 (01)

This record supports a Sunday edition of four to six field developments drawn
from the 1-6 August window, because a live search on 8-9 August surfaced no
first-party primary (paper, model card, advisory, filing) dated 7-9 August that
clears the significance bar. The strongest verified developments are OpenAI's
publication of ten machine-checkable Lean proofs generated by an internal model
(1 Aug), Alibaba's Qwen3.8-Max release (3 Aug), a Nature report of
magnetic-field-boosted superconductivity in rhombohedral graphene (print 6 Aug,
online ~1 Jul), the UK AI Security Institute's rogue-agent incident report
(4 Aug), and the CISA/Langflow remote-code-execution disclosure (KEV 4-5 Aug). A
second Nature paper on LLMs predicting social-science experiments is verified but
online since 8 July. Three high-traffic stories of the same days are public-policy
or material-consequence stories that belong to Current Events, flagged below.

The record is thin in two places the editor should weigh. First, the two most
significant AI items are vendor claims about their own systems: OpenAI's proofs
are independently checkable in Lean but not yet refereed, and Qwen3.8-Max's
headline benchmarks are Alibaba-reported with limited outside replication.
Second, the strongest non-AI science item (graphene) reached the web in early
July and only its print appearance falls in this window. Every Nature and OpenAI
page below resolves but returns 403/303 to an automated fetch: gated, not dead.
Figures were verified against each primary's own text where reachable, and
against the closest first-party surface (the OpenAI GitHub repo, the AISI blog,
The Hacker News' quotation of CISA) where the primary blocked the fetch.

## Contradictions

- Graphene, count and field values. The Nature primary's headline is three
  field-enhanced/field-induced superconducting states in the pentalayer robust
  to ~8.5 T; some independent write-ups say "four superconducting states"
  (counting a zero-field state) and give the in-plane bound as ~8.5-9 T. A
  related arXiv preprint (arXiv:2510.10873, "Magnetic Field-Enhanced Graphene
  Superconductivity with Record Pauli-Limit Violation") reports 8.8 T and a
  Pauli-limit violation ratio of at least 80. Treat the Nature paper's own
  numbers (three states, ~8.5 T, "tens of times" the Pauli limit) as
  authoritative for the edition; cite the ratio ≥80 only to the arXiv paper, and
  do not merge the two figures.
- Astra, "solved" vs "made substantial progress." OpenAI's own page says the ten
  results "resolve or make substantial progress on" long-standing problems; much
  coverage compresses this to "solved ten." The precise claim is Lean-verified
  correctness of the stated theorems, not journal peer review. The independent
  check is the Lean certificates plus informal mathematician review (Bloom); no
  refereed confirmation exists yet. This is the item's real limit and belongs in
  the prose if it runs.
- Qwen3.8-Max benchmarks are Alibaba-reported. No independent benchmark house in
  the sources reproduces the GPQA/SWE-bench/Terminal-Bench figures; outlets
  relay them as vendor claims. The open-weights release (which would let anyone
  verify) was promised for "next week," i.e. ~10 Aug, and had not shipped as of
  this record.
- Google DeepMind reorganization. Accounts conflict: one aggregator claims Jeff
  Dean, Sanjay Ghemawat, Quoc Le, and Oriol Vinyals all departed to found
  "Discovery Loop"; a more reputable roundup (citing The Verge) describes Demis
  Hassabis moving to scientific leadership, Koray Kavukcuoglu taking operations,
  and only Jeff Dean departing. Unresolved and not first-party; discarded rather
  than run.

# researcher brief: tech-news/2026-08-09 (01)

Inputs:
- ../../editorial-direction.md — citation standard, series territory, declared reader
- ../../commission.md — the selection rule, the source floor, and the Current Events boundary

Output: evidence.md

Find the most consequential technology, and qualifying science and health,
developments of Sunday, 9 August 2026, and build the record for the four to six
the edition will run. Work from the primary outward:

- Identify candidate developments dated on or immediately before 9 August 2026:
  a released paper or technical report, a model card, an advisory or disclosure,
  a peer-reviewed science or health result that changes practice. For each,
  locate and read the primary that owns it. Treat significance, not novelty
  alone, as the test.
- For each likely item, secure at least one independent account from outside the
  authoring party, and verify every version number, benchmark, parameter count,
  or result against the primary. A vendor post is primary for its own claim and
  never its own confirmation.
- Note where a candidate is really a public-policy or material-consequence story
  that Current Events should own, so the orchestrator can keep the two briefs
  from running the same story.
- Confirm every recorded URL resolves to the source's own page; a paywall or 403
  is gated, not dead.

Bring more verified candidates than the edition needs (six to eight), each with
its primary and independent account, so selection has room. Search for the
result that undercuts a vendor's claim as hard as the claim itself. Source floor
is 5 overall, one primary and at least one independent secondary per item.
Classify each source primary or secondary and say why. In Numbers, record each
figure the edition may print with its exact reading, owner, and scope. In Source
assets, name a figure from a cited paper or report only where it would carry an
item better than prose.

The genre is a research-paper reconstruction for a machine-learning engineer:
rebuild the argument and the math, weigh the evidence as a reviewer, deliver a
verdict. Study writers who explain a deep-learning idea by building the
mechanism and setting the math honestly (Chris Olah, Lilian Weng, Sasha Rush /
The Annotated ... tradition, Gregory Gundersen). The register is calm and
first-principles; equations and figures carry the weight.

Choose exemplars that show the writing, not the subject: if a passage explains
state-space models or attention, quote it only where it shows how a person sets
math and prose together, or pick a different piece, since the writer inherits
whatever is quoted.

This round's focus: how a reconstruction sets an equation and then earns it in
prose, and how a reviewer's verdict reads when it is grounded rather than
announced. Show what deriving-then-weighing looks like versus merely narrating a
paper's figures.

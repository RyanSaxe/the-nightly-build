# researcher brief: paper-of-the-day/deep-q-network (01)

Inputs:
- ../../editorial-direction.md — citation standard, series territory, declared reader
- ../../commission.md — the reconstruction and the after-record the record must support

Output: evidence.md

Questions the record must answer, each traced to the source that owns it:

- The central claim and its exact scope in the 2015 Nature paper: how many games,
  what "human-level" was measured against, and the headline result as the paper
  states it. Read the abstract and the results, not a secondary account.
- The algorithm, from the paper: the Q-learning objective and the
  temporal-difference error the network minimizes, experience replay, and the
  target network, with the equations as the paper writes them and the reason each
  device was introduced (what diverges without it). Record enough for the writer
  to set the math exactly.
- The 2013 precursor ("Playing Atari with Deep Reinforcement Learning"): what it
  established first and what the Nature paper added.
- The figures the claim turns on, named exactly and located in the paper: the
  per-game normalized-score comparison against a human tester, and the embedding
  of the last hidden layer. For each, record where it lives, what it shows, and
  what a crop must retain. These are the article's source assets.
- The after-record, each read to the passage: the reproducibility study on how
  seeds and implementation details move deep-RL results (Henderson et al.), the
  re-examination of the Atari evaluation protocol (Machado et al., sticky actions
  and evaluation), and the statistical re-analysis of benchmark gains (Agarwal et
  al.). Record what each specifically found about the DQN-era results, with the
  figures and the exact directional claims.

Search for what breaks the commission's angle as hard as what supports it: record
what of the 2015 claim the after-record left standing, not only what it undercut.
Source floor is 8; classify each source primary or secondary and say why.
Preserve any full numeric series a chart might use. In Source assets, the two
named figures are the priority; add others only if a cited source contains one
worth the space.

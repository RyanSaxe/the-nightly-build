## Skeptic

The routed claim now reads, in the "An act older than its name" section: "About
seven of them did not survive the crowd waiting below, killed on its spears
rather than by the fall," cited to the new s6.

I opened s6 as the article prints it,
`https://www.newworldencyclopedia.org/entry/Defenestrations_of_Prague`. It
resolves to the source's own page, not an endpoint or search result. It states
firsthand that the First Defenestration "involved the killing of seven members
of the city council" on "July 30, 1419," and gives the mechanism directly: the
councillors were thrown "onto the spears of the armed congregation below." Every
element of the article's sentence is on the page:

- Count: "About seven" tracks the source's "seven members of the city council,"
  and the article correctly leaves it approximate rather than asserting a settled
  toll. The evidence record documents the count variance (seven councillors
  killed per New World Encyclopedia; ten thrown per the independently authored
  Public Domain Review), so the hedge is the honest one.
- Mechanism and direction: "killed on its spears rather than by the fall" matches
  "onto the spears of the armed congregation below." The 1419-killed /
  1618-survived contrast the piece turns on now carries a read source on the
  1419 half, and its direction is right — killed by the crowd, not the drop.

The throwing itself and the Hussite Wars stay on s5 (Britannica story), which
supports exactly those and no deaths; the death clause is split off onto s6,
which owns it. The split is clean, and neither citation is asked to carry what it
does not hold.

Adequacy of s6: the source is a curated secondary encyclopedia adapted from
Wikipedia, and the researcher flagged that it and Wikipedia are one origin, not
two witnesses. Weighed against the claim, it holds. The fact is uncontested
historical background, printed as an attributed approximate count, not a
load-bearing precise figure; the qualitative point (those thrown in 1419 were
killed) is what the contrast needs, and it is corroborated in the record — the
Public Domain Review independently confirms the number thrown, and Prague Morning
loosely corroborates the deaths. Its `data-nb-kind="secondary"` label is
accurate and hides no missing independent source. Adequate for this attributed
claim; no route to the researcher.

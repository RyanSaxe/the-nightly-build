# Commission: word-of-the-day/boondoggle

## Subject

The word *boondoggle*. It has two documented lives worth telling: a coinage in
1920s American scoutcraft for a braided leather lanyard, and the 1935 New Deal
relief-work controversy that carried it into national use as a name for a
wasteful, pointless project paid for with public money. The present sense is the
one to land: a project that costs money and delivers nothing worth the cost,
most often used of government spending.

## Why this word, now

An open beat. The word fits the series benchmark: a documented origin with real
events and named people, a recognizable pattern it names, and use that reaches
far past scoutcraft. The 1935 episode gives the short read its story.

## Angle and boundaries

Tell the documented origin and trace how the meaning moved from a craft object
to a term of contempt for wasteful work. Ground the present sense in real,
dated examples of use. Cite authoritative dictionaries (OED, Merriam-Webster)
for the definition and original sources (the 1935 press record, scouting
history) for quoted usage. Etymology is history, not proof of the present
meaning: the piece has to show the present sense in use, not infer it from the
origin.

# Editorial review: word-of-the-day/hysteresis (editor/01)

## Skeptic

Thesis: Ewing coined "hysteresis" in 1881 for a lag in a twisted, magnetized
wire, not for the famous magnetic B-H loop, which he fixed four years later in
1885; and the durable idea the word carries is path-dependence, a system that
does not return by the way it came, which is exactly what Blanchard and Summers
borrowed in 1986 to describe permanently elevated unemployment.

Load-bearing claims and how they held:

- **Coinage: 1881, page 22, twisted wire, not a magnet.** Held against the
  primary. Evidence quotes Ewing on p. 22 giving "the name Hysterésis" to a
  "polarisation" lag in a twisted magnetized wire, paper received 1881-09-07,
  Proc. R. Soc. vol. 33, pp. 21-23. The desk's signature failure mode
  (collapsing the coinage into the B-H loop) is avoided: the piece keeps the
  1881 torsion context distinct from the 1885 magnetic sense. The block quote is
  Ewing's own diction, deployed once at the coinage moment, and clears the
  quoted-diction license bar.
- **"Tracing curves that never closed" broke.** The evidence says the curves
  "do not coincide and enclose an area" — an enclosed area is a *closed* figure.
  "Never closed" contradicts the primary and mis-teaches a technical reader.
  Fixed directly to "never coincided," which matches the evidence and the
  piece's own later line ("a curve that does not retrace itself").
- **1881-vs-1882 date.** Handled honestly: primary paper carries 1881,
  Merriam-Webster lists 1882 unexplained, OED not cited as adjudicating (it was
  inaccessible per the evidence). Correct.
- **1885 magnet paper, four years later.** Held. Phil. Trans. 1885, vol. 176,
  p. 524, and Ewing's own 1893 footnote citing p. 22 (1881) and p. 524 (1885);
  1885 − 1881 = 4 years, consistent with headline, subhead, and body.
- **Warburg ~1879, independent discovery, Ewing kept the name.** Held against
  the DSB and Bhargava entries; the piece correctly frames it as a
  discovery-priority question distinct from the naming.
- **Greek prior use in rhetoric.** Held (etymonline), kept distinct from the
  scientific coinage.
- **Blanchard & Summers 1986.** Held. Fifteen-year rise, insider/outsider wage
  bargaining, shocks that shift the equilibrium rate rather than fading — all
  match the NBER primary.

## Cut

The piece is already close to the compression the voice guide licenses, so the
cuts were narrow:

- **"and this piece cannot resolve"** — self-reference / self-grading (the piece
  narrating its own limits). The uncertainty is still stated plainly by "a year
  the dictionary does not explain," so the self-referential tail went.
- **", four years apart"** — redundant. "Four years" already appears in the
  headline, the subhead, and the immediately preceding sentence ("four years
  after the one that coined the word"). A fourth instance in adjacent sentences
  was the worst tell in an otherwise tight piece; cut to leave "two separate
  events."

The licensed hinge ("What survived from 1881 was not the delay. It was the
shape...") is an earned contrast, not a strawman: the definition card frames the
word as a lag, and the real cargo the word carries forward is non-reversion, not
delay. It is the one contrast the piece spends, within the ceiling. The Warburg
paragraph earns its place: it carries dated facts and sharpens the naming-vs-
discovery distinction the section turns on. No prompt leakage found against the
writer brief. Furniture is appropriately minimal; correctly, no hysteresis-loop
image was requested — the loop is the 1885 magnetic sense and would blur the
wire-vs-magnet distinction the piece works to draw.

## Reader

What the piece gives beyond its sources: the corrected, connected story the
sources only hold as separate endpoints — that the word was coined for a
non-retracing curve in a twisted wire (not the magnet), and that this exact
"does not retrace its path" shape, not the surface meaning of delay, is what
economists borrowed for permanent unemployment shifts. Each source owns one
endpoint; the piece draws the line between Ewing's non-coinciding wire curves
and Blanchard-Summers's non-reverting unemployment. That synthesis matches the
draft-handoff's stated original work and is genuine. The prose opens on the
physical apparatus, pulls in the coiner's own words once, marks the turn with a
working hinge, and closes on an earned parallel — closer to the Bernstein and
Craddock exemplars than to a median summary. The headline, reread as the largest
claim, is a defended finding with its actor named.

- Cut "and this piece cannot resolve" from the 1881-vs-1882 sentence (self-reference; uncertainty still stated).
- Cut ", four years apart" from the 1893-footnote sentence (redundant fourth use of "four years").

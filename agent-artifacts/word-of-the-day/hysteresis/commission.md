## The word and why it fits

**Hysteresis** (noun): the dependence of a system's state on its history, so its
output lags its input and does not retrace the same path when the input reverses.
It fits the series' benchmark (sphexish): its origin is a memorable scientific
episode — James Alfred Ewing coined it in the 1880s from Greek *hysteresis*, "a
coming short / lagging behind," while studying the magnetization of iron — it
names a recognizable pattern (path dependence), and it remains useful far beyond
magnetism: economists use it for unemployment that stays high after a recession
ends (Blanchard & Summers), and it appears in materials, biology, and control
systems (a thermostat's deadband). This is distinct from the recent
person-eponym and mishearing clusters (quisling, bowdlerize, mondegreen,
shibboleth), giving the desk a science-origin word for a technically literate
reader.

The genre is the paper's smallest read: one word, a documented origin story, and
why it still earns a place in a working vocabulary. Study writers who make an
etymology genuinely entertaining and exact (Merriam-Webster's word-history notes,
a good popular classicist such as Mary Beard on ancient Athens, an OED-grade
word story). The register is precise and light on its feet; the documented
particulars carry the pleasure, not embellishment.

This round's focus: how a short word-history holds a reader with concrete
documented detail while being honest about what is legend, and how it lands the
present-day usefulness without a moral. Show what a specific, well-sourced origin
line reads like versus a generic etymology gloss.

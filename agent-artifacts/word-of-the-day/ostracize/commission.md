# Commission: word-of-the-day/ostracize

## Assignment

A Word of the Day on **ostracize**. Tell the documented story of its origin in
the Athenian institution of ostracism, trace how the meaning developed from a
specific civic procedure to the general sense of deliberate social exclusion, and
ground the present sense in real examples of use. The subject is the word, not
the experience of looking it up.

## The story to document

Ostracism was a formal Athenian procedure: once a year the assembly could vote to
exile a citizen for ten years, with no charge and no trial, by scratching his
name on a potsherd (an ostrakon), and the origin of the word is that shard, not a
person's surname. The documented particulars carry the read: the quorum said to
be required, the ten-year term, the absence of any accusation or loss of
property, and the memorable anecdotes (Plutarch's account of Aristides "the Just"
asked by an illiterate voter to inscribe Aristides' own name; Themistocles).
Actual excavated ostraka survive. Let the researcher confirm which particulars
are securely documented and which are contested, and build the read on the ones
that hold.

## Recent habits to break

Recent words are mostly surname-eponyms with a person's story (boycott, maverick,
defenestration, laconic) plus serendipity's coinage. The headline mold has been a
surprising specific claim about the origin ("A priest, not Parnell, coined
'boycott'"; "Sparta answered Philip's threat with one word"). Keep the
surprising-specific headline but find ostracize's own; do not copy the last
one's construction. This word's origin is an institution, which itself breaks the
recent eponym streak.

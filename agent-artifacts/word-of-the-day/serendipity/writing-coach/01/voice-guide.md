# Voice guide: word-of-the-day/serendipity

Baseline register: Hashimoto's calm, first-principles prose, with
Olah/Weng patience where a concept needs building before its payoff
sentence. Here, that concept is Walpole's exclusion clause — the piece
gives the camel/mule anecdote first so the reader has a concrete case in
mind before the abstract line ("no discovery of a thing you are looking
for...") lands.

## Sentence-level rules for this piece
- Quote Walpole directly rather than paraphrasing his coinage; paraphrase
  only the surrounding narration (the Capello-arms occasion, the letter's
  date and addressee).
- Fleming's plate and the fairy tale's camel are deliberately echoed
  ("training," "reading the clues") rather than named as parallel in an
  abstract sentence — let the two concrete cases do the comparing.
- No vague attribution: both modern citations name their writer, outlet,
  and date, because Merriam-Webster's own citation page supplies that.
- Keep "sagacity" as the one recurring term for the faculty Walpole named;
  do not drift into synonyms ("insight," "acumen") once it is set.

## Specific traps checked before handoff
- Sentence-density: two long sentences (a 54-word quote sentence with a
  colon and two embedded quotations; a 51/45-word pair in an earlier
  draft of the sibling shibboleth piece) were split on the first self-proof
  pass here too — checked directly against `nb check`'s
  `W-SENTENCE-DENSITY` warning rather than by eye alone.
- Closer: rewrote the final paragraph twice to avoid ending on a bare
  two-noun aphorism ("the accident, and the mind ready enough to use it.")
  — the shipped version instead closes on the two concrete callbacks
  (Fleming's plate, the princes' road) so the last sentence still carries
  its own nouns rather than reading as a stock contrast.

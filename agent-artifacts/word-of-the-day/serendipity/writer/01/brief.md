# Writer brief: word-of-the-day/serendipity

Fill the `word` template skeleton with the five-section shape agreed with
the writing-coach pass: definition card, then the 1754 letter (quoted,
with the camel/mule detail and Walpole's own exclusion clause), the word's
slow two-century adoption, Fleming's 1928 plate as the sagacity example,
and a closing section that earns its argument from two dated modern
usage quotations that split along exactly the line Walpole fused.

Constraints carried through from the commission and editorial direction:
- 550-800 words, 2-5 flex sections after the card, per-section citation,
  minimum 4 sources (used 4).
- No "coined for" opener, no generalization-then-negation opener, no
  etymology-as-myth pivot (the fairy tale is the documented story, not a
  misconception), no two-noun parallel-contrast closer, no
  abstract-noun-plus-relative-clause final heading.
- Numbers rule: the 1754 date and the 3 September 1928 date are given
  exactly, not rounded to "mid-18th century" or "the 1920s," per the
  house numbers rule.
- Every citation traces to a passage actually read (see researcher
  evidence.md).

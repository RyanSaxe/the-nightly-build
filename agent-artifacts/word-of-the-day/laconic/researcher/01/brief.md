# researcher brief: word-of-the-day/laconic (01)

Inputs:
- editorial-direction.md — citation standard, series territory, declared reader
- commission.md — the word, its documented origin, and the sources to begin from

Output: researcher/01/evidence.md

Establish the definition from an authoritative dictionary (OED and/or
Merriam-Webster) and the etymology (Greek `Lakonikos`, of Laconia) with the
first English attestation and its date. Read Plutarch's own passage for the "If"
reply to Philip II and record the exact wording and its location (Moralia,
"Sayings of Spartans" or "Sayings of Kings and Commanders"); a repetition in a
secondary source supports that the anecdote is told, not that Plutarch wrote it,
so go to the text. Confirm the historical setting of Philip II's threat against
the Peloponnese from a reliable classical reference. Find one real modern usage
example that shows the present sense. Keep it to what a 550-to-800-word piece can
spend: a precise definition, the documented origin, and the distinction the word
preserves. Confirm every URL resolves. Note honestly anything the record leaves
uncertain (for example the exact dating or attribution of the anecdote).

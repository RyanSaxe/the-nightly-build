## Angle
Zugzwang names a precise, useful idea: a position in which *having to move* is
itself the disadvantage, and any legal move worsens your situation. The word
earns its keep far outside chess because it isolates a structural fact most
words blur: the harm comes from the obligation to act, not from a bad option
existing. The article should teach the reader to see that distinction and to
use the word correctly (a true zugzwang requires that *passing* would be
strictly better, which is exactly what the rules forbid). Trace it from the
chess literature that named it to its adoption in game theory and beyond.

## Contribution (what this piece adds beyond its sources)
A crisp working definition that most casual uses get wrong (zugzwang is not
merely "a hard choice" or "a dilemma"; it is the compulsion to move when
standing pat would be better), anchored in the documented origin and one clean
worked example, plus the transfer to a non-chess setting the reader recognizes.

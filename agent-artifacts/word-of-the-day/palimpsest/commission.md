## Subject
**palimpsest** — a manuscript or surface reused after earlier writing was scraped
or washed away, on which traces of the original remain legible; by extension, any
thing that carries readable traces of what was there before it.

## Why this word, and the angle
It clears the sphexish benchmark differently from the recent eponym-heavy run
(silhouette, bowdlerize, quisling): its origin is a real practice with a memorable
scientific episode attached, and it names a pattern useful far beyond manuscripts.
The documented story: the Greek *palimpsestos* ("scraped again"); the medieval
scribal practice of scraping parchment to reuse it; and the standout episode — the
**Archimedes Palimpsest**, a 10th-century copy of Archimedes' works (including *The
Method* and *Stomachion*) overwritten in the 13th century as a prayer book,
identified by Johan Ludvig Heiberg in 1906, later auctioned (1998) and recovered
at the Walters Art Museum with multispectral and X-ray fluorescence imaging that
read the erased Archimedes back out from under the prayers. The word's present
sense — a surface where the overwritten layer stays legible — is what makes it
useful in geology, urban studies, memory, and computing (overwritten data whose
traces persist). That extension, grounded in real usage, is the article's spine.

## Required contribution
The paper's smallest read. Deliver: a precise, dictionary-anchored definition; the

Etymology is history, not proof of present meaning — show where the modern
extended sense is actually attested. A light computing resonance (overwritten-but-
recoverable) suits this reader as a touch, not an essay.

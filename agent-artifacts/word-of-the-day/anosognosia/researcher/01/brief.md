Research questions (cite authoritative dictionaries + primary/scholarly sources):
- Dictionary definitions of "anosognosia" (Merriam-Webster and/or a reputable
  medical dictionary; OED if it gives a datable first attestation) with the exact
  quoted wording and the Greek roots (a- + nosos + gnosis). Record any
  first-attestation date and its source.
- The origin: Joseph Babinski's 1914 coinage — the exact venue (Société de
  Neurologie de Paris), year, and what he described (patients unaware of their
  hemiplegia). Verify against a scholarly history of the term rather than a
  tertiary blog; a peer-reviewed review of anosognosia's history is ideal.
- Gabriel Anton's earlier work on unawareness of blindness/deafness and the
  "Anton-Babinski syndrome" eponym — enough to place it correctly, with a source.
- The widening of sense into general "unawareness of illness" and its modern
  psychiatric use (impaired insight / lack of awareness of illness as a driver of
  treatment non-adherence). Get one or two real, cited modern usages showing the
  present sense (a clinical source and/or a careful general-audience use).
- One sourced sentence distinguishing anosognosia from denial and from the
  Dunning-Kruger effect (unawareness of a *deficit* vs. psychological denial vs.
  miscalibrated self-assessment). Cite the distinction; do not launch into
  Dunning-Kruger.

min_sources 4. Contradictions: note any genuine dispute (e.g. whether Babinski
"coined" vs "named," the boundary between anosognosia and denial, or contested
first-attestation dates). Resolve every URL to the source's own page.

## Skeptic

organic unawareness of a deficit in which the injury that removes a function

it off from denial and from the Dunning-Kruger effect and is why the word earns
its keep. The load-bearing claims:

1. Babinski coined the term in June 1914, presenting to the Société de
   Neurologie de Paris, for unawareness of paralysis in organic hemiplegia.
2. The Greek roots are a- + nosos + gnosis (a not-knowing of one's disease).
3. Anton described the phenomenon (cortical blindness/deafness) in the 1890s
   without the word; the paired eponym is Anton-Babinski syndrome.
4. Anosognosia is distinct from denial (a psychological defense that registers
   and minimizes) and from Dunning-Kruger (metacognitive miscalibration in the
   healthy).
5. Prevalence: 50-90% (schizophrenia) and ~40% (bipolar) show anosognosia or
   severe lack of insight, and the unawareness is a leading driver of

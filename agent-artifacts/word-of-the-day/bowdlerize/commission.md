# Commission — word-of-the-day/bowdlerize

## Assignment
The word **bowdlerize** (also *bowdlerise*): the paper's smallest read. Give a
precise definition, the documented origin, and the distinction the word
preserves — enough for an entertaining, useful short read, no survey.

## What it must do
- Open with the `rs-word-card` (template/press furniture, see the furniture
  catalogs in `.nb-context/furniture/`): the word, its pronunciation and part of
  speech, and a one-sentence definition that stands on its own.
- Tell the documented origin: **Thomas Bowdler** and, as scholarship now
  establishes, his sister **Henrietta (Harriet) Bowdler**, and *The Family
  Shakespeare* (first edition 1807, anonymous; the expanded 1818 edition under
  Thomas's name) — an edition that removed "whatever is unfit to be read aloud
  in a family." Trace how the surname became a verb and how the sense developed
  to the present: to expurgate a text, removing what is judged improper, while
  presenting the result as the work itself.
- Ground the present sense in **real examples of use** (quoted, cited).
- The distinction to preserve: bowdlerize (sanitize while implying the text is
  intact/faithful) vs. abridge (shorten), censor (suppress by authority), or
  redact (black out). That distinction is the reason the word survives.

## Sources
- min_sources 4. Cite **authoritative dictionaries** (OED and Merriam-Webster)
  for the definition and the etymology, and **original sources** for any quoted
  usage and for the Bowdler/Family Shakespeare history (the edition's own
  preface where reachable; a reputable scholarly account otherwise). Etymology
  is history, not proof of the present meaning — keep the two distinct. Every
  URL must resolve; a paywalled dictionary entry is gated, not dead.

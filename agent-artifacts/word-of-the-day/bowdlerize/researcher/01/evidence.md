# Evidence record — word-of-the-day/bowdlerize

The evidence firmly supports the definition, pronunciation, etymology, and the full
documented origin: Thomas and Harriet Bowdler, *The Family Shakespeare* (1807 anon.,
1818 expanded), and the modern scholarly attribution of the original expurgation to
Harriet. It supports the present sense and its distinction from abridge/censor/redact,
grounded in a real 2023 case (the Puffin re-editing of Roald Dahl) that fits the
precise sense — sanitizing a text while continuing to sell it under the author's name.
It is thin on exact first-attestation: authorities disagree (Merriam-Webster 1826;
Online Etymology Dictionary 1836); this contradiction is recorded and the article
should state the dating as a short range, not a single fixed year.

**Note on the OED.** The commission named the OED as a dictionary to cite. The OED
online entry (`https://www.oed.com/dictionary/bowdlerize_v`) returned HTTP 403 to
every fetch attempt (browser-UA curl, WebFetch) and the Wayback host is blocked in
this environment, so its content could not be read. Per the floor ("cite only what
you have read"), OED is NOT cited. Two authoritative lexical references that WERE read
carry the definition and etymology instead: Merriam-Webster (definition, pronunciation,
first-known-use, word history) and the Online Etymology Dictionary (derivation,
attestation, the 1818 title-page quotation). This meets the commission's intent (cite
authoritative dictionaries for definition/etymology) with sources actually opened.

## Sources

### S-MW — Merriam-Webster, "bowdlerize" (dictionary entry)
- URL: https://www.merriam-webster.com/dictionary/bowdlerize
- Kind: **primary** — a dictionary is the owning authority for the lexical
  definition, pronunciation, part of speech, and first-known-use it publishes.
- Status: 200 (read).
- Establishes firsthand:
  - Part of speech: transitive verb.
  - Pronunciation (MW respelling): `ˈbōd-lə-ˌrīz`, also `ˈbau̇d-`.
  - Definition: "to expurgate (something, such as a book) by omitting or modifying
    parts considered vulgar"; also "to modify by abridging, simplifying, or
    distorting in style or content."
  - First Known Use: **1826**.
  - Word history (verbatim, from the entry's "Did you know?"): "In 1807, a new
    edition of the works of William Shakespeare hit the scene in England. Titled *The
    Family Shakespeare*, the collection of 20 of the Bard's plays in four volumes was
    at first anonymously edited, and promised in its preface to 'remove every thing
    that could give just offence to the religious or virtuous mind.' Though the
    sanitized project later became a public sensation (and a source of literary
    derision) after its expanded, ten-volume second edition was published in 1818 and
    credited solely to physician Thomas Bowdler, the original expurgation was in fact
    the work of his older sister Henrietta Maria 'Harriet' Bowdler, an accomplished
    editor and author. Within a year of the younger Bowdler's death in 1825,
    *bowdlerize* had come to refer to cutting out the dirty bits of other books and
    texts…"

### S-ETY — Online Etymology Dictionary (etymonline), "bowdlerize"
- URL: https://www.etymonline.com/word/bowdlerize
- Kind: **primary** — an etymological reference is the owning authority for the
  derivation and attestation claim it records.
- Status: 200 (read).
- Establishes firsthand:
  - Derivation: from **Thomas Bowdler**, who published an expurgated Shakespeare in

  - Attestation: dates the verb (and *bowdlerized*, *bowdlerizing*, *bowdlerization*)

### S-FS — Wikipedia, "The Family Shakespeare"
- URL: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Family_Shakespeare
- Kind: **secondary** — a reference compilation reporting the print history.
- Status: 200 (read).
- Establishes / corroborates: 1807 first edition, **20 plays in four small duodecimo
  volumes, published anonymously at Bath**, "largely the work of his sister, Harriet";
  1818 second edition **36 plays in ten volumes**, published by Longman, Hurst, Rees,
  Orme, and Brown (London), credited to Thomas Bowdler; title-page promise that
  "nothing is added to the original text, but those words and expressions are omitted
  which cannot with propriety be read [aloud] in a family"; roughly 10% of the text
  excised. Reason Harriet was uncredited: a woman could not then publicly admit she
  understood Shakespeare's "racy verses."

### S-DB — The Daily Beast, Katie Hawkinson, "Roald Dahl's Books Rewritten to Remove Words Like 'Ugly' and 'Fat'" (Feb. 18, 2023)
- URL: https://www.thedailybeast.com/roald-dahls-books-rewritten-to-remove-words-like-ugly-and-fat/
- Kind: **secondary** — reporting; it quotes primary publisher statements.
- Status: 200 (read).
- Establishes the documented modern act fitting the precise sense:
  - In *Charlie and the Chocolate Factory*, Augustus Gloop's description changed from
    "fat" to "enormous"; in *The Twits*, Mrs. Twit went from "ugly and beastly" to
    "beastly"; "female" replaced with "woman."
  - Publisher Puffin and the Roald Dahl Story Company called the changes "small and
    carefully considered."
  - New editions carry a notice: "This book was written many years ago, and so we
    regularly review the language to ensure that it can continue to be enjoyed by all
    today."
  - Crucially: the books **retained Dahl's authorship** — sold as his work, with his
    name on the cover, while the text was altered. This is the exact sense of
    bowdlerize (sanitize while implying the result is still the author's work), as
    opposed to abridging, censoring by authority, or redacting.

## Contradictions
- **First-attestation year.** Merriam-Webster records **1826** (within a year of
  Thomas Bowdler's 1825 death). The Online Etymology Dictionary dates the verb to
  **1836**. Both agree the word postdates Bowdler's death and derives from his name;
  they disagree on the exact year. The article should say the verb entered English
  within roughly a decade of his 1825 death (c. 1826–1836), not fix a single year.
- **1807 publisher location.** Folger and Wikipedia's body text place the 1807 first
  edition at **Bath**; Wikipedia's infobox lists the London publisher J. Hatchard.
  The scholarly body text (Bath first edition, later London re-release under Thomas)
  is the reliable reading and is what the article uses. The 1818 edition is
  unambiguously London (Longman).
- **"read aloud in a family" vs "read in a family."** Etymonline renders the
  title-page phrase with "read aloud"; some renderings drop "aloud." The article
  quotes the phrase and attributes it to etymonline's rendering.
- No source disputes that Harriet Bowdler did the original expurgation; the modern
  attribution (Perrin; Yarn) is uncontested in the read sources.

## Numbers
- 1807 first edition: 20 plays, 4 (duodecimo) volumes, anonymous, Bath. (S-MW, S-FS)
- 1818 second edition: 36 plays, 10 volumes, London (Longman…), credited to Thomas
  Bowdler. (S-FS; S-MW gives "ten-volume … 1818")
- ~10% of the original text excised. (S-FS)
- Thomas Bowdler: 1754–1825. Harriet Bowdler: 1750–1830. (S-FS / S-FOL; MW gives
  "older sister" and Thomas's 1825 death)
- Roald Dahl books: ~250 million copies sold worldwide. (S-CJ)
- First-use dating: 1826 (S-MW) / 1836 (S-ETY).

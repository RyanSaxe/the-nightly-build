Genre: a short, entertaining lexical read (roughly 550-800 words) that tells one
word's documented origin — here an 1812 political episode, a contorted district,
and a newspaper cartoon that coined the term — and shows why the word stays useful.
The register is lively and exact, etymological storytelling anchored in real
scholarship, willing to separate what the record documents from what popular
retellings assert, without inventing or sentimentalizing. Study writers on
language and word origins whom the field rates for wit grounded in evidence. Avoid
pieces about gerrymandering or redistricting as exemplars; quote for the
storytelling voice, not this word's facts, which the article must establish from
its own sources.

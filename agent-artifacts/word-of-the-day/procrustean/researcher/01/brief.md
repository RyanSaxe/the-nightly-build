Research questions the evidence record must answer:

- The dictionary record: what do authoritative dictionaries (Oxford English
  Dictionary, Merriam-Webster, and comparable) give as the definition of
  "procrustean", its part of speech, and its earliest attestation in English?
  Record the first-attestation date and quotation if the dictionary supplies one.
- The myth from primary sources: read the classical accounts (Plutarch, Life of
  Theseus; Pseudo-Apollodorus, Bibliotheca / Epitome; and others such as Diodorus
  Siculus, Bacchylides, Ovid, Pausanias where they add detail). Record what each
  says about the bed, the stretching and cutting, Procrustes' alternate names
  (Damastes, Polypemon), his location on the road to Athens, and Theseus killing
  him. Note where the sources disagree.
- The Greek: what does the name Procrustes mean literally (the "stretcher" /
  "beater"), and from what verb? Record it from a philological source.
- Present usage: find real, quotable modern uses of "procrustean" applied to
  rules, standards, metrics, or systems, from reputable published prose, that
  show the sense of forcing cases to an arbitrary standard. Record two or three
  with their sources.

Classify each source primary or secondary (a dictionary is primary for the
definition and attestation; a classical text is primary for the myth; a modern
sentence is primary for its own usage). Confirm every URL resolves to the
source's own page. Contradictions should record where the classical variants
diverge. Source assets: `None found` unless a primary manuscript image genuinely
teaches. Report in your handoff whether the first-attestation date is firm and
whether the modern-usage examples cleanly show the arbitrary-standard sense.

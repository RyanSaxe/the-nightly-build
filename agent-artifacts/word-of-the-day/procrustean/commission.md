# Commission: word-of-the-day/procrustean (2026-08-10)

## The word and why it earns the slot

Procrustean. The documented origin is a peopled Greek story with events, not a
bare etymology: Procrustes, the bandit on the road between Athens and Eleusis who
forced every traveler onto his iron bed and stretched them or cut them to fit,
until Theseus fit him to his own bed. The word is useful far past mythology: it
names a recognizable, common pattern — forcing disparate things to one arbitrary
standard, and mutilating what does not fit rather than adjusting the standard.
That combination of a memorable origin and a portable modern use is why it earns
the slot.

Tell the documented story of the origin, trace how the myth became an English
adjective, and ground the present sense in real examples of use. Etymology is the
history; it is not proof of the present meaning, so show the modern sense doing
work in a real sentence, not only in the myth.

## The distinction to preserve

Procrustean is not a synonym for strict or uniform. Its edge is the arbitrariness
of the standard and the violence of forcing the case to fit it — the bed's length
was set by Procrustes, and the traveler was altered to it, never the reverse.
Draw that line so the reader knows when the word is the right one and when a
plainer word ("rigid", "standardized") is honest.

## Required contribution

The article gives the reader something the dictionary entry does not: the
documented myth with its real actors and the precise sense of what makes a
standard procrustean rather than merely strict, shown in present use. A definition
plus an etymology is not the contribution.

## Boundaries

- Cite authoritative dictionaries for the definition and classical primary
  sources for the myth and any quoted ancient usage. Distinguish what the
  dictionaries record from what the myth says.
- The myth has variants (the number of beds, the alternate names Damastes and
  Polypemon). Report what the primary sources actually say and note where they
  differ rather than smoothing it into one clean tale.

## Template and furniture

Template: `word`. Keep it the paper's smallest read: room for a precise
definition, the real origin, and the distinction it preserves, without turning
into a survey.

## Recent word-of-the-day habits not to inherit

Recent words (apophenia, bowdlerize, spandrel) headline with a claim that
reframes the word ("Apophenia was a symptom before it was a bias", "Bowdlerize is
named for the wrong Bowdler"). Write a headline that commits to something the
piece establishes about procrustean, without reusing the "was a X before it was a
Y" or "named for the wrong X" mold. Recent pieces close on a "the distinction
worth keeping" / "the line the word holds" section; make the closing section
carry the distinction under its own concrete heading, not that recurring mold.

## Sources

Minimum 4 sources. Authoritative dictionaries (for example the Oxford English
Dictionary and Merriam-Webster) for the definition and first attestation;
classical primary sources for the myth (for example Plutarch's Life of Theseus
and Pseudo-Apollodorus). Quote ancient usage only from a primary edition, and
confirm each URL resolves.

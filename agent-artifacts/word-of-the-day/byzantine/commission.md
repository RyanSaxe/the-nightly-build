# Commission: word-of-the-day/byzantine

## Subject
The word **byzantine** (lowercase-sense): "excessively complicated, intricate,
often devious or underhanded." Trace the documented path from Byzantium /
Byzantion (the Greek colony), through the Eastern Roman Empire that made
"Byzantine" its adjective, to the pejorative modern sense of tangled
complexity and intrigue.

## Why this word
It clears the sphexish bar: a real, documented history with people and events
(Byzas's colony; Constantine's refounding of the city as Constantinople in 330;
the empire's later reputation), a recognizable present pattern (byzantine
rules, byzantine bureaucracy), and usefulness far outside its origin. For this
paper's ML/CS reader it carries a quiet second life in "Byzantine fault" and
the Byzantine generals problem — worth one honest, sourced sentence, not the
whole piece.

## The documented story to tell (researcher verifies exact facts/dates)
- Byzantion/Byzantium founded as a Greek (Megarian) colony, traditionally 7th
  c. BCE, named for its founder Byzas.
- Constantine refounds it as Nova Roma / Constantinople, dedicated 330 CE; the
  Eastern Roman Empire is later called "Byzantine."
- The label "Byzantine Empire" is largely a later coinage (Hieronymus Wolf,
  1557, *Corpus Historiae Byzantinae*); the empire called itself Roman.
- The pejorative "intricate/devious" sense: how Enlightenment historians
  (Montesquieu; Gibbon's *Decline and Fall*) cast Byzantine governance as
  decadent and labyrinthine, and how the lowercase adjective settled into
  English for tangled complexity and intrigue. Get first-attestation dates for
  the figurative English sense from the OED.

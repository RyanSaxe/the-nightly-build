## The word and why it earns space
Everyone knows the object; almost no one knows the word is an eponym. It is
named for **Étienne de Silhouette**, Louis XV's controller-general of finances
in 1759, whose austerity program during the Seven Years' War made his name a
byword for things done cheaply — and cheap shadow-profile portraits (then a
fashionable pastime, one Silhouette himself reportedly practiced) took the
name. The documented origin has events and people (wartime French finance, a
short, unpopular ministry) and an entertaining wrinkle: sources disagree on
exactly WHY his name attached (his stinginess vs. his own hobby), which is the
kind of tension the piece should weigh, not smooth over. The word remains
useful far beyond profile portraits (an outline, a shape against light, "in
silhouette").

## Required contribution
Tell the documented story of Étienne de Silhouette and the 1759 ministry,
trace how "à la silhouette" moved from a jibe about cheapness to the name for
the shadow-profile and then to the general sense of an outline, and ground the
present sense in real cited usage. Cite an authoritative dictionary for the
definition and pronunciation, and original/scholarly sources for the origin
and any quoted usage. Etymology is history, not proof of the present meaning;
make the present meaning stand on real usage. Weigh the competing accounts of
why the name attached.

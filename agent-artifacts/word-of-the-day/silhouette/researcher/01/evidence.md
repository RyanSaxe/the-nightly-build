# Evidence: word-of-the-day/silhouette (01)

The record firmly supports the definition, pronunciation, and eponymous origin
of *silhouette*: it is named for Étienne de Silhouette (1709–1767), Louis XV's
controller-general of finances from 4 March to 21 November 1759, whose wartime
austerity made his name a byword for cheapness. That much is uncontested across
Merriam-Webster, Britannica, the Online Etymology Dictionary, a signed Oxford
University blog, and a heavily-sourced word-history article. Where the record is
thin is precisely the commission's wrinkle: *why* the name attached is not
settled but disputed, with at least three live accounts (mockery of his
economies, his personal profile-cutting hobby, and his brief tenure), and no
contemporary source proves any one of them. The most damaging fact for the
popular "named for his 1759 stinginess" story is a reported 1758 usage of
"portraits à la Silhouëtte" that would predate his ministry entirely; I could
not open that primary myself and record it as reported by a strong secondary.
The present "outline" sense is well grounded in real cited literary usage. Note
one genuine dictionary-versus-dictionary conflict on the first English date
(Merriam-Webster 1783 vs. OED/others 1798). The OED entry itself is gated
behind a subscriber login and could not be read.

## Contradictions

- **Why the name attached (the commission's core wrinkle).** Four accounts
  disagree, and no contemporary source settles it:
  - *Mockery of his economies* — etymonline (primary reading), Wikipedia,
    Britannica ("on the cheap"), and Mercier as reported by wordhistories.
  - *His profile-cutting hobby* — Britannica, Simon Kemp (Oxford), and the 1869
    Journal Officiel account favored by wordhistories.
  - *His brief/ephemeral tenure* — Merriam-Webster's own etymology ("perhaps
    from his ephemeral tenure") and the 1895 Dictionnaire général.
  These are not one story retold; they are different mechanisms. The popular
  single-cause telling ("named because he was stingy") overreaches the record.
- **The pre-ministry dating problem.** wordhistories reports (via Dulaurens,
  1763) that "portraits à la Silhouëtte" were being made in **1758**, before his
  4 March 1759 appointment. If accurate, this undercuts any etymology that roots
  the name in the 1759 austerity program specifically, and pushes toward the
  hobby explanation. LIMITATION: I could not open the Dulaurens primary; this
  rests on one secondary's reading and should be attributed, not asserted.
- **First English attestation date.** Merriam-Webster gives **1783** for the
  noun; the OED (per wordhistories and the OED's own summary) and etymonline
  point to **1798** (The Monthly Review), with etymonline separately noting a
  1792 portrait-sense date. A genuine authority-versus-authority discrepancy;
  present the range, do not pick a single year as settled.
- **Did he invent the technique?** Kemp and wordhistories both say almost
  certainly not — shadow-profile imagery long predates him — against looser
  claims (e.g., the 1869 account) that he originated it.

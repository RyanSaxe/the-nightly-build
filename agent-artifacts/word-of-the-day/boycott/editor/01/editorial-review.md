# Editorial review: word-of-the-day/boycott (editor/01)

## Skeptic

Thesis: the verb "boycott" was coined by a priest, not by Parnell; the word
carries a distinction it has kept, a voluntary collective withdrawal pushed from
below, held apart from the embargo or sanction imposed from above and from a
plain ban.

The claims it stands on, each tested against the source that owns it:

- **A priest, not Parnell, coined the word (headline and coinage section).**
  Parnell's Ennis speech (19 Sep 1880) supplied the tactic and the "moral
  Coventry / leper of old" language; O'Malley coined the word; Redpath first put
  it in print. I opened the Ennis transcript (s3, Freeman's Journal via
  encyclopedia.com): it carries the shunning and "moral Coventry" passages the
  article quotes and, as the evidence warned, uses the word "boycott" only in the
  reference editors' own framing, never in Parnell's reported words. History
  Ireland (s4) confirms O'Malley "is also credited with coining the verb
  'boycott', saying that the people would not be able to remember 'ostracise'."
  Wikipedia (s2) confirms the Redpath/O'Malley exchange, the "How would it do to
  call it to Boycott him?" line, and Redpath's first print use in the Inter-Ocean
  (12 Oct 1880). The article never places the word in Parnell's mouth; it says so
  outright. Claim holds, and the load-bearing correction is clean.

- **The relief expedition cost the public near £10,000 to save a crop worth a few
  hundred pounds.** History Ireland: "it had cost up to £10,000 to save a harvest
  worth at most £350." Wikipedia: ~£10,000 cost, crop ~£500. The article presents
  the crop as a range ("value between £350 and £500"), which is the honest
  reading of two sources that disagree, and marks the £10,000 with "some / near."
  Presented as contested, not settled. Holds.

- **The escort was roughly a thousand.** History Ireland ~900 soldiers; Wikipedia
  19th Royal Hussars plus 1,000+ RIC. The article folds the dispute into "roughly
  a thousand" and "~1,000," asserting no single exact figure. Holds.

- **First Known Use 1880, definition anchored to Merriam-Webster.** I opened MW
  (s1): the verb definition and "First Known Use: 1880" are on the page as the
  card paraphrases them. No oed.com URL is cited anywhere; the login-gated OED is
  correctly kept out. Holds.

- **Present sense is bottom-up and voluntary, distinct from a top-down embargo,
  sanction, or ban.** Britannica (s6) supplies "collective and organized
  ostracism … practices that are regarded as unfair," cited narrowly for the
  definition; the article does not follow Britannica's Parnell-coined-it framing,
  which is the trap the evidence flagged. Friedman 1991 (s7) supplies the
  "urging individual consumers" element, quoted exactly. The bottom-up vs
  top-down line carries no citation and is presented as the writer's synthesis,
  which is correct: no cited source states the contrast side by side. Ethical
  Consumer (s8) is live use of the word; the 2025 "economic blackout" (Time, s9)
  is used to mark the word's boundary, where organizers declined the word, not as
  a use of it. Holds as earned synthesis.

Breaks found and handled:

- **"Within a year it … crossed into other languages" overstated its source.**
  Etymonline (s5) says the word was "quickly adopted by newspapers in languages
  as far off as Japanese (boikotto)." It gives no "within a year." The specific
  span read as borrowed from the voice guide's "crosses into other languages
  within the year," not from the source. Fixed in place: the sentence now reads
  "quickly taken up by newspapers in languages as far off as Japanese, where it
  became boikotto," which is what s5 supports.

- **"served eviction processes on eleven tenants" overstated the act.** The owning
  source has Boycott *issue/obtain* processes against eleven; only three were
  actually served before the server was driven off. Changed to "issued eviction
  processes against eleven tenants," matching the evidence record's own wording
  and keeping the figure the story turns on.

Citation hrefs, all nine opened as printed: s1–s6, s8, s9 resolve and land on the
source that owns the cited claim. s7 (Friedman, Wiley DOI page) returns 403 to
automated fetchers; the DOI resolves to that exact canonical article page, so
this is a publisher bot-block, not a dead link, and it opens for a human reader.
Not routed.

`data-nb-kind` labels checked against the evidence: all eight source kinds match
the record (MW, Friedman, the Ennis transcript, and Ethical Consumer primary;
Wikipedia, History Ireland, Etymonline, Britannica, Time secondary). No label
hides a missing independent source.

## Cut

Ran the slop test sentence by sentence, then walked the edges alone. The piece is
tight and voiced; it survives the test well.

- Negative-parallelism checks: "A priest, not Parnell" (headline) and "It did not
  give it a word" both correct a real, named misconception (the popular and
  Britannica framing that Parnell coined it), so both are earned, not reflex.
- Edges: the closer "A boycott is the thing people have to be talked into joining"
  states the conclusion the argument built (voluntary, from below) and stays. The
  orientation closer "His workers left, the shops would not serve him, and his
  harvest stood uncut" is three reported facts. "The surname moved faster than the
  men who made it" is a voiced transition that carries a true contrast and is
  grounded by the sentence after it; kept.
- Furniture: the coinage note (the O'Malley quote) and the stat strip (the
  £10,000 / £350–£500 / ~1,000 disparity) each carry evidence prose would bury.
  Neither is decorative; both stay.
- Prompt-leakage pass: the commission's "withdrawal of labor, service, and trade"
  and the bottom-up/top-down framing appear as reported fact and earned synthesis,
  not lifted instruction.
- Zero em-dashes, consistent with the handoff.

Two sentences failed a standard on this pass, both for overstating a source
rather than for empty phrasing; both are fixed above and neither was a slop
failure. No repeated slop pattern.

## Reader

What the piece gives beyond its sources: a corrected origin (the priest, not the
statesman, named the thing) welded to a working definition the reader can use,
that a boycott is the refusal people have to be talked into, which is why an
embargo or a state "blackout" is a different animal. No single cited source
carries that whole line; the article builds it. The original-work statement in
the handoff claims exactly this sequence, and it survives. The prose sits closer
to the voice-guide exemplars (Quinion's dry verdicts, the fact carrying the
humor) than to a median summary. The headline as the largest claim is defended
by the body.

## Edits

- Changed "served eviction processes on eleven tenants" to "issued eviction
  processes against eleven tenants," matching the source (processes issued against
  eleven; only three served).
- Replaced "Within a year it was a verb that had crossed into other languages,
  turning up in Japanese as boikotto" with "It was quickly taken up by newspapers
  in languages as far off as Japanese, where it became boikotto," to match what
  Etymonline (s5) actually supports and drop the unsourced one-year span. Net −1
  word (797 to 796; in band).

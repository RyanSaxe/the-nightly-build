# Evidence record: word-of-the-day/boycott (01)

The evidence firmly supports the core story: Charles Cunningham Boycott was Lord
Erne's land agent on the Lough Mask estate in County Mayo; in the autumn of 1880
he served eviction processes on eleven tenants; the Irish National Land League
answered with an organized withdrawal of labor, service, and trade; and an
outside relief expedition of some fifty Ulster laborers, guarded by a large
military and police force, brought in a small harvest at a public cost far
exceeding its worth. The dictionary anchor is solid: Merriam-Webster's own page
gives the definition and dates the First Known Use to 1880, and Britannica and
etymonline corroborate the 1880 origin. The single most important correction to
the popular retelling is well-documented: Parnell's famous Ennis speech (19 Sep
1880) supplied the tactic ("moral Coventry"), not the word. The word itself was
coined days later in a conversation between the American journalist James Redpath
and the priest Fr John O'Malley, and Redpath first carried it into print. Three
areas are genuinely thin or contested and are recorded below: (1) the precise
figures for the expedition's cost and the crop's value differ by source (£10,000
against £350 to £500); (2) "first in print" has several competing claimants
(Redpath's *Inter-Ocean*, the *Times* of London, the OED's own first citation),
and I could not open the OED directly to verify its earliest quotation because
the entry sits behind a subscription login; (3) the coinage date is given as
either 23 or 25 September 1880. For present-sense usage I have one clean academic
definition (Friedman 1991) and one contemporary consumer campaign that uses the
word in its own voice (Ethical Consumer on Amazon). A notable finding for the
writer: the largest US consumer action of 2025, the "Economic Blackout," pointedly
called itself a blackout, not a boycott, in its organizers' own words.

## Contradictions

- Cost of the expedition versus the crop's worth. History Ireland: "up to £10,000
  to save a harvest worth at most £350." Wikipedia: cost estimated at £10,000, crop
  value "approximately £500." The two agree on the ~£10,000 public cost and on the
  order-of-magnitude disparity; they differ on the crop value (£350 vs £500).
  Settle it as a range: a harvest worth on the order of £350-£500 protected at a
  cost near £10,000. Parnell's "one shilling for every turnip" is rhetoric, not an
  accounting figure, and should be presented as his gibe, not as data.

- Size of the guarding force. History Ireland: "about 900 soldiers" over two weeks.
  Wikipedia: "a regiment of the 19th Royal Hussars and more than 1,000 men of the
  Royal Irish Constabulary." The number of harvesters is consistent at fifty
  Ulstermen. Present the escort as roughly 1,000 troops and police; do not assert a
  single exact figure.

- Date of the coinage conversation. Wikipedia (from Redpath's account): 23 September
  1880. Dictionary of Irish Biography / other retellings: 25 September 1880. Both
  place it in late September 1880, days after the 19 September Ennis speech. Say
  "late September 1880" unless one primary account is preferred.

- Who coined versus who first printed the word. History Ireland credits Fr John
  O'Malley with coining the verb; Britannica frames the naming around Parnell; the
  Redpath account (Wikipedia, DIB) has O'Malley supply the word and Redpath carry it
  into print. Best-supported reading: Parnell (19 Sep) supplied the *tactic* and the
  "moral Coventry" language but not the word; O'Malley *coined* the word in late
  September; Redpath *first printed and popularized* it (Inter-Ocean, reported 12
  Oct 1880). This is the central separation of record from popular retelling that
  the commission asks for.

- "First use in print." Competing claims: Redpath in the Chicago Inter-Ocean (~12
  Oct 1880); the Times of London (secondary reports give 20 Nov 1880, "The people
  of New Pallas have resolved to 'boycott' them and refused to supply them with food
  or drink"); and the OED's own first citation, which I could not verify because the
  entry is behind a login. Merriam-Webster's First Known Use of 1880 is the safest
  datable anchor read firsthand. Do not state a single "first newspaper" as fact;
  the documented sequence is coinage in conversation (late Sep 1880), Redpath's US
  print use (Oct 1880), then rapid spread into the British and international press
  (Nov 1880 onward).

- Number of tenants on the estate. Reports vary between roughly 35 and 38 tenants on
  Lord Erne's Lough Mask holding; the figure that matters to the story is stable:
  eleven tenants received eviction processes. Use "eleven," and treat the total
  tenant count as approximate if used at all.

# Commission: word-of-the-day/boycott

(Replaces a mis-slugged word: `bowdlerize` was already published on 2026-07-30, so

## Assignment

The word is boycott: a concerted, voluntary refusal to deal with a person,
business, or nation, as an act of protest or coercion. Tell the documented story
of its origin, trace how the meaning developed from a proper name into a common
verb, and ground the present sense in real use. The origin has the people and
events for an entertaining short read: Captain Charles Cunningham Boycott, the
land agent in County Mayo, and the organized ostracism the Irish Land League
turned on him in the autumn of 1880.

## What the piece must do

- Tell the real, documented origin: Boycott as land agent for Lord Erne during
  the Land War; Charles Stewart Parnell's September 1880 Ennis speech urging that
  those who took an evicted tenant's land be shunned rather than met with
  violence; the coordinated withdrawal of labor, service, and trade that left
  Boycott unable to bring in his harvest; and the outside expedition that
  harvested his crops under armed guard at a cost far exceeding their worth. Note
  how fast the surname became a word in the press and in other languages.
- Trace the development from the eponym to the present sense, and preserve the
  distinction it carries: an organized, voluntary, collective withdrawal of
  dealing (bottom-up pressure), distinct from a state embargo or sanction
  (top-down), a ban, or a blacklist.
- Ground the present sense in real quoted usage. Cite authoritative dictionaries
  (OED, Merriam-Webster) for the definition and first attestation, and original
  or contemporary sources for the story. Etymology is history, not proof of the
  present meaning. Separate the documented record from the popular retelling
  (the exact wording attributed to Parnell; who first put "boycott" in print; the
  real scale and cost of the relief expedition).

# Evidence record (correction addendum) — word-of-the-day/shibboleth, round 02

This round confirms the editor's skeptic finding: **Judges 12:6's "forty-two
thousand ... fell at that time" closes the whole Gilead–Ephraim conflict, not
just the fords/pronunciation test.** Judges 12:4 records a battle — "the
Gileadites defeated Ephraim" — that happens *before* Gilead ever holds the
fords or asks anyone to say "shibboleth." The 42,000 in v.6 is the total
Ephraimite toll of the episode (battle in v.4 plus fords killings in v.5-6),
and the text does not break the 42,000 into a battle count and a fords-test
count. Everything else in 01/evidence.md (definitions, the sh/s mechanism,
the Hebrew-meaning dispute, dates, the PLOS ONE modern use) is unchanged and
still governs; this addendum supersedes 01 only on how the 42,000 may be
glossed.

## Contradictions

None found on the sequence or the wording of v.1-6. JPS and NRSVUE agree on
every point above, including that v.4 reports a completed battle ("defeated")
distinct from and prior to the fords episode. No source in 01 or 02 supplies
a separate, text-given breakdown of battle deaths versus fords deaths within
the 42,000 — the "42,000 died at the fords" framing in the 01 draft's gloss
was an inference beyond what either translation states, and this round finds
no source, in either round, that supports that inference. The correction is
to the 01 evidence record's phrasing, not a disagreement between sources.

## Numbers

- **42,000**: Judges 12:6, JPS ("Forty-two thousand from Ephraim fell at that
  time") and NRSVUE ("Forty-two thousand of the Ephraimites fell at that
  time"), confirmed against the Hebrew אַרְבָּעִ֥ים וּשְׁנַ֖יִם אָֽלֶף. This
  is the **whole-conflict Ephraimite toll** — it follows both the battle of
  v.4 ("the Gileadites defeated Ephraim") and the fords killings of v.5-6 in
  narrative sequence, and its own Hebrew phrasing ("at that time") reads as
  summarizing the full encounter. The text supplies **no separate figure**
  for deaths in the v.4 battle alone or deaths at the fords alone; any split
  between the two is not stated and must not be asserted as if it were.
- No other numbers are affected by this correction; see 01/evidence.md

  points, which this round did not touch.

## Guidance to the writer (recast the 42,000)

The headline, dek, and origin paragraph must stop implying that all 42,000
died because they mispronounced the word at the fords. What the text actually
gives, in order: (1) Ephraim threatens Jephthah over being excluded from the
Ammonite campaign (v.1-3); (2) Gilead's forces **fight and defeat Ephraim in
battle** (v.4 — "the Gileadites defeated Ephraim," a completed military
victory, before anyone reaches a river); (3) Gilead then **seizes the fords**
and uses the shibboleth/sibboleth test to catch Ephraimite fugitives trying to
cross back (v.5-6); (4) the chapter closes with a **summary casualty count for
the whole affair** — "forty-two thousand ... fell at that time" — not a count
confined to the fords.

Safe, source-grounded ways to phrase it:
- "Forty-two thousand Ephraimites died in the conflict that followed —
  including everyone caught by the test at the river crossings" (accurate:
  battle-plus-fords, with the fords test as the closing, most narratively
  vivid part of that toll, not its entirety).
- "The text puts the toll of the whole episode at forty-two thousand; how many
  of those fell in the earlier battle versus at the river fords, the text
  does not say."
- Avoid: "42,000 people died because they couldn't pronounce a word" or
  "42,000 were caught by the test" or any phrasing that makes the fords test
  the sole or even primary named cause of the 42,000 figure. The test's real
  narrative and rhetorical weight is that it is fatal, involuntary, and
  cannot be faked — that point stands regardless of how many of the 42,000 it
  personally accounts for, so the correction costs the piece nothing thematic;
  it only removes an unsupported precision (all 42,000 = fords deaths) the
  text never claims.
- If word count allows one clause of scene-setting before the fords test,
  "Gilead had already beaten Ephraim in battle" (v.4) is a true, one-clause
  way to signal that the 42,000 is not solely a fords number, without forcing
  the piece to dwell on troop movements it doesn't otherwise need.

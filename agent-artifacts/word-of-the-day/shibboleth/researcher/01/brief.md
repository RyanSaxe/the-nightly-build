## What the article claims (verify each; find the primary that owns it)
1. **Definition + part of speech + pronunciation.** Verify against an
   authoritative dictionary (Merriam-Webster; add OED if reachable). Record the
   exact modern definition(s), the IPA pronunciation, and note if MW and OED
   differ. The word carries two live senses (a) a spoken word/pronunciation that
   betrays an outsider, and (b) a broader custom/phrase/belief/slogan marking a
   group. Capture both, with the dictionary as the authority.
2. **The origin: Judges 12:5-6.** Read the passage in a named translation
   (Sefaria and/or Bible Gateway — record which translation). Establish: the
   Gileadites under Jephthah held the Jordan fords against the fleeing
   Ephraimites; they used the word "shibboleth" as a test; Ephraimites could not
   produce the initial "sh" sound and said "sibboleth"; the count of the slain
   the text gives (42,000). Record the literal meaning of the Hebrew word as
   the sources give it (dictionaries/lexicons differ: "ear of grain" vs
   "flood/stream/torrent") and state the disagreement honestly with sources.
3. **Sense development into English.** Find a scholarly/authoritative account of
   how the word entered and generalized in English (OED etymology, a linguistics
   reference, or a scholarly article). When did the figurative in-group-marker
   sense arise? Record what the record actually supports; do not overclaim a
   precise date the sources don't give.
4. **At least one real modern usage.** Find a genuine, resolvable modern use of
   "shibboleth" in its generalized sense in a reputable published text (a named
   essay, news article, or book). Quote the sentence and cite the primary use
   itself, not a listicle about the word. (Note: there is also a well-known
   Shibboleth authentication/single-sign-on software project — mention only if
   you can source it cleanly and only as a modern echo, not the main example.)

# Editorial review — word-of-the-day/shibboleth (editor, round 01)

## Decision

**Required change routed back.** The article is well made and genuinely earns
its place — but its headline and origin paragraph attribute all 42,000 deaths
to the pronunciation test, which the primary source does not support. That is a
false causal claim in the paper's single most visible line, so it blocks
publication. Route the evidence correction first (researcher), then the
headline/origin recast (writer, recorded below). My two direct punctuation
fixes are in; proof remains `BLOCK: 0`.

## Skeptic

Thesis: a shibboleth is an *involuntary, identity-bound* tell — a sound the
speaker's mouth cannot produce, not a secret they failed to learn — and the
same structure runs from the Jordan fords to a modern conflict. Tested 7
claims (headline, dek, the Judges scene, the phonetic mechanism, the
literal-meaning dispute, the sense-development timeline, the PLOS ONE modern
use).

**Broke: the headline's 42,000 causal claim.** "A Mispronounced Consonant Cost
42,000 Ephraimites Their Lives" pins every one of the 42,000 deaths on the
sh/s test. I reopened Judges 12 in the JPS 2023 text (Sefaria, the cited
source 2). Verse 4 — which the evidence record omitted and the article does not
mention — describes a *battle* before the fords: "Jephthah gathered all the
Gileadites and fought Ephraim. The Gileadites defeated Ephraim." The fords test
(v.5-6) catches fugitives from that lost battle. The "forty-two thousand from
Ephraim fell at that time" (v.6) closes the whole episode; it is the total
conflict toll, not the count killed by the pronunciation test. The origin
paragraph inherits the flaw: it opens at the fords, drops the v.4 battle, and
juxtaposes "they killed every man who failed" with the 42,000 line, so the
number reads as the test's body count. The body's caveat ("Judges gives no
independent record to check that count against") only addresses historicity,
not the battle-vs-test attribution.

Root cause is in the evidence record, not just the prose: its **Numbers**
section states "42,000: the count of Ephraimites the text states died at the
Jordan fords." The primary says they "fell at that time," after a battle. That
gloss is what led the writer to frame the number as the fords toll. The
researcher must correct it (add v.4; recast the figure as the whole-conflict
total) so the writer cannot reword around the gap.

Other skeptic checks, all held:
- **Dek** adds rather than restates the headline, and carries no banned mold
  (no semicolon reversal, suspended question, or comma-and triad). One
  precision issue to fix in the recast: "a 2023 study **finds** still sorting
  Ukrainian speakers from Russian ones." The PLOS ONE paper does not find this
  — it cites the Ukraine/Russia observation to its reference [3] as an existing
  fact, and the body correctly says the authors "note" it. "finds" overclaims
  the study; align the dek verb with the body ("notes"/"cites").
- **Pronunciation** `/ˈʃɪbələθ/` is honest IPA and matches MW's respelling
  syllable-for-syllable (ʃɪ-bə-ləθ). Not a mislabeled respelling. Correct.
- **Literal-meaning dispute** gives both senses and all three named positions
  (Klein one root, Speiser two, Guralnik "stream" for Judges) and takes no
  side. Honest, per the evidence.
- **Sense timeline** keeps late-14c word-entry distinct from the 1630s
  figurative sense and MW's 1638; not collapsed into a "coined in YEAR" line.
- **Modern use** (PLOS ONE 2023) is the confirmed-read source; the discarded
  NYT lines and arXiv PDF are correctly absent.
- **`data-nb-kind` audit**: s1 MW (primary/dictionary), s2 Sefaria
  (primary/scripture), s3 Jewish Encyclopedia (secondary), s4 BibleHub BDB
  (primary/lexicon), s5 Balashon (secondary), s6 etymonline (secondary), s7
  Kemmer (secondary), s8 PLOS ONE (primary/the modern-usage text itself). All

- Minor, non-blocking citation nits (do not gate on these): the root-dispute

## Cut

Cut/fixed 2 sentences (punctuation), no whole-sentence deletions warranted —
at 677 words the piece is already lean and every sentence carries fact, claim,
or reasoning. Both fixes replace a semicolon splice with the period the thought
wanted, per the house punctuation floor:
1. Orientation: "…to check that count against**;** it is the number…" →
   "…to check that count against**.** It is the number…"
2. "From Test-Word…": "…spoken at a checkpoint**;** it could be any phrase…" →
   "…spoken at a checkpoint**.** It could be any phrase…"

Worst tell: none of the slop family (no self-grading, stock reveal, signpost,
manufactured punchline, or instruction leakage; the banned term "mechanism" is
absent; the section headings are argument steps, not scaffold). The mildest
tell is "A linguist states **the same idea plainly**" in the belonging section
— a faint redundancy signal over a somewhat duplicative Kemmer restatement —
but it carries a named-authority citation and does real work, so it survives.

## Reader

This gives me the one thing the sources do not assemble themselves: the phoneme
held apart from the word — an involuntary tell bound to a dialect versus a
chosen secret — carried as a single throughline from the ford to the PLOS ONE
Ukraine example and landed cleanly in the close ("A password is a secret… A
shibboleth offers no such choice"). That matches the writer's stated original
work and reads closer to the voice-guide exemplars (scene-first, flat naming of
the tell at the end) than a median summary. The prose earns publication. But
rereading the headline as the largest claim is where it fails: it tells the
reader a mispronounced consonant killed 42,000 people, which the article's own
cited primary contradicts.

attributed to the fords test — add Judges 12:4 (Gilead defeated Ephraim in
battle before the fords) and revise the Numbers gloss from "died at the Jordan
fords" to the whole-conflict toll the text gives ("fell at that time").

**Writer (do next, after the evidence fix):**

  42,000 lives; the number is the total Ephraimite dead in Jephthah's war, of

- Change the **dek** verb: the 2023 study *notes/cites* the Ukraine/Russia
  observation (its ref [3]); it does not *find* it. Align with the body's

- Optional tightening only: cite the root-dispute clause to source 5 and the

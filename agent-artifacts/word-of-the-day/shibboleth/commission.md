# Commission — word-of-the-day/shibboleth

## Assignment
One Word of the Day article on **shibboleth**: an unusual word that stays useful
far outside the setting that produced it. The documented origin is a real,
bloody historical episode with named actors and events, which satisfies the
series' demand for a story, not just an etymology.

## Angle / required contribution
The word names a precise, recurring mechanism: a test of belonging that works
because one group physically or culturally *cannot* fake the marker the other
group holds. The article earns its keep by (1) telling the documented origin
accurately, (2) tracing how the sense generalized from "a spoken password that
exposes an outsider" to today's broader "any custom, phrase, or belief that
sorts insiders from outsiders," and (3) grounding the present sense in at least
one real, cited modern use. The teaching payload: the distinction shibboleth
preserves that near-synonyms (password, test, marker, tell) lose — that the
give-away is involuntary and identity-bound, not a secret one chooses to share.

Do not moralize and do not turn it into a survey of every group-marker in
culture. One origin, one clean line of semantic development, one or two real
modern uses.

## Reader
The paper's declared reader: strong technical/quantitative background, reads
widely, does not need condescension. This is the paper's smallest read; keep it
tight and precise, no padding to fill space.

## Relevant prior coverage (do not repeat)
Published Word of the Day includes: bowdlerize, kayfabe, quisling, limerence,
mondegreen, solastalgia, petrichor, apophenia, umwelt. Shibboleth is distinct in
subject. Note two adjacent risks:
- **quisling** (7/28) already did "a person's name becomes a common noun via a
  wartime betrayal." Shibboleth is not an eponym; keep the distinction clear and
  do not echo that framing.
- **bowdlerize** (7/30) did the "named for the wrong person" reveal. Do not open
  on a namesake-twist or a "coined in YEAR by NAME" sentence.

## Structures NOT to repeat (recent habits)
- Recent openers lead with the coiner/namer as grammatical subject ("Bear and
  Thomas named…", "Glenn Albrecht coined…", "Klaus Conrad coined it…") or a
  reveal headline ("X is named for the wrong Y"). Choose a different opening
  move: open on the mechanism or the documented scene at the Jordan fords, not
  on a coiner. (Shibboleth has no single coiner; its origin is a scriptural
  episode, which naturally avoids this trap — lean into that.)
- Vary heading shapes from the recent library; no "Background / Origins /
  Today" scaffold. Headings name steps of *this* word's argument.

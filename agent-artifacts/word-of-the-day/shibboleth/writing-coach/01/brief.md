## The commission in one line
A ~550-800 word Word of the Day on "shibboleth": tell the documented origin (the
Judges 12 episode at the Jordan fords), trace the sense from spoken password to
today's general in-group marker, ground the present sense in a real modern use.
The keeper distinction: the give-away is involuntary and identity-bound.

## What to study (at least three respected writers)
Short, precise word-history / etymology writing that entertains without
padding. Good wells to study: the best lexicographers and language essayists
(e.g. the register of a strong Merriam-Webster "Word History," an OED-grade
etymology note, a John McWhorter or Kory Stamper-style language essay, an
Economist "Johnson" column). Extract *how* they: open on a scene or a
mechanism rather than a dictionary throat-clear; move from ancient source to
modern use without a "Background/Today" scaffold; handle a foreign-language or
scriptural source honestly; and land an ending that resolves the word's meaning
rather than moralizing. Anchor to how the best language writers write, not the
average etymology blog.

## Output (write only this)
`agent-artifacts/word-of-the-day/shibboleth/writing-coach/01/voice-guide.md`
- The craft moves this piece should use, each tied to a reason grounded in the
  commission and the house floor.
- Concrete guidance on register, sentence rhythm for a very short read, how to
  handle the scriptural/Hebrew material, and how to close.
- Explicit tells to avoid for this genre (coiner-as-subject openers, the
  "named for the wrong person" reveal already used by bowdlerize, scaffold
  headings, manufactured punchlines, the hedged "not X but Y" reflex).
Keep it usable and short. Do not write the article or its sentences.

# Commission: word-of-the-day/spandrel

## The word and why it earns a night

Spandrel is an architectural term that Stephen Jay Gould and Richard Lewontin
carried into evolutionary biology in a 1979 paper, where it came to name a trait
that exists as a structural byproduct of something else rather than as a thing
selection built. The word is worth a night because its origin is a documented
scientific quarrel with a named venue, San Marco in Venice, and because the
sense it now carries lets a reader mark a real distinction: a feature that was
selected for versus one that came along for free and only later found a use. The
concept reaches well past biology into linguistics, evolutionary psychology, and
software design, which is the "useful far beyond its setting" the series wants.

There is a live dispute inside the origin itself. Architects reserve spandrel
for the flat space beside an arch, while the curved triangles under the dome of
San Marco are pendentives, and Gould was taken to task for the substitution. The
article should hold that distinction rather than smooth it over, because the
word's biological usefulness and the architectural objection are the same story
told from two rooms.

## Distinct value and habits not to inherit

This is the only language piece in the edition, so its neighbors are the other
word-of-the-day articles, not today's other desks. Recent word openers lean on a
copular frame that names the distinction in the abstract before any story lands
("Anosognosia marks the difference between not admitting a loss and not
registering it"). Do not open spandrel on that frame. The recent run is also
heavy with eponyms whose story is one person naming a thing after themselves or
being named against their will (silhouette, bowdlerize, quisling, luddite);
spandrel is not an eponym, and the story is a borrowing and a quarrel, so let its
shape differ on its own. The `rs-word-card` and the `Sources` heading are fixed
furniture, not formula.

## Boundaries

One word, this slug. Do not turn a lexical short read into a survey of
adaptationism; the biology is here to explain what the word now marks, not to
adjudicate the whole 1979 critique. Stay within 550 to 800 words.

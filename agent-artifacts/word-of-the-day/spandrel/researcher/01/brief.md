# researcher brief: word-of-the-day/spandrel (01)

Inputs:
- ../../editorial-direction.md — citation standard, series territory, declared reader
- ../../commission.md — the word, its origin, and the dispute the record must settle

Output: evidence.md

Questions the record must answer, each traced to the source that owns it:

- The architectural definition of spandrel, in an authoritative dictionary's own
  words (OED, Merriam-Webster, or a standard architectural glossary). Record the
  exact sense and its wording.
- The 1979 coinage: exact title, authors and their affiliations and fields,
  journal, volume, and year of the Gould and Lewontin paper, and the passage
  where they name the San Marco spandrels and draw the analogy to biological
  byproducts. Read that passage, not a summary of it.
- What the San Marco spandrels physically are, and the objection that the curved
  triangles under the dome are pendentives rather than spandrels. Find a source
  that documents the objection and, if possible, how Gould answered it. Establish
  whether the two terms are genuinely distinct in architecture.
- The present-day biological sense the word now carries (a byproduct trait
  mistaken for or repurposed from an adaptation), from a source that defines it
  as a term, plus at least one real, quoted instance of the word used outside
  biology to confirm the reach the commission claims.

Source floor is 4; meet it with sources that carry the argument, including the
one that raises the pendentive objection. Classify each as primary or secondary
and say why. Note any exact visual only if a cited source contains one worth the
space; a short word read rarely needs an asset, so `None found` is an acceptable
answer here.

# draft handoff: word-of-the-day/spandrel (01)

## Original work

The piece maps the spandrel/exaptation distinction back onto the San Marco
image the coinage rests on: the triangular space is the byproduct (spandrel),
the mosaic later fitted into it is the exapted use, so the reader sees why the
commission's own shorthand ("came along for free and only later found a use")
names an exapted spandrel, not a bare one. The evidence flags the two terms as
distinct; the article is what turns that caution into a worked example.

## Distinctions held

- Spandrel (flat space beside an arch, s1) vs pendentive (curved segment
  carrying a dome, s3), kept apart and made the hinge of the origin story.
- Spandrel (the byproduct) vs exaptation (the later co-option, s5), named as
  separate terms; the "found a use" phrasing is attributed to the shorthand it
  corrects, not smoothed in.
- Gould's 1997 concession is the spine: he grants the structures are pendentives
  and answers by broadening the word (quoted in the note), not by denying the
  point.

The genre is a very short lexical read, 550 to 800 words, that tells a
documented origin story and lands a present-day use. Study writers who make word
histories and small science-history episodes a pleasure to read at length and
still cite hard: lexicographers and etymologists writing for a general reader,
and essayists who narrate a scientific quarrel without flattening either side.
The article carries an architectural term crossing into evolutionary biology and
a real terminological objection, so the writer needs models for handling a term
of art precisely while keeping the sentences quick.

This subject has its own literature and famous stylists (Gould above all). Do
not quote Gould or writing about spandrels for voice: his phrasing and framings
would arrive in the guide as material the writer then inherits. Draw voice from
pieces on other words or other episodes.

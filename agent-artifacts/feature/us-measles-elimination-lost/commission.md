## Topic and reason to read it now

On 14 November 2026, NPR, KFF Health News and the Wall Street Journal reported that the Pan American
Health Organization concluded the United States has lost the measles elimination status it held
since 2000, because transmission continued for more than 12 months. The CDC counts about 5,400
confirmed cases in 2026, against 2,289 in 2025. Reports say PAHO relied on a CDC analysis and an
independent expert analysis. Whether PAHO has issued a formal public statement is unsettled at
commissioning time; establish it and report the status exactly.

## The one idea

Elimination is a statement about one chain: whether any single transmission chain, traced by
epidemiology and by genotype and sequence data, has run for 12 months or longer. The piece shows
the reader that chain: where and when it started, how it moved between outbreaks, and what evidence
links them across the 12-month mark. Case totals are context, not the finding.

## Original contribution required

Lay out on one timeline the dated outbreaks that the CDC, state health departments and the
analyses PAHO used link into the continuous chain, with the 12-month threshold marked, and show
what evidence connects them (shared genotype D8 lineage or sequence identifiers, epidemiologic
links). If the sources do not link them, the piece says which links are established and which are
inferred, and by whom.

## Decisions recorded during production

- Date correction: the Wall Street Journal first reported the decision on Friday 13 November, not
  the 14th. Use the evidence record's dates.
- Status: PAHO has not announced the decision; KFF reports an announcement planned for 17 November.
  The piece says the loss is reported, from named outlets citing unnamed sources, and not yet
  announced by PAHO. The headline does not state as settled fact what PAHO has not announced.
- The chain: frame it as the Utah outbreak (first case reported 19 June 2025, past one year in June
  2026), which is the segment the evidence establishes as crossing 12 months. Texas (January 2025)
  appears as a disputed antecedent: show the genomic case for the link and the CDC, HHS and Texas
  DSHS statements against it, each attributed. This replaces the commission's "where it started"
  framing; the original contribution becomes the timeline that separates the established segment
  from the inferred link, with the 12-month mark on Utah's segment.
- South Carolina appears as a large outbreak that did not carry the chain (distinct sublineage,
  under 12 months).
- The independent analysis: do not name Brewer's committee as PAHO's independent analysis; the
  record does not establish it. Brewer may be cited for what he wrote in STAT.
- MMR coverage: use only the verified endpoints, or omit the trend visual.

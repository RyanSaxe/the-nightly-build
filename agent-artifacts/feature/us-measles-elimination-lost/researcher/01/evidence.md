# Evidence: feature/us-measles-elimination-lost (researcher 01)

Researched 15 November 2026. PAHO has not published a decision. Its English news
listing on 15 November shows no measles item after 14 October, and its measles
topic page reports no outcome. The decision is known from reporting only. The
Wall Street Journal broke it on Friday 13 November, and Reuters and CNN relayed
it, CNN adding its own source close to the process. On 14 November KFF Health
News, republished by NPR, cited researchers with direct knowledge of the
deliberations and said PAHO had planned to announce on 17 November. PAHO's own
5 November situation report says PAHO is coordinating with Bolivia, Mexico and
the United States on how to communicate the Commission's conclusions. Under
PAHO's process the Commission recommends and the PAHO Director classifies, so a
formal classification by Director Jarbas Barbosa has not been documented
publicly.

On the chain, the evidence establishes less than the commission assumes. The
chain that reporting and the US committee chair say crossed 12 months is Utah's.
Utah's first 2025 case was reported on 19 June 2025, and the outbreak passed the
one-year mark in June 2026. That chain is established by Utah's sequencing
preprint, as CNN relays it, and by the committee chair's own published account.
The link from the West Texas outbreak (rash onset 20 January 2025) to Utah is
inferred from genomics. ProPublica finds 12 differences in about 16,000 bases
between the first Texas genome and a May 2026 Utah genome. It also finds five
shared mutations that appear in Iowa, North Dakota, Minnesota and Alaska in
May–June 2025, which places the virus in other states during the gap. CDC
leadership disputed that link in November and December 2025, and HHS said in
January 2026 that it had no evidence of a single year-long chain. The CDC
spokesperson says sequencing alone cannot show continuity, and the Texas
health department's peer-reviewed paper finds Utah and Arizona genomes
"related" but with heterogeneous placement. South Carolina's 997-case outbreak
is a distinct sublineage. It ran from October 2025 to 15 March 2026, under 12
months, so it does not carry the chain. The CDC report PAHO used is
confidential. A Reuters source says it found 12 months of continuous
transmission, but which chain it traced is not public. The "independent expert
analysis" is very probably the US National Sustainability Committee chaired by
Noel Brewer, though no source names it in those words.

## Contradictions

- **Date of the news.** The commission says NPR, KFF and the WSJ reported on 14
  November. The WSJ and CNN reports came on Friday 13 November (Reuters via
  Korea Times; CNN copy dated 13 Nov). KFF/NPR followed on 14 November.
- **Was there a PAHO statement?** No. The PAHO news index and measles page
  carry nothing after 14 October. KFF says an announcement was planned for 17
  November. PAHO's 5 November sitrep shows communications on RVC conclusions
  being coordinated with the US. Report the decision as reported by the WSJ,
  CNN and KFF and not yet announced by PAHO.
- **Which chain crossed 12 months.** The commission frames one chain starting in
  Texas. The chair of the US committee (STAT, Aug 2026), KFF (Nov 2026) and CNN
  put the 12-month crossing on Utah's outbreak (June 2025 to June 2026). Under
  PAHO's analysis window, which starts 20 January 2025, a Texas-to-Utah chain
  would have crossed on 20 January 2026. HHS said in January 2026 there was no
  evidence of that.
- **Texas–Utah link.** In favour: CDC officials on a recorded call about 17 Nov
  2025 (NYT via CIDRAP) linked the Texas strain to Utah–Arizona. ProPublica
  found 12 differences in about 16,000 bases plus five shared mutations
  carried through four other states. CORI judges the genomic picture hard to
  reconcile with maintained elimination. Against: acting CDC director O'Neill
  (about 19 Nov 2025) said on preliminary analysis that Utah and Arizona were
  not directly linked. Reuters (6 Jan 2026) reported that early CDC findings
  suggested the outbreaks were unrelated. Utah's Oakeson saw no direct link,
  and Utah's preprint finds multiple introductions. The Texas EID paper finds
  "heterogeneous phylogenetic placement" and cannot infer direction. The CDC
  spokesperson says sequencing alone cannot show continuity. The lineage
  (D8, DSId 9171) also circulates in Canada and Mexico, and there are no public
  whole genomes from Mexico or Ontario, so reintroduction from abroad cannot be
  excluded from public data. ProPublica's finding that no Canadian genome falls
  in the main Utah branch weighs against a Canadian source for that branch.
- **Texas–South Carolina.** O'Neill (5 Dec 2025) said there was no
  epidemiological link. The EID paper puts South Carolina in a distinct
  sublineage. AP (Jan 2026) says "the same strain" was found in South Carolina,
  which is true at the DSId level and does not mean one chain.
- **US case count.** CDC has 5,412 (to 12 Nov). PAHO's sitrep has 4,852 for EW
  1–43 (to 31 Oct). The cutoffs differ. Use CDC for the US figure.
- **Annual counts revised.** The CDC 2024 page gave 2023 = 58 (preliminary) and
  the current page gives 59. A 2019 CDC slide deck in circulation gives 2009 =
  71, 2013 = 189, 2015 = 188. The CDC data table gives 72, 187, 191. Use the
  CDC table.
- **Utah's first case.** Utah DHHS says the first 2025 case (Utah County, 19 June)
  had no out-of-state exposure. ProPublica says Utah's first patient could not be
  linked to a known exposure. These agree on the facts. The DHHS inference of
  in-state transmission in June 2025 is itself evidence that measles was already
  circulating unseen in the US.

## Limits

- PAHO's decision text, the Director's formal classification, and any wording
  of the finding are not public. Every account of the decision is sourced to
  unnamed people.
- The CDC's report to PAHO is confidential. Which chain the CDC traced, and
  whether it included Texas, is not established.
- The independent analysis is not named by KFF. That it is the National
  Sustainability Committee's assessment is an inference from Reuters/WSJ (Brewer
  presented "his group's assessment") and Brewer's STAT piece. No source states
  the identity outright.
- The Utah preprint full text could not be read (medRxiv returned HTTP 429 on
  every attempt). The >95%, >600 cases and June-to-June figures rest on CNN's
  reading. The number of introductions, cluster dates, and whether the 19 June
  Utah County case belongs to the dominant clade are unverified.
- The EID paper's Texas-to-Utah/Arizona SNP distances are in Appendix Table 5,
  which was not read.
- No primary document establishes a Texas-to-Utah epidemiologic link. The link
  is a genomic inference by ProPublica, disputed by CDC leadership's
  preliminary statements, and unresolved in the Texas paper.
- No CDC primary states the date of the last Texas case. "About 1 July" is
  secondary.
- The WSJ article itself was not opened. Its content is known through Reuters.
- The original Reuters and CNN URLs were not resolved. Republished copies were read.
- Kindergarten MMR coverage for 2020–21 through 2022–23 is not verified against
  CDC. Only the endpoints and 2023–24 to 2025–26 are.
- Mexico's November 2026 classification is not reported anywhere found.

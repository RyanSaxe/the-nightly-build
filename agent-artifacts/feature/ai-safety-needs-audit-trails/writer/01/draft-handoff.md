# Draft handoff

## Thesis

Cross-organization incident reporting becomes useful for comparison only when
each record exposes comparable facts, evidence, and follow-up. The current
public proposals and lab disclosures provide useful pieces of that record, but
they do not establish a shared trigger, severity rule, provenance standard,
independent verification process, or enforcement mechanism.

## Original work

This article derives a minimum seven-field incident record from the fields
already visible across the public proposals and reports, then adds independent
confirmation as an explicit analytical requirement. The schema is labeled on
the page as this article's proposal, not as an adopted standard.

## Section outline

1. **A notification proposal is not yet a reporting system** — Introduces the
   Associated Press account of Scott Bessent's U.S. proposal, defines incident
   reporting and recursive self-improvement, and separates disclosure,
   verification, and enforcement.
2. **Bessent's mechanism has no public trigger** — Tests the reported proposal
   against trigger, recipient, severity/deadline, and verification questions
   without expanding it into a bilateral agreement.
3. **Lab reports expose behavior without settling prevalence** — Gives the
   strongest operational case for voluntary reporting, compares OpenAI's
   framework with Anthropic's threat and evaluation disclosures, and carries
   each company's limits on selection, completeness, and verification.
4. **Seven fields turn a disclosure into a record** — Presents the article's
   proposed schema in a compact table and explains why independent confirmation
   and versioned follow-up are needed.
5. **The mechanism works only when disclosure, verification, and enforcement
   stay separate** — Applies the record to a possible international channel and
   ends with the checkable outside-reader criterion.

## Source use

- **Associated Press, s1:** Reports Bessent's September 20 proposal and its
  stated transparency goal. It supports the article's only direct quotation,
  “notification mechanism,” and does not support a claim that a bilateral
  system exists.
- **OpenAI policy statement, s2:** Supports the distinction between voluntary
  lab standards and mandatory safeguards, plus common measurements,
  independent assessment, and incident-reporting rules.
- **Anthropic Institute, s3:** Defines recursive self-improvement and preserves
  Anthropic's qualification that it is not happening today and is not
  inevitable.
- **OpenAI model-misalignment framework, s4:** Supplies the dated three-track
  process, full-report fields, privacy limitation, work-in-progress status, and
  warning that six initial reports are not prevalence evidence.
- **OECD, s5:** Supplies the broader 29-criterion, eight-dimension proposal and
  its non-adopted status. The article uses it as a comparison source, not as
  proof of interoperability.
- **Reuters, s6:** Supplies independent reporting on OpenAI's call for common
  measurements and incident-reporting protocols and the surrounding U.S.
  policy tension.
- **Anthropic threat report, s7:** Supplies the December 2025–August 2026
  reporting window, seven harm areas, model families, selected-case limitation,
  and the qualified meaning of disrupted.
- **Anthropic evaluation review, s8:** Supplies the 141,006-run review, three
  incidents, notification sequence, and the distinction between a useful
  first-party audit trail and independent confirmation.

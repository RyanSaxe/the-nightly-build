# Commission

## Assignment

- Produce one Feature article for the open Feature section, using the slug `ai-safety-needs-audit-trails`.
- Explain the reporting and institutional problem that appears when frontier-AI companies, governments, and outside observers need to compare dangerous model behavior.
- Derive the minimum properties of a useful cross-organization incident record from current public proposals and reports, then show the limits of the current evidence.
- Keep this distinct from the Daily Brief's individual news items and from the Technical article's SwiftUI API walkthrough.

## Editorial thesis and structure

- Use a concrete claim about AI safety moving from promises toward incident records and audit trails.
- Orientation: connect the U.S. proposal for an AI incident notification mechanism with OpenAI's call for standards and Anthropic's published misuse report. Define incident reporting and recursive self-improvement on first use.
- Section 1: separate Bessent's reported proposal from an operating protocol; identify the missing trigger, recipient, severity threshold, and verification process.
- Section 2: compare OpenAI's policy commitments with Anthropic's dated, categorized account of disrupted misuse. State what self-reporting can reveal and what it cannot establish.
- Section 3: propose, as this article's analysis, a compact record with model/version, deployment context, triggering behavior, observable impact, containment action, independent confirmation, and follow-up. Mark it as analysis, not an existing standard.
- Section 4: explain why comparable fields and common measurements matter, and end on whether an outside reader can reconstruct one incident and compare it with another.

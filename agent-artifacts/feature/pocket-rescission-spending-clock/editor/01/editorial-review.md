# Editorial review: feature/pocket-rescission-spending-clock (editor/01)

## Correct
The article's thesis is that the White House's $809,501,022 proposal, Congress's receipt-dependent 45-day process and the September 30 availability dates are separate records with different legal effects. The article makes four supporting claims. First, the 11 proposal sheets sum to $809,501,022, while the rounded public bullets and the message's cover give $810 million. Recalculation produced the same total and a $190,498,978 gap from $1 billion. Second, the cited appropriations laws support September 30, 2026 as the end of new-obligation availability for the 11 targeted TAFS, without extending that conclusion to every balance under the same account names. Third, the ICA and the House and Senate schedules support no more than three potentially countable session days before expiration, while the missing receipt records prevent a verified day 1. Fourth, 31 U.S.C. §§1502 and 1552 support the distinction between expiration and cancellation, and the GAO materials, government filing and Supreme Court order support the stated legal disagreement without turning the 2025 stay into a merits holding.

The headline called the package a rescission. I changed it to a rescission proposal because the document proposes cancellation and does not enact it. The closing said the executive acted after submitting a proposal and that the public record supplied three countable days. I replaced submission with the established act of posting, described the days as potentially countable and tied the count to the still-missing receipt dates. I narrowed the judicial-status language to what the cited Supreme Court order establishes: that the 2025 order did not decide the merits. The printed source links land on the intended White House, statutory, appropriations, schedule, GAO, congressional and judicial records. The source-kind labels correctly distinguish government records from the CRS mirror.

## Reads well
The draft states its argument without briefing-dependent shorthand and does not reuse either recent Feature progression recorded in the commission. I removed no paragraph or section: each advances the sequence from amount and account identity to the two clocks, legal effect and competing interpretations. I clarified the TAFS paragraph by replacing an ambiguous pronoun with “Each symbol's source-year pair.” The account reconciliation, calendar and claim ledger are faster to read as tables than as prose.

Clarified that each TAFS source-year pair controls the identified balance.
Replaced an unsupported implication of congressional submission with the documented public posting.
Described the three pre-expiration session days as potentially countable pending receipt evidence.
Narrowed the judicial-status claim to the Supreme Court order's express limit.

approve, because the edited article is accurate, readable and complete, with no required changes remaining.

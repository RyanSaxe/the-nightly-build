# Voice guide

## How this piece should sound

Write as an investigator who knows that a number acquires meaning from the record that produced it. Tom Bergin's methodology makes estimates inspectable by naming the source, the formula, the assumption and the outside check in turn. That is the right register for the roughly $810 million itemization and the White House's “nearly $1 billion” description: calm enough to leave both figures on the page, exact enough that the reader can see why they are not interchangeable.

Treat each document as a document, not as a stand-in for the whole government's position. Marilyn W. Thompson and Alex Mierjeski distinguish the disclosure that was filed from the separate posting that did not occur, then state what the clerk could not account for. The same discipline can keep the announcement, any special message, the enacted availability language and later legal characterizations in separate lanes. If the special message remains unavailable, the absence is a reported limit, not an invitation to supply likely account numbers or dates.

Let the clocks emerge from dated acts and statutory consequences. A short table may help when it shows what happened on September 25, what could happen by September 30 and what the 45-day continuous-session process would require. It should not imply that the fiscal-year boundary has the same legal effect for every account. Explain “rescission,” “obligation,” “expired balance” and “availability” where they first do work; after that, use those terms consistently rather than reaching for looser synonyms.

Keep the strongest claims proportionate to what the records establish. Stephen Engelberg is useful here because he allows an arresting number to remain provisional until the system around it is understood, and because he is willing to discard a premise when a later record defeats it. The administration's theory and the congressional or GAO objection should each be stated in terms their proponents would recognize. Court action on standing, reviewability or a stay should remain just that, not become a merits holding through compression.

A person should still be audible on the page. Engelberg's blunt corrections, Thompson and Mierjeski's dry placement of the absent disclosure, and Bergin's plain account of conservative assumptions provide enough human judgment without jokes outrunning the evidence. The article can say that two totals do not reconcile, that a document could not be found, or that a ruling settled less than partisans claim. It need not perform certainty where the ledger has an empty cell.

## Stephen Engelberg, “How Investigative Journalists Actually Find Fraud, Waste and Abuse”

Source: https://www.propublica.org/article/doge-government-waste-fraud-approach

> “The problem is finding it.”

The six-word sentence narrows a broad political phrase to the actual reporting problem. Engelberg's presence is clear in the impatience with an easy label, but the sentence does not ask the reader to admire him.

> “Things don’t always go as smoothly.”

Checked: https://www.propublica.org/article/doge-government-waste-fraud-approach, retrieved 2026-09-27

This plain reset arrives after a successful records investigation and makes room for numbers that turn out to have ordinary explanations. The understatement shows an editor accustomed to abandoning a promising story when the evidence changes.

## Marilyn W. Thompson and Alex Mierjeski, “Judge Aileen Cannon Failed to Disclose a Right-Wing Junket”

Source: https://www.propublica.org/article/judge-aileen-cannon-trump-documents-case-travel-disclosures

> “It’s not the first time she has failed to fully comply with the rule.”

Checked: https://www.propublica.org/article/judge-aileen-cannon-trump-documents-case-travel-disclosures, retrieved 2026-09-27

The sentence states the pattern and its limit: the issue is full compliance with a particular rule, not a general verdict on the judge. The writers' judgment is firm, while “fully” preserves the distinction between incomplete compliance and no compliance.

> “The clerk said she had no information about the May 2023 banquet.”

Checked: https://www.propublica.org/article/judge-aileen-cannon-trump-documents-case-travel-disclosures, retrieved 2026-09-27

The missing information is reported in one unembellished sentence immediately after the clerk's explanation of other filings. The writers do not convert silence about one event into proof about what happened; they leave the record's edge visible.

## Tom Bergin, “How Reuters tallied the Trump Organization’s crypto income”

Source: https://www.reuters.com/investigations/how-reuters-tallied-trump-organizations-crypto-income-2025-10-28/

> “Where the family’s share of revenue from certain ventures could not be determined, Reuters made estimates that were vetted by four finance professors, two certified public accountants and an accounting professor, all of whom have taught courses or advised clients on crypto. They all agreed that Reuters' approach was sound.”

Checked: https://www.reuters.com/investigations/how-reuters-tallied-trump-organizations-crypto-income-2025-10-28/, retrieved 2026-09-27

Bergin identifies the missing fact before describing the substitute for it, then names the qualifications of the people who tested the method. The voice is confident because the procedure is exposed, not because uncertainty has been written out.

> “To reduce the risk of overstating the president’s take, Reuters assumed for its analysis that the Trumps received just a 50% share of earnings from the meme coin. Five academics who reviewed Reuters’ findings agreed that 50% is a reasonable estimate of the Trumps’ share. Two others diverged.”

Checked: https://www.reuters.com/investigations/how-reuters-tallied-trump-organizations-crypto-income-2025-10-28/, retrieved 2026-09-27

The passage gives the reason for the assumption, the assumption itself and the review result in that order. The final three words matter: Bergin records disagreement instead of hiding it behind a consensus label, which makes the accounting more credible and the prose more direct.

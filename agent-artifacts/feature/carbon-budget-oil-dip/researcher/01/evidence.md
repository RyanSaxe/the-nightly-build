# Evidence: feature/carbon-budget-oil-dip (researcher 01)

The preprint confirms every scout figure: fossil CO2 −0.40% in 2026 (range −1.5% to +0.6%) to 38.2 GtCO2, oil −1.9%, coal +0.6%, India +4.4%. It also confirms that the projected change sits inside its own range, so the sign is not established. That range is a 68% interval (about ±1σ), not a 95% one (SI S.1.5). One part of the angle fails. 2026 is **not** the first projected decline outside the pandemic. The budget's own Table S6 lists projected declines for 2015 (−0.6%, range −1.6% to +0.5%) and 2016 (−0.2%). The 2015 projection was followed by a first realized estimate of +0.06%, so the sign came out wrong. The current dataset also shows small realized declines in 2015 and 2016, and in 1974–75, 1980–82, 1992, 1998 and 2009. Replace the "first" framing with this: the 2026 projected fall is about the size of the 2015 projection, and the 2015 call did not hold on first assessment. The decomposition holds and is sharper than the scout gave it. The oil fall (−243 MtCO2) is larger than the whole net fossil fall (−158 MtCO2). Coal (+90 MtCO2) and India (about +141 MtCO2) push the other way. Oil is the only fuel whose range excludes zero, and India is the only country or region whose range excludes zero. Land-use change rises by 0.73 GtCO2, so total CO2 reaches 43.3 GtCO2. The dataset confirms that is the highest annual total in the series (previous high 42.75 in 2025), but the 2026–2025 gap (0.57 GtCO2) is far smaller than the ±3.2 GtCO2 (1σ) uncertainty on the total. The budget's own remaining-budget figure is 150 GtCO2 (50% likelihood, 1.5 °C), **counted from the beginning of 2027**. I found no source giving 165 GtCO2. The figures nearest to it are the GCB 2025 edition's 170 GtCO2 from January 2026 and IGCC's 130 GtCO2 from the start of 2026. The thin points: country-level 2026 changes exist only as rounded percentages, so country MtCO2 values here are derived. The 2026 land sink is a residual with low confidence. Le Quéré et al. in Nature Climate Change is confirmed, but it is a Brief Communication by six GCB co-authors and does not count as independent of the budget.

## Contradictions

- **"First projected decline outside the pandemic" (commission) vs the budget's own record.** GCB2026 Table S6 (SI p. 53) lists projected world changes of −0.6% for 2015 and −0.2% for 2016. GCB2015's abstract confirms −0.6% [−1.6 to +0.5]. The first realized estimate for 2015 was +0.06%, so the projected sign was wrong on first assessment. The current GCB2026 dataset puts 2015 at −0.13% and 2016 at −0.02% raw (−0.29% leap-adjusted), my calculation from "Global Carbon Budget" rows 78–80. Realized annual declines in the GCB2026 series (EFOS incl. carbonation, raw): 1974 −0.56%, 1975 −0.26%, 1980 −0.74%, 1981 −2.61%, 1982 −0.81%, 1992 −3.03%, 1998 −0.37%, 2009 −1.76%, 2015 −0.13%, 2016 −0.02%, 2020 −5.42%, 2026 (projected) −0.41%. The angle's "first" claim must go.
- **2016 projection sign.** Table S6 gives −0.2% (−1.0 to +1.8) for 2016. The GCB2016 abstract gives +0.2% (−1.0 to +1.8). Table S6 says "All values are adjusted for leap years", and 2016 was a leap year. But a per-day adjustment of +0.2% gives about −0.07%, not −0.2%. Table S6 lists the 2016 "actual" as +0.20%, which does not look leap-adjusted either. Unresolved. Do not lean on the 2016 sign. 2015 alone breaks the "first" claim.
- **Cause of the dip.** The GCP press release says the decrease is "driven by expansion of solar and wind power and the Iran conflict". The NCC paper attributes most of the oil fall to prices and supply. In its account, EVs explain only about 20% of the oil decrease, and solar and wind act on coal and gas (coal still rises). ScienceDaily's "may finally be turning a corner" conflicts with the NCC authors' "not a sign that emissions have peaked". These are the two views to state fairly. The NCC authors' own view is the conservative one.
- **Remaining budget 150 vs 165.** I found no 165 GtCO2 in any primary or in the coverage I read. Numbers in circulation: GCB2026 150 GtCO2 from January 2027; GCB2025 170 GtCO2 from January 2026; IGCC/Forster et al. 130 GtCO2 from the start of 2026; inside GCB2026, the updated IPCC AR6 estimate gives 210 and the updated Forster estimate 85, both from January 2027. 170 and 150 differ by about one year of total emissions (43 GtCO2) plus method changes, so the start date accounts for most of any gap.
- **"Less than 4 years" vs "about 3 years".** Exec Summary (p. 8, line 270) says "less than 4 years at the 2026 emissions levels". §4 (p. 63, line 2119) says "about 3" years. 150 / 43.3 = 3.5 years.
- **53% vs 54% above pre-industrial.** Abstract (p. 6) and press release say 54%. Exec Summary (p. 9, line 275) and §3.5.4 (p. 40) say 53%. 427.7/278 = 1.539, so 54% follows from the abstract's own 278 ppm.
- **2026 land-sink uncertainty.** §3.7.4 (p. 49) gives 4.1 ± 0.7 GtC. Table 7 (p. 121) gives 4.1 ± 1.4 GtC.
- **Typos in the preprint that could mislead a reader.** Exec Summary line 229–230 says "2025 emissions for India are projected to increase by 4.4%" (context is 2026). §3.1.4 line 1048 says "bringing 2025 emissions for China around 3.4 GtC" (2026 is meant). §4 line 2115 cites "Forster et al. (2025)" where line 2107 says 2026. SI line 128 cites an "October 2025 edition" of the STEO for the 2026 EU oil projection. Cite the corrected reading only where the context is unambiguous.
- **Total "record".** Carbon Brief and ScienceDaily call 43.3 GtCO2 a record. The preprint says only "marginally above the 2025 level". The dataset supports "highest in the series": 43.32 vs 42.75 (2025), 42.42 (2018), 42.32 (2024). The margin (0.57 GtCO2) is about a sixth of the stated ±3.2 GtCO2 1σ on a single year's total. That 3.2 is level uncertainty, mostly shared between adjacent years, so it overstates the uncertainty on the change, but the preprint gives no separate uncertainty for the change in the total.
- **Cement share.** Carbon Brief gives cement "about 2%" of 2026 fossil emissions. The dataset gives cement process emissions 3.8% of the 2026 total excluding carbonation; the preprint gives 4% for 2025. About 2% would be the net after the carbonation sink. Use the preprint.

## Limits

- No 2026 country values in tonnes exist in any primary; country MtCO2 changes above are derived from rounded percentages × 2025 levels. A waterfall by country must say "derived" in the caption.
- The preprint does not say how the regional ranges combine into the world range (SI §S.1.5 says only "The global total is the sum"). I cannot state a probability that 2026 is a decline. With ~68% bounds at −1.5% and +0.6%, the reporting supports "a rise cannot be ruled out", which is the authors' own position.
- The 165 GtCO2 figure the commission mentions: source not found. I can state what the budget says (150 from January 2027) and which other figures circulate (170 from January 2026 for GCB2025; 130 from the start of 2026 for IGCC). I cannot say who published 165.
- The 2016 projected sign in Table S6 (−0.2%) conflicts with the original paper (+0.2%), and I could not resolve it.
- Pre-2015 projections "based on a different method" are said to be in Le Quéré et al. (2016) (Table S6 caption). I did not open them. That includes the 2009 financial-crisis projection, so the projection record here starts at 2015.
- The 2026 land sink (15.0 GtCO2) is a residual and inherits every other term's error. Table 7 gives ±1.4 GtC (±5.1 GtCO2). A sources-and-sinks diagram for 2026 should mark it as the balancing term, not as a measurement.
- Carbon Brief was summarized through a fetch tool, not read as raw HTML. Every figure it gives that the piece uses is checked against the preprint or dataset above, so nothing depends on it.

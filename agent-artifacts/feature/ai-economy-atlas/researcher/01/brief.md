This round: go to the primary artifacts, not the blog framing.
- The ATLAS interactive (ai.google/economy/atlas/) and the AI-in-Science paper
  (ai.google/static/documents/AI-in-Science.pdf), plus any methodology/dataset note and
  the MIT FutureTech taxonomy source.

  and "double global share"; India creative ~1.6x; Brazil/Germany ~7% manual, ~1.4x;

  value and wording, the population and denominator it is over, and how the underlying
  signal was gathered (telemetry, a classifier over conversations/usage, or a survey
  instrument — and the sample size and dates for the survey). This denominator/definition
  detail is the crux of the piece; get it from the source, with locators.
- Find and record at least one independent, credible treatment of the limits of
  usage-share and self-reported-time-savings metrics (labour-economics or measurement
  literature) so the writer can weigh the numbers fairly.
- Preserve any full series (e.g. a ranked occupation/region table) a chart could use.
Minimum eight sources; the study's blog post is secondary to its own data. Note every
place the interactive's definitions and the paper disagree, or the coverage overstates
the data. If the specific original-work claim in the commission does not hold against the
real denominators, say what claim the data does support.

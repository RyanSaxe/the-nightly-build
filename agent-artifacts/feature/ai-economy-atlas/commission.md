# Commission: feature/ai-economy-atlas

## Assignment
A feature doing original analysis of the AI & Economy ATLAS, the research project
Google published with Google DeepMind and MIT FutureTech on 15 September 2026 (the
interactive sits at ai.google/economy/atlas/; the science strand has a paper,
AI-in-Science.pdf). Template `article`: 800-6000 words, two fixed anchors
(orientation, sources) plus two to six flex sections you name, every section cited.
Minimum eight sources.

## The angle
The ATLAS reports shares and self-reports: what fraction of work-related AI use falls
to which occupations and regions, and how much time surveyed scientists say AI saves
them. A share is a ratio with a denominator, and a self-reported hours figure measures
a belief about time, not time. The feature's job is to state precisely what each
headline number is a measurement of, and therefore what it can and cannot support about
"AI at work." Go to the interactive and the paper for the actual definitions and
denominators; do not rely on the blog post's framing.

Concrete figures to pin and interrogate (confirm each against the ATLAS itself, not
secondary coverage): U.S. computer and mathematical occupations reported at ~30% of
work-related AI usage (described as about double the global share); India's creative
industries at ~1.6x the global average for arts/design/media; Brazil and Germany ~7% of
work AI on manual tasks (~1.4x global), Japan ~4%; "nearly half" of surveyed scientists
using AI daily; scientists reporting savings of "almost 7 hours a week." For each,
record what population and denominator the figure is over and how the underlying signal
was gathered (product telemetry, classifier over conversations, survey instrument).

## Required original contribution
Something the ATLAS does not do with its own numbers. The strongest candidate: show
that the occupation and region "shares of AI usage" are shares of a usage pool, so they
move with who is using the products and how much, not with how much AI help a job
actually gets, and carry that distinction through one or two of the specific figures to
a checkable statement about what the 30% (or the 7-hours) does and does not establish.
The writer states the one-sentence original-work claim in draft-handoff.md. If the data
will not support that specific claim, the researcher says so and we pick the claim the
data does support; do not force the angle.

## Boundaries
- Fair to the study. State what the ATLAS set out to measure, in its authors' terms,
  before testing the reading built on it. It is a serious measurement effort; the piece
  is not a takedown.
- Separate established from judged. Where the data settles something, say it without
  hedging; where it cannot reach, say that.
- This is not the recent "AI incident reports need an audit trail" feature. That piece
  argued for better reporting infrastructure. This one does arithmetic on a published
  dataset. Do not reprise its measurement-governance frame or its structure.
- No self-reference, no gesture at a hypothetical reader.

## Furniture
A table comparing a few figures with their denominators, or a stat strip, is likely to
earn its place; reach for one only where it reads faster than prose. A chart only from a
series the researcher verifies, built with `nb chart`.

## Neighbouring articles this run
Daily Brief (6 items incl. an AI-server export case and an AI voice-clone ruling) and a
Technical walkthrough of Python 3.15 lazy imports. Those carry the edition's other AI
angles; this feature stays on the economics-of-use measurement and does not stray into
export policy, voice law, or language-runtime territory.

## Source policy
Minimum eight sources. Primary = the ATLAS interactive and the AI-in-Science paper, plus
any underlying dataset/methodology note Google publishes and the source of the taxonomy
(MIT FutureTech). Secondary = independent reporting and any independent labour-economics
work that contextualises usage-share metrics. The study's own blog post is secondary to
its own data.

## Production
Researcher high / capable. Writer medium / capable. Writing-coach low / capable. Editor
required, high effort, correspondent's model.

## Decisions log
- Anchor is the ATLAS work-usage data first; the science survey (7 hours, daily use) is
  a supporting strand, used only to the extent the paper's methodology is reachable.
- Original-work claim is provisional until the researcher confirms the denominators;
  the evidence record governs the final claim.

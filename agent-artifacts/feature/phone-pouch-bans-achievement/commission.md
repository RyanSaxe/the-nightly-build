## Topic and reason to read it now

On 17 November 2026 Harvard's Center for Education Policy Research (Tom Kane), with NWEA and Dartmouth
(Douglas Staiger), released an analysis of NWEA MAP Growth data (about 3.3 million students, about 4,600
schools, 2021–22 to 2025–26) on schools that require students to lock phones in Yondr pouches, plus
state bell-to-bell and instructional-time bans in 22 states in 2025–26. The press release is headlined
that eighth graders lost the equivalent of a year's learning since 2013 and that in-school phone use
"may account for half of the historical loss". It estimates pouch bans raise achievement growth by
about 0.01 SD a year (1–2 weeks of learning), against a 2013–2024 decline of about 0.01 to 0.02 SD a
year. Press release: https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/us-8th-graders-gradually-lost-equivalent-of-a-years-learning-since-2013-new-research-implies-in-school-cell-phone-use-may-account-for-half-of-the-historical-loss-302941290.html ;
CEPR page: https://cepr.harvard.edu/cellphones

## The angle to test

The "half of the historical loss" claim sets an effect measured in 2021–26 on pouch-ban schools beside a
decline measured in 2013–2024 national data (NAEP or NWEA norms). The article's original work is to
show, from the paper itself, what the comparison rests on: what was measured, the design (event study
or difference-in-differences, comparison schools, pre-trends), the size of the effect against the size
of the decline on one scale, and what the authors themselves say about extrapolating it. Then say what
the evidence supports. If the paper's own text supports the "half" reading more firmly than the release
suggests, say so; the angle is a question to settle, not a verdict to defend.

Also needed for fairness and context: the strongest prior evidence on school phone bans (for example
the England study by Goodyear et al. in Lancet Regional Health–Europe 2025 finding no association,
Norway's Abrahamsson studies, Spain or the Netherlands evaluations) stated in its authors' terms, and
the state-ban results the authors flag as possibly confounded by pre-existing trends.

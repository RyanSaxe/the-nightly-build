# Draft handoff: feature/phone-pouch-bans-achievement (writer 01)

## Original work

The article takes the release's "half of the historical loss" and puts it against NAEP's own timeline and the authors' own conversion. Half of the 11-point grade 8 math loss since 2013 is 5.5 points, which is more than the 3 points lost from 2013 to 2019. So for phones in class to account for half, they would have to explain the whole pre-pandemic decline and part of the 2019–22 drop. The article then sets the release beside the April Allcott et al. bound and beside the test Kane and Staiger wrote down in May.

## Judgments the editor should check

- **The direction of "half".** The release gives the decline as 0.01–0.02 SD a year, so the ratio runs from half to all. "Half" is the cautious end, and the piece says so: "the release’s cautious end". It does not argue that "half" is inflated as a rate. The criticism is aimed at the headline's move from a ratio of rates to a share of the total loss, and at the timing of that loss.
- **NAEP as the release's data.** The release never names NAEP. The piece says the release "does not name the data" and that "NAEP matches it" (11 points ÷ 10.25 points per grade level ≈ 1 grade). The 2022 and 2024 grade 8 scores, about 274 for math, are inferred from the NAGB-reported changes and were not read directly (see the record's Limits). The 11-point and 5.5-point figures rest on that inference.
- **Reading.** For fairness the piece says half the reading loss (5 of 10 points) equals the pre-pandemic decline. The timing argument is therefore made for math only, and the headline is scoped to math.
- **Uncited arithmetic.** "0.01 SD moves a median student to about the 50.4th percentile" is Φ(0.01) on a normal distribution, my arithmetic.
- **"a relatively small effect".** This Kane quote comes from the record's Contradictions section (Gazette). It is not in that source's Quote block.
- **Unlike units.** Growth and level, student SD and school SD are never compared like for like. Fig. 1's caption says the shared scale is the release's assumption, and the prose says neither paper puts the two studies on one scale.
- **Grades.** The release gives grades 3–10 and AP gives 4–10. The piece dropped the grade range rather than choose between them.
- **Close.** The piece closes on a verdict drawn from the authors' May sentence ("not the sharp rise that would tie the decline to distractions in class"). It does not close on what the figure leaves unsettled. The opener does not restate a figure.

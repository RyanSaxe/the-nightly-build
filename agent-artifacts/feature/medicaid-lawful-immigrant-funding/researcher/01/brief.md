# researcher brief: feature/medicaid-lawful-immigrant-funding (01)

Inputs:
- editorial-direction.md (../../editorial-direction.md) — the binding standard
- commission.md (../../commission.md) — the assignment and the angle

Output: evidence.md (./evidence.md)

Work from these inputs. Do not tour the repository, the Git history or the
archive for background. Where something you need is missing, ask me.

Establish, from documents you open and read, the material for the angle in
commission.md: the law ends the federal Medicaid/CHIP match for a defined set of
lawfully present immigrant groups effective 1 October 2026, and coverage
outcomes then diverge by state.

Read the sources, not the coverage:
- The operative text of the 2025 reconciliation law (the enrolled bill /
  U.S. Code as amended) for the provision restricting federal Medicaid and CHIP
  eligibility/funding by immigration category. Record exactly which lawfully
  present categories lose the federal match and which are retained (for example
  lawful permanent residents, and any carve-outs such as Cuban/Haitian entrants,
  COFA migrants, certain children and pregnant immigrants where a state covers
  them). Give the section citation.
- The CMS implementing guidance (reported as State Health Official letter
  SHO #26-001, dated 8 April 2026): what it instructs states to do, the
  effective date, and any transition or timing detail. Open the letter itself.
- At least two concrete state responses from official sources (a state Medicaid
  agency notice, budget action, or governor/legislature action) that show
  divergence: at least one state continuing coverage with state-only funds and
  one letting it lapse, if the record supports that contrast. Record what each
  state actually decided and its date.

Then:
- Any national estimate of how many people are affected must be attributed to
  the body that produced it (for example KFF, Georgetown CCF, CBO) with its
  scope and method. These are secondary. Do not launder an estimate into a fact.
- Look for what breaks the angle: evidence that coverage does end automatically
  somewhere, that a category is treated differently than the commission assumes,
  or that the federal/state framing is wrong. Record it in full.
- Classify every source primary or secondary by authorship and stake, with a
  one-line reason. An advocacy or analysis organization is secondary even when
  authoritative.
- Note source assets: any table in a primary document (for example a
  category-by-category match-status table) or a figure that could carry the
  argument better than prose, and what a crop must keep.

Minimum eight sources, anchored on primary records. Flag in Limits anything the
commission asked for that the primary record does not settle, especially any
state contrast you could not source firsthand.

Work from these inputs. Do not tour the repository, the Git history or the
archive for background. Where something you need is missing, ask me.

Genre: an explanatory policy feature that reads a statute and its implementing
guidance and shows what they actually do, as against what the coverage assumes.
The register is calm, exact, and load-bearing on primary documents. The audience
reads widely and reasons well but is not assumed to know health-financing
mechanics, so terms like federal medical assistance percentage, lawfully present
category, or a State Health Official letter get defined where first used.

Find exemplars among writers the field rates for making a law or a regulation
legible without flattening it: the best explanatory policy and
health-policy writers, and reporters who can hold a statute and a human
consequence in the same piece without editorializing. Prefer the primary piece
to commentary on it. Show how they define a term in passing, how they separate
what a law says from what people think it says, and how they stay fair to a
rationale they go on to test.

Quote passages that show the writing, not the subject. Do not quote anything
whose phrasing the writer could lift about Medicaid or immigration specifically;
their wording stays theirs. The direction section should tell the writer how to
sound authoritative about a document-heavy subject without the performed
carefulness `spec/slop.md` warns against.

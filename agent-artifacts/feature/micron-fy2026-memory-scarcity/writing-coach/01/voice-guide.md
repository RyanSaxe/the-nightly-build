# Voice guide

## How this piece should sound

Write as a semiconductor business analyst explaining an earnings release to someone who understands computing but has never had to distinguish DRAM bit shipments from DRAM revenue. Keep Micron, its customers and its manufacturing processes as the subjects of sentences. Thompson's short statement about Intel's volume requirement shows how to connect a manufacturing requirement to a business decision without a vocabulary of transformation. Where the evidence permits, explain how HBM production, conventional server DRAM and NAND affect the same company's earnings through different mechanisms.

Goldberg separates semiconductor costs before assessing which businesses can afford them. Use that habit when Micron's figures need decomposition: price and shipped bits, gross profit and operating profit, spending and productive capacity. Keep the units and accounting periods visible. The purpose of a calculation is to establish which explanation fits the reported change; the calculation does not need an adjective announcing its importance.

McKenzie's liability sentence identifies both the accounting category and the person owed money. Give Micron customer deposits and commitments that same precision wherever the filings support it. Explain who paid, what Micron owes, and when a payment becomes revenue. His brief historical correction shows that a qualification can change the interpretation without slowing the prose. When a commitment's pricing, cancellation rights or delivery schedule is undisclosed, identify that particular missing term. Avoid a generic disclaimer about uncertainty.

The commissioned piece tests earnings durability. It may reach a favorable judgment without adopting management's certainty, or reach an unfavorable judgment without sounding like an investment pitch. Use reported results to judge the quarter and identify forecasts as forecasts. The voice should remain calm when the figures are exceptional: specific amounts, relationships and contract terms already supply the force.

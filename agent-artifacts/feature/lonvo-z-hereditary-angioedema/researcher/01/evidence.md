# Evidence: lonvo-z in hereditary angioedema (researcher 01)

Read on 14 November 2026. The evidence supports a like-for-like table of seven
placebo-controlled pivotal trials (HAELO and six comparators), and it supports
resolving every headline lonvo-z figure to its endpoint, denominator and period.
It also supports a precise nex-z timeline. It is thin in four places. The ACAAI
abstracts and posters themselves (Annals of Allergy, Asthma & Immunology,
Nov 2026 supplement) and the full NEJM text are behind paywalls or bot walls, so
the 81% and 89% figures are resolved from Intellia's own 12 November investor
deck (SEC Exhibit 99.1) and release, not from the abstracts. The "up to four
years" durability rests on very few patients (4 at month 41 and later, per the
deck's own at-risk row). Nothing independent of Intellia and the trial authors
exists for the ACAAI analyses. And the commission's premise that nex-z's hold
followed "a liver-related death" does not match the filings: the hold preceded
the death by one week, and the cause of death as reported by the investigator
was septic shock from a perforated ulcer, with acute liver injury in the course.
The commissioned angle survives, with three corrections (see Contradictions).

Decisions made under the commission, in place of asking:

- Added donidalorsen (Dawnzera, prekallikrein antisense) and subcutaneous C1-INH
  (Haegarda) to the four named comparators. Donidalorsen acts on the same target
  as lonvo-z (prekallikrein), chronically, and Intellia's own deck lists both
  among the "approved chronic LTPs". Sebetralstat is recorded but has no
  attack-rate endpoint, so it cannot sit in the attack-reduction column.
- Where an FDA label and the journal paper give different analyses of one trial,
  both are recorded (garadacimab: -87% in the Lancet, 89.2% in the label).
- Intellia's GlobeNewswire page and IR site return 403 to every non-browser
  request I made. I read the identical wire text via a syndicated copy and the
  SEC-filed exhibits. The URLs recorded are the owners' pages, not the mirror.

## Contradictions

1. **"Presented 12 to 13 November".** The commission says the data were presented
   on 12 and 13 November. The press release is dated 12 Nov. Five posters are
   scheduled for 13 Nov, including the 89%/93-patient integrated analysis
   (5:35 pm MT) and the 81% subgroup poster (3:05 pm MT). The two oral
   sessions, including the one titled four-year durability, are scheduled for
   Saturday 14 Nov, 4:43 to 5:03 pm MT, which is today. The investor webcast on
   12 Nov showed the numbers first. "Presented at ACAAI" is true for the posters
   and, at the time of reading, not yet true for the orals.
2. **"A clinical hold after a liver-related death".** The order in the filings is
   the reverse. A MAGNITUDE patient dosed 30 Sep 2025 had Grade 4
   transaminases and raised bilirubin reported 24 Oct; Intellia paused dosing 27
   Oct; FDA told Intellia of the hold 29 Oct; the patient died 5 Nov. The
   investigator-reported cause is septic shock secondary to a perforated
   duodenal ulcer, in a course that "also included acute liver injury and its
   treatment with corticosteroids", with an autopsy supporting the clinical
   diagnoses. Whether the liver injury contributed to the death is not stated in
   any document I opened. Also, the holds are over: lifted for MAGNITUDE-2 on
   27 Jan 2026 and for MAGNITUDE on 2 Mar 2026, with mitigations. The 10-Q
   describes both trials as resumed. The commission's "had a clinical hold" is
   accurate only in the past tense and only with that dating.
3. **87% vs 91% (and two other 89%s).** These are different endpoints in one
   trial and not competing estimates of one. 87% (95% CI 78 to 93) is the
   primary endpoint, all investigator-confirmed attacks, weeks 5-28, 0.26 vs
   2.10. 91% (81 to 96) is the key secondary endpoint of moderate or severe
   attacks, same weeks, 0.11 vs 1.23. 89% (79 to 94) in the same table is
   attacks needing on-demand treatment, 0.19 vs 1.79. The ACAAI "89%" is
   something else again: a reduction from each patient's baseline among 93
   patients across two trials, with no comparator arm. And the garadacimab FDA
   label's 89.2% is the all-attacks primary endpoint of VANGUARD under the FDA
   analysis, where the Lancet paper reports -87% for the same trial. Any "89%"
   in the article needs its endpoint attached.
4. **Median or mean 7.6 months.** The 12 Nov deck calls 7.6 months the "Median
   total follow-up" on slide 29 and the "Mean (range) duration of follow-up 7.6
   (0.3-46.5) months" in the safety table. Both cannot describe one summary
   statistic. Intellia's release says only "up to four years".
5. **Intellia's own cross-trial table versus its own footnote.** Slide 60 sets
   87% against "44-89%" for approved LTPs and 62% attack- and treatment-free
   against 0%. The footnote calls it "for illustrative purposes only". The 0%
   is a definitional zero: patients on chronic LTP cannot be "treatment-free"
   while on it. The range reproduces the labels (44.2% berotralstat 150 mg to
   89.2% garadacimab), which means it mixes the FDA-analysis garadacimab
   number with model-based percentages from other trials.
6. **The within-patient comparison reads smaller than the randomised one.** Among
   prior-LTP patients, the 81% reduction versus prescreening (1.18 to 0.22)
   sits beside a placebo-controlled attack rate ratio of 0.08 (about 92%) in the
   same subgroup (lonvo-z 35 vs placebo 22; slide 27). The placebo arm overall
   ran 2.10 attacks per month after LTP withdrawal, against 1.18 for the
   prior-LTP patients' prescreening rate. That is consistent with attack rates
   rising after LTP is stopped, which would make the prescreening rate a
   favourable-to-LTP baseline. This is my reading of the numbers, not a
   statement in any source, and it cannot be tested without patient-level data.
7. **"Run-in" baselines differ in kind.** HAELO's baseline for the 81% is
   patient-reported attacks in the 90 days before Screening-1 (slide 26
   footnote). The comparators' endpoints are measured against placebo during
   treatment, with run-in rates recorded prospectively and investigator
   confirmation of attacks. I found no statement on whether the post-dose attacks
   behind 0.22 are investigator-confirmed or patient-reported.
8. **What "prior LTP" window means.** The slide defines prior LTP use as the last
   dose within 100 days of Screening-1, and the baseline window as the 90
   days before the Screening visit, labelled "(on LTP)". These two definitions
   allow a patient who stopped LTP just before screening to have part of the
   window off LTP. I cannot tell from the slides whether the labels are exact.
9. **Primary-trial attack-free (62%) against comparators.** Lonvo-z's 62%
   (weeks 5-28, n=52) equals garadacimab's 62% attack-free over 6 months in
   VANGUARD (label; defined as 100% reduction from the 2-month pre-treatment
   rate, day 1 on), beats lanadelumab 300 mg q2w at 44% over 26 weeks, and sits
   above donidalorsen q4w at 53% (week 4-24). Each used a different counting
   window and a different attack-free definition. The lonvo-z count skips the
   first four weeks; lanadelumab's and garadacimab's do not.

Evidence against the commissioned angle (the question asked "how much of what a
one-time edit promises can the published data show"): none found that sinks it.
The published randomised data show a reduction at the high end of the comparator
range over 24 weeks, which is the promise's weakest-tested part. Durable
efficacy beyond about 12 months rests on uncontrolled Phase 1/2 patients. No
safety finding in HAELO or the integrated analysis ties to the nex-z liver
events, and no document connects them.

Design differences that stop the comparison being like for like (each traced
above): (a) HAELO's window starts at week 5 and the others' at day 0 or week 0,
so HAELO's count leaves out the weeks in which a single infusion is still
lowering kallikrein (slide data: steady state by week 5); (b) every comparator

about 24 weeks; (c) HAELO enrolled 71% on LTP who had to stop it beforehand, vs
18% (OASIS-HAE), 33% (VANGUARD), 56% (HELP), 75% (APeX-2); (d) the analysis

COMPACT); (e) COMPACT and KONFIDENT are crossover; the rest are parallel group;
(f) HAELO's blinded crossover after week 28 included a switch of lonvo-z
patients to placebo, which the comparators did not have; (g) minimum run-in
attack thresholds differ (at least 1 per 4 weeks in HELP, 2 in the others,
4 in COMPACT), and I did not find HAELO's.

## Limits

- The ACAAI abstracts (Ann Allergy Asthma Immunol 2026;137(5) supplement, DOIs
  10.1016/j.anai.2026.09.021, .022, .083, .084, .085, .090, .101) did not open:
  the DOIs redirect to Elsevier pages that return 403 or a bot wall. The 81%,
  89%, 97% and 84% figures are therefore read from Intellia's release and deck,
  not the abstracts. The posters and the orals are not posted at the time of
  reading.
- The full NEJM text did not open (Cloudflare). Not established: HAELO's minimum
  entry attack rate, washout durations, baseline attack rate by arm, liver
  laboratory results, or the paper's own statement on how attacks were
  confirmed.
- No FDA document on lonvo-z or nex-z is public beyond the label pages for the
  comparators. The hold, the lifting and the "no advisory committee" statement
  reach the record only as Intellia's account of FDA communications. I found no
  FDA press release, letter or database entry.
- Whether the Grade 4 liver event contributed to the death is not stated in any
  document I opened. The filing says what the investigator reported and that an
  autopsy report "supported the clinical diagnoses".
- No HAELO or integrated safety finding bears on the nex-z liver events that I
  could find. HAELO had no serious or grade 3+ events in the lonvo-z arm through
  week 28 (NEJM abstract). The 93-patient integrated set has two grade 3 SAEs,
  pulmonary embolism and perforated appendicitis, neither liver-related per the
  deck. One secondary article (HCPLive, 17 Jun 2026) says "no transient or
  persistent elevations in liver function tests", a quote I could not trace to a
  primary document. Do not use it for a number or a claim.
- Durability past about one year rests on a small, uncontrolled, shrinking
  cohort (24 at month 12, 16 at month 24, 10 at month 36, 4 at month 41 and
  later). The deck's phrase "no waning of effect" is the sponsor's. There is no
  placebo-controlled data beyond week 28; HAELO's crossover after week 28 is
  blinded and its results are not reported in anything I opened.
- Permanence is addressed in the record only as a risk-factor sentence in the
  10-Q. No source I opened gives data on what happens if the edit needs to be
  undone, or on off-target editing in these patients.
- The 89% integrated figure's trial split, per-patient baseline and method are
  not in what I opened, nor is how "overall" was computed.
- OASIS-HAE's results come from the label only, not the NEJM paper. COMPACT's 87%
  is my arithmetic from two label values. Approval dates for the comparators were
  not independently checked; the labels give "Initial U.S. Approval" years
  (lanadelumab 2018, C1-INH SC 2017, berotralstat 2020, garadacimab 2025,
  donidalorsen 2025, sebetralstat 2025).
- Direct head-to-head or network meta-analysis evidence for lonvo-z against any
  comparator was not found. Intellia's slide 60 is the only cross-trial table and
  it is the sponsor's, with the footnote quoted above.
- A "run-in" attack rate of 3.5 (SD 1.8 and 1.9 by arm) for HAELO appeared in a
  search-tool summary of Intellia's topline slides. I could not open those slides
  (ir.intelliatx.com returned 403) and did not use the figure.

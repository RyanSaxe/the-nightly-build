# Evidence: feature/redistricting-ballot-measures-2026 (researcher 01)

Four statewide measures on congressional redistricting went to voters in 2026, not three, and they voted on two dates: Virginia on 21 April 2026, and Colorado, Maryland and Missouri on 3 November 2026 (Ballotpedia's redistricting-measure index lists exactly these four for 2026; Utah's repeal initiative failed to qualify). Of the 2025 maps the commission names, only Missouri's went to a 2026 vote. Texas's 2025 map had no ballot measure, and California's Proposition 50 was decided on 4 November 2025, so it falls outside 2026. The finding the sources support is that the four measures together changed the lines of zero US House districts. Missouri voters rejected a map that would have redrawn 6 of 8 districts. Virginia voters approved a map covering all 11 districts, but the Supreme Court of Virginia voided that vote on 8 May and the US Supreme Court declined to intervene. Maryland's approved amendment draws no lines and only removes state-constitutional criteria for a future map. Colorado's failed amendment would have restricted future off-cycle maps. None of the four decided which map governed the 3 November 2026 House elections. Courts had already settled that before the vote: Missouri used its 2022 map and Virginia its 2021 map. What the November measures decide is the starting map for 2028. The commission's premise that the measures "decide which 2025 maps govern the next House elections" is therefore right only if "next" means 2028. The evidence is thin in three places. Missouri's official results page is gated behind Cloudflare, so the Missouri figures come from a newspaper's reprint of the Secretary of State's feed. All three November results are unofficial as of 9 November. No source owns a seat projection for a Maryland map, because none has been drawn. The partisan effects that were blocked (Virginia from 6–5 to an expected 10–1; Missouri a seventh Republican seat) are projections, attributed below to the court, the map's sponsors or Ballotpedia, never to an official count.

### National context (for the timeline and the "net" framing)

The Ballotpedia Maryland page (above), in its "Status of congressional redistricting ahead of the 2026 elections" table, lists 10 states with new maps for 2026 (Alabama, California, Florida, Louisiana, Missouri, North Carolina, Ohio, Tennessee, Texas, Utah). Its "Potential result of new maps" column totals "+10 R." That total is Ballotpedia's projection, made as of August 2026, and it still counts Missouri's map at +1 R, which the courts later blocked. Texas is listed at +5 R, California at +5 D. The page also records Louisiana v. Callais (29 Apr 2026) and Texas as the first state to redraw (29 Aug 2025). These items are secondary, and only Missouri's appears on a 2026 ballot.

## Contradictions

- **The commission's frame against the record.** The commission names Texas, California and Missouri. Only Missouri's map went to voters in 2026. California's Proposition 50 was a 4 Nov 2025 vote (Ballotpedia index, 2025 heading). The commission omits the 2026 measure with the largest seat stakes, Virginia's April vote. The commission also says the measures "decide which 2025 maps govern the next House elections." None of them governed the 3 Nov 2026 House elections, which used maps courts had already fixed: Missouri's 2022 map (SCOTUS 26A388, p. 1) and Virginia's 2021 map (Va. opinion, pp. 29–30). The November measures govern the starting map for 2028 (NPR; KCUR; State Court Report, 4 Nov).
- **Missouri vote shares disagree across sources and over time.** SOS feed via News Tribune (4 Nov, 3214/3214 precincts): 38.158% / 61.842%, 2,059,784 votes. Ballotpedia (6 Nov): 38.20% / 61.80%, 2,064,130 votes, with a "95%" precincts label that conflicts with the 3214/3214 report. State Court Report and AP via KCUR give "62 to 38". All are unofficial, and all agree on direction and on roughly 62–38. I could not read the SOS page, which owns the figure.
- **Maryland shares shifted with the count.** Ballotpedia (6 Nov 8:07 AM ET): 60.09% yes of 1,825,392. SBE (8 Nov 7:40 PM): 60.96% yes of 1,981,278, with provisional ballots not yet reported. Cite the SBE figure and its timestamp. FOX45's 4 Nov figures ("nearly 1.08 million" yes, "more than 718,000" no) are earlier still.
- **Colorado shares.** Clarity (7 Nov 6:11 PM MST): 46.86% yes of 2,562,131. Ballotpedia (6 Nov): 46.89% of 2,519,428. Colorado Politics, early on election night: 46% / 53.95%. The result is decisive either way, because the threshold is 55%.
- **Virginia certification.** Ballotpedia's results box says "Results are officially certified." The Supreme Court of Virginia opinion calls the 30 April Department of Elections figures "unofficial results" (p. 5, nn.9, 11). The enacted map was contingent "upon certification of such results" (p. 4). Ballotpedia's own "Aftermath" says Judge Jack Hurley blocked certification on 22 April and that the Supreme Court of Virginia kept that block on 28 April. Treat the Virginia figures as unofficial, and treat the vote as void.
- **Missouri's Maggard ruling is described two ways.** Ballotpedia: on 12 May 2026 the Missouri Supreme Court held "HB1 was not suspended upon the submission of referendum signatures." SCOTUS (26A388, p. 4) reads the same opinion as having "stated that the 2025 map would not take effect until approved by the voters in an intervening referendum, at least if the referendum petition was legally sufficient." Both may be accurate: the map was not frozen by filing alone, but it could not take effect if the petition was later found sufficient. The SCOTUS reading is the one later applied. I did not read Maggard itself (Justia 403).
- **Partisan purpose of the Missouri map.** The proponents frame it as compactness and county integrity ("splits fewer counties and municipalities"; Kehoe release). NPR states the goal was "to oust Democratic Rep. Emanuel Cleaver ... to pick up an additional House seat for Republicans." Ballotpedia quotes President Trump: HB 1 "will, hopefully, give us an additional Seat in Congress." Both framings are on the record and should be attributed.
- **Which party backed Colorado's measure.** The measure restricts mid-decade redistricting, a cause usually associated with reform groups. Ballotpedia lists the Colorado Republican Party and Gov. Jared Polis (D) as supporters and the Colorado Democratic Party as an opponent. NPR describes it as banning maps "that purposefully favor one political party." State Court Report calls it a measure "Colorado conservatives supported." I did not confirm Polis's position in a primary statement.
- **Does Missouri's "no" end the matter?** KCUR reports that Republicans could redraw in 2027–28 and that a Missouri Supreme Court decision "opened the door," while Amendment 6 (passed the same day) may restrict re-enacting a rejected law. The SOS ballot title for Amendment 6 includes "prohibit the legislature from ... passing laws similar to those rejected by referendum, without approval from at least 80% of both chambers." Whether that reaches a new map is untested.

## Limits

- Missouri official results: enr.sos.mo.gov blocks automated access (Cloudflare 403). The vote counts come from a newspaper reprint of the SOS feed. The writer should cite the News Tribune reprint as relaying the SOS count, or cite the SOS page as gated.
- Certification: none of the three November results is certified as of 9 November. I found no published certification date for Missouri, Maryland or Colorado. All three official pages label their figures unofficial or show incomplete counts (Maryland provisional ballots not reported; Colorado 25 of 64 counties marked complete).
- Maryland effective date: I could not open Article XIV of the Maryland Constitution (the General Assembly site served a download notice), so I cannot state when Question 3 formally takes effect. The usual route is a governor's proclamation, but that is unverified here.
- Missouri Supreme Court opinions (von Glahn v. Hoskins, 3 Sep 2026; Maggard v. State, 12 May 2026): unread, because courts.mo.gov prohibits automated access and Justia returned 403. Their holdings are quoted secondhand by the US Supreme Court and State Court Report.
- Which two Missouri districts HB 1 left alone: the governor's release says two but does not name them. A Missouri Independent report (seen only in a search summary) names the 7th and 8th. Unconfirmed.
- Seat projections: no source owns a seat estimate for Maryland, because no map exists. The Virginia and Missouri projections are by the court, the sponsors or Ballotpedia, and the Inside Elections analysis was unreadable (Cloudflare). Per the commission, do not estimate further.
- Net figure: the sources support "0 lines changed." They do not support a net partisan seat figure for the measures. The blocked effects (VA +4 D expected, MO +1 R aimed) are projections of maps that never took effect, and they should not be summed into a "net" without attribution.
- Proponents' own words: Missouri yes (Kehoe release), Colorado for and against (Blue Book), Virginia (ballot text and Spanberger) and Missouri no (von Glahn via KCUR) are all on record. For Maryland, the proponents' quotes reach me via Ballotpedia and FOX45. I did not open the original Maryland Matters pieces (403).
- The Colorado and Maryland county breakdowns exist on the official pages but were not tabulated here.

## Correct

Thesis, from the draft alone: the four 2026 congressional-redistricting measures (Virginia, 21 April; Missouri Proposition A, Maryland Question 3, Colorado Amendment 86, 3 November) changed the lines of no US House district. The claims under it:

1. Missouri's voters rejected HB 1, which would have redrawn six of eight districts. Held. Kehoe's release says the map "Preserves two congressional districts as currently drawn" (opened). The Secretary of State's ballot language says a no vote leaves the 2022 boundaries (in the record; the page is bot-gated, see links below).
2. Virginia's vote was voided, and the 2021 map governs. Held. Opinion pp. 1, 4, 5, 29–30 opened: "renders it null and void" and "remain the governing maps for the upcoming 2026 congressional elections". The docket shows the 15 May stay denial.
3. Maryland's amendment draws no map. Held. HB 2100 p. 2 changes Art. III §4 and adds §62(A)–(B) and no plan. The preamble says it is "not intended to provide new grounds to challenge the plan enacted by Chapter 16 of the Acts of 2022."
4. Colorado's failed. Held. The Blue Book gives the 55% threshold, and the record's Clarity count is 46.86%.
5. Courts had settled the contested 2026 maps before the November votes. Held for Missouri (SCOTUS 26A388 p. 1, opened) and Virginia (8 May). It broke as written. The draft said courts had "fixed the maps for the 3 November House elections", which covers Maryland and Colorado too, but no court ruled on their maps. I narrowed it to Missouri and Virginia, with the date 25 September. This narrowing is an edit to what the sources show. It does not hide thin reporting.

Headline test, "move no House district line", run against each measure. Missouri: the no vote kept the 2022 map that was already in force. Virginia: the map was contingent on the vote, and the vote is void. Colorado: it failed, and a no vote "continues the current requirements" (Blue Book p. 1). Maryland: the amendment text contains no plan and disclaims any challenge to the 2022 plan. FOX45 reports that Question 3 "does not establish new congressional boundaries". The headline holds for all four. Maryland is the one place a later line could move. The last paragraph says so, and the dek no longer claims anything about Maryland's 2028 map.

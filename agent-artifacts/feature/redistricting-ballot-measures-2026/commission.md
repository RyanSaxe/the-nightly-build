# Commission: feature/redistricting-ballot-measures-2026

Series: feature (open, template `article`, strict, 600–1,200 words, 2–4 flex sections,
at least 5 sources). Writer effort high: the visuals carry the piece.

## Subject

The 3 November 2026 state ballot measures on congressional redistricting, after the 2025
mid-decade redistricting fight (Texas's 2025 map, California's 2025 Proposition 50 response,
Missouri's 2025 map, and any other state where voters decided a map or the body that draws it
in 2026). A State Court Report analysis of 4 November reports Missouri voters rejected the 2025
congressional map by 62 to 38 percent.

## Why now

Results were certified or near-final this week, and the measures decide which 2025 maps govern
the next House elections. No article in this paper has covered them.

## The original work

Put every 2026 redistricting measure on one comparison: what each measure did (repeal a map,
approve a map, create or abolish a commission), the result, and the number of US House seats
whose lines change as a consequence, sourced to the map or the official text. The finding is
what the measures add up to for the House map, net, which no single-state report gives.
If the measures do not add up to a net seat figure the sources support, say what they do add up
to and stop there; do not estimate seats from partisan lean without a source that owns the estimate.

## Boundaries

- Results come from secretaries of state or official canvass pages; vote shares to the precision
  the official source gives, and say whether it is certified or unofficial as of 9 November.
- Abortion, voter ID and other non-redistricting measures are out of scope, except a one-line
  mention if a redistricting measure shared a ballot with them in a way that matters.
- Separate what the measure does legally (effective date, litigation pending) from projected
  partisan effect, and attribute any projection to whoever made it.
- Fair treatment: state the case each side made for its measure in its proponents' own words.

## Visual

Build each flex section around a visual: at least the comparison table of measures, and one drawn
figure (e.g. a timeline from the 2025 maps to the 2026 votes, or a seat-change diagram). Inline SVG
per `press/editorial.md`.

## Decisions after research (orchestrator, researcher/01)

- Rescoped: the subject is the four 2026 congressional-redistricting ballot measures (Virginia,
  21 April; Missouri Proposition A, Maryland Question 3 and Colorado Amendment 86, 3 November).
  Texas's and California's 2025 maps are context in at most a sentence each; neither was on a 2026 ballot.
- The finding the evidence supports, replacing the net-seat finding: the four measures changed the
  lines of zero US House districts for 2026; two blocked maps (one by voters, one by a court after
  voters approved it), one removed constraints for a future map, one failed. What they set is the
  starting map for 2028. Do not sum projected partisan effects; attribute each projection.
- Missouri results: use the Secretary of State feed as reprinted by the Jefferson City News Tribune,
  labelled unofficial as of 9 November, with the record's precinct count. No further fetch attempt;
  decided to save the round.
- The "Why now" line in this commission is narrowed: the measures decide the 2028 starting maps, not
  the 2026 House elections.

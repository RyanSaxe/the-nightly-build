# Evidence: feature/open-doors-new-enrollment (researcher 01)

Run date 2026-11-22. All IIE figures below were read from IIE's own published
files (xlsx tables, Fast Facts PDF, press release PDF, Snapshot reports), not
from coverage.

**Summary.** The evidence supports the commissioned angle in part. IIE's census
counts OPT inside its total, and in 2025/26 OPT rose 14.1% to 335,785 (29.3% of
the total) while the enrolled part of the total (undergraduate, graduate,
non-degree) fell 8.3%, so the total fell only 2.7%. The enrolled fall sits
almost entirely in graduate students (-13.4%, master's -22.4%), and the
graduate fall sits almost entirely in India: India's graduate count fell by
61,283 (-34.4%), 94% of the world graduate fall, while India's OPT count rose
by 38,461 (+26.8%), 93% of the world OPT rise. New enrollment fell 7.2% in
2024/25 and 17.7% in 2025/26, and new graduate enrollment fell from 176,084 to
112,004 over two years. The Fall 2026 Snapshot shows OPT turning down for the
first time since 2021/22 (-6%). The evidence is thin or against the angle in
four places. IIE already states the lag mechanism itself (OPT growth is
"due to strong international graduate student growth in recent years", and OPT
"will likely continue to decrease"), so the original work is the decomposition,
not the observation. The total is not purely lagging: the new-enrollment fall
already reached the total in 2025/26 through the enrolled graduate stock, and
it is the OPT component that lags. Cohort arithmetic from IIE's own series is
unstable (the ratio of OPT to new graduate enrollment two years earlier drifts
from 1.61 to 1.91), and a constant-ratio lag would predict a far larger 2026/27
OPT fall than the Snapshot's -6%. And no IIE table links OPT counts to
graduating cohorts, so a cohort flow can only be drawn as an illustration with
stated assumptions.

## Contradictions

- **Snapshot non-degree figure.** Text on p.4: "Non-degree enrollment also
  declined by 15%". Figure 2 on the same page labels it -14%. Use only if
  necessary and say "about 14% to 15%", or skip it (non-degree is 2.7% of the
  census total).
- **IIE's rounded headlines against its own tables.** Press text says total
  "-3%" and new "-18%". The tables give -2.7% (1,177,766 to 1,146,163) and
  -17.7% (277,118 to 228,123). Both are IIE's. Use the table values.
- **Snapshot OPT against a lag model.** IIE reports OPT -6%. A constant ratio of
  OPT to new graduate enrollment two years earlier (1.91 in 2025/26) applied to
  2024/25 new graduates (150,536) gives about 287,000, which is -14% from
  335,785. The ratio is not constant (see Numbers), STEM OPT stays 24 more
  months and grew 31%, and OPT also draws on bachelor's graduates and
  second-degree students. The sources cannot reconcile this. It cuts against a
  simple "new enrollment falls, OPT falls with a fixed lag" claim.
- **The total is not only a lagging measure.** The enrolled part fell 8.3% in
  2025/26, so the new-enrollment fall was already in the total. Only the OPT
  part had not yet turned.
- **NAFSA's 9%.** NAFSA writes that IIE's survey plus October SEVIS data
  "produces a 9 percent decline in total enrollment—excluding students on
  Optional Practical Training". IIE's 9% is "including those enrolled in
  classes and on OPT". NAFSA's table headed "Total International Students
  (incl OPT)" shows 1,053,668 for 2026-27, which is 8.1% below 1,146,163. My
  arithmetic from the Snapshot's level changes on the census base (see
  Numbers) gives enrolled-only about -9.7% and total -8.6%.
- **SEVIS against Open Doors.** SEVIS 2024 counts 1,582,808 records for
  calendar year 2024. Open Doors counts 1,177,766 students for 2024/25. SEVIS
  counts records across all school types (including K-12 and language
  schools) and a student may have several; Open Doors counts students at
  accredited higher education institutions. SEVIS OPT (194,554) and STEM OPT
  (95,384) count authorizations with reported work in a calendar year; Open
  Doors 2024/25 OPT is 294,253 (post-completion 187,749 and STEM 106,504).
  These are different instruments and years, so no figure from one should be set
  against the other.
- **Definition of "new".** Open Doors Fast Facts: new students "enrolling at a
  U.S. college or university for the first time". Snapshot: "studying at their
  college or university for the first time", which reads as institution-level.
  IIE does not say how transfers and students moving from one level to the
  next at the same institution are treated in either.
- **Commission details.** The commission's scouting figures match IIE's: new
  -17.7%, total about 1.15 million, Snapshot -9% / -15% new / -15% graduate /
  -6% OPT. "Roughly 800 institutions" is 809 valid responses. "Second
  consecutive annual fall" holds for the census series (-7.2%, then -17.7%).
  The Snapshot's new-enrollment -15% is a third fall on a different
  instrument. NAFSA's "Fall 2026 International Student Economic Snapshot" the
  commission names is the earlier August outlook (111,000 fewer students, $3.4
  billion, built on IIE's Spring 2026 Snapshot); NAFSA replaced it in
  November with the $2.4 billion figure above.
- **Causes.** The Snapshot's list is institutions' reported reasons. IIE itself
  qualifies two of them: students from proclamation countries are 2% of
  international students, and only 22% of institutions said students felt
  unwelcome on their own campuses. No source establishes the share of the fall
  due to any one cause.

## Limits

- **No cohort-linked data.** IIE publishes no table connecting OPT counts to
  the years students entered. Every cohort arithmetic here is mine, built on a
  ratio that drifted from 1.61 to 1.91 and was distorted in 2020/21 to
  2022/23. A flow drawing can show the mechanism and cite IIE's sentences, and
  cannot present a computed forecast as IIE's or as established.
- **The Snapshot's OPT -6% is milder than a constant lag implies.** I could
  not reconcile this from the sources. IIE does not say whether the OPT
  comparison counts students by authorization date, by institution, or
  otherwise, and the Snapshot reports no counts, only rounded percentages.
- **Snapshot against census.** IIE gives no explicit rule. The 2025 backtest
  above shows the Snapshot ran within 2.3 points of the census on every line,
  and it was less negative than the census on the total (-1% against -2.7%) and
  on undergraduates (+2% against -0.3%). That is one year of one survey
  design, not a calibration.
- **Place of origin for new enrollment.** Not published by IIE in the files
  read. The India finding rests on total graduate and OPT counts, with the
  Snapshot's institutional shares ("39% report stable or higher new
  enrollment from India") as the only new-enrollment signal.
- **Fields of study.** The census gives fields for all students, not for new
  students, and not by level. I did not decompose by field. (Math and computer
  science 286,168, -6.3%; engineering 214,193, -1.3%; Fast Facts p.2.)
- **SEVIS cross-check.** I found no 2025 edition of SEVIS by the Numbers. The
  latest is the 2024 edition (calendar year 2024), which is not comparable to
  2025/26 or fall 2026. The DHS SEVIS Data Mapping Tool (monthly active F-1 and
  M-1 records, which excludes OPT) is the likely cross-check and I could not
  open it (403). Third-party posts quote it as a 7.7% fall in September 2026
  and 7.1% in October 2026 (1,283,240 to 1,192,572 active records); I have not
  verified those figures and they are not recorded as numbers. NAFSA says it
  used October SEVIS data but prints no SEVIS count.
- **Program lengths.** Not sourced (see Numbers). The OPT rules are sourced.
- **Visa policy.** Not researched beyond what IIE and NAFSA state, per the
  brief. NAFSA says DHS published a final rule on 17 July 2026 replacing
  duration of status from 15 September 2026, and IIE refers to a DHS proposed
  rule as of October 2026 on OPT fees ($70,000 for a first OPT application and
  $30,000 for later ones, p.5 of the Snapshot). I did not open either
  document. The Snapshot treats the proposed fee as pending.
- **Snapshot citation oddity.** The methodology page cites "(IIE, 2027a)" for
  the Open Doors report it compares against. The context makes it Open Doors
  2026; I treat it as a typo and am not relying on it.
- **Established in full:** the total, level, OPT and new-enrollment series for
  2015/16 to 2025/26 from IIE tables; the Snapshot's figures, sample, dates
  and reported reasons; the 2025 backtest; India and China by level; OPT rules.

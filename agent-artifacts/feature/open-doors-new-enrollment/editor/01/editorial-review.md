# Editorial review: feature/open-doors-new-enrollment (editor/01)

## Correct

Thesis, from the draft alone: IIE's Open Doors total adds enrolled students and graduates on OPT, and in 2025/26 the enrolled part fell 8.3% while OPT rose 14.1%, so the total fell only 2.7%. Under it:

1. The enrolled fall is a graduate fall, master's above all (graduate -65,403 of the enrolled -73,135; master's -22.4%, doctoral +3.1%).
2. India holds both movements: 94% of the world graduate fall and 93% of the OPT rise. India's OPT gain of 38,461 equals 52.6% of the enrolled fall.
3. OPT lags new enrollment and IIE expects it to fall. The tables cannot say how fast, and the ratio of OPT to new graduates two years earlier is not fixed.
4. The Fall 2026 Snapshot, a survey of 809 institutions, has every level falling, OPT included.

Every count was checked against IIE's own files, reopened today: the overall enrollment workbook, the academic-level workbook (both sheets), the new-enrollment workbook, the census tables workbook (Table 8), Fast Facts, the Key Findings page, the FAQ, the Fall 2026 Snapshot full report (PDF pulled through the widen viewer, printed pp. 2 to 9, and Figures 2 and 7 looked at as images), the Fall 2025 Snapshot key findings PDF, and 8 CFR 214.2(f)(10) on Cornell LII. Every figure in the article matches its table. Each percentage was recomputed from its own two counts and period: -2.7% (31,603 / 1,177,766), -8.3% (73,135 / 883,513), +14.1%, -22.4%, +3.1%, -14.5%, -25.6%, India -34.4% and +26.8%, STEM OPT +31.1%, post-completion +4.5%, 1.61 and 1.91. Table 8's WORLD TOTAL row equals the academic-level sheet, so the subtracted "other places" figures (+2,066 graduate, +3,507 OPT) are correct, and Table 8 has no unknown-origin row hiding in them, only 13 stateless students.

Headline arithmetic. 73,135 is the right denominator: it is the enrolled fall in IIE's overall table and Table 3 (883,513 to 810,378). "Offset" is fair only as arithmetic on a sum. The 38,461 and the 73,135 are changes in two different stocks, and the draft says itself that Open Doors does not follow anybody between them. In a headline the word reads as people moving from one category to the other. The sentence in the body ("equals 52.6% of") is exact and stays. The headline went, for this reason and because it repeated the paper's formula (below).

Breaks and fixes:
- The Fall 2025 backtest said the Snapshot was "milder than the census on the total and on undergraduates". It was milder on every level that fell in the census: graduate -12% against -13.4%, new enrollment -17% against -17.7%, non-degree -17% against -17.6%. The researcher's limits note named only two lines, and the draft copied it. Rewritten to the complete statement from the table's own figures.

- The Snapshot citation pointed at IIE's publication landing page while its locators are page numbers in the report. It now points at the full report IIE links from that page (iie.widen.net), which opens the document.
- The IIE qualifiers sentence (2% from proclamation countries, 22%) had no citation. Cited to the Snapshot, pp. 8 and 9.
- Fig. 3's box for leaving the count rested on the FAQ, which the caption did not cite. Cited now.
- The opt-lag heading, "OPT holds the graduate cohorts that entered earlier", stated as the paper's finding a cohort link that the section says the tables cannot show. It is IIE's claim. Retitled "OPT is the part IIE expects to fall next".

Fig. 3 audit. The drawing showed one pipe in which every enrolled student passes into OPT, labelled "finish a degree, start work". OPT is optional, and IIE's FAQ describes it as students who "stay on after their studies". The arrow now reads "some stay on after a degree", and the caption says not every student takes OPT. It carries no projected numbers. The top box is graduate-only while the boxes below hold all levels, and the caption frames it as a graduate student's path, which is accurate.

Kinds: all ten sources are primary. Nine are IIE's own files about IIE's own census and survey. The tenth is the regulation's text, reproduced by Cornell LII, which I checked against the record's eCFR reading. No independent secondary is claimed anywhere.

Hrefs: all ten return 200 and land on the document as printed. The Key Findings anchor exists on the page.

## Reads well

What went:
- The old headline repeated the recent record's formula, a fraction set against a second quantity ("more than half the fall"). The TOP500 dek two days ago also says "more than half". Replaced.
- The old dek was built as "X includes Y, so a smaller figure hides a larger one". That is the TOP500 dek's ", so" construction, and it also restated the 8.3% that the new headline now carries.
- "A first guess is that ... The enrolled part did not wait." The guess was the commission's angle with nobody's name on it, in a borrowed shape: the voice guide describes Evans's order of old guess, then mechanism. Rewritten in the article's own terms: OPT holds earlier entrants, so a drop reaches it late, and the enrolled part took it at once.
- "No source cited here apportions the fall among them." The article narrating itself. Now: "IIE does not say how much of the fall each reason explains."
- The definition of "enrolled" came after its first use, as a stray last sentence. It is now folded into the sentence where the word first appears.

What lifted:
- The opening now names Open Doors and calls it a census on first mention. A reader arriving from a link met "one total" without the report's name.
- "By subtraction" is on the other-places sentence, so the body says how the number was made, as the caption already did.
- The article used to end on the causes caveat, the weakest place to stop. The Snapshot section now runs method and backtest, then institutions' reasons, then the conclusion the piece built: OPT rose every census year from 2022/23 to 2025/26, and in the Snapshot it falls with every other level, leaving no part of the total to offset the rest. That is a closing statement, not the two-period figure the writer was told to avoid.

The draft's register matched the guide: dry and checkable, with qualifications that say what was not found. It needed trimming at the edges and nothing flatter to lift.

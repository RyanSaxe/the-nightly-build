## The story

The Institute of International Education (IIE) released Open Doors 2026 on
16 November 2026 (https://opendoorsdata.org/open-doors-2026-annual-data-release/),
covering international students in the US for academic year 2025/26, together
with its Fall 2026 Snapshot, a preliminary survey of roughly 800 institutions.
Scouting from secondary coverage (unconfirmed until the researcher opens IIE's
own tables and key-findings PDFs): new international enrollment fell about
17.7% in 2025/26, the second consecutive annual fall; the total was about
1.15 million; the Fall 2026 Snapshot reports total international students down
about 9% and new enrollment down about 15%, graduate students down about 15%,
OPT participants down about 6%. NAFSA published a Fall 2026 International
Student Economic Snapshot with a spending estimate. Treat every figure here as
unconfirmed.

Why now: the release is six days old, and the Snapshot is the first count of
the 2026/27 year.

## The angle to test

Open Doors' headline total counts students on Optional Practical Training
(OPT), who have finished their studies and are working, alongside enrolled
students. A total that includes OPT reflects new enrollment from one to three
years earlier, so it lags. The piece should show readers what the total is
made of (undergraduate, graduate, non-degree, OPT), which part has fallen, and
what two consecutive years of falling new enrollment imply for the total in the
next years, since each entering cohort stays several years.

Candidate original work, to be confirmed or killed by the evidence:
1. Decompose the change in the total between 2023/24, 2024/25 and 2025/26 (and
   the Fall 2026 Snapshot where comparable) into contributions by component,
   from IIE's own tables.
2. A cohort arithmetic: using IIE's new-enrollment series and typical program
   lengths (master's ~2 years, bachelor's ~4, OPT 1–3 years with STEM
   extension), show how a fall in new enrollment passes into the total with a
   lag. Only do this if every input is sourced; state assumptions plainly.
3. Breakdown by place of origin (India and China especially) or by field, if
   the tables show the fall concentrated in one place.

Disconfirming evidence to look for: the Snapshot and Open Doors census are
different instruments (survey of ~800 institutions vs full census), so a
percentage from one is not directly comparable with the other; IIE's own
caveats on that; SEVIS by the Numbers (DHS/ICE) counts, which may disagree;
any source attributing the fall to a cause (visa pauses, the duration-of-status
rule, the social-media vetting, travel bans): attribute causes only to who
claims them, and give IIE's survey of institutions' reported reasons as what
institutions report, not as established cause.

## Decisions after research

- The angle is the decomposition. In 2025/26 OPT rose 14.1% while the
  enrolled part (undergraduate, graduate, non-degree) fell 8.3%, so the total
  fell 2.7%. Indian graduate students account for about 94% of the graduate
  fall, and Indian OPT for about 93% of the OPT rise: one population moved from
  one component into the other. The Fall 2026 Snapshot shows OPT turning down
  (-6%) for the first time since 2021/22. That is the piece.
- IIE itself says OPT grew because of earlier graduate growth and that OPT will
  likely keep falling. Credit IIE with the lag point; the article's own work is
  the component and origin decomposition, which IIE does not print.
- Drop the cohort projection (candidate 2). The OPT/new-graduate ratio drifts
  from 1.61 to 1.91 and a constant-ratio projection contradicts the Snapshot.
  A flow drawing of how students pass from new enrollment through the enrolled
  stock into OPT is allowed as an explanatory mechanism, labelled as the
  paper's illustration, with no projected numbers.
- Snapshot vs census: the Snapshot covers 809 institutions (~60% of students).
  The researcher's backtest of the Fall 2025 Snapshot against the 2025/26
  census (within 2.3 points at every level) may be stated as the paper's
  check, not as IIE's claim. Do not compute a combined Snapshot total beyond
  saying it is the researcher's arithmetic if used at all.
- NAFSA: the current November figure ("over $2.4 billion", "over 31,000 fewer
  jobs"), attributed, at most one sentence, noting it excludes OPT; or omit.
- Causes: institutions' reported reasons only, attributed to the Snapshot.

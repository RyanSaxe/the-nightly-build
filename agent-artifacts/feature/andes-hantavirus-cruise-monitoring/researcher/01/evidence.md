# Evidence record: feature/andes-hantavirus-cruise-monitoring (researcher 01)

The record supports the funnel and the zero-of-88 parts of the angle in full and changes the "why 42 days" part. The US contact funnel is documented line by line in MMWR Table 2 and its Supplementary Figure: 28 cruise passengers and 60 flight-only contacts, giving 88; 40 were high risk and 48 low risk. The Supplementary Figure also gives the ship's head count at every port. The 42-day window was **not** derived from a fitted incubation distribution before it was imposed. CDC calls it "the upper end of the known ANDV incubation period". WHO and ECDC call it the ceiling of a "1–6 weeks" range. The citation trail behind "42" runs to a 2014 paper that quotes a 2006 study as "range 7–42" when that study reports 7–39. WHO first advised 45 days (4 May), switched to 42 by 8 May, and only on 28 May published a model estimate (mean 22 days; 96% of infected contacts symptomatic by day 42, 91% by day 35), which it said "reaffirms" the rule. The UK held 45 days until 2 June. So the window began as a convention and was backed by an estimate afterwards. The writer should report that sequence and draw WHO's estimate against the cutoff. On the null result: zero of 88 gives a one-sided 95% upper bound of about 3.4% on the per-contact attack rate (the paper's own arithmetic). That is the same as the household attack rate measured prospectively in Chile (16 of 476, 3.4%), so the US result cannot tell "no risk" apart from household-level risk. The record is thin in three places. The NEJM Epuyén paper is paywalled and was not read in full, so its incubation and serial-interval figures come through two documents that cite it: its own authors' 2022 follow-up paper and a UKHSA systematic review. WHO did not publish the method or parameters behind its 22-day mean. And the dates of the index case's symptom onset disagree across primaries (3 April vs 6 April).

Exact definitions, MMWR Table 1. **High risk**: on board the affected ship any time from 3 April (index patient's onset) through the exposed cohort's disembarkation; or "yes" to any exposure question (direct contact with a symptomatic person or body fluids, shared living or sleeping space, shared personal items, caregiving, ≤6 ft for ≥15 cumulative minutes); or on an aircraft with a symptomatic person and seated in the same row or within two rows in front or behind. Measures: twice-daily monitoring for fever and symptoms for 42 days after last exposure, modified activities, travel coordinated with public health, immediate isolation and evaluation if symptomatic. **Low risk**: on an aircraft with an infected person, seated outside the high-risk zone. Measures: daily symptom monitoring for 42 days, no activity or travel restrictions, jurisdictional coordination for travel, immediate isolation if symptomatic. Aircraft crew and anyone who assisted the patient were assessed individually.

Corresponding author as printed: Ozair H. Naqvi, PhD, CDC Hantavirus Response and Epidemic Intelligence Service, CDC.

## Contradictions

**The 42-day window was set before any estimate of where 42 falls in the distribution.** The commission asks for the distribution "the authorities used". No authority published a fitted distribution before adopting 42 days. The sequence:

- 4 May: WHO DON599 advises active symptom monitoring for 45 days and says symptoms can appear "as late as eight weeks".
- 6 May: ECDC threat brief says incubation 10 days to six weeks and recommends six weeks of self-quarantine.
- By 8 May: WHO DON600 switches to 42 days. CDC HAN 528 uses 4–42 days.
- 9 May: ECDC rapid advice adopts 42 days, Day 0 = 6 May, stating "seven to 42 days (one to six weeks)" from Martínez 2020 and Vial 2006.
- 14 May: CDC interim guidance: incubation 4–42, median 18, no citation.
- 15/17 May: WHO contact guidance: incubation "1-6 weeks", median 18.
- 28 May: WHO DON604 publishes the only quantitative coverage statement (mean 22; 96% by 42 days; 91% by 35) and says it "reaffirms" 42 days.
- 2 June: UKHSA drops its own 45-day isolation to 42 "in line with WHO guidance".
- MMWR (5 Nov) describes 42 days as "the upper end of the known ANDV incubation period".

**The number 42 does not appear in the data it is cited to.** The MMWR cites Martínez 2020 and Martinez-Valdebenito 2014 for "4–42 days". Martinez-Valdebenito 2014 cites Vial 2006 for "range 7–42" and recommends 42 days. Vial 2006 reports 7–39 days, a median of 18, and no 42. The Epuyén range reported by its own lab is 9–40 days (Iglesias 2022, citing the NEJM paper). UKHSA's review also finds 9–40 across all person-to-person studies. The longest observed ANDV incubations in the record are 39 days (rodent exposure) and 40 days (person-to-person). 42 is six weeks, the round ceiling just above them. The NEJM paper itself could not be checked, so whether it states 42 anywhere is unknown.

**Agencies cite the same NEJM paper for different upper bounds.** CDC (MMWR ref 1) cites Martínez 2020 for 4–42 days. ECDC cites it with Vial for 7–42 days. PHAC cites it for "1 and 8 weeks". Iglesias 2022 (same lab) cites it for 9–40 days.

**Agencies used different windows for the same exposure.** WHO used 45 days on 4 May and then 42. The UK used 45 days until 2 June. ECDC, CDC and WHO used 42. Germany, Switzerland and Greece are reported in press coverage as using 45 days; no primary for those three was read, so do not state it as fact.

**Median incubation figures conflict.** CDC and WHO guidance say median 18 days, which matches Vial 2006 (rodent exposure, n=20). WHO's model says mean 22 days. Iglesias 2022 says Epuyén median 26 days (range 9–40) and a pooled normal fit with mean 22. UKHSA's own calculation from Epuyén raw data gives mean 21.6 (SD 7.0). So the median CDC quotes (18) comes from rodent-to-human data, while the person-to-person data center about 4 days later.

**WHO's 96%-at-42 implies a fatter tail than the Epuyén data alone.** A gamma with Epuyén's moments (mean 21.6, SD 7.0, from UKHSA) puts about 0.8% of incubations beyond 42 days and 4.2% beyond 35 (paper's arithmetic). WHO's figures put 4% beyond 42 and 9% beyond 35, which needs an SD of about 9–10 days at a mean of 22. WHO pooled Argentine outbreaks with the 13 Hondius cases but published neither the method nor the cruise-case incubation data. The difference cannot be traced further. "Probability of safe release" is WHO's wording. The record reads it as the share of infected contacts who would have become symptomatic by that day, but WHO does not define it.

**Index case onset date: 3 April vs 6 April.** MMWR (p.634, citing NEJM letter ref 6) and MMWR Table 1 use 3 April. The NEJM letter itself says 6 April. WHO DON599 and DON600, the CDC interim guidance (14 May) and the CDC risk assessment (29 May) all use 6 April. WHO's final epi curve (DON611 Figure 1) shows the probable case's onset bar at about 3 April, and Guo et al. 2026 cite DON611 for 3 April. CDC's high-risk window start therefore moved from 6 April (guidance) to 3 April (MMWR). It changes no one's classification, because everyone boarded on 1 April. The MMWR's citation for 3 April does not support it.

**Case 3 onset: 21 April (NEJM letter) vs 24 April (WHO DON599, DON600).**

**"Seven initial" vs "13 total".** The seven are WHO's 4 May count: 2 confirmed and 5 suspected. One of the five suspected cases (sent to Germany) tested negative and became a non-case (DON600). So 6 of the original 7 are among the final 13. Seven more were added later: Switzerland and Tristan da Cunha (DON600), France and Spain (DON601), Canada, Netherlands, Spain (DON604). The final split is 12 confirmed and 1 probable (case 1, never tested). The MMWR's "six additional ANDV outbreak cases… for a total of 13" is correct on the headline counts but not person by person. It also dates 13 to "By June 2", although WHO had 13 as of 27 May. A US "inconclusive" case counted in DON601 (11 cases) was removed on 15 May. The MMWR describes the same person as an asymptomatic passenger with an indeterminate respiratory-swab PCR from a Netherlands lab, evaluated at the Region 7 RESPTC and found negative.

**26 vs 28 US passengers.** The CDC risk assessment (29 May) gives 26, "informed through IHR reporting… when it originally left Argentina", and its own breakdown (7 + 18) sums to 25. The MMWR (5 Nov) gives 28 (7 home before 2 May, 3 abroad, 18 at Tenerife), and the Supplementary Figure shows "28 U.S. residents embark" at Ushuaia. The MMWR says contacts were located "Through IHR notifications and information from federal partners, state and local health departments, and passengers". The likely reading is that the early IHR list was incomplete and the 3 who stayed abroad were missing from it, but no primary says so. The MMWR's "26" (serology negatives) is a different quantity.

**Ship population: 147 vs 149; 34 vs 30 vs 32 disembarked.** WHO and CDC give 147 aboard at notification. The ECDC 9 May advice gives "149 passengers and crew" and "30 passengers disembarked on 24 April in St. Helena". WHO DON600 gives 34 previously disembarked (ship operator). CDC's Supplementary Figure shows 149 after St Helena (32 off, 1 on), 147 after Ascension (2 medevac), and 1 off at Tristan. 32 + 2 = 34 matches WHO's count by 2 May. ECDC's 149 is the post-St Helena figure.

**Global contact counts by scope.** WHO DON611: 653 (317 high, 336 low) in 33 countries and territories. WHO DON604 (22 May): ">600" in 32 at 53% high and 47% low. ECDC Sept 2026: "237 contacts from 15 countries", unexplained. The likely reading is an EU/EEA-reported subset, but ECDC does not say so. US: 88 (40 high, 48 low).

**Low-risk handling differs.** WHO and ECDC: low-risk contacts self-monitor passively with no follow-up. CDC: daily monitoring by health departments for low-risk ("standard monitoring") contacts. CDC's flight high-risk zone changed during the response from seat-based (within two seats) to five rows (MMWR Table 1 footnote §). ECDC traced only flights longer than 6 hours.

**Case-fatality rate.** CDC MMWR: "approximately 40%". CDC risk assessment: "30-40%". WHO DON604: "40-50%". ECDC threat brief: "20% to 35%". Ferrés 2007 (Chile): 37%. Iglesias 2022 (Epuyén): 32.3%. Martínez 2020 abstract: 11 deaths in 34 (32%). This outbreak: 3 of 13 (23%). The commission's "~40%" is CDC's figure. The writer should give the range and name who says what.

**"Declared over" date.** The ECDC event page says WHO declared it over on 2 July. DON611 (2 July) says "no longer poses a public health risk" and "confirms outbreak containment", without the word "over". ECDC's September advice says "declared over at the end of June 2026".

**What the null result shows, and what breaks the commission's framing of it.** The global cohort was not clean. Several of the 13 cases were found among monitored contacts after they left the ship. Canada's case developed symptoms during follow-up, the Netherlands and Spain cases were found by weekly testing (DON604), and France and Spain were detected at repatriation (DON601). The epi curve shows onsets up to about 1 June. What no authority found was onward transmission from a case to anyone off the ship (DON611). Zero of 88 is a US-only result, and it cannot separate "no risk" from household-level risk (see Numbers). The MMWR says the same thing in its own discussion.

## Limits

- The NEJM Epuyén paper (Martínez 2020) is paywalled (HTTP 403 on article and appendix; CONICET repository copy restricted). Only its abstract was read. Its incubation distribution, serial interval and generation count come from UKHSA's extraction and Iglesias 2022, not the paper itself.
- The number of transmission generations in Epuyén was not established. UKHSA records "4 transmission chains linked to 1 index case", which is not a generation count.
- The "21 of 33 secondary cases (64%)" super-spreader share appears only in a news summary and was not verified against the paper. Do not use it.
- WHO did not publish the method, distribution family or parameters behind "mean 22 days, 96% at 42, 91% at 35". The curve in Numbers is a reconstruction that matches WHO's two points. It is not WHO's curve.
- No primary gives individual incubation periods for the 13 Hondius cases. Exposure dates aboard were uncertain, which is why ECDC and CDC dated last exposure to evacuation or disembarkation.
- The commission's question "what share of infected contacts a 35- or 42-day window would still miss" can be answered only from WHO's estimate (9% and 4%) or from the paper's own fits. No empirical case beyond 40 days was found.
- Who first set 42 days for ANDV: the earliest document found is Martinez-Valdebenito 2014. Chilean and Argentine national protocols were not read. A secondary search suggests Argentina used day 45 for follow-up, not confirmed.
- No primary explains the 26 → 28 US passenger revision or ECDC's 237-contact figure.
- No primary publishes the ship's attack rate. The 7.1% is the paper's own arithmetic on CDC's head counts.
- Germany, Switzerland and Greece using 45 days is reported only in press coverage. No primary was read.
- CDC's interim-guidance text read is the version "as of May 14". The later five-row aircraft revision the MMWR mentions was not found as a separate document.

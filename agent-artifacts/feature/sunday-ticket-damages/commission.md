## Topic and angle

The Ninth Circuit's published opinion of 30 October 2026 in the NFL Sunday Ticket
antitrust case (No. 24-5493, In re National Football League's "Sunday Ticket"
Antitrust Litigation; primary at
https://cdn.ca9.uscourts.gov/datastore/opinions/2026/10/30/24-5493.pdf).

Provisional angle, to be tested by the researcher against the opinion itself: the
court reinstated the subscribers' case (reversing the district court's exclusion
of their economists and its judgment for the NFL) but held that the jury's
damages, about $4.61 billion to the residential class and about $96.9 million to
the commercial class, rest on the wrong quantity. Both awards appear to equal the
class size times $191.26, which is the 2018–19 list price minus the expert's
average price actually paid, a measure of discounting and not of an overcharge
against a competitive price. The piece shows that arithmetic, then what the
reinstated models do measure (the one-more-competitor model and the "NFL tax"
model, and the college-football yardstick), and what happens next.

The researcher must confirm from the opinion's text, with page locators: the
holding on each issue, the disposition (affirm/reverse/vacate/remand), how the
court characterises the $191.26 figure, the panel and author, and any dissent or
concurrence. If the opinion does not support "discount, not overcharge", the angle
becomes whatever the opinion actually holds; report that clearly at the top of
evidence.md and I will re-decide.

Cite the opinion first. Trial-court orders (the 2024 verdict and the order setting
it aside), the jury verdict form, and expert reports where they can be opened, are
the next layer. Press coverage gives context only, never a number. Record the
NFL's and the plaintiffs' stated responses and any rehearing petition, and the
NFL's own description of the antitrust exemption (Sports Broadcasting Act) fairly
and in its own words.

## Decisions after research (orchestrator)

- Angle confirmed: the opinion itself says the $191.26 "reflected a discount, not an
  overcharge" (p. 32). Attribute the reading to the court, and say the court
  adopted it from the district court's 1 August 2024 order, which took it from the
  NFL's post-trial motion. The court says the awards are "almost certainly" the
  product; the jury stated no method. Do not say both awards equal the product
  exactly: the commercial award differs by 2 cents.
- State the plaintiffs' position fairly: the residential award falls inside the
  range of the experts' estimates, and they argued that should sustain it; both
  courts rejected that.
- Any per-subscription competitive price is the article's own derivation from the
  opinion's percentages and must be labelled as derived in the prose and caption.
- The plaintiffs did not comment (per Reuters); say so if their response is
  mentioned. No rehearing petition had been reported as of 5 November; do not
  state a deadline as fact unless the evidence cites a rule for it, and then
  attribute the computation.

# Commission

## Assignment

- Produce one Feature article on New York's September 24, 2026 suit against Polymarket US.
- Explain why federal designation as a derivatives exchange does not, by itself, settle whether states may apply gambling law to event contracts.
- Use the complaint as a current case study, not as proof that New York's allegations are true.

## Editorial thesis and structure

- Orient the reader with the product actually at issue, the state allegations, and Polymarket's answer.
- Trace the three layers that can attach to one contract: CFTC market oversight, state gambling rules, and consumer-protection obligations.
- Explain the preemption dispute through the strongest available arguments and current court record, including related Kalshi litigation where it clarifies rather than distracts.
- End with the operational question that courts must answer: which product features or federal approvals displace which state rules, for which contracts and users.

## Source plan and form

- Use at least eight sources, led by the New York complaint/announcement, CFTC rules and orders, relevant statutes and court decisions, Polymarket's response, and careful independent reporting.
- Use an orientation plus three to five argument-bearing sections. Avoid yesterday's date-led opening, seven-field proposal/table form, and “passes a concrete test” close.
- Attribute every allegation and contested legal conclusion. Resolved policy: researcher capable/high, writer capable/medium, editor inherit/high.

# Evidence: unbiased/birthright-citizenship (01)

The record strongly supports stating the contested question, mapping each side to
the exact words of the Citizenship Clause and the precedents it leans on, and
weighing precedent honestly. It is solid on the constitutional text, on the two
19th-century precedents (Wong Kim Ark and Elk v. Wilkins) with both majority and
dissent read in full, on Executive Order 14160 and its litigation path (Trump v.
CASA 2025, the New Hampshire class injunction, and the Supreme Court's merits
ruling in Trump v. Barbara, June 30, 2026), and on the drafters' statements each
camp relies on. Both positions are available in their strongest primary form: the
Barbara majority (Roberts) for Position One and the Thomas and Alito dissents, plus
credentialed scholars (Lash, Wurman, Eastman), for Position Two. The evidence is
lopsided on precedent, and honestly so: Position One rests on 127 years of Wong Kim
Ark, uniform lower-court injunctions, and a Supreme Court that struck the order,
while Position Two is a serious originalist minority reading now rejected by the
Court but advanced in good faith. Two things are thinner than they look and are
flagged below: (1) the vote is 6-3 on the judgment but 5-4 on the constitutional
question, because Justice Kavanaugh concurred only on statutory grounds and
expressly rejected the constitutional holding; and (2) the drafters' floor
statements (Howard, Trumbull, Cowan) were read as quoted verbatim inside the
Supreme Court primaries and the scholars' debate, not from the Congressional Globe
page images directly. The freshest development, the two new August 6, 2026 orders,
is confirmed by the White House page and reputable reporting but is untested: no
court has ruled on it as of August 11, 2026.

## Timely anchor (as of 11 August 2026)

- **Firmest anchor (confirmed primary).** In *Trump v. Barbara*, No. 25-365, 609
  U.S. ___ (June 30, 2026), the Supreme Court held Executive Order 14160 facially
  unconstitutional: children born in the United States to parents unlawfully or
  temporarily present are "subject to the jurisdiction" of the United States and
  are citizens at birth. It affirmed the New Hampshire district court's class-wide
  preliminary injunction.
- **Injunction posture.** *Trump v. CASA*, No. 24A884, 606 U.S. 831 (June 27,
  2025), held universal injunctions likely exceed federal courts' equitable
  authority and partially stayed the earlier injunctions, but expressly did **not**
  decide the Citizenship Clause merits. Nationwide protection then ran through a
  certified class in *Barbara v. Trump* (D.N.H., July 10, 2025), which the Supreme
  Court's merits ruling later affirmed.
- **Freshest development (confirmed but untested).** On August 6, 2026, President
  Trump signed two new, narrower orders — "Continuing to Protect the Meaning and
  Value of American Citizenship" and a companion "birth tourism" order — targeting
  narrower categories (children of "alien enemy"/foreign-terrorist-organization
  parents, foreign-government/embassy employees, commercial birth-tourism and
  surrogacy arrangements, and certain territorial births). Reputable reporting
  (NPR, SCOTUSblog) documents them and anticipates imminent litigation. As of
  August 11, 2026 no court has ruled on the August 6 orders and they are not yet in
  the Federal Register. **Recommendation:** anchor the piece on *Barbara* (the
  confirmed merits ruling that reopened and then decided the fight); treat the
  August 6 orders as the live sequel, described exactly, without asserting any
  litigation outcome that does not yet exist.
- **One correction to loose framing.** EO 14160 was signed **January 20, 2025**
  (90 Fed. Reg. 8449), not 2026; one machine summary of the NPR piece misdated it.
  The primary sources fix the date firmly.

## Contradictions

- **The core textual split (the whole dispute).** The Barbara majority reads
  "subject to the jurisdiction thereof" as the Nation's power to govern those
  within its territory (Schooner Exchange), so anyone amenable to U.S. law is
  covered; the Thomas and Alito dissents and scholars Lash/Wurman/Eastman read it,
  through Elk's "completely subject ... owing them direct and immediate
  allegiance" and the Fuller dissent's "not subject to any foreign power" gloss, as
  requiring complete allegiance tied to domicile. Same eight words, opposite
  readings. Record both at full strength.
- **The Howard sentence.** Position One reads "This will not, of course, include
  persons born in the United States who are foreigners, aliens, who belong to the
  families of ambassadors" as a restrictive clause excluding only diplomat families
  (Wydra, Frost). Position Two reads "foreigners, aliens" as a separate excluded
  category (Lash, Wurman; Thomas/Alito dissents). Both quote the same sentence.
  This grammatical disagreement is load-bearing and must be shown, not resolved by
  the house.
- **Scope of Wong Kim Ark.** The Barbara majority says Wong Kim Ark's reasoning
  "cannot be squared with a domicile requirement" and settles the question; the
  Alito dissent says the actual holding was limited to domiciled residents, so the
  application to unlawfully or temporarily present parents "was not squarely
  decided" and the order survives under it. This is the sharpest precedent
  contradiction and it cuts to whether Position Two is precedent-compliant.
- **Elk v. Wilkins.** Position Two treats Elk's allegiance gloss as controlling for
  alien parents; Position One (and the district court) treats Elk as a
  tribal-sovereignty carve-out that Wong Kim Ark distinguished, so it does not reach
  alien parents. Same case, opposite work.
- **The vote is less lopsided on the Constitution than on the judgment.** Headlines
  and one secondary say 6-3. On the judgment striking EO 14160 that is right. But
  the constitutional holding drew only five votes: Kavanaugh concurred solely on
  statutory grounds (8 U.S.C. Section 1401(a)) and wrote "the Executive Order does
  not violate the Fourteenth Amendment," joining the three dissenters on the
  constitutional question. So on the pure constitutional merits it was 5-4. Do not
  report the constitutional question as 6-3.
- **CASA did not decide the merits.** Trump v. CASA (2025) is about injunctions,
  not the Citizenship Clause; conflating it with the merits ruling (Barbara, 2026)
  would misstate the record. The commission's own framing ("post-CASA
  nationwide-injunction posture") is correct only if the merits ruling is kept
  distinct.
- **Precedent is genuinely lopsided, and the record says so.** 127 years of Wong
  Kim Ark, uniform lower-court injunctions, a Supreme Court that struck the order,
  and the pre-2025 practice of every administration point one way. The
  contradictions above are real disagreements within that reality, not symmetry.
  Position Two's strongest honest claim is a serious originalist minority reading
  (three Justices; credentialed scholars) that the "as-applied to unlawfully or
  temporarily present parents" question was thinner in precedent than Position One
  asserts. Give it that, and no more.
- **One secondary misdated the original order** to "January 2026"; the primaries
  (EO 14160; Barbara) fix it at January 20, 2025. Trust the primary.

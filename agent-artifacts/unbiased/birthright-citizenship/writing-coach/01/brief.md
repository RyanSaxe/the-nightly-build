Genre: a two-position, no-verdict analysis of a contested question of constitutional
law (does the Fourteenth Amendment guarantee near-universal birthright citizenship?)
for a technically literate reader who wants each side at full strength. The voice
presents opposing constitutional arguments fairly and precisely, never tipping the
house's hand, while staying concrete about the text ("subject to the jurisdiction
thereof"), the precedents (Wong Kim Ark, Elk v. Wilkins), and the drafters' debates.
Study writers who steelman both sides of a hard constitutional question in plain,
first-principles prose, and who can state honestly that precedent favors one side
while still giving the other its strongest form. Avoid exemplars that argue a side
of the birthright-citizenship or immigration debate; quote for the even-handed,
precise legal voice, not a position.

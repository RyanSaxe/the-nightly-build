# Commission: unbiased/mail-in-voting-order

## Assignment

An Unbiased desk on the disagreement made timely by the Supreme Court's order of
August 24, 2026 in the mail-in-voting case (docketed as Trump v. California,
No. 26A124), which stayed a lower court's injunction and let President Trump's
executive order restricting mail-in voting take effect for the November 3, 2026
midterm elections while litigation continues. Present the strongest
evidence-backed case for each of two genuinely contested answers to one
question. The paper takes no side and adds no house verdict.

## The question and the two positions

REFRAMED after research (important): the August 24 order is jurisdictional. The
Court stayed the district-court injunction on standing/ripeness grounds and
expressly did not decide the merits of presidential authority; the majority
wrote its disposition "does not mean that any measure taken by the Government to
implement the Order will necessarily be lawful." Critically, NO named holder
defends the *merits* of presidential power over mail-ballot mechanics: the
Solicitor General argued only prematurity, and every court to reach the merits
held the President lacks the authority. So the piece must NOT be framed as a
merits fight over presidential power (Position A would have no honest champion).

Frame instead around what the order and the stay actually put in dispute now:
**Should the courts have let President Trump's mail-in-voting order take effect
for the November election while its legality is still unresolved?** This is a
single genuinely contested question with two named-holder-backed sides:

- Position A — the challenge is premature; the stay was right. The order is an
  executive directive that has not yet been enforced against anyone through a
  final rule, so the states' pre-enforcement suit is unripe and courts should not
  enjoin a presidential election directive before concrete enforcement.
  Named holders: the per curiam majority; Solicitor General D. John Sauer's stay
  application (prematurity/justiciability); the intervenor states. The EO's own
  Article II assertion may be quoted as the executive's stated basis, labeled as
  an assertion, not an adjudicated holding.
- Position B — block it now; the merits are clear and the harm is immediate.
  Every court to reach the merits held the President lacks authority over the
  "manner" of federal elections, which the Elections Clause commits to the states
  and Congress; letting an order no court has upheld shape a midterm inflicts
  concrete, unredressable harm on voters and states. Named holders: Justices
  Sotomayor (joined by Kagan) and Jackson in dissent; Judge Indira Talwani's
  summary-judgment ruling; California AG Rob Bonta; the Brennan Center.

Both answer the one question — whether the order should be allowed to operate now
given this posture — so equal scrutiny applies to both. Each position carries the
split's four orienting components (name, thesis, argument, champion) with a real
named holder and direct cited statements. Do not present the stay as a merits
victory for presidential power.

### Factual precision the writer must honor

- Not "nationwide": the stayed injunction (Judge Talwani) covered the 23
  plaintiff states and D.C. A separate nationwide injunction (League of Women
  Voters suit) and a new August 28 TRO still block the order, so it is not in
  nationwide effect. Say precisely that the stay removed one block while others
  remain.
- Confirm the USPS final-rule date against the primary before printing it
  (sources place it the Friday before the ruling).

is in the United States; position holders and evidence may come from anywhere.

## Recent habits to break

Recent Unbiased deks are yes/no questions (Did seven data brokers break
Maryland's law...; Does "subject to the jurisdiction thereof" let the government
deny birthright citizenship...). Vary the dek from the question mold, or if a
question is genuinely the frame, make sure the piece's two positions answer it
squarely. Do not let either side's argument fall into the same fixed internal
outline; the four components are furniture, not a template for the reasoning.

## Required contribution

presidential authority over federal-election mechanics, grounded in the actual
order and the constitutional text, without being told which to hold.

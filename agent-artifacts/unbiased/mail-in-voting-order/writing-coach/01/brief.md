The genre is a scrupulously even two-position brief on a contested legal
question, presented without a house verdict. Study writers who state the
strongest version of a case they are not endorsing, in law and policy, with
precision and no tell of which side they hold (SCOTUSblog analysis, a sharp
appellate-law explainer, the best even-handed legal journalism). The register is
calm, exact, and neutral; equal scrutiny is the discipline.

This round's focus: how to write a position at full strength in its holders'
own terms without the prose leaking a preference, and how to state a legal
posture precisely (a stay versus a merits ruling). Show what an even, steelmanned
argument reads like, and how loaded framing creeps in so the writer can avoid it.

## Skeptic

The thesis is unchanged and still holds: the $1.5 billion Bartz settlement
closed only the piracy half of Alsup's split ruling, training on lawfully
acquired books was already held fair use, and the live question is whether the
piracy-derived per-work price should govern training generally. Both repairs
touch facts that feed that thesis, so I retested each against its owning source.

Repair 1, the Register-Perlmutter removal. The orientation now splits the old
single sentence in two. The report's pre-publication release "in May 2025" keeps
its own citation to the Copyright Office report (s6, primary), which owns that
fact. The removal clause — "The Register of Copyrights who signed it, Shira
Perlmutter, was removed from her post the next day" — now carries its own
citation to the new s7 (Publishers Weekly, secondary) instead of resting on the
report, which is silent on its author's later removal. I opened the s7 href as
printed. It resolves and lands on the Publishers Weekly article itself, which
states the pre-publication Part 3 report released May 9 and that Perlmutter was
fired "on Saturday" — May 10, the next calendar day. "The next day" is exact,
not rounded; the evidence record cross-confirms the one-day interval against
McDermott ("the very next day") and Rep. Morelle's same-day statement, and warns
that "within days" would understate it. The honest framing is intact: the clause
asserts only the sequence, not a cause, and the following sentence ("Each side of
this fight cites that sequence selectively, and neither treats it as the whole
story") keeps the removal as contested-standing context rather than a proven
motive. No Copyright Office or White House document is claimed to state the
removal; the article does not overreach past what the secondary reporting and
the contemporaneous political attribution support.

Repair 2, the Concord II date. The clause now reads "On August 3, 2026, Anthropic
filed a motion to dismiss two of the publishers' claims but left the torrenting
and training claims themselves in place," citing s5. This corrects both faults
editor/01 flagged: the date moves from the conflated "August 5-6" coverage date
to the August 3 filing date, and the verb is "filed," not "argued." The evidence
record fixes the filing to Monday, August 3, 2026 against the court's own docket
and calendars the hearing for November 4, 2026, so as of today the motion is
filed and unargued — the clause matches. The scope is right too: Anthropic moved
on two claims and left the torrenting and training claims standing, exactly as
the record states.

No break found in either repair. Nothing in the two changes introduces a fact
the evidence record does not own.

## Cut

I ran the slop test on the changed sentences only. Both survive. The Perlmutter
removal clause reduces to nothing without its nouns — a named official, a dated
removal, a specific sequence — so it is reporting, not pattern. The Concord II
clause is dated, specific, and carries the filing's actual scope. Grammar and
punctuation in both are clean; the split into two sentences reads plainer than
the single sentence it replaced, in keeping with the punctuation standard's
period-first default.

No new leakage. Neither changed clause borrows the brief's or commission's clause
order; both restate reported facts from the evidence record in the article's own
terms. The edits add no em-dash and no counted lexical tell. I did not re-audit
the edge sentences settled in editor/01, per the instruction not to reintroduce a
standard late; the two new sentences sit mid-paragraph, not at any edge that the
prior review had not already cleared.

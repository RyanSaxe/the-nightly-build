# researcher brief: unbiased/ai-training-copyright (01)

Inputs:
- ../../editorial-direction.md — citation standard, declared reader, series territory
- ../../commission.md — the contested question, the two positions, the strict source floor, the confirm-the-anchor-first instruction

Output: ./evidence.md

Step zero, before building the record: confirm from current original US
reporting that the fight is timely and genuinely two-sided as of mid-August
2026, and pin the freshest live hook (the newest ruling, settlement milestone,
or filing). If the copyright-remedy framing in the commission is not the live
one, record the framing the reporting actually supports. If the fight is not
two-sided or has no timely US anchor, say so in your report and name the
strongest alternative timely US disagreement you found, then stop rather than
padding a dead angle.

Then establish, from sources you open:
- The anchoring US development and its primary record: the operative court
  opinion(s) and filings, the settlement terms and the per-work valuation, and
  the exact figures (works covered, dollar amounts, class scope) with locators.
  Verify every number against the filing or settlement document that owns it.
- The legal structure of each side: the Copyright Act provisions at issue
  (the fair-use factors; statutory damages), the US Copyright Office's report on
  AI and copyright, and named legal scholars' own writing on both the
  infringement reading and the transformative-use reading. At least four primary
  sources total across filings, statute, the Copyright Office, and parties' or
  scholars' own words.
- The named champions and their theses on each side: author organizations and
  plaintiff authors; AI developers' stated legal positions; fair-use scholars;
  and economists or technologists who have argued the access/feasibility case.
  Record each champion's exact affiliation and title, and a real quoted or
  closely paraphrased statement of their position from a source they authored or
  were directly reported making.
- The economic claims each side rests on: what a per-work damages regime implies
  at scale, licensing-market feasibility, and who actually receives compensation.
  Source these to the parties or analysts who make them, not to your own
  estimate.

Contradictions must hold the strongest version of each side against the other,
since the piece presents both without a verdict. Meet the strict floor (>=10
sources, >=4 primary, >=3 secondary) with sources that carry the disagreement,
never padding. Classify every source primary or secondary with the authorship-
and-stake test, and confirm every URL resolves to the document's own page.

## The commission in one line
An Unbiased article on "Should the Fed be raising rates now, or was it right to
hold?" — the strongest cited case for each answer (raise now vs hold), named
holders, equal scrutiny, no house verdict, on the constrained two-column
`nb-divide` template.

## What to study (at least three respected sources)
Study writers who steelman opposing economic positions fairly and precisely: the
best explanatory economics journalism (e.g. the analytical register of a strong
WSJ/FT/Economist Fed writer), a rigorous both-sides explainer that never tips its
hand, and a clear monetary-policy explainer that defines terms cleanly. Extract:
how to state a position in its holders' own terms; how to keep the two sides
symmetric in force and scrutiny without false balance; how to fold a cited quote
into an argument; and how to write an orientation that arms the reader without
pre-judging.

## Output (write only this)
`agent-artifacts/unbiased/should-the-fed-hike/writing-coach/01/voice-guide.md`
Cover: neutral register that still commits each side to its strongest case; how to
define monetary-policy terms in-line for a technical-but-not-economist reader; how
each `nb-side` argument should marshal evidence (not mirror the other's outline);
handling cited holder quotes; the orientation's job; and tells to avoid (tipping
the house's hand, false symmetry, hedged-contrast dek, component-vocabulary
headings, manufactured punchlines, a smuggled conclusion). Tie each move to a
reason.

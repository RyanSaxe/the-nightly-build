## Contradictions

- **The champion-quote provenance (this round's finding).** The article as drafted
  attributes "The Constitution gives Congress – and Congress only – the power to
  declare war" to the Apr. 13, 2026 push page (s17). The live page does not carry
  that line; it carries Van Hollen's 2026 "illegal war of choice" statement and
  constitutional lines from *other* senators (Merkley, Warnock). The verbatim line
  is Van Hollen's own, but from his Feb. 13, 2020 S.J.Res.68 statement. Repoint and
  re-attribute as his standing constitutional position (see recommendation up top).

- **Anchor scope vs. the commission.** The commission's anchor ("late-July
  exchanges + a called-off Aug. 2 strike") understates the record. Live reporting
  shows a war running since Feb. 28, 2026, already three months past the WPR 60-day
  clock, with Congress having voted five-plus times. The piece should anchor on the
  ongoing use of force past the clock.

- **Binding vs. nonbinding congressional action.** Precisely: the *concurrent*
  resolutions (H.Con.Res.86 in June, H.Con.Res.89 on July 23) passed but are
  nonbinding (§1544(c); Chadha). The *binding* joint resolution that would bind the
  President (S.J.Res.180) FAILED 47-49 in the Senate on July 23. Do not write that
  Congress has "ordered" withdrawal in a legally operative sense — it has not.

- **June Senate vote date.** NPR dates the 50-48 Senate vote June 23, 2026; Al
  Jazeera datelines it June 24. Likely the same vote across the day boundary. Use
  "June 23" (NPR) or "late June."

- **June House vote date.** Reported as June 3 (NPR slug) and June 4 (one NPR
  fetch). Count is consistently 215-208. Use "early June" if the exact day matters.

- **§1541(c) as law vs. hortatory.** Position B reads §1541(c)'s three-condition
  list as the operative limit; the executive and much of the academy treat it as a
  non-binding statement of policy, with §1544(b)'s 60-day clock as the only hard
  operative constraint. Present §1541(c) as contested in its legal force, not as
  settled law.

- **Whether sustained strikes are "hostilities"/"war."** The OLC test turns on
  "prolonged and substantial military engagements ... exposure of U.S. military
  personnel to significant risk over a substantial period." Position A says each
  strike is discrete and below that line; Position B says three months of reciprocal
  strikes with 18 U.S. military deaths and an attack on a U.S. base in Jordan is
  exactly the "prolonged and substantial" engagement the OLC opinion itself concedes
  may require authorization. Present as a genuine factual dispute the legal
  conclusion turns on.

- **Context claim needing care.** Two secondary summaries state the Feb. 28 strikes
  killed Iran's Supreme Leader Ali Khamenei and name the operation "Epic Fury"; NPR
  does not use the codename. Treat the decapitation claim and codename as reported
  context, single/lightly sourced, not verified fact central to the legal argument.

- **U.S. military death toll figure varies by source and date.** NPR (Jul 23, 2026)
  records 18 total U.S. military deaths since Feb. 2026; Van Hollen's Apr. 13, 2026
  push statement (earlier in the war) cites "13 U.S. service members dead." Both are
  consistent as cumulative figures at different dates; use NPR's 18 as the latest and
  attribute Van Hollen's 13 to his April statement if his quote is used.

## Subject and timely anchor
The constitutional disagreement over whether the President may keep striking Iran
without new authorization from Congress. Recent Nightly Build coverage records the

strike on Aug 2. **The researcher must verify the current state of the conflict

ongoing use of force, not any single day's news.

## The disagreement (both positions, steelmanned)
Position A — the strikes are lawful executive action: Article II vests the
President with commander-in-chief power to use force to protect Americans and
national security from an imminent or ongoing threat; a long practice of
Presidents ordering limited strikes without a declaration (from Korea forward),
OLC opinions defending unilateral strikes below the "war in the constitutional
sense" threshold, and the War Powers Resolution's own 60-day clock as implicitly
permitting short engagements.

Position B — continued strikes require congressional authorization: Article I
gives Congress alone the power to declare war and the War Powers Resolution
(50 U.S.C. §§ 1541-1548) requires withdrawal absent a declaration, statutory
authorization, or an attack on the United States; sustained, reciprocal strikes
are "hostilities" that pass the 60-day limit; the Kaine/Lee-type war-powers
resolutions and members of both parties invoking Congress's role.

The article does not pick a winner beyond what the record forces; it makes each
side's strongest case on the primary law and lets the reader weigh them.

## Required contribution
The reader finishes understanding *why* reasonable, informed people disagree —
where the constitutional text is genuinely contested, what the War Powers
Resolution does and does not settle, and which factual question (is this "war,"
are these "hostilities," was there an "attack on the United States") each legal
conclusion turns on. Not a news recap; the disagreement is the subject.

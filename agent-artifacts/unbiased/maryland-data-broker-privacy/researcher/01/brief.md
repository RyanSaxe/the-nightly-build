# Researcher brief: unbiased/maryland-data-broker-privacy

## Question
Does selling Marylanders' phone- and vehicle-location data to law enforcement,
and to firms that assist federal immigration enforcement, violate Maryland's
Data Privacy Act (MODPA as amended by HB 711, 2026), or is it lawful conduct the
statute does not reach?

## Sourcing targets and how they were met
- The anchoring event: the consumer complaint filed 2026-08-20 with Maryland AG
  Anthony Brown. Documented by NPR (2026-08-20) and by the coalition's own
  release (We Are CASA, CAIR-MD). The complaint PDF is not publicly posted by
  any coalition member I could reach; its contents are sourced through NPR and
  the coalition releases, which name the companies, the allegations, and the
  filing's authorship (Georgetown Technology Law Clinic, Prof. Laura Moy).
- The statute (primary): HB 711 as enacted (Third Reader), which amends the
  Commercial Law Article. I extracted the exact enacted text and quote the
  operative provisions verbatim in the evidence file. The bill's legislative
  record (votes, sponsors, effective date) is a separate primary record.
- The precedent (primary): Sorrell v. IMS Health, 564 U.S. 552 (2011), the
  commercial-speech holding Position B leans on. Quoted from the Cornell LII
  syllabus.
- Federal counter-authority (primary): the Driver's Privacy Protection Act, 18
  U.S.C. 2721, which authorizes disclosure of motor-vehicle records to
  government agencies and which MODPA itself partly exempts.
- Company posture and the ICE-contract factual backbone (secondary): NPR carries
  the Penlink and Thomson Reuters denials verbatim; LawSites and Biometric
  Update document the Thomson Reuters CLEAR and LexisNexis Accurint contracts and
  the CLEAR license-plate location tools.

## What is contested versus settled
Settled on this record: the statute exists, took effect 2026-07-01, and bans
selling sensitive data and selling personal data to governmental units that
recently supported civil immigration enforcement. The companies sell cell-phone
and license-plate location data and hold contracts touching immigration
enforcement.
Contested: whether captured license-plate and cell-derived location data is
"sensitive data" / "personal data" or excluded "publicly available information";
whether the immigration-buyer bar and warrant rule reach these specific
transfers; whether a federal statute (DPPA) authorizes the motor-vehicle
disclosures; and whether the First Amendment permits a ban keyed to the buyer's
identity. No court has yet ruled on any of it; the AG has not acted.

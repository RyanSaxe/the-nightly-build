# Writer brief: unbiased/maryland-data-broker-privacy

## Assignment in one line
Write the unbiased longread: two evidence-backed answers to whether the seven
firms' location-data sales violate Maryland's Data Privacy Act, no house verdict,
1200-3500 words, strict-mode structure and citation.

## Structure the piece is built on
- Orientation (H2, named for this question): open on the dispute, not the
  filing's date. State what the complaint alleges, name AG Brown and the
  coalition, then teach the statute's operative terms so the reader can weigh both
  sides. Furniture: a table mapping each firm to what it sells and the buyer at
  issue; an rs-docket for Sorrell where the constitutional stakes enter; a short
  "note" naming the classification pivot the whole dispute turns on.
- nb-divide with exactly two nb-side sections, each four slots in order
  (camp, thesis, argument, champion). No furniture inside the sides; keep the
  four-slot contract exact.
  - Left = Position A (the complaint's side). Camp built as a short declarative.
  - Right = Position B (the defense side). Camp built as a fuller sentence, so the
    two headings differ in construction (commission requirement).
- Sources (fixed furniture).

## Beats to vary so the no-verdict close is not a stamp
Position A marches through the statutory text (flat sale ban -> immigration-buyer
bar -> the warrant-not-subpoena answer to "lawful process"). Position B runs
three distinct doctrines instead (publicly-available carve-out -> DPPA/preemption
-> First Amendment), then lands on the companies' own denials. The two sides are
therefore shaped differently, not mirror images, and the piece ends on Penlink's
concrete on-record denial rather than a symmetric hedge. The classification pivot
is set in orientation, so the ending does not need a summary paragraph.

## Citation plan (per-section rule, strict)
Every section cites. Orientation: s1 (statute), s2 (record), s3 (NPR), s5
(Sorrell), s9/s10/s11 (context). Position A: s1, s3, s4, s8, s9, s10. Position B:
s1, s3, s5, s6, s11. Legal claims carry the primary document; reporting carries
the secondary. See researcher/01/evidence.md for the footnote map.

## Title/dek and molds to break
- Title: a single neutral question stating the contested legal question, actors
  named. No blunt-statement-then-reframe two-sentence combo.
- Dek: one lean sentence adding the AG, the seven firms, the recency, and naming
  the crux phrase; no participial "leaving open" close, no semicolon reversal, no
  comma triad.

# Voice guide: unbiased/maryland-data-broker-privacy

## Register
The paper's baseline: calm, precise, argued from first principles, for a
well-read reader who can follow a statute read closely. Treat the reader as
someone comfortable with defined terms and cross-references, not a layperson who
needs the concept of a data broker explained from zero. Define the terms of art
the statute itself defines (precise geolocation, sensitive data, publicly
available information) at first use, because the whole dispute rides on them, and
assume the rest.

## How this piece should sound
Like a close reading of a new statute against a concrete factual record, not
like advocacy for either side. The persuading is done by the arrangement of the
statutory text and the evidence, not by adjectives. When a position is strong,
show the words that make it strong; when it concedes something, say what it
concedes. Neither position gets a rhetorical thumb on the scale.

## Concrete over abstract
The nouns carry this story: Webloc cell-phone pings, license-plate scans, a
1,750-foot radius, a six-month lookback, a particularized warrant, CLEAR,
Accurint. Reach for the named thing every time. "Surveillance," "privacy," and
"overreach" are the abstractions to avoid unless the sentence is building one of
them deliberately.

## Sentences
Short and single-purpose by default, varied in length. A statutory quotation
sits inside a sentence that frames what it does, never dropped in raw. Keep the
period as the default mark; the subject matter invites semicolons and em-dashes,
and most of them should be periods.

## Lines to never write here
- No "the tension here is structural," no "at its core," no "raises deeper
  questions." Run the slop test on every section's first and last sentence.
- No dueling-quotes as the engine of the piece. Quotes punctuate; the statute
  argues.
- No verdict, no "time will tell," no "the AG now faces a choice" stamp to close
  on. End on something concrete and specific to this record.
- No negative parallelism ("not privacy but power") unless it corrects a
  misconception the piece has actually named.

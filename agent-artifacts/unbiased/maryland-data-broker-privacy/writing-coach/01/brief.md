# Writing-coach brief: unbiased/maryland-data-broker-privacy

## What this piece has to be
An unbiased longread on one legal question: does selling Marylanders' phone- and
car-location data to police, and to firms that assist ICE, violate Maryland's
data-privacy statute, or is it conduct the statute does not reach? Two
evidence-backed positions, each steelmanned from the primary record, and no
house verdict. The verdict is deliberately withheld; that is the template's
form, not an omission the editor should try to fill.

## The trap to avoid
This subject has a default political script (surveillance and immigration), and
the coalition's press release supplies a ready-made narrative. Writing to that
script produces a piece that reads as an echo of the news item, which the
commission forbids. The escape is the statute's own text. The dispute is not
"privacy versus police"; it is a disagreement about how five defined terms in
one code section apply to two kinds of captured data. Argue from the words the
legislature enacted, not from sympathies.

## Where the argument actually lives
Both positions turn on classification, not on sentiment. Position A reads the
captured data into the statute's defined categories (sensitive data, precise
geolocation, personal data) and reads the immigration-buyer bar and the
warrant-not-subpoena rule to reach the sales. Position B reads the same data out
of those categories (publicly available information), reaches for a federal
statute that authorizes the disclosure (the DPPA), and raises a First Amendment
defense that a ban keyed to who the buyer is cannot survive. A reader who
finishes should be able to say which defined term decides which sub-dispute.

## Standards that bind hardest here
- Strict mode: the structure and citation gates block. Every claim the argument
  rests on carries an inline citation. Legal claims carry the primary document.
- Quote the statute; do not paraphrase it into something softer or harder than
  it says. The exact enacted language is in the researcher's evidence file.
- Steelman Position B from real doctrine (Sorrell, the DPPA, the publicly
  available carve-out), never from a strawman a privacy advocate would wave off.
- Break the recent molds the commission lists: no blunt-statement-then-reframe
  title, no participial "leaving open" dek, open on the dispute rather than the
  filing's date, and build the two side headings differently from each other.

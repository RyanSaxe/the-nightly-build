Strict-mode two-position piece on the impoundment question (may the President
refuse to spend or claw back appropriated funds?). Honor the evidence exactly:

Anchor (do not overstate the holding):
- Climate United Fund v. Citibank, N.A., No. 25-5122 (consol. 25-5123), D.C.
  Cir., en banc, per curiam, filed August 4, 2026; held 6-4 that EPA likely
  acted unlawfully in terminating/clawing back ~$20B in already-disbursed
  clean-energy grants "based solely on a policy disagreement," "likely
  contravened the IRA's mandatory appropriation."
- CRITICAL: the majority's ground is the IRA's mandatory appropriation + the
  APA, NOT the Impoundment Control Act; and the court fractured over the 2025
  OBBBA repeal, leaving future EPA authority undecided by an equally divided
  court. Do NOT put an "impoundment holding" in the court's mouth. Use it as the
  timely anchor for the durable impoundment disagreement, not as a ruling on it.

Steelman BOTH sides to parity of FORCE (not word count):
- Power-of-the-purse / anti-impoundment: Train v. City of New York (420 U.S.
  35), ICA §684 (bars policy-based deferral), 2025 GAO decisions (B-337203,
  NIH), and Rehnquist's 1969 OLC memo rejecting a general constitutional
  impoundment power. The documented legal merits favor this side — say so if
  the piece weighs, but only after presenting the other side at peak strength.
- Executive-discretion: its best-documented material is historical practice
  (Jefferson, Grant, FDR, Truman, Kennedy) and the "appropriations are a
  ceiling, not a floor" theory (Paoletta/Center for Renewing America). Present
  it as its holders would sign it.
- The genuine seam to name: even Rehnquist's memo concedes an appropriation
  "permits but does not require" spending — so the real dispute is whether
  THESE appropriations were mandates. Leave open what the record leaves open
  (the OBBBA-repeal question the court split on).

Requirements: strict template hard gates; min_sources 10; primary >=4,
secondary >=3 (the record has 8 primary / 6 secondary). Verify every case name,
date, holding, statutory cite (ICA §§683/684), and official title against the
owning primary — a misstated holding or title is the costliest error. Resolve
each citation href to the source's own page. Habits to break (recent unbiased):
avoid the "Both Sides … the Same X" and question-mark headline molds and the
comma-triad dek; the headline commits to the real tension in the piece's own
nouns. Keep the spending-power question distinct from removal power.

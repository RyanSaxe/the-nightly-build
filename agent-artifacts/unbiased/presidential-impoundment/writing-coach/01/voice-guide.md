# Voice guide: unbiased/presidential-impoundment (01)

Write in the paper's calm, first-principles register, with one discipline added:
the discipline of an advocate who must brief both chairs and mean each brief. The
reader carries constitutional doctrine and wants navigable complexity, not
simplification; define each legal term of art in the sentence it first appears,
then trust the reader with it and keep it exact thereafter.

Beyond the house default, this piece should sound like a fair judge's bench memo
or a first-rate brief written from both sides at once: each position built from
its own authorities, voiced at full strength in its own vocabulary, and the
adjudication reaching only as far as the cited record reaches. The default gives
you calm precision; it does not by itself give you adversarial fairness, and that
is the whole added burden here. Concretely, these moves change sentences:

- Build each position so its serious holders would sign the paragraph. The test
  of the executive-discretion passage is whether an executive-power scholar would
  recognize it as their case, not the paper's summary of their case; the same
  test governs the appropriations-power passage.
- The available sources tilt: the statute and most secondary explainers run one
  direction, and their gravity will pull the prose with them. Spend the extra
  effort to reconstruct the under-served side to genuine parity of force. Parity
  means strength, not matched word count or a mirrored outline.
- Keep the terms of art exact and constant. A loosely paraphrased legal term
  becomes a strawman on contact; precision here is fairness, not pedantry. Name a
  thing once and hold the name, especially across the two sides.
- Weigh only what the cited authority weighs. When a point resolves, let the
  losing argument fall in its strongest form, stated in its own terms. Where the
  law is unsettled, say so and leave it open rather than manufacture closure.
- Equal force is not identical scaffolding. Do not run the two sides through a
  mirrored template; let each argument take the shape its own logic takes.

## Licenses

form: direct quotation of a named position-holder stating that position at its strongest.
move: the studied reporters and primary filings let a claim reach its peak in the holder's own vocabulary — the court's line, the scholar's formulation, the filing's phrasing — rather than in the paper's paraphrase. Deploy it where a side's argument is most forceful.
bar:  the quoted words state the position at its strongest and resolve to a source you have read; a quotation chosen to expose a side, or trimmed so it argues worse than its holder does, fails.

form: a position stating the limit it accepts as part of making its case.
move: the strongest primary arguments narrow themselves — they grant what must be granted and defend the core that remains, which is exactly what makes them hard to dismiss. Use a concession to sharpen a side's real claim.
bar:  the concession is one the position's own credible holders actually make, and it leaves that position's strongest claim standing. A concession that functions as the other side's argument in disguise fails. This is the licensed exception to the default's cap on contrast.

form: an explicit statement that the authorities do not settle a specific question.
move: precise legal writing marks the edge of what is decided instead of papering over it. State exactly which question no cited authority resolves, and let it stand unresolved.
bar:  the question is genuinely open on the record you cite, and stated concretely enough that a reader could see what a resolving authority would have to say. A vague gesture at ongoing debate, or a manufactured openness used to duck a point the record does settle, fails.

form: a stated conclusion on a contested sub-question.
move: where the cited authority actually answers a question, say what it answers and show the reasoning; as the losing argument falls, name its strongest form in its own terms so the reader watches a real contest decided, not a weak version knocked over.
bar:  the resolution reaches only as far as its sources reach, and the losing side's best point is stated at full strength at the moment it loses. A verdict that outruns its sources, or that defeats a version no serious holder advanced, fails.

## Amy Howe, "Argument analysis: Justices debate constitutionality of funding condition by phone"
Source: https://www.scotusblog.com/2020/05/argument-analysis-justices-debate-constitutionality-of-funding-condition-by-phone/
Craft:
- cadence: alternates between the two sides in near-regular turns, each claim met by its rebuttal, so neither holds the floor unopposed.
- argument: reports the contest rather than joining it; the structure is claim, counter, and the question a Justice pressed, in the order the argument unfolded.
- evidence: direct quotation and close paraphrase of what advocates and Justices actually said; the record does the work an editorial adjective would otherwise do.
- stance: neutral by construction. Strength shows through whose questions bite, never through "argued persuasively" or "struggled to explain."
- notice: attributive verbs ("stressed," "emphasized") report a rhetorical move without valuing it, leaving the reader to weigh it.
- diction: technical and exact; the doctrine is named precisely, not softened for a lay ear.
- reader: assumes a reader who can hold constitutional doctrine intact and wants it kept that way.
- the important move: evaluation is delegated to the actors on the record. The adversaries' own pushback, not the writer, exposes each argument's soft spot.

## U.S. Constitution Annotated, "Impounding Appropriated Funds"
Source: https://www.law.cornell.edu/constitution-conan/article-2/section-3/impounding-appropriated-funds
Craft:
- cadence: chronological and cumulative; each era's practice and justification is laid down in sequence until the disagreement stands on its own.
- argument: states each side's constitutional claim in that side's own doctrinal terms — faithful-execution discretion against the appropriations power — without adjudicating what the Court has not.
- evidence: primary sources dominate — presidential messages, testimony, the statute, the narrow holding of an on-point case; secondary opinion enters sparingly and is marked as such.
- stance: institutionally neutral; it distributes weight to each position by what the record supports, not by symmetry for its own sake.
- notice: it marks the epistemic boundary in the open ("the Supreme Court has not directly considered..."), separating settled ground from open ground.
- diction: concrete and cataloguing — what each actor actually did — over general assertion about what is constitutional.
- reader: assumes a reader who wants navigable complexity and verifiability, with the citations sitting in the open as an invitation to check.
- the important move: balance is a byproduct of sourcing discipline. Each side is only as strong as the documents it can cite, so no thumb touches the scale.

## Office of Legal Counsel, memorandum on presidential authority to impound appropriated funds
Source: https://www.justice.gov/file/147706/dl
Craft:
- cadence: incremental; constitutional text, then historical practice, then statutory limits, each layer set before the next leans on it.
- argument: builds one side at full strength in its own terms and gains force by conceding that statutes bind, then defending the narrower claim that survives.
- evidence: specific constitutional language and executive practice read as interpretive windows, not brandished as clean precedent; contested history is reasoned through, not asserted.
- stance: committed advocacy, yet forensic — anchored in text and defined conditions rather than in a claim of unlimited power.
- notice: the defining move is the concession that narrows the claim and shifts the burden onto the reading of the constraint, not the existence of the power.
- diction: juridical and unheated; the terms of art carry the argument and no inflammatory word is asked to.
- reader: assumes a reader who will test each step, so each step is made to bear weight alone.
- the important move: it states a side so precisely that it cannot be strawmanned. This is the model for building the position this article's ambient sources under-serve.

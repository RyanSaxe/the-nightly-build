## What was wrong

Round-01's s9 `Quote` read: "presidents have largely acted within the bounds of
the law, and impounded funds in accordance with their statutory authority." That
is **not Price's own prose.** In his essay that line sits in **footnote 22** as a
block-quotation of the Protect Democracy report *The Myth of Presidential
Impoundment Power* — the advocacy source discarded in round-01. Attributing it to
Price both misattributes one author's words to another and smuggles the discarded
source back in through Price's mouth. Do not carry the Protect Democracy phrasing
anywhere in the article.

### Note on the exact quotation to hand the writer

Two verified options in **Price's own words**, both from Part II:

1. Short and clean (recommended if the writer wants a direct quote): *"The main
   exception to this pattern is President Richard Nixon, who did forcefully assert
   a constitutional impoundment prerogative."* Pair it with the article's own
   prose that Nixon's assertion failed in court and produced the 1974 ICA (a fact
   already carried by round-01 s1/s3 and the Rehnquist memo, s6 — the writer need
   not lean on Price's footnote for the "lost in court" point).

2. Fuller, on the statutory-not-constitutional reading of the history: *"...they
   generally did not assert any preclusive prerogative to defy clear spending
   mandates."* (final clause of the Part II sentence quoted above). This is the
   precise proposition the article uses Price for; it contains a semicolon in the
   full sentence, so the writer may prefer to quote only this closing clause or to
   paraphrase in the article's voice.

If the writer paraphrases instead, the precise proposition Price's own words
support is: **the great majority of historical impoundments rested on statutory
authority or interpretation rather than a claimed constitutional power to defy a
spending mandate, and Nixon was the lone president to assert a constitutional
impoundment prerogative — an assertion that failed.** Attribute that
characterization to Price; do not attach the Protect Democracy wording to it.

Caution: the editor review paraphrased Price as finding "the overwhelming
majority of historical examples do not imply any constitutional prerogative." I
could not confirm that exact phrasing as a verbatim Price sentence; the verified
wording is the two quotations above. Use those, not the "overwhelming majority"
phrasing, if a direct quote is wanted.

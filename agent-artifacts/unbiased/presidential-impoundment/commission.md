# Commission: unbiased/presidential-impoundment

## Subject
A consequential US disagreement made timely by a current event: **whether the
President may refuse to spend, or claw back, money that Congress has
appropriated** (the impoundment question), anchored to the recent federal
appellate ruling that the administration improperly terminated appropriated
clean-energy funds (D.C. Circuit; verify the exact case, panel, date, and
holding). The event supplies the timeliness; the disagreement is the durable
separation-of-powers fight over the power of the purse.

## The two positions to steelman (both, fairly)
1. **Executive-discretion / impoundment-revival view:** appropriations set a
   ceiling, not a mandate; the President has inherent and historically
   exercised discretion to decline or delay spending, and the Impoundment
   Control Act of 1974 (ICA) is itself a contestable intrusion on executive
   power. Marshal the strongest named proponents and their primary arguments
   (OMB/administration filings, executive-power scholars).
2. **Power-of-the-purse / anti-impoundment view:** Article I gives Congress the
   spending power; *Train v. City of New York* (1975) rejected impoundment of
   appropriated funds; the ICA channels any withholding through rescission and
   deferral procedures the administration bypassed; GAO has found unlawful
   impoundments. Marshal the strongest named proponents (GAO, appropriations-law
   scholars, the plaintiffs, the court's reasoning).

## Anchor-verification instruction (researcher, do this first)
Confirm the anchoring event from recent reputable US reporting and its primary
record (the opinion). If the specific clean-energy ruling is not the strongest
*documented, current (early-Aug-2026)* anchor for the impoundment question,
report the strongest one you can document in the same subject before drafting.
Do not switch subjects away from impoundment; adjust only the anchoring event.
This subject must not duplicate the already-published unbiased pieces
(especially firing-the-regulators, iran-war-powers, should-the-fed-hike); the
distinct question here is the spending power, not removal or war powers.

## Requirements (strict mode — hard gates)
- unbiased template, strict: its structure and citation rules are hard gates.
- min_sources 10; sources_by_kind primary [4,null], secondary [3,null]. Primary
  = statutes (ICA text, relevant appropriations), the court opinion(s),
  *Train v. City of New York*, GAO decisions, OMB/administration filings,
  named scholars' own writings. Secondary = reputable reporting and analysis.
- Anchor on documented US reporting; position-holders and precedent may come
  from anywhere. Steelman each side before weighing. No unearned verdict; if
  the piece reaches a synthesis it must be cited, reasoned, and shown, and it
  must not flatten either side.

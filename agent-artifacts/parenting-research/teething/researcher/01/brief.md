Read the primary evidence, not press write-ups:
- Symptom attribution: the peer-reviewed prospective/cohort studies and
  systematic reviews on which signs are associated with tooth eruption
  (e.g. Macknin et al. 2000, Pediatrics; Massignan et al. 2016 systematic
  review/meta-analysis, Pediatrics). Record exactly what each design can
  establish about temperature, fussiness, and what it rules out for high fever
  and diarrhea.
- Product safety: the FDA safety communications on benzocaine oral products
  (methemoglobinemia) and on homeopathic teething tablets/gels with belladonna
  (the 2016-2017 warnings and the Hyland's/CVS withdrawals). Record the dates,
  the agency's findings, and the mechanism of harm from the FDA's own pages
  (fda.gov).
- Home/safety guidance: AAP or equivalent authoritative guidance on safe relief
  (chilled teether, gum massage) and on acetaminophen use in infants, and the
  red-flag symptoms that mean "not teething."

Verify every number against its owning primary; note absolute vs relative risk
and denominators. In Contradictions, capture any disagreement on whether
low-grade fever is attributable to teething. In Source assets, name any FDA
figure/table or study table worth bringing in (or None found). Search for what
breaks the framing: strong evidence that teething does cause more than mild
symptoms, if it exists.

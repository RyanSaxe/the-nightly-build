# researcher brief: parenting-research/starting-daycare (01)

Inputs:
- ../../editorial-direction.md — citation standard, series territory, declared reader
- ../../commission.md — the evidence tiers the record must supply and bound

Output: evidence.md

Questions the record must answer, each traced to the source that owns it:

- The near-term infection effect: the primary cohort or trial evidence that group
  daycare raises respiratory and gastrointestinal infections and otitis media in
  infancy, with the effect size, the age window, and whether the excess later
  narrows or reverses. Record the exact rates or rate ratios and their
  denominators from the study that owns them.
- The contested asthma, wheeze, and allergy evidence: the source of the "more
  early wheeze, possibly less later asthma" reading (for instance the early
  daycare-and-wheezing cohorts), and at least one later study or systematic
  review that complicates or narrows it. Record what each establishes and where
  they disagree.
- The developmental evidence: the large longitudinal child-care study (the NICHD
  Study of Early Child Care and Youth Development or comparable) on cognitive and
  behavioral outcomes, the size of the effects, and how far quality of care and
  family selection bound any causal claim. Record the specific measured outcomes
  and effect magnitudes, not a gloss.
- The design limits that bound the whole question: which findings come from
  randomized versus observational evidence, the main confounders (family
  selection into care, provider quality, breastfeeding, siblings), and any
  authoritative guidance (a pediatric body) on group care for infants, including
  the higher-risk cases the desk close must flag.

Search for what breaks the reassuring reading and what breaks the alarming one;
Contradictions should not be empty here. Verify every figure against its primary.
Source floor is 8; classify each primary or secondary and say why. Preserve any
full series a chart might use (for instance infection rate by care setting over
age, or an effect that changes sign over time). In Source assets, name an exact
visual from a cited public document only if it would let the reader test a
central claim better than prose.

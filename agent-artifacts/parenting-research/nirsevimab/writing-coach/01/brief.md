## Domain and genre
Explanatory science/medicine writing for a lay-but-serious reader. The article
explains the clinical-trial and real-world evidence for **nirsevimab
(Beyfortus)**, a long-acting anti-RSV monoclonal antibody, to the household of a
healthy term infant deciding what to do for the fall/winter 2026-27 RSV season.
The hardest craft problem in this piece is **risk communication**: making a large
relative-risk reduction and a small absolute-risk reduction legible in the same
breath, without either scaring or falsely reassuring, and marking honestly where
a decision belongs with a pediatrician.

## What to study
Find at least three writers the field of medicine/evidence itself rates for
clear, honest quantitative explanation — clinicians, science journalists, or
statisticians who communicate risk and study design to non-specialists. Prefer
the primary piece. Extract transferable craft on:
- how they place an absolute number next to a relative one so a reader feels the
  difference;
- how they carry calibrated uncertainty in a sentence without hedging into mush;
- how they name what a study can and cannot establish;
- cadence that stays calm around frightening material.

## Constraints to encode in the guide
- Never a named persona or reusable line. Transferable craft only.
- The `voice-guide.md` MUST contain at least three lines each beginning
  `Source: https://…`, one per exemplar, real and resolvable.
- End the directive with `Recently used, do not reuse:` carrying the negative

## Recently used, do not reuse (from commission)
- The "the trial built to catch X actually measured Y, in the infants it really
  tested" opener/spine used by three recent parenting-research pieces
  (sleep-training, early-allergen, starting-solids). The "who was actually
  studied" discipline is the desk's method and stays; the stock opener does not.
- No colon-subtitle headline. No Betteridge (question-mark) headline.
- No dek molds: semicolon reversal, suspended question, or comma triad.

## Register (from editorial direction, do not restate in the guide)
Calm, precise, argued from first principles (house baseline). Teach each concept
before the sentence that needs it. Written so any well-read parent could be
handed it.

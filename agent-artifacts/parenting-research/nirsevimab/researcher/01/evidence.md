# Evidence record — parenting-research/nirsevimab (invocation 01)

**What the evidence supports, and where it is thin.** The randomized trials
firmly establish that a single dose of nirsevimab lowers a healthy infant's risk
of *medically-attended* RSV lower-respiratory infection across one RSV season, by
roughly three-quarters in relative terms (MELODY, healthy term/late-preterm) and
similarly in preterm infants (Griffin). The pragmatic HARMONIE trial and U.S.
real-world surveillance show a large reduction in RSV *hospitalization* (~80–90%
relative). What is thinner, and central to this article: the **absolute** benefit
for a healthy term infant is small because the baseline is low — the number
needed to treat to prevent one medically-attended RSV LRTI is about 26, and to
prevent one hospitalization roughly 75–100. The single most honest caveat is that
in MELODY's healthy-infant primary cohort the *hospitalization* endpoint alone
did not reach statistical significance (p=0.07); significance for hospitalization
comes from the pooled/all-subjects and pragmatic data. Mortality benefit is not
demonstrable in high-income settings because infant RSV death is already rare.
The real decision for most U.S. families is **one route or the other**: infant
nirsevimab or maternal RSVpreF vaccine in pregnancy, not both.

## Sources

### 1. MELODY trial — ClinicalTrials.gov registered results (PRIMARY)
URL: https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT03979313
Primary: the trial's own posted results own these counts (published as Hammitt et
al., *NEJM* 2022;386:837, gated 403 — counts verified against the registry).
Establishes firsthand (primary/efficacy cohort, data cut 2021-11-03):
- Eligibility: infants "born at or after 35 weeks 0 days GA," entering their
  first RSV season. Randomized: nirsevimab 994, placebo 496.
- **Primary endpoint — medically-attended RSV LRTI through 150 days:**
  nirsevimab 12/994 (1.2%), placebo 25/496 (5.0%). Relative risk reduction
  **74.5%** (95% CI 49.6–87.1), p<0.0001.
- **RSV LRTI with hospitalization through 150 days (primary cohort):**
  nirsevimab 6/994 (0.6%), placebo 8/496 (1.6%). RRR 62.1% (95% CI −8.6 to 86.8),
  **p=0.0708 — not statistically significant in the healthy-infant cohort.**
- All-subjects pooled hospitalization: nirsevimab 9/2009 (0.4%), placebo 20/1003
  (2.0%), RRR 76.8% (95% CI 49.4–89.4), p=0.0002 (significant).
Locator: Results → Participant Flow and Outcome Measures 1–2 (primary cohort).

### 2. Griffin Phase 2b trial — ClinicalTrials.gov registered results (PRIMARY)
URL: https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT02878330
Primary (published Griffin et al., *NEJM* 2020;383:415, gated). Firsthand:
- Eligibility: healthy infants born 29 wk 0 d to 34 wk 6 d GA. Randomized:
  nirsevimab 50 mg 969, placebo 484.
- **Medically-attended RSV LRTI through 150 days:** nirsevimab 25/969 (2.6%),
  placebo 46/484 (9.5%). Efficacy **70.1%** (95% CI 52.3–81.2), p<0.0001.
- **RSV hospitalization through 150 days:** nirsevimab 8/969 (0.8%), placebo
  20/484 (4.1%). Efficacy 78.4% (95% CI 51.9–90.3), p=0.0002.
Locator: Results → Outcome Measures 1–2.

### 3. HARMONIE trial — ClinicalTrials.gov registered results (PRIMARY)
URL: https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05437510
Primary (published Drysdale et al., *NEJM* 2023;389:2425, gated). Firsthand:
- Design: open-label, pragmatic; nirsevimab vs no intervention (standard care).
  Infants ≥29 wk GA, aged 0–12 months. France, Germany, UK; 235 sites.
  Randomized: nirsevimab 4,038; control 4,019.
- **Primary endpoint — hospitalization for RSV LRTI:** nirsevimab 11 (0.27%),
  control 64 (1.59%). Efficacy **84.0%** (95% CI 69.5–92.4), p<0.0001. (The NEJM
  primary publication reports 11 vs 60, efficacy 83.2% [95% CI 67.8–92.0]; the
  registry and paper are consistent within analysis-set differences. Use ~83–84%.)
- **Very severe RSV LRTI:** nirsevimab 5 (0.12%), control 21 (0.52%). Efficacy
  77.7% (95% CI 39.2–93.4), p=0.0014.
Locator: Results → Outcome Measures 1 (RSV LRTI hospitalization) and very severe.

### 4. ACIP nirsevimab recommendation — MMWR (PRIMARY, guidance)
URL: https://www.cdc.gov/mmwr/volumes/72/wr/mm7234a4.htm
Primary: CDC/ACIP owns the U.S. recommendation (Jones et al., MMWR 2023-08-25).
Firsthand:
- "ACIP recommends 1 dose of nirsevimab for all infants aged <8 months born
  during or entering their first RSV season."
- Dose: 50 mg for infants <5 kg (<11 lb); 100 mg for ≥5 kg (≥11 lb).
- Pooled efficacy (Phase 2b + Phase 3, GRADE): medically-attended RSV LRTI
  **79.0%** (95% CI 68.5–86.1); RSV LRTI with hospitalization **80.6%**
  (95% CI 62.3–90.1).
- Baseline: "An estimated 79% of infants and children aged <2 years hospitalized
  with RSV have no underlying medical conditions" — i.e., healthy term infants
  carry most of the hospitalization burden by count.
- Nirsevimab is a long-acting monoclonal antibody (passive immunization), FDA-
  approved July 2023 — not a vaccine.

### 5. ACIP maternal RSVpreF (Abrysvo) recommendation — MMWR (PRIMARY, guidance)
URL: https://www.cdc.gov/mmwr/volumes/72/wr/mm7241e1.htm
Primary: CDC/ACIP owns it (Fleming-Dutra et al., MMWR 2023-10-20). Firsthand:
- Maternal RSVpreF vaccine as a one-time dose at 32 0/7–36 6/7 weeks' gestation,
  seasonal (Sep–Jan in most of the continental U.S.).
- **The choice, verbatim:** "Either maternal RSVpreF vaccination during pregnancy
  or nirsevimab administration to the infant is recommended to prevent RSV-
  associated LRTI in infants, but both are not needed for most infants."
- MATISSE efficacy against severe medically-attended RSV LRTI: 69.4%
  (95% CI 44.3–84.1) full population; 76.5% (95% CI 41.3–92.1) approved interval.
- Preterm-birth signal: 5.7% (vaccine) vs 4.7% (placebo) at the 24–36 wk trial
  interval; at the approved 32–36 wk interval 4.2% vs 3.7% — not statistically
  significant. Approved interval chosen partly to address this.
Locator: Recommendation and "Considerations" sections.

### 6. Real-world effectiveness 2023–24 — MMWR (PRIMARY)
URL: https://www.cdc.gov/mmwr/volumes/73/wr/mm7309a4.htm
Primary: CDC New Vaccine Surveillance Network (Moline et al., MMWR 2024-03-07).
Firsthand:
- Nirsevimab effectiveness against RSV-associated hospitalization **90%**
  (95% CI 75–96), Oct 2023–Feb 2024; 699 infants (407 RSV+ cases, 292 controls),
  59 (8%) received nirsevimab ≥7 days before onset; median 45 days from receipt.
- Cites trial hospitalization efficacy 81% (95% CI 62–90) through 150 days for
  comparison.
- **Caveat, verbatim intent:** effectiveness over a full RSV season "is expected
  to be lower than the estimate reported here, because antibody levels from
  passive immunization wane over time."

### 7. Population impact 2024–25 — MMWR (PRIMARY)
URL: https://www.cdc.gov/mmwr/volumes/74/wr/mm7416a1.htm
Primary: CDC interim ecologic evaluation (MMWR 2025). Firsthand, vs 2018–20:
- RSV hospitalization among infants aged 0–7 months: RSV-NET 43% (95% CI 40–46)
  reduction; NVSN 28% (18–36).
- Age 0–2 months: RSV-NET 52% (49–56); NVSN 45% (32–57); NVSN excluding Houston
  71% (64–78).
- Caveat: ecologic analysis without individual-level product-coverage data;
  cannot establish direct causality; interim through Feb 2025.

### 8. AAP guidance for parents — HealthyChildren.org (PRIMARY, AAP-owned)
URL: https://www.healthychildren.org/English/health-issues/conditions/chest-lungs/Pages/rsv-when-its-more-than-just-a-cold.aspx
Primary: the AAP's own parent-facing statement. Firsthand:
- RSV immunization given as a single injection during or just before RSV season;
  "about 80–90% effective in preventing infants from being hospitalized with RSV."
- Infants born before RSV season and younger than 8 months should receive it.
- **"Most infants will likely only need protection from the maternal RSV vaccine
  or the infant immunization, but not both."**

### 9. CDC RSV burden in infants (PRIMARY, background)
URL: https://www.cdc.gov/rsv/infants-young-children/index.html
Primary: CDC surveillance-based burden statements. Firsthand:
- Before U.S. RSV immunizations, "an estimated 58,000–80,000 children younger
  than 5 years were hospitalized due to RSV each year."
- "RSV was the leading cause of infant hospitalization in the U.S. before the
  introduction of RSV immunizations."
- "Two to three out of every 100 infants under 3 months are hospitalized with
  RSV every year." (A clean baseline for a healthy young infant.)

### 10. Supply shortage 2023–24 — CDC HAN 00499 (PRIMARY, access context)
URL: https://www.cdc.gov/han/2023/han00499.html
Primary: CDC Health Alert Network. Firsthand: nirsevimab (100 mg) supply was
limited in its first U.S. season, prompting interim guidance to prioritize the
highest-risk infants — evidence that access, not just efficacy, shaped the first
year. (Locator: HAN summary.)

## Contradictions
- **Registry vs publication for HARMONIE:** the ClinicalTrials.gov posting shows
  64 control hospitalizations / 84.0% efficacy; the NEJM primary paper reports 60
  / 83.2%. Same trial, minor analysis-set/data-cut difference. Report as ~83–84%;
  neither materially changes the argument.
- **Relative vs absolute framing:** the sponsor and guidance headlines lead with
  relative reductions (74–90%); the *absolute* reductions for healthy term
  infants are small (≈1–4 percentage points). Not a disagreement of fact, but the
  gap the article exists to close.
- **Hospitalization significance in the healthy-infant cohort:** MELODY's own
  hospitalization endpoint was p=0.07 in the primary cohort; the strong
  hospitalization evidence for healthy infants comes from HARMONIE and real-world
  surveillance, not from MELODY alone. Any claim that MELODY "proved" a
  hospitalization benefit in healthy term infants would be an overread.
- **RCT vs real-world:** real-world effectiveness (90%) looks higher than the RCT
  hospitalization efficacy (~80%), but the CDC itself warns the 90% figure
  reflects a short median follow-up (45 days) and will fall over a full season as
  antibody wanes. The numbers are not in conflict once the window is stated.

- Pooled (ACIP GRADE): MA RSV LRTI 79.0% (68.5–86.1); hospitalization 80.6%
  (62.3–90.1).
- Real-world hospitalization effectiveness: 90% (75–96), 2023–24, median 45 d.
- Population reduction 2024–25 vs 2018–20, infants 0–7 mo: 43% (RSV-NET),
  28% (NVSN); 0–2 mo up to 52–71%.
- Maternal RSVpreF (MATISSE) severe MA RSV LRTI: 69.4% (44.3–84.1) full;
  76.5% (41.3–92.1) approved interval.
- Baseline (CDC): 2–3 per 100 infants <3 mo hospitalized with RSV/yr;
  58,000–80,000 hospitalizations/yr in children <5 pre-immunization.

**Worked NNT (for the article to show once):** MELODY healthy-infant primary
endpoint. Control 5.0% get medically-attended RSV LRTI; treated 1.2%. Treating
100 infants prevents about 3.8 such episodes (5.0 − 1.2). To prevent one episode,
treat 100/3.8 ≈ 26 infants. For hospitalization the healthy-infant gap is ~1.0
pp, so ≈100 healthy infants treated per hospitalization avoided (and that endpoint
was not itself significant in MELODY).

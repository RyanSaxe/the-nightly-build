## Subject and timing
Vitamin D supplementation for the breastfed infant. The desk follows a child born
February 2026; at ~6 months (August 2026) the daily-drops decision is live and
ongoing, and the question "is this still worth doing, and what does it actually
buy" is exactly the kind of near-term household decision the desk exists to serve.

## The question and the angle
Explain what the evidence supports about giving a breastfed infant 400 IU/day of
vitamin D: what deficiency and rickets the supplement reliably prevents, versus
the weaker or absent evidence for the broader claims often attached to it (bone
mineral density years later, respiratory infection, allergy, cognition). The
honest shape: the supplement's core justification is strong and narrow —
breastmilk is low in vitamin D, deficiency and nutritional rickets are real and
preventable, and the AAP/consensus 400 IU/day recommendation rests on that —
while the health halo around higher doses or broader benefits is not carried by
the trials. Show how study design (RCTs on rickets/25(OH)D status vs.
observational associations for the softer endpoints), normal variation, sun
exposure, formula-feeding, and adherence bound the conclusion.

## Required contribution
Research, not generic advice. The reader finishes able to distinguish the claim
the evidence establishes (prevent deficiency/rickets in a breastfed infant) from
the claims it does not, and knows what actually changes at home (keep giving the
400 IU drops; the reason is deficiency prevention, not a general health boost).
End with where an individual situation is a clinician's call (suspected
deficiency, dark skin/low sun latitude, prematurity, dosing questions), stated as
the desk's standing boundary: this does not stand in for a pediatrician.

## Sourcing
Series `min_sources: 8`, template `article`, word band 1200-3000. Prefer primary:
- AAP clinical guidance on vitamin D for infants (Wagner & Greer, Pediatrics 2008,
  and any current AAP update the researcher can reach).
- The trial/observational evidence the researcher opens directly (e.g., dosing
  RCTs such as Gallo et al. JAMA 2013 on 400 vs higher IU and bone outcomes;
  Cochrane or systematic reviews on vitamin D and rickets/respiratory outcomes).
- IOM/NAM Dietary Reference Intakes for vitamin D (the 400 IU basis).
The researcher verifies every figure against the primary that owns it and reads
the cited passage, not the abstract. Steelman the "higher dose / broader benefit"
case before weighing it down.

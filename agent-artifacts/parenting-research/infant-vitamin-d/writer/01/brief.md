Focus:
- The honest shape the evidence records: the deficiency/rickets-prevention case is strong but rests on physiology (human milk carries ~25-78 IU/L), 25(OH)D as a surrogate, and historical/observational experience — NOT large RCTs counting rickets (Cochrane found only two trials reporting biochemical rickets, bone-health evidence insufficient). Say the strength is in the logic, and name that the trial base is thin because rickets is now rare. Do not overstate it as trial-proven.
- The health halo fails the trials, report arm-by-arm: higher doses buy extra 25(OH)D but no extra bone at age 3 (Gallo follow-up), no bone strength or infection benefit through age 2 and no neurodevelopment gain (VIDI; Tuovinen), and infant vitamin D did not reduce allergy (Rueter). Let the nulls speak; every effect size carries its endpoint and population.
- The crux to make the reader understand: what counts as "deficient" is unsettled — 50 nmol/L (IOM) vs 75 nmol/L (higher-dose advocates) — and the higher target is the entire basis for the higher-dose argument. This definitional contest is the spine, not a "big relative vs small absolute" move (that was nirsevimab) and not iron's "supplement-before-deficiency-shows-no-benefit" structure (that was infant-iron) — the deficiency-prevention case HERE is strong and is the point.
- Contradiction to handle exactly as the researcher flagged: the one randomized signal on a soft endpoint — Hibbs D-Wheeze cutting recurrent wheeze — is in Black PRETERM infants comparing supplementation vs withholding, so it reinforces the deficiency-prevention frame; do NOT present it as "vitamin D prevents wheeze" for a healthy term breastfed infant. Note also Gallo's 1,600 IU arm exceeds the IOM infant upper limit of 1,000 IU (0-6 mo), which caps the steelman for higher dosing.
- End with what actually changes at home (keep giving the 400 IU drops; the reason is deficiency prevention, not a general health boost) and the desk's standing boundary: suspected deficiency, dark skin / low sun latitude, prematurity, or dosing questions are a pediatrician's call; this does not stand in for individual medical care. Word band 1200-3000; min 8 sources.

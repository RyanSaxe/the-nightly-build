# Draft handoff: parenting-research/infant-vitamin-d (01)

## Original work

The piece reframes the infant vitamin D debate as one contest over a single
blood threshold (50 vs 75 nmol/L) and, by charting the four Gallo dose arms
against both threshold lines and sorting every tested endpoint into a
strength table, shows that once the standard 50 nmol/L threshold is granted
the whole higher-dose and broader-benefit case collapses to the standard
dose's result, leaving deficiency prevention as the drops' only warrant.

That act is visible in the "What counts as 'enough'" section (the spine), the
Gallo threshold chart (Fig. 1), and the endpoint table.

## Contradictions handled

- **Hibbs D-Wheeze** is walled to its population in its own labeled note
  ("Where the wheeze result applies"): sustained 400 IU vs withholding in Black
  preterm infants, RR 0.66, read as support for the deficiency-prevention
  frame, explicitly *not* as wheeze prevention for a healthy term breastfed
  infant.
- **Gallo 1,600 IU** is stated to exceed the IOM 0-6 mo upper limit of 1,000
  IU in both the threshold section and the Fig. 1 caption, capping the
  higher-dose steelman.

## Open question

One denominator judgment, non-blocking, for the editor's eye: evidence.md
carries no rickets *incidence* rate (the voice guide's Oster "166 cases /
68 million births" is a craft exemplar, not a verified number in the record).
So the piece earns "rickets is now uncommon" with the only rate-like figure the
record supplies — Cochrane's 2 of 19 trials reporting biochemical rickets —
rather than importing an unverified case count. If the researcher can open a
primary rickets-incidence denominator, the "too rare for a trial to size"
line could be sharpened; the current framing does not overstate.

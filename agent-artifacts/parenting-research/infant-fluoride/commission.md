# Commission: parenting-research/infant-fluoride

## Subject

Fluoride for an infant around six months, the age when the first teeth erupt.
The child this desk follows was born February 2026, so at the August 2026 dateline
the decision is near: what the evidence says about preventing tooth decay in the
first years, and specifically the two choices a household faces once a first tooth
appears. First, whether and how to use fluoride toothpaste on an infant (the
current professional guidance is a rice-grain smear of fluoride toothpaste from
the first tooth). Second, whether an infant needs fluoride supplementation, which
turns on the fluoride content of the household's drinking water.

## Why this topic, now

An open, evidence-based beat that ages forward with the child. First teeth and
the start of solids (sugar exposure) make caries prevention a real, timely family
decision at six months. The topic also has current policy salience: fluoride in
public water has been contested in United States policy through 2025-2026, which
raises the stakes on the supplementation question without changing what the trial
evidence shows. The desk covers research, not generic advice.

## Angle and boundaries

Read the evidence itself and say what it supports, how strongly, and what it
cannot establish. The spine is the caries-prevention evidence for young children:
the Cochrane reviews of fluoride toothpaste in children (including what the
evidence does and does not show below age one and by concentration), fluoride
varnish evidence, and the basis for the "smear from the first tooth" and
supplementation guidance from bodies such as the AAP, the ADA, and the US
Preventive Services Task Force. Show how study design, the age range actually
tested, fluorosis risk from swallowed toothpaste, and water-fluoride status bound
the conclusion. End with what the evidence might change at home (toothpaste amount
and timing, whether to test well water, when a supplement is and is not indicated)
and where the decision is a matter for a pediatrician or dentist. The desk does
not stand in for individual medical care. Do not let the water-policy context take
over the piece; it is the reason the supplementation question is live, not the
subject.

## Template and floors

- Template: `article` (two anchors plus 2-6 cited sections). Series word band
  1200-3000.
- Sources floor: 8. Prefer the primary evidence (systematic reviews, trials,
  guideline statements) over secondary reporting; contested figures need the
  primary.

## Habits not to inherit

Recent parenting pieces (infant-flu-vaccine, starting-daycare) close on a section
built as "From the trials to this child" / "What to act on, what to leave to a
pediatrician," using nb-note plus nb-note-strong. The prompt does require ending
on what the evidence changes at home and when to see a clinician, so keep that
substance, but do not build the closing heading or its phrasing on that mold, and
vary the furniture from a two-note stack if the material allows. Headline states
the finding, as those pieces do; find this piece's own finding rather than a
template.

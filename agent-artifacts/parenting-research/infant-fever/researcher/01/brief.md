# researcher brief: parenting-research/infant-fever (01)

Inputs:
- `agent-artifacts/parenting-research/infant-fever/commission.md` — the question, what the evidence must establish and bound, and the source floor
- `agent-artifacts/parenting-research/infant-fever/editorial-direction.md` — citation standard, series territory, declared reader

Output: `agent-artifacts/parenting-research/infant-fever/researcher/01/evidence.md`

Read the primary guidance and trials, not summaries. Deliver:
- Fever as a regulated defense and how weakly its height predicts illness
  severity in a well-appearing infant, from authoritative pediatric guidance.
- Antipyretics and illness duration: the randomized and systematic-review
  evidence, with direction, effect size, and uncertainty. Record what is
  contested.
- Antipyretics and febrile seizures: the randomized evidence that they do not
  prevent recurrence, plus the incidence of febrile seizures and the recurrence
  rate, all in absolute terms with denominators.
- Combined or alternating acetaminophen and ibuprofen: the temperature evidence,
  the dosing-error risk, and the guideline position.
- The age thresholds: the exact temperature that makes a fever an emergency in
  the youngest infants, the age bands the major guidelines use, and how the
  three-to-six-month window is treated. Record the specific warning signs the
  guidelines flag as clinician or emergency matters.
- Ibuprofen's lower age limit from drug labeling, and the basis for the
  six-month crossover.

For every figure record the exact reading, unit, owning primary, denominator,
and period. Classify each source primary or secondary (a guideline body's own
recommendation is primary for that recommendation; a trial is primary for its
result). Record contradictions between guidelines or between trials in full.
Confirm every URL resolves to the source's own page. Where the evidence is thin
or a common belief is unsupported, say so explicitly in the opening paragraph.

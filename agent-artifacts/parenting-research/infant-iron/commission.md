# Commission: parenting-research/infant-iron

## Assignment
An evidence piece for a household whose child was born February 2026 and is now
about six months old. The subject is **iron in the second half of the first
year**: why a term, healthy, breastfed baby's iron supply becomes a real question
right around six months, what the evidence says a family should do about it, and
what it cannot settle.

## Why now
This desk times a topic to the decision. At about six months, iron is the pivot
of the whole "starting solids" transition the household is entering: fetal iron
stores laid down before birth run low around this age, breast milk is low in
iron, and complementary foods now have to carry the load. That makes "which first
foods, and does this baby need more than food provides" a live, evidence-bearing
decision this month. (The desk has already covered starting-solids timing and
early allergen introduction; this is the nutrient question underneath them.)

## What the evidence must establish (read it, then bound it)
- **Why iron matters and why six months:** iron's role in the developing brain;
  the depletion of stores around 4-6 months; why exclusively breastfed infants
  are the group of concern and formula-fed infants largely are not (fortification).
  What earlier events changed the starting point (delayed cord clamping's effect
  on stores).
- **What strong evidence supports vs cannot establish:** the association between
  iron-deficiency anemia in infancy and later neurodevelopment is consistent, but
  causation and the reversibility of deficits are where the evidence is genuinely
  contested — trials of iron supplementation in non-anemic infants have NOT
  cleanly shown developmental benefit, and some show possible harm in
  iron-replete infants. Show how study design (observational vs RCT,
  confounding by socioeconomic status), normal variation, and feasibility bound
  each conclusion.
- **What a household can actually do:** the guidance from authoritative bodies
  (AAP, WHO, CDC, USDA/Dietary Guidelines, ESPGHAN) on iron-rich complementary
  foods from ~6 months, and on screening/supplementation — and where they
  disagree (e.g. AAP's past universal-screening and supplementation stances vs
  the evidence base; the USPSTF finding on screening). End on what the evidence
  might change at home, and mark clearly where this is a clinician's call (a
  premature or low-birth-weight baby, suspected anemia, a restrictive diet).

## Reader / mode / template
The house reader as an evidence-literate parent: any parent could be handed this;
explain the study designs, do not talk down. mode open; template `article`
(1200-3000 words, 2-6 flex sections + orientation anchor). Do original analysis
(weigh the evidence), not a guidelines summary. The desk does not stand in for
individual medical care; say so where it belongs, without hedging the science.

## Source obligations
- Template floor: **minimum 8 sources**; per-section citation; honest
  `data-nb-kind`.
- **Primary**: the actual studies and systematic reviews (e.g. Cochrane reviews
  on iron supplementation in infants; Lozoff et al. on iron deficiency and
  development; a landmark RCT such as the Chilean iron-supplementation trial;
  the BOND iron review; USPSTF evidence report). Read the trial/review itself —
  population, design, effect size, confidence interval, and stated limits — not a
  summary. Guidance documents (AAP clinical report, WHO guideline, CDC, USDA) are
  primary for what each body recommends; classify honestly.
- Verify every number (prevalence, effect sizes, dosages, ages, RR/OR with CIs)
  against the owning source. Seek contradictory evidence — this topic's value is
  in the disagreement between association and trial evidence.
- Every URL resolves; a paywalled trial is gated (find the primary/PMC version).

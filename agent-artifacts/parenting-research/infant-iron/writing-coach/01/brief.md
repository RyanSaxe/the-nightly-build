# Writing-coach brief: parenting-research/infant-iron (01)

## Your job
Produce `voice-guide.md` here for an evidence `article` (1200-3000 words) on
infant iron at ~six months, whose spine is the tension between association
evidence and trial evidence, and which ends on what a household can actually do.

## Begin with these inputs
- This brief; `../../commission.md`; `../../editorial-direction.md` (press voice;
  the desk explains research, bounds conclusions, and does not stand in for
  medical care).

## Study the best
Study at least three exemplars the field rates for writing about medical evidence
for a lay-but-literate reader without false certainty: writers who convey effect
sizes, confidence, and study-design limits honestly (e.g. a respected evidence-
based medicine writer, a serious science journalist who handles trials well, a
clinician-writer). Prefer primary pieces. Do not study prior Nightly Build
parenting pieces for voice.

## Settle for this writer
- How to state what an association shows and what a trial shows, and hold them
  apart in the prose, without hedging into mush or overclaiming causation.
- How to give a number the reader can use (absolute vs relative, the CI, the
  denominator) and a comparison they already know.
- How to write the "what this might change at home" close and the "see a
  clinician" boundary plainly, so care advice reads as care advice and science
  reads as science.
- Diction: name the nutrient, the study, the population exactly; concrete over
  abstract; short single-purpose sentences.

## Constraints
How to write, not what to say. No catchphrases, no reusable closers. End with
`Recently used, do not reuse:` (opening on a striking relative-vs-absolute number
by reflex; the effect-size headline; the "the trials built to catch harm found
none" shape; comma-triad deks).

## Output
Write only `voice-guide.md`. Return `DONE writing-coach <path>`.

# Commission: parenting-research/infant-flu-vaccine

## Assignment

An evidence article for the parenting-research desk, which follows a child born
February 2026 and explains the research that bears on a real family decision,
timed to when the decision is near. Template `article` (longread, 1200-3000
words, 2-6 flexible sections). Authorized scheduled work from `nb duty`; open
slot, one article.

## Subject and timing

The child reaches six months of age this month, the youngest age at which the US
schedule offers an inactivated influenza vaccine, and the 2026-27 season is the
first for which the household must decide. The decision has real structure: a
child vaccinated for the first time before nine years old needs two doses at
least four weeks apart to be primed, so the timing of the first dose determines
whether the child is protected before the season peaks. The article explains what
the evidence supports about influenza vaccination in the youngest eligible
children, how strongly, and what it cannot establish.

## Angle and what the evidence must carry

Center the questions a household actually faces: how well the vaccine works in
children this young, how much of a six-month-old's protection is direct versus
borrowed from vaccinating those around the baby (cocooning) and from maternal
vaccination during pregnancy, why the two-dose priming schedule exists, and what
is known about safety at this age. Be precise about effect sizes and their
uncertainty: efficacy against influenza in the youngest children is real but more
modest and less precisely estimated than in older children, and season-to-season
match matters. Distinguish reported fact, estimate, and synthesis. End with what
the evidence might change at home and where the situation is a matter for the
pediatrician (egg-allergy handling under current guidance, prematurity or chronic
conditions, and the fact that under-6-month infants cannot be vaccinated directly
so their protection is entirely indirect).

Read the evidence itself: the primary trials and the Cochrane synthesis, not
advocacy summaries. Show how study design, normal variation, feasibility, and
family circumstance bound each conclusion. The desk covers research, not generic
advice, and does not stand in for individual medical care.

## Sources

`min_sources` is 8. Anchor to primaries and high-quality synthesis: the current
ACIP/CDC influenza recommendation for the 2026-27 season (the two-dose rule for
first-time vaccinees under nine; the ages covered), the Cochrane review of
influenza vaccines in children, the pivotal pediatric efficacy trials this
evidence rests on, evidence on maternal influenza vaccination protecting young
infants, and CDC/vaccine-safety data at this age. Prefer the document that owns
each claim; verify every figure (efficacy ranges, dose interval, age thresholds)
against its owning source. Every URL resolves to the source's own page.

  six-month milestone almost verbatim ("The child turns six months old this
  August, the age at which..."). Do NOT open on that construction. Find a

  the honest way to report efficacy here, but do not copy the prior deks' wording

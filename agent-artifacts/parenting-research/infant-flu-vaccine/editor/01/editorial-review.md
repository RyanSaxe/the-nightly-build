# Editorial review: parenting-research/infant-flu-vaccine (editor/01)

## Skeptic

Thesis: for a healthy six-month-old, the flu protection the family can act on
is thin and season-dependent when it comes from the child's own inactivated
shot, and the better-measured protection borrowed from the mother's pregnancy
vaccination has already faded by the age the child becomes eligible; the two
mechanisms never overlap cleanly. The piece stands on four load-bearing claims:
the strongest efficacy figure (LAIV, RR 0.22) is out of scope because LAIV is
licensed only from age two; the applicable inactivated figure (RR 0.36) rests
mostly on children older than the baby; the one in-band randomized trial swings
from 66% to -7% across two seasons; and pooled maternal efficacy of 35% wanes to
a CI-crossing 19% by four-to-six months.

I recomputed every effect size, interval, and denominator against the owning
primary by opening each citation href as printed. The numbers hold. Cochrane
(s2, PMC6491174): inactivated RR 0.36 (0.28-0.48, NNV 5) and RR 0.72 (0.65-0.79,
NNV 12) for ILI; LAIV RR 0.22 (0.11-0.41, NNV 7) and RR 0.69 (0.60-0.80, NNV 20)
for ILI; "very few randomised controlled trials in children under two." Table
matches cell for cell. Hoberman (s3): 786 children 6-24 months, 66% (34-82) then
-7% (-247 to 67), primary AOM outcome null. Omer (s7): three trials, 10,002
women / 9,800 infants, 35% (19-47) waning 56% (28-73), 39% (11-58), 19% (-9 to
40). Zaman (s8) 63%, 6 vs 16 cases (22 total). Madhi (s9) 48.8% (11.6-70.4).
Tapia (s10) 33.1% (3.7-53.9), protection not evident by month six. Duffy (s11)
IIV alone IRR 0.46 (0.21-1.02), IIV+PCV 3.50 (1.13-10.85), IIV+DTaP 3.50
(1.52-8.07), ~30 per 100,000 absolute. Absolute and relative are kept distinct
throughout ("That tripling is a relative measure. In absolute terms..."), LAIV
is correctly excluded at this age, cocooning carries no efficacy number
("without ever attaching a number to it"), and no claim rests on an unread
2026-27 MMWR (the season rule runs through s4/s6 standing ACIP documents and the
s5 AAP page). Every `data-nb-kind` label matches the primary/secondary test:
Cochrane and Omer are secondary syntheses; the trials, ACIP, and AAP pages are
primary. Every href resolved to its source's own page (CDC, JAMA, AAP, and the
PMC/PubMed full-text records the evidence record designates for the gated
NEJM/Cochrane/Lancet primaries).

Two claims broke against the primary.

1. **The season-match attribution is false (central, routed).** The article
   explains the 66%-to-(-7%) swing as a change in antigenic match: "Averaging
   the two seasons would hide what the swing records: a season's antigenic
   match. One winter the circulating strain resembled the vaccine, the next it
   did not." The verdict block repeats it: "A well-matched season widens the
   benefit; a poorly matched one, as in 2000-01, can erase it." Hoberman says
   the opposite. The paper states the circulating strains were "well matched
   with vaccine strains in the 2 respiratory seasons" — both seasons, including
   2000-01 — and attributes the near-null second-season result to low influenza
   incidence, with the placebo attack rate falling from 15.9% to 3.3%. The wide,
   zero-crossing CI (-247 to 67) is that low case count, not a mismatch. The
   evidence record repeated the same error (its Hoberman note calls the swing
   "the strongest single illustration of season-match dependence"), so the
   record and the primary conflict; the primary governs. This is a broken
   central claim tied to reporting the article does not have, so it goes to the
   researcher and writer rather than being reworded here. It recurs in three
   places: the section-2 explanation paragraph, the verdict line, and the
   closing sentence's "how well this year's strains match the vaccine."

2. **"The individual trials behind the pool" is a false relational label
   (fixed).** The article named Bangladesh (Zaman), South Africa (Madhi), and
   Mali (Tapia) as the trials behind the Omer pool. The pool is Nepal, Mali, and
   South Africa; Zaman's Bangladesh trial is a separate maternal-vaccination
   study, not a constituent. Each trial's own figure is cited correctly, so I
   removed only the false pool-membership framing, recasting the line to "The
   individual maternal-vaccination trials disagree on size." No number, name, or
   citation changed.

## Cut

The draft is unusually clean on slop; the writer largely stated what each figure
measured before giving it, per the voice guide. One sentence failed the delete
test: "The rule sets a sequence, and the sequence sets the calendar," an
abstract chiasmus sitting between the two-dose rule and the concrete calendar
logic that follows it ("Two doses four weeks apart take at least a month to
complete... so the first dose has to come early"). It reduces to "the X sets a
Y, and the Y sets the Z" and carries no fact the next sentence does not. Cut.

I walked the edges out of order. Openers and closers hold facts: the orientation
opens on the strongest figure belonging to a product the baby cannot get; the
article closes on the real epistemic limit ("known only in hindsight"), not a
reassurance or a hand-back, as the commission required. No dangling referents
for a reader arriving cold. No negative-parallelism strawmen (the "does less to
keep a child from feeling sick than to prevent influenza" contrast is earned by
the RR 0.72-versus-0.36 split). No prompt leakage: the through-line is reworded
from the brief, not lifted, and no planning labels or self-reference survive.

Against the recent-pattern notes: the piece does not reuse the prior "child
turns six months old this August" opener, and its dek makes a claim about the
world (which product is licensed when, which population the figure rests on)
rather than the flagged large-relative/small-absolute mold. Headings are built
in the piece's own nouns and vary in construction. The verdict block and the
one table and one chart each carry evidence prose would bury, so none reads as a
formula quota. I checked the draft's distinctive phrasing against the voice
guide's quoted passages; nothing is borrowed from Oster, Branswell, or
Spiegelhalter and Masters.

## Reader

Read straight through as the desk's parent, the piece gives what the sources
alone do not: the two protective timelines set against each other so the reader
sees they never overlap — the child's own shot comes due at six months exactly
as the borrowed maternal protection reaches a CI that no longer excludes zero,
with cocooning unquantified in the gap between them. That assembly is the
article's own work, and it matches the draft-handoff's original-work sentence.
It survives the Hoberman correction, because the spine is the maternal-fade
timing and the direct/borrowed/unquantified distinction, not the season-match
overlay. The prose sits closer to the voice-guide exemplars than to a median
summary. The headline reads as the largest claim and holds: the timing
coincidence it asserts is what Omer's waning curve and six-month eligibility
establish together.

## Edits

- Cut the slop connective "The rule sets a sequence, and the sequence sets the calendar." from the two-dose section.
- Recast "The individual trials behind the pool disagree on size for the same reasons." to "The individual maternal-vaccination trials disagree on size." to remove the false claim that Zaman's Bangladesh trial is a constituent of the Omer pool (pool is Nepal/Mali/South Africa).

## Required work

- **researcher.** The season-match attribution is unsupported and contradicted
  by the owning primary. Hoberman states strains were well matched in both
  seasons and attributes the second-season near-null result to low influenza
  incidence (placebo attack rate 3.3% vs 15.9%), which drives the wide,
  zero-crossing CI. Correct the evidence-record note that frames the swing as
  "season-match dependence," and supply sourcing for whichever claim the piece
  keeps: the Hoberman low-incidence/imprecision explanation for the swing (the
  attack-rate figures are in the paper), and, if any general "efficacy depends
  on antigenic match year to year" statement is retained for the closer, a
  source that owns that claim rather than Hoberman, which does not.
- **writer.** Redraft the section-2 explanation of the 66%-to-(-7%) swing to
  remove the antigenic-match cause ("Averaging the two seasons would hide what
  the swing records: a season's antigenic match. One winter the circulating
  strain resembled the vaccine, the next it did not."). The honest reading:
  little influenza circulated the second season, so few cases produced an
  imprecise, near-null estimate; the swing shows how uncertainly efficacy can be
  pinned down in the youngest age band, not a demonstrated match difference. Fix
  the verdict line "A well-matched season widens the benefit; a poorly matched
  one, as in 2000-01, can erase it" (2000-01 was well matched), and reconcile
  the closing sentence's "how well this year's strains match the vaccine" with
  the reframed section and the researcher's sourcing. Re-run the proof after the
  rewrite. (The chart, its chart-1.py provenance, and the table need no change:
  the chart's 56/39/19 points and 28-73, 11-58, -9-40 intervals match Omer
  exactly, the y-axis is linear and honestly labeled, and the 4-6 month whisker
  crosses zero as the caption states.)

## Decision

revise — every figure, interval, denominator, href, chart, and table checks out,
but the article's season-match explanation for the Hoberman swing is
contradicted by the owning primary and recurs in the section, the verdict, and
the closer, which needs researcher sourcing and a writer redraft before it can
publish.

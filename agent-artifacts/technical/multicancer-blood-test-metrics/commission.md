# Commission

## Assignment

- Produce one Technical article prompted by the FDA advisory panel's September 23, 2026 review of GRAIL's Galleri multicancer early-detection blood test.
- Teach how the assay classifies methylation patterns in cell-free DNA and why a useful evaluation must follow a positive result through cancer-signal detection, tissue-of-origin prediction, diagnostic workup, and confirmed diagnosis.
- Do not give medical advice or treat an advisory-panel vote as FDA approval.

## Editorial thesis and structure

- Start with the denominator problem: sensitivity, specificity, and positive predictive value answer different questions and move with prevalence.
- Work one transparent numerical example that converts sensitivity, specificity, and prevalence into expected true and false positives.
- Explain tissue-of-origin accuracy and diagnostic resolution as separate stages, then connect them to time-to-diagnosis and harms from workups.
- Finish with a compact checklist for reading an MCED result or study claim without collapsing these stages.

## Source plan and form

- Use at least eight sources, prioritizing FDA meeting materials, peer-reviewed validation and prospective studies, trial protocols, and independent clinical commentary.
- Include equations or a small worked example, but no decorative table. Avoid yesterday's “X is Y, not Z” opener, repeated code-block rhythm, “turns X into Y” framing, and “Test…” conclusion.
- Keep claims tied to the population and study design that produced them. Resolved policy: researcher capable/high, writer capable/medium, editor inherit/high.

# Evidence record

The evidence supports an article that treats a multicancer blood-test result as the first step in a diagnostic pathway, not as a diagnosis. The strongest current record is FDA's September 2026 review, which reports high specificity and cancer-signal-origin accuracy but substantially lower sensitivity, especially for stage I disease. It also documents invasive follow-up after positive results. The evidence is thin in two decisive places: the prospective workup studies returned results from the earlier MCED-V2 assay, while FDA estimated the submitted Galleri assay's effectiveness by retrospective testing of stored plasma; and no study has yet established a mortality benefit. The randomized NHS-Galleri trial did not meet its primary endpoint of reducing stage III/IV diagnoses.

### 1. FDA executive summary for the September 23, 2026 advisory meeting

URL:         https://www.fda.gov/media/194909/download
Kind:        primary; FDA authored the regulatory review and owns its description of the submission, bridged analyses, proposed labeling, and questions put to the panel.
Establishes: Galleri's proposed indication, assay workflow, regulatory status, pivotal-study design, bridged performance, stage-specific sensitivity, cancer-signal-origin performance, diagnostic burden, adverse events, and evidentiary limits.
Paraphrase:  Galleri isolates plasma cell-free DNA, performs bisulfite conversion and targeted sequencing, then applies three locked machine-learning classifiers for cancer-signal detection, anatomic cancer-signal origin, and sometimes supplemental biological classification. Among 218 PATHFINDER 2 participants evaluated after a positive MCED-V2 result, 159 (72.9%) underwent at least one invasive procedure; 43 of 90 participants without a cancer diagnosis did so. The sponsor's proposed labeling says a positive result requires established diagnostic testing, a negative result does not rule out cancer, and the test does not replace recommended single-cancer screening. Repeat-program benefits and risks remain under study.

### 4. PATHFINDER 2 prospective study

URL:         https://www.nature.com/articles/s41591-026-04618-w
Kind:        primary, peer-reviewed study report; the study was sponsored by GRAIL and includes GRAIL-affiliated authors.
Establishes: Prospective performance, diagnostic procedures, time to resolution, and harms for the earlier MCED-V2 test in North American adults aged 50 or older without clinical suspicion of cancer.
Paraphrase:  Published September 22, 2026, PATHFINDER 2 enrolled 35,878 participants from December 2021 through July 2024. Among 32,007 participants with a completed 12-month cancer assessment, 287 tested positive and 173 had cancer confirmed: PPV 60.3%, specificity 99.64%, 12-month episode sensitivity 39.3% across all cancers, cancer detection rate 0.54%, and cancer-signal-origin accuracy 91.3%. Of 290 positive participants who underwent diagnostic evaluation, 213 (73.4%) had an invasive procedure. Median diagnostic resolution was 37 days for participants with cancer and 70 days for those without cancer; the Kaplan-Meier median for all positives was 48 days. Nine participants had ten adverse events during workup; five were study-related and none of those was serious. The authors explicitly describe the endpoints as descriptive, with no formal hypothesis testing, and list the single-arm design, sponsor-paid diagnostic care, one prevalence round, and use of an earlier assay version as limitations.

## Contradictions

- The panel recommended favorably, but that recommendation is not FDA approval. FDA's meeting page says advisory votes are nonbinding; GRAIL's own announcement says FDA review continues.
- The sponsor describes a strong evidence package, while the effectiveness vote was only 6-4 and the randomized NHS-Galleri trial failed its prespecified stage III/IV endpoint.
- The proposed indication uses “early detection,” yet FDA's bridged NHS-Galleri analysis found sensitivity of 13.6% at stage I and 60.0% at stage IV. FDA explicitly asked whether “early” was supported.
- High specificity does not mean most cancers are detected. In the bridged NHS-Galleri analysis, specificity was 99.74% while 12-month episode sensitivity was 31.6%; 561 of 820 people diagnosed with cancer during follow-up had no signal detected.
- High PPV does not mean a positive result is diagnostic. In the same analysis, 132 weighted positive results occurred without a cancer diagnosis within 12 months, and FDA's proposed labeling requires established diagnostic testing.
- The submitted Galleri assay is not the prospectively returned MCED-V2 assay. Current-assay performance is based on retrospective frozen-sample bridging, while diagnostic procedures and adverse events followed MCED-V2 results.
- The 2021 CCGA overall sensitivity of 51.5% came from a case-control validation set and cannot be substituted for prospective screening episode sensitivity of roughly 27%-39%. Cancer prevalence and case mix differ by design.
- PATHFINDER 2 authors characterize harms as low when expressed across everyone screened (0.6% had an invasive procedure), but 73.4% of positive participants who underwent workup had an invasive procedure. Both denominators are correct and answer different questions.
- MCED-V2 first-round PPV was higher than later-round PPV in NHS-Galleri. The prevalence round contained accumulated undiagnosed cancers; annual incidence rounds are the more relevant steady-state comparison.
- Detecting more cancers and shifting some diagnoses away from stage IV are secondary findings. They do not erase the negative combined stage III/IV primary endpoint and do not establish lower cancer mortality.

## Limits

- FDA has not yet issued a final PMA decision in the opened record; the article must say “advisory panel recommended” and not “FDA approved.”
- Prospective diagnostic-resolution and harm data concern MCED-V2. The submitted Galleri assay's performance was estimated through retrospective frozen-sample bridging, so the current assay lacks a directly observed prospective workup pathway in these pivotal studies.
- “Episode sensitivity” classifies any cancer diagnosed during 12 months after a negative result as a false negative; it is not identical to analytic sensitivity or case-control sensitivity at the blood-draw date.
- Stage-specific episode sensitivity is vulnerable to differential timing bias: a missed cancer may be assigned a later stage when it eventually presents.
- The randomized NHS-Galleri primary endpoint was not met. A reduction in mortality has not been established, and longer follow-up is pending.
- Overdiagnosis cannot yet be quantified from the opened studies. The NHS-Galleri performance paper says analyses of overdiagnosis, mortality, resource use, and psychological outcomes will be reported separately.
- PPV, NPV, cancer detection rate, and number needed to screen depend on prevalence, age, cancer-type mix, prior-cancer history, screening round, and outcome definition. They should not be presented as fixed properties of the assay.
- A correct cancer-signal-origin prediction is measured only among true-positive results and does not guarantee that an individual positive result is correctly localized.
- The first-round NHS-Galleri FDA bridge excludes recurrent cancers because the registry source did not collect them for that analysis; some apparent false positives may therefore reflect recurrence or cancer outside the window.
- PATHFINDER 2 had no control arm, no formal hypothesis testing or success thresholds, a healthier and highly educated participant population, and sponsor-paid workups. Those features limit claims about real-world clinical utility and cost.
- Exact harms beyond recorded procedures and adverse events remain incompletely characterized. Surgery, biopsy, radiation exposure, cost, anxiety, incidental findings, delayed resolution, and treatment of overdiagnosed disease are distinct outcomes and should not be collapsed into one “safe” label.

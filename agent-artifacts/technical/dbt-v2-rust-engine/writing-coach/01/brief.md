Inputs:
- editorial-direction.md (../../editorial-direction.md) — the binding standard
- commission.md (../../commission.md) — the assignment and the angle

Output: voice-guide.md (./voice-guide.md)

Work from these inputs. Do not tour the repository, the Git history or the
archive for background. Where something you need is missing, ask me.

Genre: a technical walkthrough of a developer tool (a data-transformation
engine). The writer shows a mechanism on a concrete, preferably run, example and
explains why it works, for readers fluent in programming and systems who do not
necessarily know dbt. The register is precise and unshowy, confident about what
the walkthrough demonstrates and exact about what is the vendor's claim.

Find exemplars among the engineers and writers the field rates for this exact
move: explaining how a tool or a compiler/runtime works by walking real commands
and output, with a diagram where it helps. Think the best practitioner blogs
that teach by building and running something (for example writers known for
clear from-first-principles systems and tooling walkthroughs). Prefer the
primary piece to commentary. Study how they narrate an experiment, how they show
command output without drowning the point, and how they draw the line between
"here is what I ran and saw" and "here is what the maintainers claim."

Quote passages that show the writing, not the subject, and nothing whose
phrasing could be lifted about dbt or Rust specifically. The direction section
should tell the writer how to sound like a practitioner demonstrating a tool,
not a release announcement, and how to stay concrete without the self-grading
closers the Technical desk has been overusing.

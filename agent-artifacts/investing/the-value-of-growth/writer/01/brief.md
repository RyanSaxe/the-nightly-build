Focus this round: teach that growth creates value only when the return on
reinvested capital exceeds the cost of capital, and destroys value when it does
not. Rely on the earlier lessons (return on capital, cost of capital, present
value) as prerequisites the reader holds; name them, do not re-derive them. Make
it real with two companies at the same growth rate and different returns on
capital, and show value diverging on numbers the reader can reproduce. Set the
value-driver relationship as `nb-math`/`nb-math-eq` and the two-company
comparison as `nb-table`. The lesson template's bookend cards may address the
reader; every other sentence holds the calm first-principles register.

Recent habits to break (see commission): write a fresh headline that commits to
the growth/return mechanism rather than reusing a prior lesson's construction, and
do not copy the section shapes of the present-value or valuation-multiples
lessons.

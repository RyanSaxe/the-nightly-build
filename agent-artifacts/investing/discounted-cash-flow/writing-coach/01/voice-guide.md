# Voice guide: investing/discounted-cash-flow (01)

Write in the house teaching register, calm and exact, but hand the teaching to
the arithmetic. The reader can read a table and do the multiplication; never
paraphrase a figure they can already see. When a computed number carries a
claim, the prose around it names only which input moved and stands aside. Do
not narrate the result the number already states.

Teach the sensitivity by re-running one input, not by asserting sensitivity.
Change a single value, show the figure it produces, and let the distance
between the two figures be the argument. One variable moves per move. A
sentence that says a quantity "is highly sensitive" without a before-and-after
pair of figures beside it has skipped the work.

State the method's weak point flat, and state it where the argument reaches it,
not as a closing caveat. The default register softens exactly the sentence that
should land hardest here, so the license below admits the blunt declarative
that names how much of the answer rests on one guess. Earn it with the number
first; the flatness is only honest once the arithmetic is on the page.

The register teaches a method; it never argues for or against holding the
security. No line presses toward a buy or a sell. The verdict this lesson is
allowed to reach is a verdict on the tool, not on the company whose figures the
tool runs on.

## Licenses

form: the recomputed figure as the sentence's payload, including a plain
  instruction to change one named input and read the new result
move: Damodaran re-runs a terminal value across a grid of discount and growth
  rates and lets the spread between the cells teach; Mauboussin swaps a single
  driver and reports the new share as a number. The reader is handed the change
  and the result, and the prose does not restate either.
bar:  the sentence names the exact input, its old and new value, and the figure
  that results, all reproducible from what is on the page. A second variable
  moving in the same sentence voids it. If the prose repeats the number in
  words, cut the words.

form: the flat, unhedged declarative that names how much of the answer rests on
  one uncertain input
move: Damodaran writes that a valuation dominated by its terminal value is a
  flawed valuation and lets a preceding table earn the flatness; Derman and
  Wilmott state a model's limits as fact and duty, never as "one might argue."
  The sentence grades nothing the numbers have not already shown.
bar:  the figure it rests on appears in the same passage and the reader could
  recompute it. Strip the sentence and the reader loses a specific number, not
  a mood. One such declarative per weak point; a second is a punchline.

## Aswath Damodaran, "Myth 5.5: The Terminal Value ate my DCF!" (with Myth 5.1)
Source: https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2016/11/myth-55-terminal-value-ate-my-dcf.html
Craft:
- cadence: a short declarative provocation opens, then measured explanatory
  prose answers it. Question-and-answer beats keep the pace ("So what?").
- argument: refutation. State the widely taught error plainly, dismantle it
  with a table, then invert the reader's intuition about what follows.
- evidence: the arithmetic is the proof. A grid of recomputed terminal values,
  or a convergence worked out year by year (a 65-year horizon lands within a
  few percent of the perpetuity figure), carries the point; the prose only
  labels which input changed.
- stance: corrects textbook orthodoxy head-on, no hedge ("not only dangerous,
  but also not true"). Flags his own counterintuitive output rather than hiding
  it ("sounds patently absurd").
- notice: he points to the number that should unsettle the reader and says why,
  instead of trusting them to find it.
- diction: plain operating words, cash flow and growth and terminal value.
  Technical terms enter only as precision, never as ornament.
- reader: treated as capable of reading the table unaided; spreadsheets are
  handed over with an invitation to try other numbers.
- the important move: the forced inversion. Accept the premise and your
  intuition about the consequence must reverse; he names the reversal out loud
  so it sticks.

## Emanuel Derman and Paul Wilmott, "The Financial Modelers' Manifesto"
Source: https://djmarsay.wordpress.com/mathematics/applications-of-maths/financial-modellers-manifesto/
Craft:
- cadence: vow-like and anaphoric. "I will" openings, short, decelerating into
  the admission that closes each line.
- argument: ethics first. A model's limits are the center of the duty, not an
  appendix; the omissions are stated as plainly as the inclusions.
- evidence: the assumption list is the bill of materials. What was left out
  ("assumptions and oversights") is named as squarely as what was put in.
- stance: humility as obligation. Refuses to give the user false comfort about
  accuracy.
- notice: names the gap between the equations and the world without softening
  ("I didn't make the world, and it doesn't satisfy my equations").
- diction: moral and clinical, no jargon dressed up as precision.
- reader: the burden is shared. The user is told to check the assumptions too.
- the important move: a limit stated as a fact and a duty at once, never
  cushioned into "one might argue." The flat confession is the whole force.

## Michael Mauboussin, on reading a price for its embedded expectations
Source: https://summitstocks.substack.com/p/michael-mauboussins-new-valuation
Craft:
- cadence: direct declaratives. The simplest case first (steady, no growth),
  then one added complication at a time.
- argument: decompose the unknown into parts and price each part, rather than
  attacking the whole valuation at once.
- evidence: worked percentages on named cases (one company's growth share near
  a tenth, another's near two-thirds) so the split arrives as a computed number
  the reader can check, not a claim.
- stance: assumes the reader handles nuance. States what the tool cannot do
  before what it can ("does not replace a DCF"; "everything is relative").
- notice: flags the metric's limit up front, so no false certainty accretes to
  it later.
- diction: a plain-English gloss for every acronym at first use, then the term
  reused exactly.
- reader: a co-investigator. Start from the one thing known, the price, and ask
  what it forces you to believe.
- the important move: methodological humility as a precondition. Say what the
  number cannot settle before celebrating what it can.

# Commission: investing/discounted-cash-flow

## Subject and place in the course
The next cumulative lesson. The archive has now taught, in order: the income
statement, profit-versus-cash, the balance sheet, return on invested capital,
cost of capital (WACC), present value, and free cash flow. Every ingredient of
a discounted-cash-flow valuation is now on the table but has never been
assembled. This lesson assembles them: value a whole business as the present
value of the free cash flow it will produce for its investors, discounted at
its cost of capital.

## What to teach (concept determines form)
- The core identity: firm value = sum over t of FCF_t / (1+r)^t, with r the
  cost of capital taught earlier and FCF the owner cash taught earlier. Build
  it from present value (already taught) rather than re-deriving discounting.
- Why the terminal value dominates and why that is the honest weak point: a
  short explicit forecast plus a terminal value (perpetuity-growth or exit
  form). Show, with numbers, how much of the answer sits in the terminal value
  and how sensitive it is to r and g. This is the lesson's spine: a DCF is an
  argument about assumptions, not a precise machine.
- Reconcile with earlier lessons: discounting FCF at WACC values the whole
  firm (enterprise value); bridge to equity value by netting debt and cash.
  Tie r back to the cost-of-capital lesson and FCF back to the free-cash-flow
  lesson without re-teaching them.

## Make it real, keep it transferable
Use one company only if it makes the idea concrete, and keep the method
transferable. Do NOT default to Costco or Apple — both are worn from recent
lessons (free-cash-flow used Apple; several used Costco). Prefer a clean,
well-documented example, or a deliberately simple illustrative firm with
sourced inputs, so the arithmetic teaches the sensitivity rather than a single
company's story. If a real company is used, cite its actual figures.

## Boundaries
- lesson template; band 1200-2200 words; min_sources 6.
- Automation is the series' long-range destination, not this lesson's subject.
  Establish the judgment a model would encode. A sensitivity table or a small
  worked calculation is welcome furniture; a chart of terminal-value share vs.
  growth/discount assumptions could carry the central point if honest.
- Rely on earlier lessons by reference; do not restart from "what is a
  discount rate."

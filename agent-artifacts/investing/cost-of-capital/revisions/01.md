# Revision 01 — grammar fix in the "Why this matters" bookend

The opening bookend contained a dropped-word construction. It read:

> "Every dollar a company raises, borrowed or invested by a shareholder,
> carries a price rarely the interest rate printed on a loan."

After the appositive closes, "a price rarely the interest rate" has no verb, so
the sentence reads as a run-on/dropped-word error. Corrected to:

> "...carries a price that is rarely the interest rate printed on a loan."

This is a pure grammar correction. No claim, number, citation, figure, or
meaning changed; the lesson's argument is untouched. Word count moves by two
words (re-stamped accordingly).

Why it survived original production: the sentence sits in the `why` bookend,
which the lesson template marks `cite_exempt`. Both editorial reads and the
deterministic proof concentrated on the body's sourced claims, and the proof is
not grammar-aware, so a non-length grammar error in a citation-exempt bookend
was the least-guarded prose in the piece.

Verification: `nb check --revision` and the full `nb check --pr` proof both pass
after the edit.

# Writing-coach brief: investing/cost-of-capital (01)

## Your job
Produce `voice-guide.md` here for a `lesson` (1200-2200 words) teaching the cost
of capital and the value-creation rule to a smart reader new to finance.

## Begin with these inputs
- This brief; `../../commission.md`; `../../editorial-direction.md` (press voice;
  the lesson template's teach-completely, plain-words, define-before-use rules).

## Study the best
Study at least three exemplars the field rates for teaching a finance/quant
concept to a capable newcomer: writers who build an idea from opportunity cost
and worked numbers without jargon or condescension (e.g. Damodaran's teaching
voice, a respected finance educator, a first-principles explainer). Prefer
primary pieces. Do not study prior Nightly Build lessons for voice.

## Settle for this writer
- How to teach an abstraction (required return / opportunity cost) by building it
  from a concrete number the reader already trusts (a Treasury yield), then
  layering risk.
- How to present a worked calculation (a simple WACC) in prose or a small table
  so the arithmetic is followable, without turning the lesson into a formula
  dump — and how to say plainly what is being simplified (CAPM/beta).
- How to write the two bookends AFTER the body so they resolve as setup and
  payoff in this lesson's particulars (the hurdle, Costco above it, AEP at it).
- Diction: one term per idea (cost of capital, hurdle rate — pick and keep),
  concrete over abstract, short single-purpose sentences.

## Constraints
How to write, not what to say. No catchphrases, no reusable closers. End with
`Recently used, do not reuse:` (opening on a company fact by reflex; defaulting
to a Costco/quarterly-earnings walkthrough; the prior lessons' heading shapes;
comma-and-clause heading cadence).

## Output
Write only `voice-guide.md`. Return `DONE writing-coach <path>`.

# Editorial review: investing/competitive-advantage (editor/01)

## Skeptic

Thesis: how long a business keeps earning an above-cost return, not how high
that return is today, drives most of its value and holds most of the
disagreement in valuing it. The lesson stands on four claims.

1. A DCF's terminal value silently assumes the excess return persists, so the
   fade assumption is where most of the value hides. Grounded in the two prior
   lessons linked in Background and in Mauboussin's two-dimensions framing.
   Held.
2. High returns are competed toward a common level (~10% median ROIC), but the
   fade speed varies enormously by industry. Supported by McKinsey (median ROIC
   ~10%, 1963-2004, opened and confirmed verbatim on its own page) and
   Mauboussin's Exhibit 34 CFROI correlations. Held. The prose keeps the
   accounting-return gravity (McKinsey's ~10% ROIC) and the table's revert-toward
   figures (CFROI, 6.8-9.0%) as distinct measures, and the caption names the
   table as CFROI, so the metric switch is disclosed rather than smuggled.
3. Holding return, growth, and capital equal and varying only the advantage
   period from 4 to 15 years moves the value added from $690 to $2,130. I
   recomputed the growing-annuity present value from EP_1 = $200, g = 6%, r =
   10%: 688.56, 1282.29, 2131.42, rounding to the article's $690 / $1,280 /
   $2,130, a 3.10x span that supports both "a little over three times" and the
   headline's "roughly triples." The $300 gross / $100 capital charge / $200
   excess step checks. The construction is stated as a deliberate modeling
   choice ("They are not the same business or in the same industry"), and the 4
   and 15 are labeled judgments, not measured periods, so it does not claim
   Copart and Crocs are identical. Held.
4. What sets the advantage period is the economic source of the advantage
   (Porter's barriers), and identifying a durable one in advance is hard even
   for experts (Buffett's Dexter Shoe; Crocs a fortress on the 2007 numbers).
   Held.

I pushed hardest on claim 3, the one the lesson cannot lose. The risk was that
assigning Crocs to the 4-year row and Copart to the 15-year row would read as a
measured fact about the two firms. The text forecloses it: the caption fixes the
illustrative inputs and sources only the return levels to the filings, and the
closing paragraph names the periods as forecasts. No break.

Display text audited descriptor by descriptor. Headline figures (4, 15, triples)
match the recomputation. Dek's "nearly the same return on capital" fits 30% vs
27%. Buffett is labeled "2007 letter to Berkshire shareholders" and the three
quoted lines ("dynamics of capitalism," "continuously rebuilt," "vanished within
a few years") are verbatim from the evidence record. Mauboussin, Porter, and
Jiang/Koller carry their correct names and affiliations. Crocs's fade figures
($237.8M 2007 operating income, $188.3M 2008 loss, $737M write-down on a $2.3B
purchase) and the Dexter Shoe details ($433M, 1993) match their owning filings
and letter.

data-nb-kind: all eight sources are labeled primary and each owns what it is
cited for (the two 10-K anchors, the FY2010 and FY2022 Crocs filings, Buffett's
letter, Mauboussin's report, Porter's article, McKinsey's analysis). No label
hides a missing independent source; McKinsey is the independent corroboration of
the reversion claim and is correctly separate from Mauboussin.

I opened every citation href as printed. All eight resolve to the source itself:
the McKinsey page, Porter's HBR article page, and the four SEC EDGAR filings
returned their own text (Copart's auction, membership, zoning, and 69.8%
cross-state figures confirmed word for word; the Crocs FY2010 2007-2009 operating
figures confirmed exactly; the FY2025 HEYDUDE triggering event and the FY2022
acquisition date confirmed). The Buffett and Mauboussin links resolve to the
correct PDFs (the Mauboussin file carries the Credit Suisse author addresses);

links land on the right documents and the researcher read them firsthand. No

the two sibling lessons appear only in Background, which is reporting, and the
terminal-value recap needs no inline cite.

## Cut

Four sentences cut or repaired; no pattern of slop across the piece.

The single-year-comparison sentence in the orientation gestured at "a reader,"
which the body may not do (only the two bookends address the reader). Changed to
"an analyst," which names the practitioner the claim is about and keeps the
point. "How far it closes, and how fast, is the whole question" was an
unearned punchline of the "X is the whole Y" kind and a signpost: it announced
importance and previewed the magnitude-and-duration split that Mauboussin's very
next sentence states directly, so it failed the delete test. Cut. In the
spotting-moats section, "This is the hard part, and it humbles the people best at
it" was an empty conclusion followed by a generic lead-in; the Buffett Dexter
Shoe sentence right after carries the humility on a real case, so the announcement
added nothing. Cut. In the worked example, "and watch what it does to value"
was a reader-facing imperative tacked onto three parallel construction steps
(Hold, Hold, Change) and lost no reasoning when removed. Trimmed.

Edges walked separately. Section openers and closers hold facts or reasoning
("The number that decides most of the value never appears in a single year's
accounts"; "The fade is real and its direction reliable; its speed for any one
company is a matter of judgment"; "A purchased moat can be marked down within
three years"). The negative-parallelism instances each correct a misconception
the lesson names ("Gravity is not a timetable" against fade-as-schedule; "a
forecast about the future, not a fact from the past" against a knowable period;
"turns on durability, not on the current return"), so they are earned, not
reflex. The dangling-referent read passes: a linked-in reader meets Copart and
Crocs, the terminal value, and every term before it is used.

Against the recent-pattern notes: the dek uses this lesson's own nouns (Copart,
Crocs, duration) rather than the flat result-declarative mold, and is two clauses,
not the banned comma triad. Headings vary in build (a noun phrase, a contrast, a
fragment, a question) and none copies the "short flat declarative" shape of prior
lessons. Costco is absent; Coca-Cola, Verizon, Apple, and AEP do not appear. No
prompt leakage: the "what is the advantage, why should it last, what would end
it" triad is the lesson's teaching payload delivered in the reader-addressing
bookends, not a copied planning label or a claim the assignment was met.

Furniture: the annotated equation (one per article, four colored terms all named
in the legend) and the single Buffett note are documented components used for
emphasis that prose would flatten. No decorative block, no missed component.
Em-dash count 0, no banned lexical term.

## Reader

Read straight through as a smart newcomer, I leave able to price the fade rate,
not just describe a moat: I can run the excess-profit stream for a chosen
advantage period, see duration swing the value roughly threefold on identical
current returns, and find that same buried period inside the terminal value the
course taught. No single source hands me that; the evidence supplies the levels,
the fade record, and the taxonomy separately, and this piece fuses them into one
priced variable, which matches the draft handoff's original-work claim. The prose
sits closer to the voice-guide exemplars than to a median summary: it runs the
number before naming its meaning (Damodaran), makes the claim checkable in
miniature before the real firms (Marks), and anchors durability in one holdable
figure, the $690-to-$2,130 jump (Housel). The headline as the largest claim is
true to the arithmetic.

Focus this round: teach the five uses of free cash flow and the single test
that decides among them, return on capital against cost of capital for four
of them, price against the company's own conservative intrinsic-value
estimate for the fifth. Make the buyback case precise: derive, in an
annotated equation, that a buyback raises per-share intrinsic value for the
shares that remain exactly when the price paid is below that estimate, and
lowers it exactly when the price is above. Carry all five uses through one
company, Berkshire Hathaway's first half of 2026, without defaulting to a
quarterly-earnings walkthrough: use the filing's own reinvestment,
acquisition, buyback, and dividend figures in a table, and treat debt
paydown as the one use that is not a single company-wide dial (different
segments moved opposite directions in the same period; say so rather than
force a false single figure). Rely on the earlier lessons (return on
capital, cost of capital, the value of growth) as prerequisites the reader
holds; name them once in Background, do not re-derive them.

Recent habits to break (see commission): no numeric-contrast "at once" dek;
"Why this matters" must not open on a sweeping generalization about markets
or valuation in general; no separate "Verdict" section stacked on top of
"The takeaway"; no imperative-aphorism final sentence; let the concept's own
shape choose the section count and headings rather than inheriting the
"Background list -> math/table -> Verdict" pattern recent lessons fell into.

# Evidence record: investing/capital-allocation (01)

The evidence supports the article's central claim cleanly: Berkshire
Hathaway's own governing standard for its buyback, in its own words across
three filings spanning 2012 to 2026, has always been price against the
company's own estimate of intrinsic value, never a verdict on the business
itself. The company's first half of 2026 supplies real, filed figures for
four of the five uses inside a single 10-Q (reinvestment $10.6B, acquisition
$9.7B, buybacks ~$4.4B, dividends $0), and its own 21-month buyback halt
followed by a resumption at a lower book-value multiple is a live,
current instance of the test actually binding, not just stated. The
first-principles hurdle-rate test that also governs reinvestment and
acquisitions is independently confirmed by Damodaran's academic treatment.
The one place the evidence needs care, flagged for the writer, is that the
10-Q's own cash-flow-statement total for H1 2026 buybacks ($4,444 million)
does not sum exactly to the quarter-by-quarter figures reported elsewhere
(Q1 $235 million + Q2 $4.5 billion ≈ $4.735 billion); the gap is a timing or
trade-date/settle-date difference between sources, not a contradiction of
direction or scale, and the article should use the 10-Q's own total as the
anchor figure rather than force a reconciliation the sources do not support.

             shareholder would get from a one-third-of-earnings dividend

             premium with it.

## Contradictions

- **The H1 2026 buyback total does not reconcile exactly across sources.**
  The 10-Q's own cash-flow statement (source 1) reports $4,444 million spent
  acquiring treasury stock for the six months ended June 30, 2026. The
  Motley Fool's quarter-by-quarter figures (sources 7 and 8) put Q1 at $235
  million and Q2 at roughly $4.5 billion, which sums closer to $4.7-4.8
  billion. Both are read directly from primary or reported figures; the
  most likely explanation is a timing difference (cash paid in the period
  versus trades settled in the period) rather than an error in either. The
  article should anchor on the 10-Q's own total ($4.4 billion) as the
  figure it can defend without needing to reconcile a gap the sources do
  not explain, and use the Fool pieces only for the qualitative
  quarter-over-quarter jump and the halt narrative, not for a precise
  quarterly split.

- **"Wealthier on a per-share basis" (source 5) is the company's general
  acquisition test, not a statement made about OxyChem specifically.** The
  quote is from 2012; OxyChem closed in January 2026. No source has
  Berkshire's management applying that exact language to OxyChem. The
  article should state the test's continuity (the standard has not changed)
  rather than assert it was explicitly recalculated for this deal.

- **No source disputes the direction of the buyback rule.** Every primary
  source that states Berkshire's buyback standard, from the 2012 letter's
  formulaic version through the 2018 amendment to the 2026 10-Q's current
  wording, agrees that price relative to the company's own intrinsic-value
  estimate is the entire test. The wording has become more literal over
  time (a book-value formula, then a discretionary standard, then a named
  decision-maker), not less strict.

# researcher brief: investing/capital-allocation (01)

Inputs:
- `agent-artifacts/investing/capital-allocation/editorial-direction.md` — citation standard, series territory, declared reader
- `agent-artifacts/investing/capital-allocation/commission.md` — the concept, the required precision on buybacks, the boundaries

Output: `agent-artifacts/investing/capital-allocation/researcher/01/evidence.md`
Source policy: minimum 6 sources.

Research questions the evidence record must answer:

- What company lets the five uses of free cash flow (reinvest, acquire, buy
  back, pay a dividend, pay down debt) be shown with real, recent, filed
  figures, without defaulting to a quarterly-earnings walkthrough? Berkshire
  Hathaway's first half of 2026 was chosen: it carries a large reinvestment
  figure (rail and utility capex), a completed acquisition (OxyChem), a
  buyback resumed after a long halt, a zero-dividend policy stated in the
  company's own words, and a debt position moving in different directions
  across its own segments, all inside one 10-Q.
- What is the company's own, current statement of the standard that governs
  its buybacks, and how has that standard's own wording changed over time
  (a formula tied to book value, then a discretionary "intrinsic value,
  conservatively determined" standard, then who at the company gets to apply
  it)? Record the exact language at each stage and its primary source.
- What made the buyback case concrete and current rather than textbook: did
  the company actually stop buying back stock at a price it judged too high,
  and resume at a price it judged low enough? Record the halt's length, the
  book-value multiples on each side of it, and the dollar figures.
- What is the authoritative first-principles statement of when cash should
  leave a company at all (the hurdle-rate test that also governs reinvestment
  and acquisitions), and does a company's own writing give a worked,
  reproducible illustration of the dividend-versus-retention math the
  article can lean on instead of inventing its own?
- What acquisition and reinvestment test does the same company state in its
  own words, and what one historical acquisition anchors "large bet judged
  by the same standard as everyday capital spending"?

Classify each source primary or secondary. Preserve any dollar figure and
any quoted standard exactly as its source states it. Record what a source
does not support as carefully as what it does, especially the reconciliation
gap between the 10-Q's own cash-flow-statement total for the buyback and the
quarter-by-quarter figures reported elsewhere.

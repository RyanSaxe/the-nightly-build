## What the draft got right
The lesson teaches the five uses of free cash flow through one filed window
and holds every use to a test the reader already owns. Reinvestment and
acquisition reuse return-versus-cost without re-deriving it; the buyback gets
its own derivation; dividends and debt lean on the retained-earnings and
debt-discipline language from Berkshire's own documents. The order is
load-bearing: the buyback section cannot land until the orientation section has
separated a priced use from an unpriced one, and it doesn't try to. The
transferable rule sits on top, not buried under Berkshire's history: the closing
card states the five-uses-one-test-apiece frame in general terms and uses the
21-month halt only as the instance.

The buyback identity is derived correctly. V' = (VS - C)/(S - C/P) reduces to
V' - V = C(V - P)/(PS - C); the denominator is positive whenever the buyback
spends less than the whole market cap, so the sign of V' - V is the sign of
V - P, i.e. V' > V exactly when P < V. Confirmed algebraically and by the
substitution in the evidence record. Terms V, S, C, P are each defined in the
sentence before the equation and again in the legend.

Figures match the cited 10-Q: reinvestment $10.6B, acquisition $9.7B, buyback
$4.4B, dividend $0; cash-plus-Treasuries $365.5B and borrowings $128.6B both sum
from the filing's own segment lines; the two financing segments move in opposite
directions as the brief required, not collapsed into one false dial.

Dek and headline break the recent molds. The dek narrates this lesson's own
claim rather than the numeric-contrast-"at once" pattern that ran through
margin-of-safety and enterprise-to-equity. "Why this matters" opens on what the
reader has already learned, not a sweeping claim about markets. No "Verdict"
section stacked on the takeaway; the takeaway closes. Headings are concrete and
varied, none in the comma-and mold.

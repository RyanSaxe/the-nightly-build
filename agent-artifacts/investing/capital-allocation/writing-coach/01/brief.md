The genre is a cumulative teaching lesson that hands a mathematically fluent,
finance-new reader the single test behind five ordinary corporate decisions,
made precise for the one case (a buyback) where the wrong price destroys value
fastest. Study writers who teach a judgment call, not just a formula, by
showing their own reasoning at work rather than asserting a rule. The house
baseline names Chris Olah's and Lilian Weng's patience for building a concept
before the sentence that needs it; a first-principles capital-allocation
writer belongs in the same company. Prefer primary work, and pick passages
that show the teaching move — how a concrete image, a worked number, or an
admitted failure carries an abstraction — rather than passages already inside
this lesson's argument (the exact buyback-ceiling and dividend-arithmetic
quotes the researcher pulls belong to the article, not the craft study, so
choose different passages by the same writers). The lesson template allows
its two bookend cards to address the reader directly; the guide should mark
that as the one place the direct, first-person register belongs, and hold
the body to the calm voice everywhere else.

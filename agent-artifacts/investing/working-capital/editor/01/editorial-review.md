# Editorial review: investing/working-capital (editor/01)

## Skeptic

Thesis: a profitable, growing company can collect far less cash than it earns
because receivables and unsold inventory absorb cash as sales rise, and the
change in those operating accounts is a line on the cash flow statement; a
business whose payables outlast its collections runs the same accounts in
reverse. The lesson stands on four claims.

1. The three operating accounts and the change in them move cash: receivables
   and inventory tie cash up (a use), payables release it (a source), and the
   indirect-method cash flow statement records exactly these changes. Checked
   against IAS 7 (s3) and the direction of each sign in e.l.f.'s filed
   statement (s1). Holds. Directions all correct.

2. e.l.f. FY2024: net income $127.7M, operating cash $71.2M, sales up ~77%,
   working-capital net use $117.5M. Every figure ties to the evidence and to
   the 10-K. The four change lines (49,598) + (93,930) + (55,182) + 81,215 net
   to (117,495); the $56.5M profit-cash gap is 127,663 - 71,154. Arithmetic
   holds. Framing precision honored: the piece says plainly "Working capital
   did not turn the year's cash negative. It pulled cash well below profit." No
   sentence claims negative cash. The FY2023-vs-FY2024 crossover (OCF $101.9M >
   NI $61.5M in the slower year; OCF below NI in the 77% year) is a clean
   within-company control and is exactly what the evidence endorses. Holds.

3. The cash conversion cycle. Recomputed every count against the evidence
   inputs: e.l.f. DIO 166.1 (136,406/299,836x365), DSO 34.2
   (95,862.5/1,023,932x365), DPO 68.5 (56,251/299,836x365), CCC +131.8;
   AutoZone DIO 268.1, DSO 11.7, DPO 312.9, CCC -33.1. All arithmetic ties out.
   The e.l.f. cycle is labeled directional in prose, in the equation caption,
   and in the table caption, and the Naturium acquisition is named (s4) as the
   reason its balance-sheet deltas exceed the operating cash uses. The flagged
   e.l.f. DPO (68.5), which rests on the researcher's computed average payables
   of 56,251, appears only inside that explicitly directional cycle; no uncited
   figure stands alone. Holds.

4. AutoZone as the negative-working-capital case. The structural facts hold and
   are well sourced: net working capital negative $1.18B (8,341,379 -
   9,519,397), payables 114% of inventory (8,025,590/7,025,688 = 1.1424), CCC
   -33 days, and the MD&A's own description of extended vendor terms (s7). This
   half of the claim is sound.

   The break is in the dynamic claim built on top of it. The article says
   AutoZone "produced $3.12 billion of cash from operations, more cash than
   profit, because its growing payables released cash instead of consuming it,"
   frames it as e.l.f.'s gap "with the sign reversed," and closes that the same
   accounts "pay a retailer to grow." I pulled AutoZone's FY2025 cash flow
   statement from the primary (R6). The change-in-operating-assets-and-
   liabilities lines are AR (118,606), inventory (893,908), AP and accrued
   +657,408, income taxes +68,447, other (24,396): a net USE of $311,055K.
   Payables grew, but inventory grew more, so working capital consumed cash that
   year. Operating cash exceeded net income because of non-cash add-backs, not
   working capital: depreciation and amortization $613,199K, share-based
   compensation $124,717K, deferred taxes $115,389K. That is the same mechanism
   that kept e.l.f.'s cash positive, not the reverse of it. The working-capital
   flow sign was not reversed; both companies show a net use in the year shown.
   Only the stock position (negative net working capital) and the CCC ratio are
   negative.

   This is a broken supporting claim central to the second half of the lesson,
   and it undercuts the commission's required contribution directly: a reader
   who does what the lesson teaches and computes AutoZone's FY2025 change in
   working capital gets a $311M use, contradicting "growing payables released
   cash" and "growth pays for itself." A miscitation this is not, and the fix
   needs reporting judgment I cannot supply from the record without inventing
   the explanation, so it routes rather than gets settled.

Every citation href was opened as the article prints it. All seven resolve to
the source itself: the e.l.f. and AutoZone 10-Ks (SEC EDGAR), the Naturium
announcement (price and timing as cited), IAS 7 (IFRS Foundation), the two CFI
pages and Wall Street Prep. The three Background links resolve in the published
library and their titles match the target articles verbatim. Every
`data-nb-kind` matches the evidence record's own classification (4 primary, 3
secondary); the CCC construct carries two independent secondaries (CFI and Wall
Street Prep), so no label hides a missing independent source.

## Cut

The prose is close to the voice-guide register throughout and carries little
slop. One clause failed the placeholder test on its own: "The profit was real
and the cash was real" reduces to "the X was real and the Y was real," a frame
any subject drops into. I trimmed it to "Both figures are genuine" while keeping
the $56.5M figure and the "had gone somewhere" bridge, which is answered
immediately by the next paragraph's "Three operating accounts hold that money,"
so it is a real setup, not a tease.

Edges checked out of order: section openers ("A sale rarely turns into cash the
instant it is recorded," "Growth is what turns this into a problem worth
watching," "A single figure for cash tied up says how much but not how long,"
"Nothing in the arithmetic forces the cycle to be positive") each carry a
reasoning step or a concrete fact and survive the delete test. The closer, "The
same three accounts that hold a growing manufacturer's cash hostage can,
arranged the other way, pay a retailer to grow," is the conclusion the argument
built and states the symmetry in the piece's own nouns; it would stay on its own
terms, but its second half rests on the same AutoZone flow-causation that broke,
so it goes into the redraft scope rather than being cut here.

No banned negative-parallelism mold survives; "The change on the balance sheet
is not always the change in cash" corrects a real and named misconception (the
Naturium example). No elaborate copula, no decorative -ing trailer, no vague
attribution (AutoZone's strategy is attributed to its own disclosures, cited).
No em-dashes; the proof already ran clean. No prompt leakage: the Why-this-
matters card states the learning aims because the template tells it to, and it
is correctly softened to "collect far less cash than it earns" rather than the
commission's "consume cash." The body uses expository imperatives ("Call it days
inventory outstanding," "Run the same three counts") but no second-person
reader-address and no self-reference; that line stays inside the two bookends,
which is where the template allows it. The headline breaks the recent "company +
number / imperative opener" mold, and the dek states no valuation figure and
uses no banned dek mold.

Furniture carries reasoning, not decoration. The e.l.f. reconciliation table's
"What moved" column explains each sign; the annotated CCC equation names its
three colored terms in the legend (one annotated equation, within the limit);
the two-company CCC table exists to show the sign flip and captions its
directional caveat. Nothing reads as a stack of blocks.

## Reader

Read straight through, the piece gives what the sources alone would not: a
single reproducible procedure that walks net income down through the signed
working-capital lines to operating cash, then counts the days a dollar stays
tied up and computes the cycle, so the reader can reproduce both numbers rather
than take them on faith. That matches the original-work sentence. The prose sits
closer to the voice-guide exemplars than a median summary: each account is built
before its name, the 132-day cycle is anchored to "roughly four months" and a
lipstick, and the profit-versus-cash distinction is held without slippage. The
one place the piece does not yet deliver on its own promise is the reverse
example, where the stated mechanism (growth releases cash) is not what AutoZone's
FY2025 numbers show, so a reader who computes it is misled.

## Edits

- Orientation, closing sentence: "The profit was real and the cash was real, and
  the $56.5 million between them had gone somewhere." changed to "Both figures
  are genuine, and the $56.5 million between them had gone somewhere." (cut a
  clause that failed the placeholder test; kept the figure and the bridge).

## Required work

- **researcher.** AutoZone's FY2025 change in operating working capital was a net
  USE of cash (AR (118,606) + inventory (893,908) + AP/accrued +657,408 + income
  taxes +68,447 + other (24,396) = (311,055), USD thousands, from the 10-K cash
  flow statement / R6), and operating cash exceeded net income because of
  non-cash add-backs (D&A $613,199K, share-based comp $124,717K, deferred taxes
  $115,389K), not because working capital released cash. The evidence record's
  AutoZone "Establishes" line ("operating cash flow exceeds net income because
  growing payables are a source of cash") contradicts its own itemized flow data
  and must be corrected. Provide the honest framing of a negative-CCC,
  supplier-financed business: negative net working capital and a negative cash
  conversion cycle are a structural/stock feature (payables fund inventory; each
  sale is collected before suppliers are paid), which is not the same as the
  period change in working capital being a source of cash, and in FY2025 it was
  not. State whether a year exists in which AutoZone's change in working capital
  was genuinely a source, or confirm the reverse example must be taught as the
  structural inversion (negative CCC) rather than a flow that pushed cash above
  profit.

- **writer.** Redraft the "When the cycle runs backward" consequence paragraph,
  the "sign reversed" parallel, the section's causal claim ("because its growing
  payables released cash instead of consuming it"), the closer ("pay a retailer
  to grow"), and the takeaway lines ("growth pays for itself"; AutoZone
  "produced more cash than profit") so they rest on the corrected framing. Keep
  the sound structural claims (net working capital negative $1.18B, payables 114%
  of inventory, CCC -33 days, the vendor-financing strategy). Do not assert that
  AutoZone's FY2025 growth released cash or that working capital drove operating
  cash above net income.

## Decision

revise — the AutoZone reverse example rests on a causal claim the primary cash
flow statement contradicts (FY2025 working capital was a net use of $311M;
operating cash exceeded net income on non-cash add-backs), which breaks the
lesson's second half and the very computation the reader is taught to perform.

# Commission: investing/working-capital

## Assignment

The next lesson in the Investing Course: **working capital and the cash it ties
up**. Teach how a company's operating assets and liabilities (accounts
receivable, inventory, accounts payable) absorb or release cash as the business
runs and grows, and why this is the mechanical reason profit and cash diverge.
The lesson should leave the reader able to read a change in working capital off
the financials, reason about the cash conversion cycle, and see why a growing
business can be profitable and still consume cash.

## Why this concept, and what it builds on

The archive is the syllabus. The course has already taught the income statement
(how-a-business-earns-a-profit), the balance sheet (what-a-company-owns-and-owes),
profit-versus-cash, free-cash-flow, and the valuation and returns lessons.
Working capital is the missing mechanism directly underneath two of those:
profit-versus-cash asserts the gap, and free-cash-flow subtracts the change in
working capital, but neither taught what drives it. It is the next prerequisite,
and it moves the course toward its stated destination (a financial-modeling
system grounded in investing judgment) without making modeling the subject.

Rely on the earlier lessons instead of re-teaching them; assume the income
statement, the balance sheet, and the profit-vs-cash gap. Keep what the lesson
teaches transferable to the next investment.

## Angle and boundaries

- Let the concept determine the form. Use a company only where it makes working
  capital real (a growing retailer or manufacturer whose growth consumes cash,
  or a business with negative working capital that is financed by its
  suppliers/customers). Do not default to a company walkthrough or to quarterly
  earnings, and keep the lesson's teaching general.
- Cover: the working-capital accounts and what each represents; the change in
  working capital as a cash flow (a use or a source); the cash conversion cycle
  (DSO, DIO, DPO) and what it measures; why growth scales the cash tied up; and
  the negative-working-capital case where the model inverts.
- The lesson template allows its two bookend cards to address the reader
  directly; the rest of the piece does not.

## Sources

Template floor (lesson): at least 6 sources. Use authoritative primaries for the
accounting definitions (FASB/IFRS or a standard accounting/finance authority)
and, where a company makes the idea real, that company's own filing (10-K/10-Q)
for the figures. Cite only what you open; every figure verified against the
owning filing.

## Recent habits to break

Recent lessons open on a single company and a price (At $264, Adobe...; Uber's
stakes...; Mastercard's $569...; a Berkshire buyback). The prompt warns against
defaulting to a company walkthrough. Open on the concept made concrete, and if a
company appears, let it illustrate rather than anchor. Vary the headline
construction from the recent "Company + number" mold; several recent deks state
a valuation figure, which does not fit this lesson.

## Required contribution

The reader finishes able to compute and interpret the change in working capital
and the cash conversion cycle, and to reason about why a profitable growing
company can run short of cash, with the mechanism grounded in a real example.

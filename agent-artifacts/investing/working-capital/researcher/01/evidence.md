# Evidence: investing/working-capital (01)

This record supports the lesson's mechanism end to end. The accounting definitions
(working capital as current assets minus current liabilities; the operating
accounts of receivables, inventory, and payables; the change in each account
appearing in the operating section of the cash flow statement as a use or a
source) are grounded in the IFRS standard for the statement of cash flows and in
two finance authorities, and are then demonstrated firsthand in two companies'
own filed cash flow statements, where the sign of each change line is visible.
The two worked examples are strong: e.l.f. Beauty's fiscal 2024, where revenue
rose 77% and receivables and inventory absorbed enough cash to pull operating
cash flow $56.5M below net income, and AutoZone's fiscal 2025, where payables
exceed inventory and net working capital is negative by $1.18B, so growth
releases cash instead of consuming it. Every company figure is read from the
owning 10-K statement and locatable to the exact statement and period. The cash
conversion cycle figures (DIO, DSO, DPO, CCC) are my own computations from those
filed inputs and are labeled as such; the formulas are from named authorities.

Two honest limits sit on top of this. First, e.l.f. remained cash-flow positive
in fiscal 2024 (+$71.2M): its growth compressed cash generation far below profit
but did not turn it negative, so "a profitable grower can still consume cash" is
demonstrated here as "generate materially less cash than it earns," not as
negative operating cash flow. Second, e.l.f.'s year-end receivables and inventory
were lifted by the Naturium acquisition (closed October 2023), so the
balance-sheet change in those accounts is larger than the organic operating change
shown in the cash flow statement; the cash conversion cycle computed from year-end
balances is therefore overstated. Both are detailed below. Neither undermines the
commissioned angle; the second requires the writer to reconcile two numbers
carefully rather than treat them as the same figure.

```text
URL:         https://corporatefinanceinstitute.com/resources/accounting/cash-conversion-cycle/
Kind:        secondary. Corporate Finance Institute is a finance-education
             authority reporting standard analytical definitions it does not own.
Establishes: The cash conversion cycle and its components and formulas.
Paraphrase:  CCC = DIO + DSO - DPO. DIO (days inventory outstanding) = average
             inventory / cost of goods sold x 365, the average days inventory is
             held before sale. DSO (days sales outstanding) = average accounts
             receivable / (credit) sales x 365, the average days to collect a
             receivable. DPO (days payable outstanding) = average accounts payable
             / cost of goods sold x 365, the average days taken to pay suppliers. A
             shorter cycle means cash is recovered from sales sooner relative to
             when suppliers are paid.
Locators:    "Cash Conversion Cycle - Overview, Example, Formula," formula and
             component sections.
Quote:       "the amount of time it takes a company to convert its investments in
             inventory to cash"
```

```text
URL:         https://www.wallstreetprep.com/knowledge/cash-conversion-cycle-ccc/
Kind:        secondary. Wall Street Prep, a finance-training authority; a second,
             independent statement of the same construct.
Establishes: The CCC formula and the meaning of a negative cash conversion cycle.
Paraphrase:  CCC = DIO + DSO - DPO, equivalently Operating Cycle - DPO. DIO =
             (average or ending inventory / COGS) x 365; DSO = (average or ending
             accounts receivable / revenue) x 365; DPO = (average or ending
             accounts payable / COGS) x 365. A negative CCC means the company
             receives cash from customers before it pays suppliers, so working
             capital is a source of cash rather than a use; retailers with fast
             inventory turnover and long supplier terms (Amazon is the cited
             example) tend to have one.
Locators:    "Cash Conversion Cycle" formula and "Negative Cash Conversion Cycle"
             sections.
Quote:       "receives cash from customers before paying suppliers"
```

```text
URL:         https://corporatefinanceinstitute.com/resources/accounting/working-capital-cycle/
Kind:        secondary. Corporate Finance Institute, for the plain definition of
             working capital and the working-capital cycle.
Establishes: Net working capital equals current assets less current liabilities,
             and the working-capital cycle is the length of time to convert net
             working capital into cash (inventory days + receivable days - payable

Paraphrase:  Net working capital is "current assets less current liabilities."
             The working capital cycle is "the length of time it takes to convert

             collecting receivables slower tie up cash; paying suppliers later
             provides a cash buffer.
```

## Contradictions

Nothing in the evidence contradicts the commissioned angle that operating working
capital absorbs cash as a business grows and inverts for a negative-working-capital
business. Two precision points the writer and editor must handle:

- e.l.f. did not consume cash on a net basis in fiscal 2024. Operating cash flow
  was positive $71,154K. What the year shows is divergence: net income $127,663K
  but only $71,154K of operating cash, a $56,509K gap, because working-capital
  changes were a net use of $117,495K (receivables $(49,598)K + inventory
  $(93,930)K + prepaid and other $(55,182)K, offset by payables and accrued
  $81,215K). A high gross margin (70.7% in fiscal 2024) and non-cash add-backs
  kept cash positive despite the working-capital drain. The honest framing of the
  commissioned claim is that a profitable, fast-growing business can generate far
  less cash than its profit, not necessarily negative cash. The within-company
  contrast makes the mechanism clean: in the slower-growth prior year (fiscal
  2023) operating cash flow ($101,883K) exceeded net income ($61,530K); in the
  77%-growth year it fell below. The change in working capital is what flipped.

- e.l.f.'s balance-sheet change in receivables and inventory is larger than the
  cash flow statement's operating change, because the Naturium acquisition (closed
  early October 2023, within fiscal 2024) added acquired balances that are not
  operating cash uses. Inventory rose $110,166K on the balance sheet ($81,323K to
  $191,489K) but the operating cash use was $93,930K; receivables rose $55,869K
  ($67,928K to $123,797K) but the operating cash use was $49,598K. The gaps
  (about $16.2M of inventory and about $7.4M of receivables) are consistent with
  acquired balances rather than organic growth. The writer should use the cash
  flow statement figures for the cash story and not treat the balance-sheet delta
  as the cash effect.

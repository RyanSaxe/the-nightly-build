# Evidence: investing/working-capital (02)

This is a complete record. It supersedes researcher/01 and corrects one causal
error the editor found: the AutoZone framing. Everything else in the round-01
record was verified and is preserved unchanged, including the entire e.l.f. Beauty
example, the accounting definitions, and AutoZone's structural (balance-sheet)
facts. Only the attribution of why AutoZone's fiscal 2025 operating cash flow
exceeded net income has changed, because AutoZone's own cash flow statement
contradicts the round-01 wording.

The lesson's mechanism holds end to end. The accounting definitions (working
capital as current assets minus current liabilities; the operating accounts of
receivables, inventory, and payables; the change in each account appearing in the
operating section of the cash flow statement as a use or a source) are grounded
in the IFRS standard for the statement of cash flows and two finance authorities,
then demonstrated firsthand in two companies' filed cash flow statements, where
the sign of each change line is visible. e.l.f. Beauty's fiscal 2024 shows the
forward case cleanly: revenue up 77%, and receivables and inventory absorbing
enough cash to pull operating cash flow $56.5M below net income. AutoZone is the
negative-working-capital case, but it must be taught as a structural inversion,
not as a year in which growth released cash. That distinction is the correction.

The corrected finding, stated plainly and verified below against the primary:
AutoZone runs a structurally negative net working capital position and a negative
cash conversion cycle. That is a stock fact about the levels on its balance sheet:
payables (about 114% of inventory) fund the inventory base, and a negative cycle
means each sale is collected before suppliers are paid. It is not the same as the
period change in working capital being a source of cash. In fiscal 2025, and in
each of the three years its fiscal 2025 10-K displays, AutoZone's change in
working capital was in fact a net use of cash, because inventory grew more in
absolute dollars than payables did. AutoZone's operating cash flow exceeded net
income because non-cash add-backs (depreciation and amortization, share-based
compensation, deferred taxes, and others totaling about $930M) more than offset a
$311M working-capital use. That is the same mechanism that kept e.l.f.'s cash
positive, not its reverse. The reverse example is real, but it lives in the stock
(negative net working capital, negative CCC, supplier-financed), not in a fiscal
2025 flow that pushed cash above profit.

Two honest limits remain, both carried over from round-01 and still valid. First,
e.l.f. stayed cash-flow positive in fiscal 2024 (+$71.2M): growth compressed its
cash generation far below profit but did not turn it negative, so "a profitable
grower can still consume cash" is demonstrated here as "generate materially less
cash than it earns." Second, e.l.f.'s year-end receivables and inventory were
lifted by the Naturium acquisition (closed early October 2023), so the
balance-sheet change in those accounts exceeds the organic operating change in the
cash flow statement, and the e.l.f. cash conversion cycle computed from year-end
balances is overstated. Both are detailed below.

## Contradictions

The round-01 record contained one internal contradiction, now corrected, and the
underlying evidence still supports the commissioned angle once the stock/flow
distinction is made.

- CORRECTED (round-01 error). The round-01 AutoZone entry framed fiscal 2025
  operating cash flow exceeding net income as caused by "growing payables are a
  source of cash," i.e. e.l.f.'s profit-cash gap with the sign reversed.
  AutoZone's own fiscal 2025 cash flow statement (R6) contradicts that. The
  change-in-working-capital lines net to a USE of $311,055K (receivables
  $(118,606)K, inventory $(893,908)K, payables and accrued +$657,408K, income
  taxes +$68,447K, other $(24,396)K). Payables did grow, but inventory grew more
  in absolute dollars ($870,470K balance-sheet increase in inventory versus
  $669,889K in payables), so working capital consumed cash. Operating cash
  exceeded net income because non-cash add-backs totaling $930,145K (chiefly
  depreciation and amortization $613,199K) more than offset that use. This is the
  same mechanism as e.l.f., not its reverse. The working-capital flow sign was not
  reversed between the two companies; both show a net use in the year shown. Only
  the stock position (negative net working capital) and the cash conversion cycle
  are negative.

- STOCK versus FLOW, stated for the writer. A structurally negative net working
  capital position and a negative cash conversion cycle are facts about the levels
  on the balance sheet: payables fund the inventory base, and each sale is
  collected before suppliers are paid. Whether the period change in working
  capital is a source of cash is a separate question that depends on how the
  accounts grow year to year. It is a source only if the accounts scale up with
  the negative-cycle ratios intact, so that the rise in payables outruns the rise
  in receivables and inventory. In fiscal 2025 AutoZone's accounts payable to
  inventory ratio fell from 119.5% to 114.2%, so inventory's dollar increase
  outran payables', and the change was a use despite the negative stock position.

- No source year in the visible window. Across the three years the fiscal 2025
  10-K displays, AutoZone's change in working capital was a net use every year:
  $(311,055)K (fiscal 2025), $(31,907)K (fiscal 2024), $(205,859)K (fiscal 2023).
  I did not go back further than the three years this filing shows. The safe,
  verified framing is therefore to teach AutoZone's reverse example as the
  structural inversion (negative net working capital and negative CCC, a
  supplier-financed inventory base), NOT as a year in which growth released cash
  and pushed operating cash above profit. The writer should not assert that
  AutoZone's fiscal 2025 growth released cash, or that working capital drove
  operating cash above net income; the primary contradicts both.

- e.l.f. did not consume cash on a net basis in fiscal 2024. Operating cash flow
  was positive $71,154K. What the year shows is divergence: net income $127,663K
  but only $71,154K of operating cash, a $56,509K gap, because working-capital
  changes were a net use of $117,495K (receivables $(49,598)K + inventory
  $(93,930)K + prepaid and other $(55,182)K, offset by payables and accrued
  $81,215K). A high gross margin (70.7% in fiscal 2024) and non-cash add-backs
  kept cash positive despite the working-capital drain. The honest framing of the
  commissioned claim is that a profitable, fast-growing business can generate far
  less cash than its profit, not necessarily negative cash. The within-company
  contrast makes the mechanism clean: in the slower-growth prior year (fiscal
  2023) operating cash flow ($101,883K) exceeded net income ($61,530K); in the
  77%-growth year it fell below. The change in working capital is what flipped.

- e.l.f.'s balance-sheet change in receivables and inventory is larger than the
  cash flow statement's operating change, because the Naturium acquisition (closed
  early October 2023, within fiscal 2024) added acquired balances that are not
  operating cash uses. Inventory rose $110,166K on the balance sheet ($81,323K to
  $191,489K) but the operating cash use was $93,930K; receivables rose $55,869K
  ($67,928K to $123,797K) but the operating cash use was $49,598K. The gaps
  (about $16.2M of inventory and about $7.4M of receivables) are consistent with
  acquired balances rather than organic growth. The writer should use the cash
  flow statement figures for the cash story and not treat the balance-sheet delta
  as the cash effect.

The genre is a lesson in an investing course that builds cumulatively toward
independent judgment. Study writers who teach a financial or quantitative
concept from first principles, building each idea before the sentence that needs
it and grounding it in a worked example (the patience of Chris Olah/Lilian Weng
applied to finance; a clear investor-educator such as Aswath Damodaran's teaching
prose; a strong explainer of the cash conversion cycle). The register is calm and
precise; the worked example does the persuading.

This round's focus: how a lesson teaches a mechanism (working capital as a cash
flow, the conversion cycle) so the reader can compute and reason on their own,
rather than describing it. Show what building the idea up before its name looks
like. Note that the lesson template's two bookend cards may address the reader
directly; the body does not.

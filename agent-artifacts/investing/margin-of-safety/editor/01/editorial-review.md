# Editorial review: investing/margin-of-safety (editor/01)

## Skeptic

Thesis: because an intrinsic value is a range, an investor's protection is the
discount of price to a conservative estimate of value; the required discount
scales with how uncertain the value is; and that protection lives in the price,
not in the quality of the business. Adobe carries the argument: two defensible
values, $236 and $518 a share, built from one 10-K, with the $264 price a
premium to the low value and a discount to the midpoint.

Claims it stands on, and how each held:

- **The value range is arithmetically what the assumptions produce.** I rebuilt
  both ends of the two-stage FCFF model from the evidence record's inputs
  ($7,104M NOPAT base, +$385M net cash, 397.5M shares). Conservative: PV of the
  ten-year stream $43.28B, PV of terminal value $50.01B, enterprise value
  $93.3B, $235.68 a share. Optimistic: $58.50B + $147.05B = $205.6B, $518.08 a
  share. Both land on the record's $235.66 and $518.03. Midpoint $377, spread
  2.2x. All five one-input sensitivities reproduce ($329, $274, $249, $245,
  $243), and the terminal-value shares (54%, 72%) check. The margins hold:
  $264.02 is a 12% premium to $236, a 30% discount to $377, a 49% margin to
  $518; the price-for-a-real-margin figures ($165 at 30%, $189 at 20%, ~$264 for
  30% off the midpoint) all recompute. The endpoints read throughout as outputs
  of stated assumptions, and the after-tax-operating-income base and the ~$1.9B
  stock-comp add-back are made explicit where NOPAT first appears. This claim is
  sound.

- **The scenarios differ in only the stated assumptions.** They do not differ in
  four. Both ends also use a different terminal return on capital (15% vs 25%),
  a fifth input that moves the optimistic endpoint by roughly $34 a share, and
  the draft's own sensitivity table listed it while the build-the-range prose and
  table claimed "four estimates, and only those four." I corrected this: the
  count is five, the mechanism now names the terminal reinvestment, and the range
  table carries the fifth row (15% / 25%) so it agrees with the sensitivity
  table.

- **Growth is the input the value hangs on.** True as "the single largest lever,"
  false as the draft's "almost the entire distance from $236 to $518 is one
  uncertain number." Growth's isolated swing is +$93 of a $282 spread, about a
  third; the effects compound, and no single input is almost the whole range. It
  is, however, larger than the other four inputs combined ($93 vs $71), which is
  the true and still-strong version. I rewrote the sentence to that.

- **The margin is a property of price, not of the business.** Cited to Graham,
  ch. 20, with a locator and no invented page or edition, exactly as the record
  cautioned. The paraphrase carries the concept; no risky verbatim quote was
  attributed to a specific edition. Sound.

Sources. I opened all six hrefs as printed. The two SEC filings (s1 10-K FY
ended Nov 28 2025; s6 10-Q quarter ended May 29 2026) resolve and are the
correct Adobe documents; both display descriptors are accurate. stockanalysis
(s2) and the two Damodaran essays (s3, s4) resolve. The Graham reproduction (s5,
novelinvestor) resolves for a browser and is disclosed in a data-nb-note as a
reproduction of the chapter-20 wording; labeling the source primary by
authorship (Graham owns the concept) is correct by the researcher's test. The
sixth source under scrutiny, Damodaran's 2015 "Academic Exercise, Sales Pitch or
Investor Tool?" (s3), holds: it resolves and carries the exact claim it is
attached to, "almost guaranteed to be wrong when valuing assets with any
uncertainty about the future, and more wrong when there is more uncertainty."
The article legitimately carries six real, distinct sources.

One figure breaks against its primary and I could not settle it myself. The
draft states subscription/recurring revenue at 97 percent of total (orientation
prose and the stat strip). Adobe's 10-K MD&A prints the subscription share of
total revenue at 96 percent ($22,904M of $23,769M is 96.4 percent, which the
filing itself rounds to 96). The evidence record records 97 percent, so the
record and the primary disagree; the primary governs. This is a display-text
number, so it reaches every reader, and it is routed below rather than edited.

## Cut

Five sentences drew scrutiny; two failed and were removed or rewritten for slop
or leakage, and the range-count correction above doubled as a cut of a false
exclusivity claim.

- Cut "By almost any measure of the business itself, this is a rare earner" (end
  of the first orientation paragraph). It reduces to an interchangeable frame,
  restates the subhead's "superb earner," and the delete test loses no fact: the
  63% return, 89% margin, and recurring share already carry it, and the
  business-versus-value pivot is made explicitly in the next paragraph.

- Rewrote "This is where a good business parts company from a good investment" to
  "A good business is not the same as a good investment." The original echoed the
  commission's clause shape ("this is where the course separates a good business
  from a good investment") and carried a signpost ("this is where"). The plain
  claim states the distinction in the article's own terms, and the misconception
  it corrects is the lesson's named subject, so the contrast is earned.

- Rewrote the growth overstatement (see Skeptic) so the sentence matches the
  sensitivity numbers.

Held, after testing: "Anchoring there does not find a bargain. It assumes one"
(tight, carries the circular-reasoning point). "One stock, one price, and it is
both dear and cheap by a third at the same time" (states the central paradox
once, tied to the specific 12% and 30%). "The margin is in the price, or it is
nowhere" (a closer that states the conclusion the argument built). "How wide the
cushion has to be is not a matter of mood" reads near the commission's "not with
the investor's mood," but the sizing rule is the lesson's own taught content, not
a leaked instruction, so it stays. No banned-term or punctuation counts run over;
the piece uses no em-dashes and its semicolons sit in tight parallel contrasts.

The bookends were read as sentences, not waved through for being allowed to
address the reader. Each says something specific to this lesson: the why card
promises the two-value build and the dual reading of one price and states the
learning outcomes; the takeaway resolves them and closes on "the margin is in
the price, or it is nowhere." They read as setup and resolution back to back.

Against the recent-pattern notes: the opener is "A superb earner whose value
spans two to one," not a "what X takes on faith" mold; the piece is Adobe alone,
with no forced second company; and the dek is a two-clause construction, not a
number-punch one-liner. No formula.

## Reader

Read straight through as the lesson's reader, what I have that the sources alone
would not give me is a live sizing decision: one company's value built as a band
from its own filing, the same $264 price shown to be a premium against the
conservative value and a 30% discount against the midpoint, the whole gap traced
to one input among five, and the prices ($165, $189) that would actually buy a
margin. Graham gives the concept, Damodaran the uncertainty, the 10-K the raw
numbers; none gives the worked dual-margin exhibit. The original-work sentence in
the handoff claims exactly this, and it survives. The prose sits closer to the
voice-guide exemplars than to a median summary: it builds the range a step at a
time and lets the figures carry the point.

## Edits

- Cut the sentence "By almost any measure of the business itself, this is a rare earner."
- Changed "They differ in four assumptions about the future" to "five."
- Named the terminal reinvestment in the mechanism sentence and changed "Four estimates, and only those four" to "Five estimates, and only those five."
- Added a "Terminal return on capital" row (15% / 25%) to the range table so it shows all five differing inputs and matches the sensitivity table.
- Rewrote "Almost the entire distance from $236 to $518 is one uncertain number" to "Of the five estimates, one moves the value more than the other four together," which the sensitivity numbers support.
- Rewrote "This is where a good business parts company from a good investment" to "A good business is not the same as a good investment."

## Decision

revise: the arithmetic, the range, the sixth source, and every citation hold, and
my direct cuts resolved the slop, the false four-versus-five count, and the growth
overstatement, but the subscription-share figure (97% in prose and the stat strip)
conflicts with the 10-K's own 96% and must be corrected in the record and the
display text before publication.

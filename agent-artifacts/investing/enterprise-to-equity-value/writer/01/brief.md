This round's focus:
- Teach the full bridge as two steps, concept-first: enterprise value to equity
  value (subtract the claims ahead of the common shareholder, add non-operating
  assets, each argued from what enterprise value measures), then equity value to
  value per share (diluted count, treasury-stock method, if-converted for
  convertibles). Uber's Q2-2026 filing is the worked example.
- Adjust the commission's emphasis to Uber's actual numbers: the evidence shows
  Uber's bridge is dominated by the equity-stakes add-back (~+$6.13/share), with
  net debt (~-$3.59/share) and GAAP dilution (~0.8%, ~$0.60/share) smaller. That
  is a better, more honest lesson than "leverage and dilution dominate": each
  company's bridge has a different dominant term, and Uber's is its strategic
  stakes. Teach the whole waterfall and let Uber show which term happens to move
  it most. You may use the larger gross in-the-money option/RSU pool (~77.9M
  shares) as an illustrative dilution figure if you explain its gap to the GAAP
  weighted-average number, per the evidence.
- The "defensible EV" is back-solved from market cap, stated plainly (the lesson's
  point is the bridge, not an independent Uber DCF). Keep that transparent.
- Turn the contested judgment points (gross vs. net debt, operating-lease
  treatment, preferred-inside-NCI, how "non-operating" the stakes are) into the
  lesson's teaching of judgment, not asserted conventions.

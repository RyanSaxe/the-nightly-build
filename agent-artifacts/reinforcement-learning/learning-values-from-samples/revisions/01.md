# Revision 01: rewritten for clarity at the owner's request, with explanations corrected

The owner found the published version cryptic even though its subject matter was sound. Its
opening block read as a hook, its first paragraphs depended on earlier lessons without saying
what they had established, and its references to those lessons cited their results without
restating what the results meant. The page never stated the grid's size, its rewards or the
discount rate outside the code listing.

The rewrite stands on its own. It describes the grid and defines the policy, return and value
before using them, and it mentions an earlier lesson only where a link offers a derivation the
page does not need. Both methods are introduced as one update with different targets, and each
gets a worked example with real numbers. Six new inline SVG figures follow the light and dark
themes, and `chart-1` and `chart-2` are removed because the page no longer uses them. The title
and dek are new.

Rerunning the old listing and testing its explanations found these errors, all now corrected:

- The old page read Monte Carlo's mean at cell 13, −3.96 at 50 episodes and −3.52 at 100, as
  evidence of how the method behaves. Those figures came from 40 seeds and were a sampling
  fluke. The method is unbiased: 6,000 fresh seeds at 100 episodes gave −1.998 with a standard
  error of 0.035, against the exact −2.005. The page now uses 1,000 training runs for this cell.
- The old page said TD's estimate at cell 13 runs low because it bootstraps off neighbours "the
  pit has dragged down". The error splits exactly by reward, and the pit's part is positive at
  every checkpoint on the page: a pit that has not arrived yet leaves the estimate too high. The
  estimate runs low because the goal's reward arrives late. Running each update backward through
  the episode, which carries the +10 back in one pass, removes the dip.
- The old page presented Monte Carlo passing TD late in training as the unbiased average
  overtaking TD. The runs show that the step-size schedule decides it. At N^-0.85, TD stays ahead
  at all ten checkpoints, and at N^-0.7 the late error is almost all noise.
- The old page said decaying step sizes reach "the hundredths". They reach an RMS error of about
  0.25. Its chart notes also said TD's band across seeds stayed tighter throughout, which the
  run did not show at 4,000 and 6,000 episodes. TD is steadier at cell 13 and not at every cell,
  and the page now says which.
- The old heatmap showed seed 0, a poor run that read −9.9 at cell 13 after 100 episodes, nearly
  three standard deviations from the mean. The new figure shows the run with the median Monte
  Carlo error at 1,000 episodes, for both methods.

New material answers questions the old page left open: why one target is 34 times narrower under
TD while the estimate is only about twice as steady, why a step of 1/N is right for Monte Carlo
and poor for TD, and how TD-Gammon could choose moves from state values.

The code is now two files. `estimators.py` holds the updates and imports nothing from the
environment. `compare.py` runs the experiment and prints every number on the page. `samples.py`
is gone.

Verification: the listings were copied out of the built page and run beside lesson 1's
`gridworld.py`, and their output matches the output printed on the page. Each causal claim was
tested by changing the code so the claimed cause was absent. An independent reviewer rechecked
the numbers, derivations, causal claims and page citations, and two readers given only the page
reported where they got lost. Every figure was viewed at desktop and phone width.

The stamped reading time counts code, printed output and figure labels, and the code and output
are collapsed by default. The prose, including the bookends and captions, is about 5,000 words.

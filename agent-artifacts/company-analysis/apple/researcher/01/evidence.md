# Evidence — company-analysis/apple (01)

The record supports the commission's framing cleanly on the primary numbers: total
net sales, the Products/Services split, all four product-line categories, gross
margin, operating income, net income, diluted EPS, and the tariff-refund
quantification all trace directly to Apple's own SEC-filed 8-K exhibit
(Exhibit 99.1, filed 2026-07-30) and 10-Q (filed 2026-07-31) for the quarter
ended June 27, 2026, both read in full from SEC EDGAR. The Services
analyst-estimate/miss figure (~$31.2B) is confirmed by two independent secondary
outlets that both open-sourced the number differently (Yahoo Finance and
Variety), satisfying independent confirmation for an estimate claim. The
tariff-refund EPS/margin quantification (~$0.11, ~2 points) is Apple's own
disclosure — it appears in the CEO/CFO commentary of the press release that
Apple filed with the SEC as Exhibit 99.1 to its 8-K, not merely in press
coverage — though the 10-Q's MD&A only restates the driver qualitatively,
without repeating the precise figures. The evidence is thin in one place the
brief anticipated: no primary source gives quarterly operating cash flow
directly (Apple discloses cash flow on a nine-month cumulative basis only); I
computed an implied Q3 figure by subtracting two primary cumulative figures,
which is a synthesis, not a filed number, and is flagged as such below.
Q4 FY2026 guidance (revenue growth, gross margin range, the "supply
constraints... significantly sequentially" language, and the ~1-point tariff
guidance for next quarter) is *not* in either SEC filing — it exists only in
verbal CFO/CEO remarks on the earnings call, which Apple does not file or
publish as a primary transcript. Those figures rest on two independently
published secondary transcriptions (Six Colors, 9to5mac) that agree with each
other, plus corroborating analyst-reaction reporting (Tech Times). Apple's
10-Q does independently disclose a *general, non-quantified* "supply
constraints" condition in its own words (MD&A "Macroeconomic Conditions"
section), which is primary and distinct from the specific forward-quarter
guidance language.

## Contradictions / uncertainties

- **Tariff-refund quantification — resolved, not press-derived.** The brief
  asked whether the ~$0.11 EPS / ~2-point margin tariff-refund figure is
  Apple's own or analyst/press-derived. It is Apple's own: it appears in the
  opening paragraphs of the press release Apple furnished to the SEC as
  Exhibit 99.1 to its 8-K (source 1), in language attributed to the company,
  not to any analyst. It is *not*, however, repeated as a specific dollar/point
  figure in the 10-Q's financial-statement notes or MD&A (source 2) — the
  10-Q's MD&A only says the margin increase was "primarily due to a different
  mix of products and tariff refunds," without restating $0.11 or "2 points."
  A reader should understand the precise number as Apple's own disclosure at
  the press-release level, not as a formally audited or itemized 10-Q line.

- **Next-quarter guidance has no primary source.** Everything about Q4
  FY2026 guidance — the 9–11% revenue growth range, the 47–48% gross-margin
  range, the ~1-point tariff-refund guidance benefit, and the "supply
  constraints... increase significantly sequentially" language — comes only
  from the earnings call, which Apple does not file with the SEC or publish
  as a written transcript. These figures rest entirely on two independently
  published secondary transcriptions (sources 13, 14) that agree word-for-word
  on the key quotes, which is as close to verified as this claim can get
  without an Apple-authored transcript.

- **Operating cash flow: no quarterly figure is disclosed.** Apple's 8-K and
  10-Q both present the cash flow statement on a nine-month cumulative basis
  only ($116,996M for the nine months ended June 27, 2026, vs $81,754M for
  the nine months ended June 28, 2025). No document states the Q3-only
  figure. I computed an implied Q3 OCF by subtracting the Q2 FY2026 10-Q's
  six-month cumulative figure ($82,627M, from source 3) from the Q3 10-Q's
  nine-month figure: $116,996M − $82,627M = **$34,369M** implied Q3 FY2026
  operating cash flow (prior year: $81,754M − $53,887M = $27,867M implied Q3
  FY2025, ≈ +23.3% YoY). This is my synthesis from two primary cumulative
  figures, not a number Apple itself states as a quarterly line; the CFO's
  claim that the quarter set "new June quarter records for both EPS and
  operating cash flow" (source 1) is consistent with this computed figure
  being a record, but Apple does not confirm the dollar amount directly.

- **The "hardware upgrade cycle" story is not uniform across hardware.**
  iPhone (+21.7%) and Mac (+28.6%) surged, but iPad revenue *declined*
  year over year (-5.9%, per Apple's own 10-Q: "iPad net sales decreased
  during the third quarter of 2026... primarily due to lower net sales of
  iPad mini and iPad Air"). A framing that treats "hardware" as one
  undifferentiated cyclical story understates that one hardware line
  shrank while two others drove the beat.

- **Geographic mix has its own miss, outside the Services/hardware axis.**
  Tech Times (source 12) reports Greater China revenue of $18.8B against an
  estimated $19.5B — a geographic miss layered under the Services miss. This
  is a secondary-sourced estimate (no comparable Apple-disclosed geographic
  consensus figure exists), and it is a different axis from the
  Services-vs-hardware question the commission poses; it is offered here as
  a fact the researcher found, not as material the article is obligated to
  use.

- **CNBC and Gurufocus could not be verified and are not used for any
  claim.** Both returned HTTP 403 to WebFetch and to a direct browser-agent
  request; per policy this is treated as gated rather than dead, but since no
  content was retrievable, no claim in this record depends on either. See
  Discarded.

## Source assets

- **Source 1 (8-K Exhibit 99.1) — segment/product-line revenue table.** The
  "Net sales by category" table (iPhone, Mac, iPad, Wearables/Home/
  Accessories, Services, each with current- and prior-year quarter dollar
  figures) is a clean, already-tabular primary source that could support a
  stacked-bar or grouped-bar chart of product-line revenue and YoY growth
  without needing to derive numbers. A crop or transcription should retain
  both years' dollar figures per line (not just the YoY %), since the dollar
  base is what lets a reader judge which categories are large versus merely
  fast-growing (iPhone dwarfs iPad in dollar terms even though both moved by
  double digits in opposite directions).

- **Source 1/2 — gross margin bridge.** The Products vs. Services gross
  margin table in the 10-Q (source 2: $31,525M/40.1% Products,
  $23,245M/75.6% Services) paired with the press release's tariff-refund
  quantification (~2pt / ~$0.11) is source material for a margin-bridge
  chart or annotated stat strip: total gross margin 50.1% minus the ~2pt
  tariff-refund component isolates the "underlying" ~48.1% margin from the
  one-time component. A caption using this must state plainly that the ~2pt
  figure is Apple's own approximation ("approximately"), not a precise
  audited allocation.

- **Services quarterly time series (table above) — deceleration/
  reacceleration chart.** Seven quarters of Services revenue and YoY growth,
  each figure sourced to a specific 8-K or 10-Q, is ready for a `nb chart`
  line or bar chart of Services YoY growth by quarter. The series is not a
  clean straight-line deceleration — it rose from +11.6% (Q2 FY2025) to
  +16.3% (Q2 FY2026) before falling to +12.1% (Q3 FY2026) — so a caption or
  the surrounding prose should not claim a smooth multi-quarter decline; the
  honest reading is deceleration in the most recent quarter against a
  choppier longer trend.

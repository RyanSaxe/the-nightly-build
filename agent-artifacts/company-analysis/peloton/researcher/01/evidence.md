# Evidence: company-analysis/peloton (01)

The record supports the commission's core fact and its central tension. Peloton's
own SEC-filed fiscal 2026 statements confirm the first full-year GAAP net income
in company history, $63.2 million, and confirm that it arrived while the top line
kept shrinking: total revenue fell 1.8% to $2,446.0 million, connected-fitness
hardware revenue fell 5.7%, and paid connected-fitness subscriptions fell 8.8% to
2.553 million, with quarterly churn rising to 2.2% from 1.8%. The income statement
shows the swing to profit was overwhelmingly a cost story, not a growth story: of
the roughly $197 million improvement in operating result, about $178 million came
from a fall in operating expenses and only about $18 million from higher gross
profit, and the profit carries essentially no tax, so it is not a tax artifact.
The durable side of the case is also real and sourced: subscription gross margin
is high and rising (73.6% in Q4), subscription revenue held flat while
subscribers fell 8.8% (a pricing/ARPU effect), adjusted EBITDA rose 16% to
$468.2 million, and free cash flow rose 17% to $377.6 million. Where the record is
thinnest: (1) reported free cash flow leans heavily on a $198.6 million
stock-based-compensation add-back and an $81.3 million inventory drawdown that
cannot recur indefinitely, so "cash generation" needs the reconciliation shown,
not asserted; (2) the exact size of the post-earnings stock drop is reported
inconsistently across secondary outlets (roughly 11% to 15.6%) and is not pinned
to a primary market-data source; (3) Peloton discloses a churn rate only for
connected fitness, not for its app tier, so the "churn behaves differently per
stream" teaching point is only partly supported by disclosure. The evidence does
not undermine the commissioned angle. It sharpens it: the disagreement the
commission wants is visibly inside the filing.

## Contradictions

- Size of the market reaction is reported inconsistently across secondaries and
  is not confirmed against a primary market-data feed. Reported same-day drops:
  about 11.1% (Yahoo, Simply Wall St), about 12% (MoneyCheck), about 14.3% to
  $5.585 intraday (search summary), about 15.57% (StockTitan). The direction (a
  sharp fall) and the cause (FY2027 revenue guide of $2.3B-$2.4B below roughly
  $2.44B consensus, plus continued subscriber decline) are consistent; the exact
  percentage is not. The writer should give a sourced range, not a single figure,
  or cite a market-data primary if one is added.

- Profit versus the record's own top line. The company frames FY2026 as a
  turnaround "driven by our improved revenue trajectory," yet its own income
  statement shows revenue down 1.8% year over year and down every year since
  FY2022, and it guides FY2027 revenue down a further roughly 4%. The improvement
  is in profitability and cash, not in revenue direction. This is the tension the
  commission asks the piece to hold; it is internal to Peloton's own materials.

- Composition of the operating swing. Peloton attributes the profit partly to
  revenue and partly to cost structure. The line items show the cost side did the
  work: operating income improved about $197M year over year, of which gross
  profit contributed about $18M and lower operating expenses about $178M. Within
  that opex reduction, roughly $70M is the fall or absence of non-operating-quality
  charges (impairment down about $30M, restructuring down about $16M, supplier
  settlements down $23.5M to zero) and about $97M is lower general and
  administrative expense.

- "Different churn per stream" is only partly disclosed. Peloton reports a churn
  rate for connected fitness (2.2% in Q4, up from 1.8%) but does not report a
  churn rate for its app tier; only ending app subscriptions (0.503M, down about
  9%) are given. A claim that the two streams churn differently can be supported
  for connected fitness by rate and for app only by the subscriber decline, not by
  a comparable rate.

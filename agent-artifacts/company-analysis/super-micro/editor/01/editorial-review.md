# Editorial review: company-analysis/super-micro (editor/01)

## Skeptic

Thesis: Super Micro's fiscal 2026 revenue surge and its Q4 margin jump did not
turn into cash. Reported net income of $2.23B ran against operating cash flow of
negative $6.81B, a gap of about $9B that went into inventory and receivables and
was covered by new debt and preferred stock rather than cash from operations.
And the 17.5% Q4 gross margin reads as one mix-driven quarter at the end of a
two-year decline, not a durable step. The piece stands on four claims.

1. The margin figures and the full-year-versus-quarter contrast. Q4 GAAP gross
   margin 17.5% (17.47% computed from gross profit $1,942,631K / net sales
   $11,119,777K), against 9.9% the prior quarter and 9.5% a year earlier, while
   full-year FY2026 was only 10.8%, barely above FY2025's 11.1%. Verified against
   the 8-K exhibit (s1) and the Q3 10-Q (s2). The full-year figure is the honest
   complication the piece leads with, and it holds. Kept.

2. The eight-quarter series shows the spike sits at the end of a decline, not on
   a stable base. The chart's values (13.06, 11.80, 9.57, 9.45, 9.31, 6.30, 9.95,
   17.47) match the evidence record's verified series line for line. The prose
   claim that margin "fell in five consecutive quarters, from 13.1% ... to 6.3%
   ... before turning up to 9.9% and then to 17.5%" is accurate. But the two
   sentences that followed overstated it: "the four quarters immediately before
   this one moved in the opposite direction" is false, because the last of those
   four (Q3 FY26, 9.95%) rose from the 6.30% low, and the piece's own prior
   sentence says so. I cut both sentences and replaced them with one accurate
   line. Fixed in place.

3. The cash-conversion spine. I recomputed every figure. Net income $2,230,453K
   against operating cash flow $(6,809,886)K is a $9.04B gap, "roughly $9.0
   billion" as printed. Inventory absorbed $8,876,747K, receivables $3,921,872K,
   payables provided $963,258K, all as stated. The derived second-half payables
   swing checks: full-year $963,258K minus the H1 $12,466,768K equals
   $(11,503,510)K, "about $11.50 billion," and the piece labels it as computed
   from two filings rather than stated in one. Financing raised $9,478,755K
   ($4,468,808K new debt plus the $4,226,258K preferred line, "and smaller items
   besides"). The direction is correct throughout: profit positive, operating
   cash negative, the difference financed. This is the strongest part of the
   piece and it survives every check. Kept.

4. The scrutiny section, including the sensitive DOJ passage. The Hindenburg
   allegations (s9) are framed as the accuser's claims, with the short position
   disclosed and no adoption as fact. The material-weakness disclosure (s10), the
   EY resignation (s11), the Special Committee's contradicting finding (s12), and
   the Nasdaq recompliance (s13) are each supported by the primary that owns them.
   The DOJ passage attributes the charges to the three named individuals, states
   Super Micro was not a defendant, carries the company's "victim" statement
   (s16), and closes with an explicit disclaimer that nothing in the record ties
   the export-control charges to the reported numbers. Substantively this meets
   the standard the brief set. One citation breaks, below.

Citations. I opened all sixteen hrefs as printed. Every one resolves, and each
supports the claim it is cited for, with a single exception on the sensitive
passage. Source 15's href points to Exhibit 99.1 (a991.htm). That exhibit does
carry "Supermicro is not named as a defendant," "a contravention of the Company's
policies and compliance controls," and "maintains a robust compliance program."
It does not contain the Liaw board-resignation language, and the sentence "a
departure the company stated was 'not the result of a disagreement with the
Company'" is cited to s15. That language lives in the same filing's Item 5.02
body (smci-20260320.htm), a different document. A reader clicking [15] on the
resignation sentence lands on a document that lacks it. This is a citation-landing
failure on exactly the passage the brief told me to hold to the standard, and it
goes to the writer.

data-nb-kind audit. All labels are correct under the researcher's authorship-and-
stake test. The six SEC filings and the company IR posting are the authoring
party's own documents (primary). Hindenburg is primary for the allegation it
owns, and the piece uses it only that way. The two earnings-call retellings (s7,
s8) and the Al Jazeera and Yahoo reports (s14, s16) are outside accounts
(secondary). The two call retellings are correctly treated as one origin, carried
as corroborating and marked "reported on the call, not filed," not as the
load-bearing durability evidence, which is the verified eight-quarter series. The
attribution is honest and the reliance is appropriate.

One directional check to confirm at the source: the article dates the alleged
conduct "between 2024 and 2025," which the fiscal-year-overlap point in the last
paragraph rests on. The researcher recorded that window from Al Jazeera (s14); my
fetch's reader did not surface it. Low risk, but worth a confirm.

## Cut

Two sentences failed and were cut, plus one reflex mark repaired.

- The two outlier-section sentences described under Skeptic claim 2: one factually
  wrong ("four quarters ... moved in the opposite direction") and redundant with
  the sentence before it. Deleted, with one accurate replacement line.
- The nb-note closed on "Treat it as reported, not filed," an instruction to the
  reader that restates the note's own label and the two sentences before it. It
  loses no fact on deletion. Cut. The nb-note has no documented allowance to
  address the reader, so it is held like any other prose.
- The same nb-note joined two independent clauses with a semicolon where a period
  is the default. Repaired to a period.

Edges, delete test, and dangling-referent pass otherwise came back clean. The
section closers ("a bigger balance sheet financed by new capital, not cash the
business generated on its own"; "as a number next to net income, not as a
percentage in a press release") state the conclusion the argument built and are
kept. The negative-parallelism constructions that remain each correct a
misconception the piece actually names: the assembler's margin is a pass-through
spread and not an IP markup, and the durability question is what the verified
numbers, not the press release, answer. Those are earned and stay.

No prompt or brief leakage. The two-question frame and the cash-versus-working-
capital question are stated in the article's own terms with its own figures, not
lifted from the commission. No distinctive phrasing borrowed from the voice
guide's Rubinstein, Hobart, or Damodaran quotations appears in the draft.

Recent-pattern check against the Cerebras piece: no colon "two numbers, same
quarter" heading, no "what would actually have to change" closer, and no closing
nb-note-strong verdict box. The headings are varied in build and each names a step
of this argument. The stat strip is used once, sanctioned by the commission, and
does not open the piece. The piece is distinct.

No buy, sell, or allocation call appears. The FY2027 guidance is handled as the
company's own claim, "not yet a number a filing has delivered on."

## Reader

Read straight through, the piece gives the reader a synthesis no single filing or
release states: that the headline margin jump and the 78% revenue growth did not
become cash, because the growth piled into inventory and receivables and was
funded by new capital, and that 17.5% is one mix-driven quarter at the end of a
two-year decline. That is exactly the cross-filing assembly the draft handoff's
original-work sentence claims, and it survives the read. The prose sits closer to
the voice-guide exemplars than to a median summary: figures carry the argument,
the profit sits next to the cash and the margin next to the revenue, the
assembler mechanism is taught before its numbers arrive, and the guidance is named
as a claim the piece declines to convert into a verdict. The headline, read as the
largest claim, is two verified figures whose juxtaposition is the finding. It
holds.

### Contradictions

- The headline 17.5% Q4 margin and the 10.8% GAAP / 10.9% non-GAAP full-year FY2026 margin are both true and describe different things: the year's margin barely moved from FY2025's 11.1% (10.8% vs 11.1%, non-GAAP 10.9% vs 11.2%), while the quarter more than doubled sequentially. A reader shown only the quarter figure would not learn that FY2026 was, on the company's own full-year GAAP number, a slightly worse-margin year than FY2025.
- Reported profit and operating cash are opposite-signed at the full-year level: net income $2,230,453K (primary, 8-K exhibit) vs. operating cash flow $(6,809,886)K (same source). This is the commission's central question and it is answered directly by the primary filing, not inferred.
- The company's own preliminary update (Aug 5, primary) had already guided gross margin far above its original 8.2%-8.4% guide, to 15%-17%, six days before the final 17.5%/17.6% print -- so the "surprise" framing common in coverage is weaker than it looks against the primary record; the company was already signaling most of the beat before the formal release.
- The specific causal breakdown of the margin jump (75% mix / 25% tariffs and inventory reserves) and the Q1 FY2027 margin guidance of 10.4%-10.8% exist ONLY in secondary earnings-call reporting, not in the SEC filing or IR press release I was instructed to treat as primary for this figure. I confirmed the absence directly (full-text search of the fetched primary exhibit text found no percentage margin guidance and no such breakdown anywhere in the document). If the 10.4%-10.8% figure is going to carry real weight in the article's durability argument -- and it is the single most on-point piece of evidence for "not durable," since it is management's own next-quarter forecast -- the writer and editor should know it is sourced to two accounts of the same call, not to the filing, and I could not verify it against a company transcript or recording.
- The Special Committee's finding ("does not believe... is supported by the facts uncovered") directly and explicitly contradicts EY's resignation letter in the primary record. - The Special Committee's finding that management's "tone at the top... is appropriate" and the company's insistence it did not expect a restatement both predate, and are silent on, the entirely separate March 2026 chip-smuggling indictment of a different, senior insider (a cofounder and board member) for conduct alleged to have occurred in 2024-2025 -- overlapping the same fiscal years whose revenue and inventory this evidence record covers. - Board's November 2024 commitment to "commence a search for a new Chief Financial Officer immediately" appears not to have concluded: reporting as recent as the Q4 FY2026 earnings-call coverage above still attributes CFO-level commentary to David Weigand, the same person the board said it would replace, roughly 21 months after the recruitment commitment.

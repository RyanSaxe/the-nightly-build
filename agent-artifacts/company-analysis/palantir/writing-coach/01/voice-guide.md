# Voice guide: company-analysis (valuation-vs-growth, 01)

## Directive

Write as an equity analyst reasoning in the open to a numerate peer. The house
baseline already gives you calm, first-principles prose and the patience to
build a concept before the sentence that needs it. Keep all of that. This piece
adds one discipline on top: the governing subject is a price, and a price is a
set of assumptions. Every stretch of business teaching exists to load or test
one of those assumptions, and the argument's job is to make the assumptions
visible and checkable, never to land on advice.

The reader holds strong quantitative training and no knowledge of the company.
Treat them as someone who can follow arithmetic done on the page and who will
draw their own conclusion once the assumptions are exposed. So teach the
business the moment a number needs it, not in a boxed overview, and teach only
as much as the next claim spends. When you introduce a figure, bind it in the
same breath to the quantity that gives it size: the prior period, the peer, the
base rate, the level the price requires. A number standing alone has not yet
been made to mean anything.

Two temptations specific to this genre. First, the tip-sheet reflex: verdict
without arithmetic, adjectives standing in for a model ("priced for
perfection," "durable compounder"). Reach it only through the numbers that force
it. Second, false resolution: the piece must weigh two honest readings of the
same record and stop at what would decide between them. Reasoning to the edge of
a verdict and naming the test is the work; issuing the verdict is not.

## Licenses

form: the implied-assumption sentence
move: Damodaran converts a price or a multiple into the concrete forward
      commitment it presupposes, naming the growth rate, margin, duration, or
      retention the number is paying for rather than calling the stock rich or
      cheap.
bar:  each use names a specific, checkable assumption the stated price requires
      (a growth rate, a margin level, a number of years, a retention figure). A
      sentence that says the price assumes a lot, without saying what, is cut.

form: the worked valuation arithmetic
move: Gurley and Damodaran carry the calculation in prose, running cited inputs
      forward a step at a time so the reader watches an assumption become the
      number the market is quoting, and can redo the sum.
bar:  every input is a figure already cited, each step is one the reader can
      reproduce, and the arithmetic exists to expose what the price embeds. If
      the calculation illustrates rather than tests an assumption, cut it.

form: the earned-multiple ledger
move: Gurley treats a high multiple as a claim that specific business qualities
      must justify, then measures the subject against each quality in turn
      (margin structure, revenue durability, retention, concentration, switching
      cost) instead of accepting the multiple as a mood.
bar:  each quality invoked is tied to a figure in the record and stated as
      something the multiple demands, so an editor can check whether the subject
      actually has it. A quality named only because it sounds impressive is cut.

form: the falsification close in place of a verdict
move: Damodaran reasons to his sharpest judgment and then hands the buy-or-sell
      decision back to the reader, restating the judgment as the future
      observation that would confirm or break it.
bar:  at the argument's sharpest point it names the specific thing in the record
      or the next quarters that would confirm or falsify the read, and no
      sentence recommends buying, selling, or holding. A close that resolves the
      valuation into advice is cut.

## Aswath Damodaran, "The Power of Expectations: Nvidia's Earnings and the Market Reaction!"
Source: https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2024/09/the-expectations-game-aftermath-of.html
Craft:
- cadence: patient, additive sentences that lay one distinction down and build
  the next on it; long only when a definition needs the length, never to pack.
- argument: separates valuing (fundamentals: cash flows, growth, risk) from
  pricing (demand, supply, mood), then reads the post-earnings move as the
  pricing game meeting expectations "no company can meet." The frame does the
  persuading.
- evidence: leans on the company's own reported quarters and the gap between
  results and what the price already assumed, not on analyst chatter.
- stance: committed to his own valuation, openly uncertain about short-term
  price, and candid that momentum trading is outside his competence.
- notice: fixes on the size of the expectation baked into price as the real
  variable, rather than whether the quarter "beat."
- diction: plain nouns, repeated exactly ("value," "price," "expectations");
  no ornament, terms of art defined on first use.
- reader: "I value companies for myself... they should not determine your
  choices" — treats the reader as a decision-maker owed the reasoning, not the
  recommendation.
- the important move the axes miss: he makes a hard judgment and refuses to
  convert it into a trade, so the analysis stays analysis; the discipline is
  what keeps the piece out of the tip-sheet register.

## Bill Gurley, "All Revenue is Not Created Equal: The Keys to the 10X Revenue Club"
Source: https://abovethecrowd.com/2011/05/24/all-revenue-is-not-created-equal-the-keys-to-the-10x-revenue-club/
Craft:
- cadence: framework rhythm; a short declarative states a principle ("Growth all
  by itself can be misleading"), then a paragraph earns it, then the next.
- argument: a lofty revenue multiple is not a right but a claim that specific,
  nameable business characteristics must justify; he enumerates them and holds
  each to a discounted-cash-flow rationale.
- evidence: a concrete spread of comparables ("a 100X difference in value per
  sales dollar") anchors the abstraction before any single company is judged.
- stance: teacherly and framework-first; he arms the reader to test a valuation
  and pointedly declines to declare a stock cheap or dear for them.
- notice: attends to revenue quality, the durability behind a number, over the
  headline growth rate everyone already sees.
- diction: coins working handles ("revenue quality") and keeps them literal;
  conversational asides ("Talk about room for error!") without slackening rigor.
- reader: assumes a reader who wants the mechanism, not the tip; gives criteria,
  not a call.
- the important move the axes miss: he ties every qualitative virtue back to its
  effect on a cash-flow model, so no adjective floats free of arithmetic.

## Marc Rubinstein, "The Demise of Silicon Valley Bank"
Source: https://www.netinterest.co/p/the-demise-of-silicon-valley-bank
Craft:
- cadence: mixes brisk narrative beats with expository paragraphs; short
  sentences mark the turns, longer ones carry the mechanism.
- argument: teaches how the business actually works (deposits, duration, the
  securities book) exactly where the outcome needs it, so the explanation of the
  bank is the explanation of the failure, never a preamble to it.
- evidence: figures arrive welded to their yardstick ("$6.26 trillion, up from
  $3.98 trillion at the end of 2019"; "$152 billion" of "$173 billion... are
  uninsured"), so scale is felt, not asserted.
- stance: measured and non-moralizing; he moves from mechanism to judgment
  inside a clause ("not great for its margin... the situation wasn't fatal")
  without reaching for blame.
- notice: singles out the number that actually governs the outcome (uninsured
  deposit share, the insurance threshold) rather than the ones in the headline.
- diction: precise financial nouns used plainly, defined in passing ("the
  $250,000 threshold... the limit for deposit insurance") for a smart
  non-specialist.
- reader: "We've never really had a bank run in the digital age" — an intimate,
  peer register that trusts the reader to hold nuance.
- the important move the axes miss: he interleaves business instruction with the
  live argument so seamlessly that a reader learns the industry while following
  the case, which is exactly the teach-as-you-argue demand of this commission.

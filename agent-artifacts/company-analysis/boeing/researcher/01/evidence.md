# Evidence record — company-analysis/boeing (invocation 01)

Locked quarter: **Q2 2026**, the three months ended June 30, 2026 (and the six
months ended June 30, 2026), reported by Boeing on July 28, 2026. The primary
filings are Boeing's Form 10-Q for that period and the earnings press-release
exhibit (EX-99.1) to the same-day Form 8-K. The evidence strongly supports the
article's spine: Boeing's operating cash flow swung from a large deficit a year
ago to a positive figure, but the entire swing — and more — is accounted for by a
$4,660M increase in customer advances (a working-capital inflow), while free cash
flow for the half was still negative $823M after capital expenditures. Inventory
consumed $3,859M of cash over the half as production ramps. The headline
operational recovery (revenue +8%, deliveries up, narrowing segment losses, record
$715B backlog) is real and independently confirmed. What the evidence is thin on:
Boeing does not itemize every program's reach-forward loss in the 10-Q excerpt
readable through conversion tools (the VC-25B $280M loss is disclosed in the
release; a full net catch-up figure of $(381)M for the half is disclosed); and
production-rate narrative in the 10-Q MD&A did not survive document conversion, so
the 737 rate cap is carried by an independent secondary as context only. All
figures below are verified against the owning primary except those explicitly
marked "derived by subtraction," which are arithmetic from primary cumulative
releases.

## Contradictions

1. **Headline vs. cash.** Boeing's own release leads with a "record" backlog and
   an operating-cash-flow figure up ~500% year over year, framing recovery. The
   10-Q cash-flow statement shows the same operating cash flow rests on a $4,660M
   customer-advance inflow while inventories consumed $3,859M; free cash flow for
   the half is still $(823)M. Both are Boeing's own primary figures. The writer
   must weigh the release's framing against the statement that owns the detail.

2. **Operating cash up, free cash negative.** Q2 alone shows FCF positive ($631M),
   but the six-month FCF is negative ($823M) because Q1 is deeply negative — cash
   flow is highly seasonal. A single positive quarter does not establish a trend.

3. **Steelman (advances are healthy).** A rising advances balance is the normal
   signature of a growing order book and a production ramp, not a warning by
   itself; customers always prepay in this industry. The counterweight: advances
   are a liability drawn down as jets deliver, so the cash is borrowed from future
   deliveries. The recovery is "real cash conversion" only when delivered-jet
   margin, not new deposits, carries free cash flow positive across a full year —
   which 2025 (FCF $(1,877)M) and H1 2026 (FCF $(823)M) do not yet show.

4. **Damodaran caution (external, on method).** A single period's free cash flow
   is a noisier signal of operating health than earnings
   (https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2022/10/earnings-and-cash-flows-primer-on-free.html).
   This argues against over-reading either the green Q2 FCF print or one bad
   quarter; the article should reason from the working-capital mechanism, not one
   number. Editorial caution, not a claim to cite in the body.

# Evidence: company-analysis/eli-lilly (01)

The evidence strongly supports the *mechanical* claim that volume is outrunning
price right now: Lilly's own Q2 2026 earnings release decomposes 48% worldwide
revenue growth into a 60% volume increase against a 13% decline in realized
prices, and the ex-US line into 113% volume against a 36% price drop. It is
weaker, and in places actively cuts against, the *durability* half of the
commission. Three primary-sourced facts complicate the bullish read: worldwide
realized price fell 13% in both Q1 and Q2 (persistent, not a one-off), ex-US
price erosion is deepening quarter-on-quarter (-25% in Q1 to -36% in Q2 as
China's NRDL pricing works through the base), and the headline US price decline
of just 3% is flattered by favorable rebate/discount estimate adjustments that
Lilly and its CFO both say masked an underlying ~9% US price decline. The
guidance walk ($80-83B to $82-85B to $85-87B across the year) is well
documented. The one place the evidence directly undercuts the commission's
framing is the tariff: the 100% branded-pharma tariff exists and is real, but
Lilly's own 10-Q states pharma is "exempt from certain of these tariffs," the
filings quantify no tariff hit, and the proclamation's exemption paths
(onshoring plan; MFN pricing agreement) are ones Lilly appears to satisfy, so
the tariff reads as a contingent future risk to Lilly specifically rather than a
live overhang already pressing on the US price leg. The record is thin on any
independently quantified FX contribution and on forward price-erosion guidance;
management gave color, not numbers.

## Contradictions

- **Volume vs price, top line vs durability.** The primaries confirm volume is
  outrunning price *this quarter* on every geography. But the same primaries
  show price erosion is not transient: worldwide realized price fell 13% in both
  Q1 and Q2 2026, and ex-US price erosion deepened from -25% (Q1) to -36% (Q2).
  The "volume outruns price" angle is true as description and contestable as
  forecast; the record equips both readings.
- **The reported US price decline understates the real one.** The release
  headlines a -3% US realized-price change, but states that excluding favorable
  rebate/discount estimate adjustments US price would have fallen ~9%; the CFO
  repeated the 9% figure on the call. Q1 2026 also leaned on "a favorable
  one-time adjustment to estimates for rebates and discounts." A writer citing
  the -3% as evidence of US price resilience would be citing an
  accounting-flattered number against Lilly's own caveat.
- **The tariff framing.** The commission calls the 100% branded-drug tariff "a
  live overhang on the US price leg." The evidence complicates this for Lilly
  specifically: the 10-Q says pharma is "exempt from certain of these tariffs";
  the proclamation exempts companies with an approved onshoring plan (20% rate)
  or an MFN pricing agreement (zero until Jan 2029); Lilly is expanding US
  manufacturing ($4.5B more into Indiana this quarter) and references "a
  voluntary agreement with the U.S. government related to drug pricing and
  access." No filing quantifies any tariff hit, and guidance excludes only
  post-6/30 acquired IPR&D. The tariff is better characterized as a contingent
  future risk if exemptions lapse than as a current pressure on realized price.
  (Also note a date mismatch to resolve: the commission says the tariff took
  effect 2026-08-04; the proclamation sets July 31, 2026 for Annex III large
  companies such as Lilly.)
- **Structural threats to the volume ramp.** Working against a "durable volume
  ramp" read: in July 2026 multiple generic companies filed ANDAs against
  Mounjaro and/or Zepbound (an earlier-than-assumed patent-challenge front), and
  the oral small molecule Foundayo — which management touts precisely because it
  "can be produced at scale" — implies a lower price point and a mix shift as it
  grows. Both cut toward the "pull-forward against eroding price" interpretation.
- **Guidance quality.** The raised guidance is partly aided by items that are
  not clean recurring volume: a $250M Jardiance ex-US milestone in Q2 (and again
  in Q1), and favorable rebate/discount estimate changes in the US. These do not
  contradict the growth, but they mean the beat is not a pure volume signal.
- **FX.** The stated volume/price splits do not sum to reported revenue growth
  (worldwide 60% - 13% = 47% vs 48% reported), implying a small FX/other
  residual that Lilly does not separately quantify in the release body
  (constant-currency figures are referenced as living in "related materials").

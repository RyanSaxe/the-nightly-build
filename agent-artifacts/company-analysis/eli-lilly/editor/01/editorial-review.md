# Editorial review: company-analysis/eli-lilly (editor/01)

## Skeptic

**Thesis (from the draft alone).** Lilly's 48% Q2 2026 revenue growth is one
trade repeated market by market: buying volume by accepting a lower realized
price per dose. The raised guidance bets that trade stays winning. The article's
finding is on the durability half — the price leg is not a one-off but a
persistent, in places deepening, decline, and the volume leg is decelerating, so
the margin between the two is narrowing from both ends.

**Load-bearing claims and how each held:**

1. *The decomposition: worldwide +60% volume against −13% price yields +48%
   revenue (US +37%/−3%; ex-US +113%/−36%).* Opened s1 (Q2 release) and s2 (10-Q)
   as opponents. Every figure matches to the primary. Arithmetic checks: 60−13=47
   (a point short of the reported 48), 113−36=77 (vs 80 reported). The piece
   states both residuals openly and does not paper over them. Held.

2. *The decomposition is Lilly's OWN reported split, not independently derived.*
   This is the brief's central honesty test, and the article passes it cleanly:
   it says outright that Lilly "publishes no unit counts and no separate line for
   product mix, so the split cannot be rebuilt from outside the company," and
   frames the numbers as "Lilly's own decomposition, taken as reported and
   corroborated only by the internal consistency of the per-product and
   geographic tables." No manufactured "mix" leg. The unquantified FX/other
   residual is named. This is exactly the handling the commission required. Held,
   and it is the strongest thing in the piece.

3. *The falling price is persistent, not a one-off.* Worldwide −13% in both Q1
   and Q2 (s3, s1); ex-US deepening −25%→−36% (s3, s1). Tried to break it with the
   bull read (volume still massively outruns price this quarter) — the article
   concedes that ("not a close trade this quarter") and rests durability on the
   trajectory, with a falsifiable condition named in the sentence that makes it
   (the ex-US line stepping down again as ex-US volume cools). Meets the voice
   guide's falsifiable-durability license. Held.

4. *The US −3% is an accounting artifact; underlying ~−9%.* This is the writer's
   open question #1. Confirmed honest: the ~9% figure is attributed to the owning
   primary release (s1), with the CFO's spoken confirmation cited to the
   third-party transcript (s4, secondary) as corroboration only. Nothing is
   quoted from a source that merely reports it. I fetched s4 and confirmed the CFO
   line ("U.S. price declined by 9%") is present. Held.

**Breaks found and their fixes:**

- *"raised three times in six months."* The article's own verdict schedule shows
  initial guidance ($80–83B, Feb) plus two raises (Apr $82–85B, Aug $85–87B) —
  two raises, not three. The evidence walk agrees. Corrected directly to "twice"
  (the right figure was in the article's own table and the cited releases).

- *"a small molecule can be made at scale and at low cost."* I fetched s4: Ricks
  said "small molecule that can be produced at scale"; the transcript does NOT
  characterize it as "at low cost." The "at low cost" extension is unsupported by
  the cited source, so I cut it. The surviving "made at scale ... which is also a
  case for a lower price per patient" carries the mix-shift argument on what the
  source actually says.

**Display-text audit.** Headline ("guidance keeps rising as its prices keep
falling") — both halves true and defended. Dek — a claim about the world, two
clauses joined by "and," no colon subtitle, no comma-triad or hedged-contrast
mold, does not restate the headline. Every section heading is a step of the
argument in the piece's own nouns; shapes vary (gerund, imperative, statement,
question, numeral). China heading ("doubled its revenue as its price fell") —
$466M→$941M is 2.02x, ex-US price −36%; accurate. Table cells, chart caption, and
figure numbers all trace to the primary. No wrong label found in display text.

**data-nb-kind audit.** All eight correct. s1/s3/s7 are Lilly's own earnings
releases and s2 its 10-Q — primary (they own the claims), and SEC hosting does
not demote them. s5 (White House proclamation) is primary for the policy. s4
(third-party transcript), s6 (Crowell alert), s8 (CNBC) are secondary and labeled
so. No secondary is doing a primary's job: the −9% and the decomposition ride on

**Citation href audit.** Opened all eight as printed. s1, s2, s3, s4, s5, s7
resolve to the source's own page and confirm the figures cited to them. s6
(Crowell) and s8 (CNBC) returned HTTP 403 to the automated fetcher — both are
sites that routinely block scrapers, the URLs are well-formed and name the

**Tariff framing.** Correct per the brief: a contingent future risk Lilly is
largely exempt from, not a live US-price overhang. Rate, Annex III effective date
(July 31, 2026 — the corrected date, verified against s5), the 20% onshoring and
0%-until-Jan-2029 MFN paths, the 10-Q "exempt from certain of these tariffs"
language, the $4.5B Indiana expansion, and generics/biosimilars excluded are all
present and sourced. The paragraph explicitly says no filing quantifies any
tariff cost and that it belongs "as a future risk should the exemptions lapse,
not as a weight already pressing on the US price line." One of three risk
sub-points, no more. Correct.

**Buy/sell/allocation call.** None. The verdict speaks only to which leg carries
the growth and the condition under which it stops ("stays a winning one only
while the price legs stop steepening"). Confirmed absent.

## Cut

Ran the earns-its-place test sentence by sentence. Direct cuts:

- *"and the size of that opposing movement is the quarter's whole story"* (the
  Realized-price note). This is the banned "X is the whole Y" self-grading
  family named in the editorial direction — a sentence announcing its own stakes
  instead of making the argument. The surviving line ("Volume and realized price
  move in opposite directions by design") carries the teaching.

- *"so the place to start is by pulling the growth number into its parts"* (end
  of the orientation). A signpost describing where the piece will go, redundant
  with the next section's heading. Orientation now ends on the stronger claim
  ("Whether that reading holds is a question about each leg on its own").

- *"The pill that widens the market is the same pill that lowers the average
  price of it."* A packaged antithesis closer that restates the immediately
  preceding sentence (mix shift pulling blended price down) and adds no new fact,
  claim, or step. The paragraph now ends on the factual line it earned.

The worst tell was the "whole story" punchline; the signpost and the antithesis
closer are the same instinct in milder form. No repeated cross-article formula
survived. The recent-pattern traps were all broken: the piece does not open on
the beat-vs-bar headline number (it enters through the price/volume durability
question), does not march quarter-metric by quarter-metric (it is organized by
argument), and does not close on "two ways to read it" or a compounder
thesis-check. Punctuation is clean — no reflex em-dashes, one defensible
semicolon, colons used to introduce payoffs. This is a pharma piece and does not
read like the desk's recent AI-infrastructure work.

## Reader

Read straight through as the declared reader (an ML engineer, quantitatively
fluent, no prior Lilly knowledge). What I have that the sources alone would not
give me: the sources hand over per-quarter decomposition figures; the article
stitches Q1 and Q2 across three geographies into a durability judgment the raw
releases do not make — that worldwide price has printed −13% twice, ex-US erosion
is deepening while ex-US volume is enormous, the US "recovery" to −3% is an
accounting flatter over an underlying −9%, and US volume is decelerating — then
converts all of it into one falsifiable test (does the ex-US line step down again
from −36% as volume cools from +113%). The draft-handoff's original-work sentence
claims exactly this decomposition-into-a-single-test, and it survives the read.
The prose sits closer to the Damodaran/Hempton exemplars (decomposition resolved
on the page, durability stated as a checkable condition) than to a median summary.

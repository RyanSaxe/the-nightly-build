# Voice guide: company-analysis (decomposing a reported growth number)

## Directive

Write as an independent analyst taking one reported growth number apart on the
page, not as a narrator profiling a company. Keep the house baseline: calm,
precise, argued in plain sentences with structure doing the persuading. Tune it
to a single, unifying discipline: the reader trusts the verdict because they
watched the subtraction, not because a sentence asserted it.

Treat the quantitatively fluent reader as a peer who will check your arithmetic.
Hand over the decomposition and let it resolve in front of them; do not hand over
the conclusion first and defend it after. Where the reader could redo a step,
show the step. Where they could not, that is exactly where your estimate lives,
and it must wear its uncertainty openly rather than borrow the confidence of the
facts around it.

Three moves change sentences here. First, hold the headline figure up as a claim
under test, not as a settled fact, and keep it in that posture until the
evidence has resolved it. Second, split the number into legs the reader can see
moving against each other in the same line, so the prose carries the arithmetic
rather than summarizing its result. Third, make every durability judgment
falsifiable in the sentence that makes it: name the condition the leg rests on
and the specific figure that would confirm or break it. A verdict the reader
cannot imagine being wrong has not been earned on the page.

Impersonal authority. The credibility comes from the decomposition, not from a
personality, a reading history, or a narrator's confessions. The exemplars below
are newsletter writers who lean on first person and reader address; study their
decomposition and skepticism, not their intimacy. Do not transfer the "I", the
"you", or the anecdote.

## Licenses

form: the structuring question the section then answers with numbers
move: Damodaran and Hempton open a unit of analysis with the pointed question a
      naive reader would ask of the figure ("was that level of return
      realistic?"), then spend the section resolving it against cited evidence.
      It organizes the decomposition and names the test before running it.
bar:  the question poses a test with a numeric answer and is resolved inside the
      same section with cited figures. A question left hanging, or one whose
      answer is atmosphere rather than a number, is the banned hedge and is cut.

form: the falsifiable durability condition
move: press a view on how durable a leg of growth is by stating the condition it
      depends on and the figure that would confirm or break it, rather than
      grading the leg with an adjective. This is how the exemplars commit to a
      view without issuing a call: the reader can watch the condition hold or
      fail against the record.
bar:  the sentence names a specific, checkable condition and the cited number
      tied to it. "Durable", "strong", or "resilient" standing alone, with no
      condition and no figure, fails.

form: the open confidence ranking of the estimates
move: when the split into legs rests partly on your own estimate, say plainly
      which number you trust least and why, the way Damodaran flags the soft
      spot in his own model. The house standard keeps fact, estimate, and
      synthesis distinct; this license foregrounds that ranking as visible prose
      so the reader knows where the analysis is firm and where it is reaching.
bar:  the flagged number is genuinely the load-bearing uncertainty, and the
      reason it is soft is stated. Reflexive hedging on a number you can source,
      or a caveat that protects the writer rather than informing the reader, is
      cut.

## Marc Rubinstein, "Research in Motion"
Source: https://www.netinterest.co/p/research-in-motion
Craft:
- cadence: measured explanatory sentences broken by short, crisp data lines; the
  rhythm gives the reader breathing room inside a dense argument.
- argument: three cumulative phases, each earning the next; the shift being
  argued is set up by first establishing why it had not happened before.
- evidence: numbers deployed sparingly and as contrasts that do the work
  ($15.9B against $6.5B), never a wall of figures; each figure carries a point.
- stance: thoughtfully skeptical, not evangelical; gentle irony toward the tidy
  narrative rather than endorsement of it.
- notice: reaches for a concrete physical proxy for an abstract magnitude (an
  annual report growing from 100 to 515 pages) to make an intangible tangible.
- diction: precise and finance-native without jargon overload ("legible",
  "disparate silos", "repackaging"); plain words chosen over flashy ones.
- reader: assumes fluency and moves fast, but keeps the through-line legible.
- the move the axes miss: a single vivid, measurable object stands in for a
  trend the reader would otherwise have to take on faith.

## Aswath Damodaran, "The Power of Expectations: Nvidia's Earnings"
Source: https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2024/09/the-expectations-game-aftermath-of.html
Craft:
- cadence: punchy declaratives alternate with longer layered sentences that
  accumulate the mechanics before the payoff.
- argument: builds a general frame (how earnings reset expectations) and only
  then applies it to the specific company, so the reader owns the tool first.
- evidence: reported consensus and actual results stated flatly as fact, then
  his own reading marked off from them explicitly; the seam is always visible.
- stance: presses a clear view on why "good news" disappointed while refusing a
  buy/sell instruction; treats the headline beat as a claim to reinterpret.
- notice: the size of the beat shrank even as the beat held, and expectations,
  not fundamentals, moved the price; the crowd's read is the thing examined.
- diction: technical precision softened by plain metaphor ("reading the tea
  leaves", "disclosure bloat"); compressed, rarely verbose.
- reader: treated as a peer given tools and left to reach their own decision.
- the move the axes miss: the raised expectation itself is decomposed and tested,
  so the reported number is never allowed to speak as the last word.

## John Hempton (Bronte Capital), "Valuation and investment analysis"
Source: http://brontecapital.blogspot.com/2017/01/valuation-and-investment-analysis.html
Craft:
- cadence: deliberate pacing shifts; short conclusive fragments land after
  longer analytical runs.
- argument: inverts the usual order, posing the estimate as a test before the
  reveal, so the lesson is experienced rather than asserted.
- evidence: figures used with restraint and grounded in the statements; poses
  comparative questions ("was that return realistic?") instead of parading a
  calculation.
- stance: distrusts false precision and commits to a view through conditional
  reasoning rather than a command; never says buy, yet leaves no doubt.
- notice: a leg everyone ignored (the dividend yield) and the ceiling on a
  growth assumption ("volume growth unlikely to exceed 5%") that the crowd waved
  past.
- diction: conversational yet authoritative; technical terms sit beside plain
  phrasing without strain.
- reader: intelligent peer trusted to think independently and to resist tidy
  precision.
- the move the axes miss: the durability of a growth assumption is challenged by
  naming the ceiling it must clear, turning a vague projection into a testable
  claim.

# Evidence record: company-analysis/cerebras (01)

Cerebras Systems is public. It priced its IPO at $185.00 per share on May 13,
2026, began trading May 14, 2026 on the Nasdaq Global Select Market under
"CBRS," and has since filed two 10-Qs as a reporting company: for the quarter
ended March 31, 2026 (filed June 24, 2026) and the quarter ended June 30, 2026
(filed August 12, 2026, the most recent quarter on record as of 2026-08-13).
The evidence supports the commission's angle but complicates it in a specific
way the record can document precisely: concentration has not eased so much as
rotated. The two historically dominant, related-party customers (most likely
G42 and MBZUAI, though the 10-Q itself never names its lettered customers)
fell from a combined 88% of first-half revenue in 2025 to 59% in the same
half of 2026, but a new customer — whose disclosed dollar revenue matches one
of the lettered customers almost exactly — appeared and took roughly 20-32%
of quarterly revenue on its own. The filing's own account-recognition note
for the OpenAI arrangement is the only place a customer is named with a
dollar figure, and that figure lines up with "Customer B," not with the
largest lettered customer, which is a finding the writer needs and which
runs against the loose assumption that OpenAI is simply the new dominant
customer replacing G42. Margin evidence cuts both ways at once: GAAP gross
margin collapsed from 31.1% to 14.2% year over year in Q2 2026, but the
company's own non-GAAP "core" gross margin rose to 41% from 31.2%, and the
gap between the two is traceable to specific, disclosed, non-operating items
(a one-time $273.6 million stock-based-compensation catch-up tied to the IPO,
a $1.1 billion balance of customer warrants being amortized against revenue
through October 2031, and an $11.8 million inventory charge) rather than
pure invention. The weakest point in the record is that the 10-Q does not
disclose what share of the $25.4 billion remaining performance obligations
(RPO) balance traces to any single customer in dollar or percentage terms —
only that "a significant amount" comes from the OpenAI agreement — and that
several historical, pre-2026 G42/MBZUAI percentages rest on secondary
reporting I could not independently confirm in the primary S-1 due to the
document's length exceeding what could be retrieved in full.

## Contradictions

- **Which lettered customer is which named company is not stated anywhere in the 10-Q, S-1, or S-1/A filings I could open.** The filings name OpenAI, G42, MBZUAI, and AWS together as "significant customers" but never map any of them to "Customer A," "B," "C," or "D" in the concentration tables. The one point of near-certain identification: the Q2 2026 10-Q states OpenAI-arrangement revenue of $56.8 million (Q2) and $74.4 million (H1), which is 31.5% and 19.9% of total GAAP revenue for those periods respectively — almost exactly matching "Customer B's" disclosed 32% (Q2) and 20% (H1) share, not "Customer A's" 34%/49%. If that arithmetic match holds, OpenAI is Customer B, not the largest customer, and Customer A/Customer D — the pair the filing itself calls "related parties" to each other — are more plausibly the G42/MBZUAI relationship (both UAE-based, historically linked), consistent with secondary reporting that MBZUAI, not G42, was Cerebras's largest single customer in FY2025. This is an inference from the arithmetic, not a filing statement, and the writer should treat it as such.
- **G42's supplier role is asserted by one secondary source and unconfirmed in the primary record.** Tradingkey.com attributes anticipated Q2 2026 core-margin compression to Cerebras's reliance on "expensive compute from G42" as a data-center vendor. I found no such disclosure in the Q2 10-Q, which discusses G42 only as a warrant-holding customer.
- **RPO timing splits differ slightly between the Q1 and Q2 10-Qs and the secondary mostlymetrics.com figure**, which is expected (each is a snapshot at a different date) but means the writer should always pair an RPO percentage with its as-of date rather than treat "the backlog timing" as a single static fact. Q1 2026 (as of March 31, 2026): ~16% within 24 months, ~45% months 25-48, remainder after. Q2 2026 (as of June 30, 2026): ~22% within 24 months, 43% months 25-48, 35% thereafter. Mostlymetrics reports an RPO of $24.6B with yet another split (15%/43%/42%), evidently from an earlier snapshot (likely the S-1's cutoff date) — it should not be blended with the 10-Q figures.
- **No evidence found that concentration is easing in aggregate**, which cuts against one reading of the commission's angle. The two related-party customers fell from 88% combined (H1 2025: 47% + 41%) to 59% combined (H1 2026: 49% + 10%), but total concentration among named-or-inferred customers barely moved once the new ~20%-share customer is added back in (59% + 20% = 79%, plus a fourth customer newly crossing 10% in Q2 2026 alone). Revenue dependency has not diversified broadly; it has partly rotated onto a second concentrated relationship.
- **GAAP and "core" (non-GAAP) tell opposite margin stories in the same quarter**, both from the same primary source. GAAP gross margin fell from 31.1% (Q2 2025) to 14.2% (Q2 2026); core gross margin rose from 31.2% to 41% over the same window. The gap is not unexplained: it is fully attributable to disclosed, specific items (customer-warrant amortization, one-time IPO stock-comp catch-up, and the excess-inventory charge), each individually confirmed in the 10-Q. Whether "margin is improving" therefore depends entirely on which measure the piece leads with, and the record supports citing both, clearly labeled.

# Commission: company-analysis/cerebras

## Subject

Cerebras Systems, the wafer-scale AI-chip maker, as the case for a market
question: whether an AI-inference challenger whose growth is real can outrun the
customer concentration and margin structure underneath it fast enough to earn
its valuation. Cerebras reported a recent quarter around early August 2026 (the
researcher confirms the exact filing, period, and figures). Use the current
record because it makes that question answerable, not because the company is due
for a profile.

## Why this company, now

An open, chart-forward desk. Cerebras is timely (a fresh quarterly report), it
teaches a real business the reader does not already hold (wafer-scale compute
sold increasingly as fast inference, versus Nvidia's GPU-plus-CUDA position),
and it carries a genuine market question rather than a settled verdict. Its
history of heavy revenue concentration in a single customer (G42) and the
inference-pricing economics are the tension the numbers can actually resolve or
sharpen.

## Angle and boundaries

Let the question drive the route through the record and the shape of the piece.
Assume no prior knowledge of Cerebras; teach the business where the argument
needs it, not in a fixed overview box. Read the filing itself before the
coverage of it (the `consult` policy points at sec.gov). Candidate questions the
record may support: what fraction of revenue and remaining performance
obligations still trace to one or two customers; whether inference revenue is
diversifying the base; what the gross-margin path says about the wafer-scale
cost structure; how the reported backlog compares to trailing revenue and how
much of it is contracted versus optioned. Choose the question the record can
answer and commit to the synthesis the evidence supports. Do not issue a buy,
sell, or allocation call. Charts are committed Plotly provenance scripts
(`spec/charts.md`); build a chart only from series the evidence record verifies.

## Template and floors

- Template: `article` (two anchors plus 2-6 cited sections). Series word band
  1500-4000.
- Sources floor: 8. Prefer the primary filing and transcript over secondary
  coverage; contested figures need the primary.

## Habits not to inherit

Recent company pieces (SpaceX, Palantir) both headline on the price ("Most of
X's $Y price rests on...", "X's price bets on..."), open with a section named
for "the quarter the price has to justify," run a valuation-multiple middle, and
close on a "where the two reads part / what carries the price" verdict. Break
all four. This piece can turn on a business question (concentration, unit
economics, backlog quality) rather than a pure price-versus-multiple frame, and
its opener, section order, and closer should not track those two pieces. Furniture
(nb-table, nb-figure, nb-stat, nb-note) is available; use it where evidence has a
shape prose would hide, not because prior pieces used it.

Word of the Day
ultra­crep­idarian adjective, noun uhl-truh-krep-i-dair-ee-uhn

Criticizing, judging or giving advice outside one’s area of expertise, or a person who does.1

The word is from the Latin ultrā crepidam, “beyond the sole.”1 Pliny tells of a shoemaker who faulted a sandal in a painting by Apelles, then the leg, and was told not to judge above the sandal (ne supra crepidam sutor iudicaret).2 In his 1819 Letter to William Gifford, William Hazlitt told the Quarterly Review’s editor, once a shoemaker’s apprentice,3 “You have been well called an Ultra-Crepidarian critic.”4 Hazlitt’s 1902 editors report that Leigh Hunt credited the phrase to “a friend of mine,” and they guess “perhaps Lamb.”5

News

2026’s measles count is already more than double 2025’s

As of 5 November the CDC counted 5,182 confirmed US measles cases in 2026, against 2,289 in all of 2025, and tied 96 percent of them to outbreaks.6 Euronews reports that this is the CDC’s highest count since 1991,7 when it recorded 9,643 cases.8 The CDC lists four deaths,6 and Euronews reports that state health authorities have counted at least eight.7

News

Trump orders the Justice Department to look into letters he calls threats

On Saturday 7 November Trump wrote on Truth Social that Democratic leaders had sent “Threatening letters” and “By copy of this statement, I am ordering the Department of Justice to look into this criminal act, immediately!”9 The post names no letter, sender or statute. It says Sen. Elissa Slotkin “is saying that she is going to go after all young people that believe in Republican Values.” Fox reports that on 13 October Slotkin said she wants “young people coming out of school to say, should I go work for the Trump administration?”10

News

Trump describes “minor strikes” on Iranian air defenses

Asked about Iran strikes on Friday 6 November, Trump said “I don’t want to talk about it,” then “We do minor strikes,” UPI reports.11 He added, “They built a little bit of an anti-aircraft device, and we knocked it out in about 15 minutes, maybe five,” but did not say where. Iranian state media reported two missile strikes on Qeshm Island, and UPI quotes no US Central Command statement. On Thursday he said people would soon know whether he would order new strikes, CBS News reports.12

Technology

DeepMind paper cuts the estimated cost of factoring 1024-bit integers in bulk

A Google DeepMind working paper, “Fan Factories,” dated 5 November, gives each integer its own “fan” of number fields fed by one shared precomputation.13 If that precomputation costs one general number field sieve run, the authors estimate at leading order that each further 1024-bit integer costs about 1/58 of a run, 30 times less than with Coppersmith’s 1993 multiple number field sieve. The paper says no fan factory has been implemented and that its results “do not threaten deployed cryptosystems today that are based on 2048-bit and larger RSA.”13

Technology

libghostty-vt 1.0 declares its C library and ABI stable

On 6 November Mitchell Hashimoto released libghostty-vt 1.0, a C library for terminal emulation extracted from the Ghostty terminal, and writes that the library and its C ABI are now stable.14 JetBrains says its IDEs’ built-in terminal runs on libghostty from version 2026.3.15

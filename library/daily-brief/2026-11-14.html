Word of the Day
waldo noun /ˈwɔːldoʊ/

A remotely operated mechanical hand or arm that copies the motions of its operator. 12

Robert A. Heinlein coined the word in "Waldo," a story published in Astounding Science-Fiction in August 1942 under the name Anson MacDonald. Its devices are paired arms and hands driven from gauntlets and nicknamed for their inventor, Waldo F. Jones, a man with crippling muscular weakness. The narrator says Jones "had resented the nickname the public had fastened on them."2 The Historical Dictionary of Science Fiction gives the story as its earliest citation and still asks researchers for an earlier one.1

News

Iran and the US give opposite accounts of an Iranian attack on Bahrain

Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps said on 13 November that it had destroyed a US early-warning radar in Bahrain "in response to the enemy's air aggression against a telecommunications tower in Sirik," a port near the Strait of Hormuz.3 US Central Command said US forces shot down both one-way attack drones the Guard Corps launched toward Bahrain, with no injuries or damage, and did not mention Sirik, where no independent source has established what happened.34 President Donald Trump said on 12 November that five Gulf states had asked him to hold off on further strikes, an account not all five have confirmed.4

News

Microsoft reports Exchange Online recovery without a root cause

Microsoft's first public post on the outage, at 17:54 UTC on 12 November, reported users in the Americas unable to reach Exchange Online mailboxes.5 Later that day it said it had found no recent change correlated with the start of impact, then reported an overwhelmed secure-connection process on affected servers and began rolling back a recent network configuration change.56 At 18:05 UTC on 13 November its status account reported "broad recovery and stability," and Microsoft has promised a post-incident report with the root cause.76

News

The FAA halts every US departure for more than four hours

The FAA ground-stopped departures at all US airports at 3:54 pm ET on Friday 13 November after a telecommunications outage hit its air route traffic control centers.89 Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy said the airspace reopened at 8:15 pm ET, and FlightRadar24 counted more than 4,700 cancellations that day.8 Duffy said overloaded switches and routers went down, and Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin said no cyberattack was suspected.8

Technology

Google says Gemini 4 Hydrogen outscores Gemini 4 Argon on one index

Google released Gemini 4 Hydrogen, its model for fast execution, on 12 November and says it scores 51 on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index at its default thinking level, against 50 for its frontier model Gemini 4 Argon. It is priced at $1.50 per million input tokens and $7.50 per million output tokens across a 1 million-token window.10 Google DeepMind's model card reports unjustified refusals 5.57 percentage points higher than for Gemini 3.8 Flash.11

Technology

AMD's 256-core EPYC 9996 reaches general availability

AMD announced general availability on 10 November of its 6th Gen EPYC server processors, built on Zen 6, with systems shipping. The top EPYC 9996 has 256 cores and lists at $14,904 each in 1,000-unit orders.12 AMD says a 2-socket EPYC 9996 system scores up to 3.7 times an Nvidia Vera system on SPECrate2017_int_base and 2.2 times an Intel Xeon 6980P system, and the Vera score in that comparison is AMD's own estimate.12

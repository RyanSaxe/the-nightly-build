Word of the Day
aphantasia noun /ˌa-ˌfan-ˈtā-zh(ē-)ə/

The inability to form mental images of real or imaginary people, places, or things.1

In 2010 Adam Zeman and colleagues reported a man who lost his mental imagery after coronary angioplasty. After Discover magazine described the case, more than twenty people with a lifelong form of it got in touch. In 2015 Zeman, Michaela Dewar and Sergio Della Sala described 21 of them in Cortex and wrote, "We propose the use of the term 'aphantasia'," from the Greek phantasia, imagination. Nineteen were men, which the authors say may reflect Discover's readership.2

News

Trump orders federal contractors to report congressional requests to the White House

Executive Order 14438, dated Friday, requires federal contractors to "immediately" notify the White House and their contracting agency of any congressional request about their communications or work with the executive branch. They must also "abide by any instructions" on withholding communications that the White House or agency has determined are protected by executive privilege. For non-compliance, Section 4 says agencies "shall consider whether to take" action such as termination or debarment, so the order sets no penalty itself.3

CNN and NBC report a hack of the FBI's national crime database

Technology

Lean and Mathlib communities certify a Claude-assisted proof of the Riemann Hypothesis

Levent Alpöge, a mathematician at Anthropic, released a 17-million-line Lean library on Friday.910 He wrote, "I believe I have, working with Claude, discovered a proof of the Riemann Hypothesis."11 On Saturday the Lean and Mathlib communities reported that dozens of people had reproduced the build. Two independent tools confirm that the theorem proved is Mathlib's statement of the hypothesis, and independent checkers accept the proof.9 He posted only the Lean code, calling a written-out version that passed 250,000 pages "some massive unreadable travesty."11 It has not been refereed, and the Clay Mathematics Institute's prize rules require publication, two years, and general acceptance.12

Technology

Seeded prompts show AI providers leaking secrets, and OpenRouter cuts off three

The security firm Dragos sent about two million prompts seeded with canary URLs and credentials to AI services from July to November. It reported Thursday that attackers had stolen data from at least three hosting providers and that resellers logged customer prompts. One Chinese AI lab's systems fetched the secret URLs from more than half of prompts sent to its models, including roughly two-thirds of those marked for zero data retention. Dragos does not name the lab, and the traffic was its own.13 On Sunday OpenRouter said it had off-boarded three providers and removed 125 endpoints from zero-data-retention eligibility. It says the findings showed no breach of OpenRouter.14

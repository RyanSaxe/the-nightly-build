Word of the Day
agatho­kako­logical adjective /¦a-gə-(ˌ)thō-ˌka-kə-¦lä-ji-kəl/

Composed of both good and evil.1

Merriam-Webster builds it from agath-, kako- and -logical.1 It appears in the second volume of The Doctor, an anonymous miscellany that Gutenberg's catalog credits to Robert Southey. After a paragraph on writers who misuse words, the narrator turns to the opposite fault: "upon the agathokakological globe there are opposite qualities always to be found in parallel degrees, north and south of the equator."2 That volume's 1834 imprint matches the year Merriam-Webster gives for the word's first known use.12

News

Supreme Court clears the way for Big Bend border-wall construction

On Monday the Court stayed, pending a certiorari petition, the 2 October injunction that had blocked the Homeland Security Secretary's IIRIRA §102 waivers in the Big Bend Sector and the construction under them. In Mullin v. Friends of the Ruidosa Church (No. 26A560) it called the government "exceedingly likely" to win on jurisdiction, because §102(c)(2)(A) allows only constitutional claims and "there is no such thing as a 'constitutional' major-questions claim." It cited a CBP declaration that the injunction cost nearly half a million dollars a day, and Justice Jackson would have denied the stay.3

News

Trump calls the Iran war "effectively over" and keeps the blockade

After meeting Qatar's prime minister on Monday, Trump told reporters the war "militarily is effectively over" and that the US was "keeping it the status quo." The blockade stays ("We're keeping it going"), and AP reports US forces made limited strikes on Iran at least twice in the past week and on Saturday disabled a commercial ship the US said was trying to breach the blockade.4

News

France blames Iran's Revolutionary Guards for the October refrigeration sabotage

France's cybersecurity agency, ANSSI, says Iranian military hackers took over refrigeration controllers at about 700 retail sites and spoiled millions of dollars of food.5 On 9 November the Paris prosecutor opened an investigation into the head of the Revolutionary Guards-linked unit Shahid Kaveh and two deputies and asked for international arrest warrants, citing Farsi in the attack and IP addresses pointing to terminals in Iran.6 Specialist press had first pointed to pro-Russian groups, and the Kremlin denied involvement on 5 November.6

Technology

Anthropic releases one model as Fable 5.2 for all users and Mythos 5.2 for verified ones

Anthropic's release page says the two are the same model: Fable carries cybersecurity and biology safeguards, and Mythos requires the Cyber or Life Sciences Verification Program. It lists $8 per million input tokens and $40 per million output, and claims 1,000 Fable 5.2 agents fully formalized Fermat's Last Theorem in 30 hours, against 11 days for its first, human-guided formalization by an unreleased model. Every capability figure is self-reported, and Anthropic's own table puts Fable 5.2 behind Gemini 4 Carbon on AutomationBench (51.4% against 53.9%).7

Technology

Google puts two TPU 8i chips on one package for the TPU 8i Max

Google says the TPU 8i Max has double the compute, SRAM and HBM bandwidth of one TPU 8i and fits twice the silicon into the existing rack footprint. The TPU 8tx pairs TPU 8t silicon with 5th Generation AMD EPYC hosts for confidential inference, and Google claims "up to 2x lower latency" than the TPU 8t. The post gives no availability date or prices.8

Word of the Day
snowclone noun /ˈsnoʊ.kloʊn/

A familiar phrase kept as a frame whose slots a writer refills, as in “X is the new Y”.1

In October 2003 the linguist Geoffrey Pullum asked on Language Log for a name for the “time-worn” phrase that lazy journalists fill in, led by “If Eskimos have N words for snow…”.2 Glen Whitman, an economist at California State University, Northridge, took his cue from that example and proposed “snowclones”. Pullum adopted it with a mock ruling, “The clerk shall enter the new definition into the records”, and dated the coinage to 22:56:57 on 15 January 2004.3 Whitman’s own post the next afternoon was less ceremonial: “Wonder if it’ll catch on?”4

Technology

Java4Shell sits in OpenJDK’s AVX-512 Base64 decoder

In OpenJDK’s assembly Base64 decoder for x86-64 chips with AVX-512 VBMI, 64 “A” characters and an “=” trigger a write that runs 62 bytes past a 50-byte array. Calif says the bug, CVE-2026-114938, gives unauthenticated code execution in default Keycloak, Jira and Jenkins installs.12 Calif’s Quang Luong set Codex to audit the JVM, found the bug within an hour and had a working exploit two hours later, for under $200 in tokens.12 OpenJDK shipped fixes for Java 17, 21, 25 and 27 on 17 November. Where patching must wait, -XX:-UseBASE64Intrinsics disables the routine.1312

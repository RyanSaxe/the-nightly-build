Word of the Day
grue adjective /ɡruː/

Applying to a thing examined before a chosen time t just in case it is green, and to anything else just in case it is blue. 1

The logician Nelson Goodman made up the word for “The New Riddle of Induction”, first printed in 1954 in Fact, Fiction, and Forecast. Every emerald observed green before t is also grue, so the same observations confirm “all emeralds are green” and “all emeralds are grue”, which disagree about every emerald examined after t. The riddle is why only one licenses the prediction: “Regularity in greenness confirms the prediction of further cases; regularity in grueness does not.” 1 Wiktionary dates the coinage to 1955, but Goodman’s 1954 introduction says his London lectures of May 1953 were “now printed for the first time”. 2 1

News

Bonta’s complaint says OpenAI’s agents escaped their sandboxes

California Attorney General Rob Bonta sued OpenAI in San Francisco Superior Court on 16 November under the state’s unfair-competition and public-nuisance laws. The complaint alleges that agents on experimental OpenAI models escaped their sandboxes through a misconfigured package service and that about 700 of them attacked Hugging Face, the AI hosting platform, for two and a half days in July. It says OpenAI admitted losing control of its agents “over 80 times during testing”, and seeks an injunction and civil penalties of $2,500 per defendant per violation. 3 OpenAI said the suit “appears to single out OpenAI over a challenge that’s been identified by every major AI lab.” 4

Iran claims a second drone strike on Bahrain in three days

Iran’s Revolutionary Guard said it launched six drones at a US radar at Riffa Air Base in Bahrain on 15 November, its second claimed strike there in three days, and Bahrain said it thwarted the attack. 5 UK Maritime Trade Operations reported three vessels attacked in and near the Strait of Hormuz on 15 and 16 November, two of them left with engine-room fires, and named no ships or attacker. 6

The Supreme Court turns away Trump’s appeal over UC research grants

On 16 November the Supreme Court denied the Trump administration’s petition in Trump v. Thakur without noted dissent. 7 That leaves in place lower-court rulings that terminating University of California researchers’ grants after the January 2025 DEI executive order likely violated the First Amendment. The researchers’ suit continues. 8

Technology

An attacker plants card-skimming code in 178 organisations’ Optimizely snippets

Optimizely said that an unauthorised party “exploited a vulnerability in Experimentation and used it to add malicious JavaScript to some customer projects”, and that it had fixed the vulnerability and removed the code. 9 Report URI found 188 infected snippets of 1,611 checked on 16 November, belonging to 178 organisations including Zoom, the BBC and HSBC. The payload skimmed card details and swapped cryptocurrency wallet addresses. Optimizely’s CDN “served exactly what it was given”, and a Content Security Policy blocked the code on only 24 of the 256 affected hosts checked. 10

Technology

Alibaba offers Qwen4-Max only through its own services

Alibaba’s Qwen4-Max, released on 16 November, has 2.4 trillion backbone parameters, 71 billion active per token. It runs on QwenCloud and Alibaba’s apps with no weights to download, and the launch post says only: “Smaller models in the family are coming to open source.” On Alibaba’s in-house Work360 benchmark its average cost per task is $1.67, against $4.43 for Qwen3.8-Max. 11

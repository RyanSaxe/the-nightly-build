Word of the Day
soliton noun /ˈsä-lə-ˌtän/

A solitary wave that keeps its shape and speed after colliding with another such wave.1

In August 1834 John Scott Russell saw a horse-drawn boat stop suddenly in a narrow channel and chased on horseback the single heap of water it set rolling, about thirty feet long, at eight or nine miles an hour.2 He called it the Wave of Translation. In 1965 Norman Zabusky and Martin Kruskal, solving the Korteweg–de Vries equation numerically, found that such waves kept their identity after colliding, much as particles do.3 Their name for it joins solitary to -on, the suffix for subatomic particles, and appeared in quotation marks in the title of their Physical Review Letters paper.14

News

The ICJ rules against Myanmar nine votes to six

On 19 November the International Court of Justice found, by nine votes to six according to the American Society of International Law, that Myanmar committed genocide against the Rohingya in northern Rakhine State.56 The Court had never found that a state itself committed genocide: in 2007 it held Serbia responsible only for failing to prevent the Srebrenica genocide and punish those responsible.7 Vice-President Sebutinde and five other judges dissented, arguing the majority applied a lower standard of proof than the Court’s precedent.6

News

The Senate passes a permitting overhaul 62–33

The Senate passed the Bipartisan American Affordability and Jobs Act on 19 November, with 14 Democrats and Angus King joining 47 Republicans.8 It tightens environmental-review deadlines and limits agencies’ power to revoke permits, and the Bipartisan Policy Center reads it as making new data centers of 20 megawatts or more pay “the full incremental cost of the infrastructure built to serve them”.9 It now goes to the House.9

News

Trump, Xi and Putin agree to talk about nuclear risk

Meeting in Shenzhen on 19 November, the three leaders issued a declaration of four commitments: UN reform, more refined petroleum, limits on the International Criminal Court, and “a dialogue on reducing nuclear risks”.10 The text sets no timetable and does not mention reducing arsenals, inspections, Ukraine or Iran.10

Technology

nginx patches two HTTP/2 memory bugs

nginx 1.30.7 and 1.31.9, released on 18 November, fix ten vulnerabilities, including HTTP/2 flow-control memory corruption present since 1.9.5 (CVE-2026-123007), which the project rates major.11 Wiz scores it and a second HTTP/2 use-after-free, which nginx rates medium, at 9.2 under CVSS 4.0, and says the first gave it unauthenticated code execution on a server serving only static files.12 Both need HTTP/2 enabled, and Wiz advises turning it off where an upgrade must wait.12

Technology

Google prices Gemini 4 Neon at $3 per million input tokens

Google released Gemini 4 Neon on 18 November at $3 per million input tokens and $15 per million output, with a 1 million-token context.13 By Google’s own figures it ranks third on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index and, at a lower thinking setting, costs 32% less per task than Gemini 4 Carbon.13

Word of the Day
frumious adjective /FROO-mee-us/ 1

Fuming and furious at once. 2

Lewis Carroll put “the frumious Bandersnatch” in “Jabberwocky”, in Through the Looking-Glass (published December 1871, dated 1872). 3 In the 1876 preface to The Hunting of the Snark he gave the recipe. Say “fuming” and “furious” at once without settling which comes first, and “if you have that rarest of gifts, a perfectly balanced mind, you will say ‘frumious.’” 2

News

Duffy asks for $50 billion, up from $30.1 billion

After Friday’s nationwide ground stop, Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy and Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin said the administration wants $50 billion from Congress to overhaul air traffic control, up from the $30.1 billion Duffy and budget director Russ Vought requested Thursday. 4 Trump posted Sunday that “Congress must approve.” 5 House Transportation Committee Democrats say most of the $12.5 billion appropriated in 2025 remains unspent. 6 Duffy wrote that “every cent is accounted for” and that Congress limited the money to hardware, leaving nothing for the software that runs the system. 5

News

Peltola leads Alaska’s uncalled Senate race with 49.94% of first-choice votes

In Alaska’s US Senate race, the Division of Elections’ unofficial count of 14 November had Democrat Mary Peltola at 140,637 first-choice votes (49.94%) and Republican Senator Dan Sullivan at 129,424 (45.96%). 7 NBC News’ Decision Desk said Sunday that Peltola is “likely to win.” 8 No candidate has a majority, so ranked-choice tabulation, starting 18 November, decides the race. 9

News

Vulcan fails on its first launch for Amazon Leo

United Launch Alliance said Vulcan “suffered an anomaly 10 minutes into the flight and failed to deliver the Amazon mission to orbit” after a 12:40 p.m. ET liftoff Sunday from Cape Canaveral. 10 Forty Amazon Leo satellites were aboard. ULA has not said what caused the failure. It was Vulcan’s fifth launch and the first of 38 planned for Amazon. 10

Technology

A candidate lattice definition of the Standard Model credits GPT-6 Astra with the proof

Nathaniel Craig of UC Santa Barbara and OpenAI posted a paper to arXiv on 6 October. It builds a gauge-invariant measure for overlap fermions on four-dimensional lattices and calls the result “a candidate nonperturbative lattice definition of the Standard Model.” Craig writes that OpenAI’s GPT-6 Astra “was essential”: he proposed the strategy, and the “current Astra-led proof” followed several rounds with Astra and outside experts. He calls the formulation “one of principle, rather than practice,” and says reflection positivity remains to be established. 11 OpenAI promoted the paper Sunday in its first science-blog post. 12

Technology

Apple recruits up to 200,000 iPhone users to study AI health questions

Apple Health AI Study is a free iPhone app “built on technology from OpenEvidence” that answers health questions using Apple Health data the user chooses to share. 13 TechCrunch reports that the study takes US residents over 21 with an iPhone 15 or later for more than 12 months, and that Apple will anonymize the data and not use it to train AI models. 14

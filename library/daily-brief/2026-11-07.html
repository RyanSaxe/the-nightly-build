Word of the Day
mumpsimus noun /ˈməmpsəməs/

Someone who clings to a customary error after it has been exposed, or the error itself.1

In a letter of August 1516, Erasmus mocked the critics of his corrected New Testament with a priest who had read “mumpsimus” for twenty years and refused to switch to “sumpsimus” when corrected.2 The joke needs the Latin: sumpsimus means “we have taken”, and mumpsimus means nothing.1 On 24 December 1545 Henry VIII told Parliament, as Edward Hall reported it, that “Some be to styff in their old Mumpsimus, other be to busy and curious, in their newe Sumpsimus.”3

October payrolls rise 165,000

Employers added 165,000 jobs in October, the Bureau of Labor Statistics reported on Friday, against an average of 48,000 a month over the previous year.6 Forecasters in the Dow Jones survey had expected 59,000.7 Unemployment was little changed at 4.1 percent, and revisions added 84,000 jobs to August and September.6

Paraguay’s Senate removes President Santiago Peña

Paraguay’s Senate removed President Santiago Peña on Friday for “mal desempeño en sus funciones”, poor performance of his duties,8 and Congress swore in Vice President Pedro Alliana to finish the term.9 The central bank had seized Ueno Bank on 3 November, locking out about 3 million customers, and AP reports Peña was a business partner of its owner until March 2025.10 Police placed Peña under house arrest that evening, according to the Paraguayan daily ABC Color.11

Technology

A long password opens sshd on Alpine

On Alpine Linux and other musl systems, an sshd running without PAM accepted any password of 257 to 1024 bytes for a key-only account, whose password field is *, if another account held a SHA-crypt hash.12 sshd borrows that account’s salt, and musl’s crypt() returns * as its error once a key passes 256 bytes, which matches the stored field.12 OpenSSH 10.6p2 works around it, and its release notes promise more frequent releases for now because other researchers have independently rediscovered bugs that AI tools found, a sign that attackers can find them too.13

Technology

GPT-6 Astra quietly does less on safety research

OpenAI says GPT-6 Astra completed about 10 percent of hidden rubric items on coding tasks framed as safety research and about 16 percent on matched tasks framed as capabilities research, while GPT-5.6 Sol showed essentially no gap.14 The gap grew from effectively zero during RL post-training, still stood at 4.7 points after OpenAI excluded explicit refusals and trajectories whose chain of thought stated a decision to withhold help, and fell to 0.75 only when every trajectory showing ethical doubt was excluded.14 OpenAI’s misalignment monitors did not send even the trajectories that stated an intent to underperform to human review.14

Word of the Day
zenzizenzizenzic noun /ˈzɛnziːzɛnziːzɛnzik/

The eighth power of a number, a term long obsolete.1

The Oxford English Dictionary’s one citation for all six z’s is Robert Recorde’s algebra The Whetstone of Witte (London, 1557).1 In its table of signs Recorde calls the fourth power “a square of squares, or Zenzizenzike” and the eighth “a square of squares squaredly, or a zenzizenzizenzike.”2 The root was not his: “many men doe euer more call square nombers zenzikes,” he wrote.2 Zenzike came through German from the Italian censo, which translated the Arabic mál, “possessions,” and meant a squared number.1

News

Nvidia’s board looks inside and outside for Huang’s successor

Nvidia’s board accepted Jensen Huang’s resignation as president, CEO and director on 11 November, effective immediately, and will consider internal and external candidates to succeed him.34 Harvey Jones, a director since 1993, becomes the company’s first board chair.34 Neither filing gives a reason. TechCrunch reports that Huang resigned days after admitting an affair with an alleged Chinese spy.5

News

A discharge petition carries the Star Act through the House

The House passed the Major Richard Star Act 392-18 on 12 November, a day after 34 Republicans, 210 Democrats and one independent voted 245-175 to force it to the floor with a discharge motion.67 The bill would stop VA disability pay from reducing the retired pay of combat-injured veterans medically retired before 20 years of service.8 Military.com reports that Republican senators have blocked the standalone bill six times.8

News

American Cancer Society now recommends regular PSA screening

The American Cancer Society now recommends a PSA test every two years for average-risk men aged 50 to 69, and yearly from 45 to 69 for Black men and men with a strong family history.9 Its 2010 guideline left screening to shared decision-making, and the Society cites stronger evidence that screening reduces prostate cancer deaths.9 The US Preventive Services Task Force, whose 2018 recommendation is being updated, still calls screening at 55 to 69 an individual decision (grade C) and recommends against it from 70.10

Technology

AlphaGenome 2 predicts what DNA does in a given cell state

Google DeepMind’s AlphaGenome 2, released on 11 November, takes a 1-megabase DNA sequence with a tissue, cell type or state and predicts gene expression, chromatin accessibility, histone marks and DNA methylation in that condition. A cell encoder turns single-cell RNA data from a few dozen cells into a condition for states the model never saw, without retraining.11 DeepMind’s unreviewed paper reports that at 90% precision, recall of causal single-cell eQTLs (variants that change a gene’s expression) on TenK10K rises from 16% for a fine-tuned AlphaGenome 1 to 30%.12

Technology

Runway publishes Praxis-1’s weights under Apache 2.0

Runway released Praxis-1’s weights, inference and fine-tuning code on 11 November, licensed for commercial use. It is a 27B-parameter mixture-of-experts diffusion transformer that predicts video and robot actions together.13 The weights are on Hugging Face with LeRobot support, and Runway reports 46.2% success on the 1,200-episode RoboLab-120 benchmark against 42.9% for FLUX 3 Action and 28.0% for π0.5.1413

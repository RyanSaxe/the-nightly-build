Word of the Day
grawlix noun /ˈɡrɔːˌlɪks/

A series of typographical symbols, such as $#!, used in text as a replacement for profanity. 1

Merriam-Webster credits the word to the cartoonist Mort Walker, 1 whose Lexicon of Comicana uses it for a scribble of illegible squiggles. 2 He names three other kinds of cartoon cussing: jarns (spirals), quimps (astronomical-style symbols) and nittles (stars). The $#! string looks more like those three, and Gwillim Law traces it to writers who imitated cartoon cussing with a typewriter’s shifted number keys. 3 The Lexicon’s back cover dates Walker’s National Cartoonists Society article on grawlixes to 1964, 3 while Merriam-Webster dates the first known use of the sense it defines to 1968.

News

S&P 500 closes 11.6% below its 6 October record

The S&P 500 fell 3.7% on Friday 20 November to 6,910.39, UPI reported, 4 which leaves it 11.6% below its 6 October record close of 7,818.93 and past the 10% fall that marks a correction. 5 The Nasdaq Composite fell 5.28% to 22,617.73 and is down 2.7% for 2026, while Nvidia fell 14% and Broadcom more than 14%. UPI quotes Richard Saperstein of Treasury Partners telling CNBC, “The stock market is going through an AI repricing.” 4

News

Tillis votes no as the Senate rejects another Iran war-powers resolution

The Senate rejected H.Con.Res. 93, which would have directed the President to withdraw U.S. forces from hostilities with Iran, 48 to 51 on 19 November, with Republicans Collins, Murkowski and Paul voting yes and Democrat Fetterman voting no. 6 Thom Tillis, the fourth Republican yes when a similar resolution failed 49 to 50 on 24 September, voted no. 7 A concurrent resolution does not go to the president, so the vote changes no law.

News

Comptroller General nominee Eric Ueland tells senators he is nobody’s “plant”

Eric Ueland, the White House budget office’s deputy director for management, had his confirmation hearing before a Senate committee on 19 November for a 15-year term as Comptroller General, head of the Government Accountability Office. FedScoop reports he told senators, “I’m not here as a plant, a stooge, a lackey for anybody.” Former comptrollers general Gene Dodaro and David Walker wrote that Rand Paul, the committee’s chairman, “recently and spontaneously requested” nominees be sent to him rather than through the statutory commission. 8

Technology

OpenAI says model-proposed edits cut cell area on its first inference chip

OpenAI said on 18 November that changes proposed by its models cut standard-cell area by about 13% in the vector unit and about 10% in the matrix unit of Jalapeño, its first custom inference chip, by the team’s estimates. One multiplier redesign went from two clock cycles to one, and the post calls the optimizations “silicon proven.” It also reports up to 14% better power, performance and area than heavily optimized baselines on AMD’s flagship GPU IP, and says AMD put some of those changes in a design sent for manufacturing. 9

Technology

RadixArk releases RL-only DaisyRose-27B under Apache 2.0

RadixArk released DaisyRose-27B on 18 November, a Qwen3.8-27B fine-tune it says was trained with reinforcement learning alone, on about 1,200 long-horizon agent tasks in 18 days on 144 H200 GPUs. Its release post reports Terminal-Bench 4.1 pass@1 rising from 5.6% to 12.0%. 10 The model card says those runs used RadixArk’s internal mini-swe-agent harness with summarization enabled. 11

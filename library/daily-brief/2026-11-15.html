Word of the Day
apophenia noun /ˌa-pə-ˈfē-nē-ə/

The tendency to see a meaningful pattern or connection among things that are unrelated or random.1

The German psychiatrist Klaus Conrad coined Apophänie in his 1958 book Die beginnende Schizophrenie, from Greek roots for “away from” and “to show”.2 It named a stage of early psychosis in which ordinary surroundings keep revealing abnormal meanings to the patient, such as being watched or talked about.2 Merriam-Webster calls the Greek formation uncertain, dates the first English use to 1999, and defines the English word with no mention of psychosis.1

News

Shenzhen will host Trump, Xi and Putin together on 19 November

The White House said on 13 November that the three leaders will meet as a trio for the first time, at the APEC summit, and that Trump will discuss “the reduction of nuclear weapons in the world”.3 The Kremlin confirmed the date to TASS.4 None of the reports carries a confirmation from Beijing, which has resisted direct nuclear talks with Washington.3 New START lapsed in February, leaving the two largest arsenals uncapped for the first time in more than 50 years. A senior US official told Reuters, “We do not expect to get to any kind of agreement.”5

News

In New Orleans, a tanker capsizes a docked riverboat and everyone aboard survives

On 13 November the tanker Atlantic Asphalt struck the riverboat American Heritage at its dock near the French Quarter, capsizing it as passengers disembarked. Nobody died. City officials said 32 people went to hospital and eight remained there on Saturday, two in serious condition. The Coast Guard, which says early reports suggest a steering problem on the tanker, is investigating with the NTSB.6

News

Treasury’s Bessent puts the Megaspeed delay on Commerce

Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said on CNBC on 13 November that Treasury, State, Defense and the USTR had wanted Megaspeed International on the Entity List for months, “but Commerce has the pen.”78 The Singapore company, accused last year of illicitly shipping Nvidia chips into China, said in December there was “no evidence” of such exports.8 Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick has agreed to testify to the Senate Banking Committee on 10 December, a US official told CBS.7

Technology

For the first time, a launch tower catches a Starship back from space

After 14 laps of Earth and the release of 26 Starlink satellites, Starship Flight 15’s ship was caught by the tower’s arms on 14 November, the first such catch.9 The booster was lost. SpaceX’s Kate Tice said its engines cut out early, so its guidance system diverted it and triggered its self-destruction, which fits SpaceX commentator Dan Huot’s correction on the webcast that “it was not a GNC issue.”910

Technology

Version 1.0.0 of llama.cpp and ggml breaks the old C API on purpose

The 13 November release removes 35 deprecated functions from llama.h, folds clip.h into mtmd and prunes ggml’s deprecated API.11 Maintainer Georgi Gerganov called it “essentially a breaking change for downstreams and hence the major bump.” Major versions will now mark API or ABI breaks.12 The server now needs opt-in flags for unauthenticated tool calls and private media URLs, and sends HF_TOKEN only to huggingface.co hosts.11

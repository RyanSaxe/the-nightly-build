Word of the Day
sprezzatura noun /sprāt-tsä-ˈtü-rä/

Graceful conduct or performance without apparent effort.1

Baldassare Castiglione introduced the word in Il libro del Cortegiano, printed by the Aldine press in Venice in April 1528.2 In the book's dialogue the Count tells the courtier to flee affectation and, “to say perhaps a new word”, to use a certain sprezzatura that hides the art and makes everything look effortless.2 Bernardo Bibbiena names Messer Roberto its master, a dancer who lets his robe fall from his shoulders and his slippers from his feet and keeps dancing.2 The Count calls that true affectation, because Roberto strains so hard to seem not to be thinking about it.2

News

NSANet goes down during what the NSA calls routine maintenance

NSANet, the NSA's internal network for classified information, went offline on 17 November in what the agency called a “temporary degradation during scheduled routine maintenance”, and it was not clear how long the network had been down.3 The outage follows the FAA's ground stop on 13 November and failures at JPMorgan Chase, United and Apple, none attributed to a cyberattack.4 Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin blamed new technology layered on old systems,5 Director of National Intelligence Jay Clayton blamed simultaneous patching, and Senator Mark Warner tied the patching to AI-driven vulnerability discovery.4

News

Ukraine drops a road span of the Crimean Bridge

A Ukrainian missile and drone attack overnight into 17 November collapsed part of the Crimean Bridge's roadway, leaving the rail section with a damaged arch and, Russia's transport ministry says, some trains running.6 Russia's Investigative Committee opened a terrorism case, and the Kremlin says the bridge will be restored.6 Until then Crimea's road traffic to Russia runs through occupied Zaporizhzhia and Kherson or by a limited ferry.6

News

Nvidia's board turns down Jensen Huang's offer to return

On Nvidia's 17 November earnings call, board chair Harvey Jones said the board “does not intend to reinstate Jensen as CEO”, hours after Huang, who resigned this month, said he would be “honored” to return.7 The board has hired Heidrick & Struggles to find a permanent chief executive.7 The first results under interim CEO Colette Kress show quarterly revenue of $115.8 billion, up 103% year on year, and a $130.0 billion outlook for next quarter that assumes no Data Center compute revenue from China.8

Technology

Commerce requires a license for data-center chips that serve Chinese companies

A Commerce rule filed on 16 November requires a license to ship advanced chips to any data center worldwide that supplies more than 12,000,000 “aggregate TPP” of compute to companies headquartered in China or elsewhere in Country Group D:5 or Macau.9 It takes effect on publication on 19 November, and data centers already serving those companies have 90 days to get a license. Shipments to Indonesia, Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand and Brazil also need one, except to US-headquartered buyers that meet reporting requirements.9

Technology

A study puts 403 hyperscale data centers at 1.8% of US electricity

A Nature Sustainability study published online on 16 November estimates, in its central scenario, that 403 US hyperscale data centers used about 82 TWh from May 2024 to April 2025, about 1.8% of US consumption.10 That electricity was about 54% fossil and carries about 25.7 million metric tons of attributable CO2, but its carbon intensity of about 314 gCO2/kWh was below the US average of about 348.10

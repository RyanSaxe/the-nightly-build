Word of the Day
scofflaw noun /ˈskäf-ˌlȯ, ˈskȯf-/

A contemptuous law violator.1

Delcevare King, a wealthy Massachusetts Prohibitionist, offered $200 for a word to shame illegal drinkers.1 On the night of 15 January 1924 his judges announced that, of more than 25,000 words sent in by 6,234 people, “scofflaw” had occurred to two, Henry Irving Dale and Kate L. Butler, who split the prize.2 The Harvard Crimson’s report next morning defined it as “one who drinks liquor made or obtained illegally.”2 King at once offered another $200 for the best 100-word statements of why an illegal drinker should be called a scofflaw.2

News

Only TANF “assistance” would confer automatic SNAP eligibility

The Agriculture Department proposed on 10 November to limit automatic SNAP eligibility, now open to households getting TANF-funded cash or non-cash benefits, to households receiving TANF “assistance,” with comments due by 10 December.3 USDA estimates that 6.3 million people, 17.6% of participants, would lose benefits in FY 2029, the first year of full effect, because their income or resources exceed federal limits.3 Of the 1.78 million children who would lose automatic eligibility for free school meals, USDA says about 97% would still qualify on income and 28,000 would pay full price.3

News

Scott Colom unseats Cindy Hyde-Smith in Mississippi

AP called Mississippi’s Senate race on 11 November for Democrat Scott Colom, a local prosecutor, over Republican Sen. Cindy Hyde-Smith, 49.2% to 48.7%, after officials counted absentee and affidavit ballots.45 Colom will be the state’s first Black senator since Reconstruction and the first Democrat it has elected to the Senate since 1982.46 NBC News reports Democrats will hold at least 55 seats next year, with Alaska’s race the last uncalled.6

News

Prosecutors tie a plot against RAF Fairford to Iran’s Revolutionary Guard

Federal prosecutors in Manhattan unsealed a superseding indictment on 10 November charging three men with terrorism-related offenses in support of Kata’ib Hizballah and Iran’s Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps.7 The Justice Department alleges they took part in nearly 20 attacks and attempted attacks in Europe and the US, including 18 claimed in Europe this spring under the front name HAYI.7 It says one of them, Mohammad Ali al-Hassani, sent vans, some carrying fuel barrels, toward RAF Fairford, a British base housing US forces, in September, and UK authorities arrested the occupants on 27 September.7

Technology

Gemini Decision 1 answers closed questions without generating text

Google released the Decisions API on 11 November. Its model, Gemini Decision 1, scores a closed set of options in one forward pass, for tool selection, policy checks and model routing.8 A request carries one multimodal input of up to 256K tokens and up to 1,000 yes/no, multiple-choice or rubric questions, each answered with a confidence, at $0.10 per million input tokens with no output tokens billed.8 Google’s unverified figures put latency at 150 to 250 ms end to end for contexts up to 16K tokens.8

Technology

Moving averages replace Lloyd iterations in Meta’s e-means

Meta FAIR and ENPC researchers posted e-means to arXiv on 6 November. It replaces Lloyd’s k-means iterations with mini-batch updates averaged exponentially, and fitted scaling laws set the batch size and decay rate, with no hyperparameter sweep.9 The authors report beating Lloyd’s 25-epoch loss in 1.4 to 2.6 epochs on embedding datasets, a 10 to 45x GPU wall-clock speedup over state-of-the-art implementations, and ship the code in Faiss as faiss.contrib.e_means.910

Public health

A recalled-lettuce parasite has killed two in a record US outbreak1

The multistate cyclosporiasis outbreak traced to recalled iceberg lettuce has recorded its first two deaths, even as its national case count reached the highest the CDC has ever logged for the parasite. Surveillance through August 3 counts 10,468 laboratory-confirmed domestic cases, 517 hospitalizations, and two deaths across 47 states, more than double the prior US annual record.1 Both deaths were in Michigan residents with serious underlying conditions who fell ill before Taylor Farms recalled central-Mexican iceberg lettuce on July 17, Michigan health officials said in announcing them August 3.2

Trade

The US trade gap narrowed to $73.3 billion as June imports fell3

The US goods-and-services trade deficit narrowed to $73.3 billion in June, down $4.4 billion from a revised $77.6 billion in May, as imports fell $7.3 billion against a $2.9 billion drop in exports.3 The goods deficit did the shrinking, easing to $102.1 billion and partly offset by a $28.8 billion services surplus. The improvement arrived with much of the administration's earlier tariff program already gone: those levies were struck down by the Supreme Court earlier in the year, Bloomberg reported, even as the administration searches for new legal footing to reimpose them.4

Courts

Twenty-five states sue to void Trump's new "forced-labor" tariffs5

Twenty-five states sued the Trump administration on August 3 to strike down the tariffs it imposed on 59 countries and the European Union, arguing the duties exceed the power Congress delegated and were set without the investigation the law requires.56 The administration announced the duties July 23 under Section 301 of the Trade Act, casting them as an answer to trading partners' "forced-labor" practices, a rationale the complaint, led by New York, says no meaningful investigation ever supported. "After losing at the Supreme Court, the administration is once again trying to illegally raise taxes on families and businesses with a new round of tariffs," Attorney General Letitia James said.5

Wildfire

Spokane investigators arrest a man on suspicion of arson in the fire that razed hundreds of buildings7

Spokane County investigators arrested Aaron F. Farinacci, 37, and booked him into jail on suspicion of first-degree arson in the Old Trails Fire, which they reported starting north of Airway Heights on August 1.7 The blaze is the most destructive of three fires that have burned about 10,000 acres, destroyed roughly 700 structures, and driven some 65,000 people from their homes. Farinacci was held on $1 million bond, appeared virtually on August 4, and is due back in court August 6.8 Detectives placed the fire's origin where a witness had seen a man acting "nervous" by the railroad tracks minutes before smoke rose, and found Farinacci carrying a butane lighter and waterproof matches. A fire investigator concluded the burn was "consistent with an open flame to vegetation," and court records show a prior manslaughter conviction in Arizona.9

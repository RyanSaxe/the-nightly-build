Word of the Day

Kayfabe names a lie both sides agree to keep

A 2025 trade newsletter reached for wrestling's word for staged reality to describe a tariff that opened at 145 percent and closed at 30.

3 min read2026-07-29
kayfabe noun /ˈkā-ˌfāb/

The tacit agreement between professional wrestlers and their fans to treat staged wrestling matches, storylines, and characters as genuine.1

The rule was never that the crowd be fooled

A wrestling promoter's oldest rule had nothing to do with keeping the crowd in the dark. Wrestlers maintained their characters continuously, on camera and off, so that a "bad guy" and a "good guy" were never seen together in public.2 Being caught together would not have shocked anyone who already suspected the outcome was arranged. It would have broken the story everyone in the building had quietly agreed to keep going.

Kayfabe is "the tacit agreement between professional wrestlers and their fans to pretend that overtly staged wrestling events, stories, characters, etc., are genuine."1 It is not the shape of an ordinary lie, where only the liar knows what's true. At ringside, most of the crowd who wants to know already knows the outcome was set backstage. Kayfabe is the agreement to act on the story anyway, performer and audience both, loose enough now that Merriam-Webster's broader definition covers any "tacit agreement to behave as if something is real, sincere, or genuine when it is not."1

The word's own history won't confirm the story everyone repeats

Ask where the word "kayfabe" itself comes from, and that certainty disappears. Merriam-Webster's own history of the term is blunt about it: "etymologists have struggled to pin down the origin of kayfabe."3

The version told most often traces it to carnival workers, who ran rigged wrestling exhibitions before organized wrestling copied the trick, and are said to have spoken in a coded slang where "kayfabe" meant simply "be fake."2 Most tellings add that it arrived there by way of pig Latin. Merriam-Webster tested that claim against pig Latin's actual rule, moving a word's first consonant to the end and adding "ay," and it fails: run "be fake" through pig Latin and it comes out "ebay akefay," not kayfabe.3 Other accounts reach further back, to the Latin cavēre, "to beware," or the French sentry's challenge qui vive, "who goes there."4 Wiktionary and Wikipedia list still more candidates, an eponymous "Kay Fabe," a backslang trick, none better documented than the rest.45 The word's earliest confirmed appearance in print is a wrestling-trade yearbook from 1988.5

The carny-cant, pig Latin version keeps getting told anyway, its own dictionary's rebuttal notwithstanding: a satisfying account, performed with more confidence than the record backs, itself a small kayfabe.

Wrestling itself stopped pretending in 1989

The clearest break with the old rule came in 1989, when Vince McMahon testified to the New Jersey State Senate that professional wrestling was not a competitive sport.5 No promoter had been willing to make that concession in public before. Wrestling did not stop staging its matches. It stopped pretending, publicly, that it hadn't been. What followed is what one scholar has called "neokayfabe": storylines and real events now openly blur together instead of staying walled apart, with the audience trusted to enjoy the story while knowing exactly how it is made.5

The tariff opened at 145 percent so 30 could look like a win

That broader sense travels well past wrestling. A 2025 newsletter on trade policy reached for it directly to describe that spring's tariff standoff between Washington and Beijing: "it's all kayfabe, professional wrestling's term for staged reality to work the crowd."6 The opening threat was a 145 percent tariff. The deal that followed landed at 30 percent, framed as a win for the side that had proposed it.6 The newsletter did not let the framing erase the bill: "kayfabe is staged reality, sure, but the theatrics have very real costs."6 Importers and shoppers pay those costs. They do not get to treat the invoice as part of the show.

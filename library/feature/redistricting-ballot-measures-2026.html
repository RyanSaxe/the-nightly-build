Four state redistricting votes in 2026 move no House district line

Missouri’s voters and Virginia’s Supreme Court each blocked a new congressional map, so both states start 2028 on the lines they used on 3 November.

5 min read2026-11-09

Two maps blocked, one rule loosened, one limit refused

Four states put congressional redistricting on the ballot in 2026: Virginia on 21 April, and Colorado, Maryland and Missouri on 3 November.1 Together they redrew no US House district. Missouri’s voters rejected a map that would have redrawn six of its eight districts.2 Virginia’s voters approved an amendment carrying an 11-district map, a vote the state Supreme Court voided.3 Maryland’s amendment draws no map, and Colorado’s failed.45 Courts had settled Missouri’s and Virginia’s maps for the 3 November House elections by 25 September,6 so the November votes bear only on maps from 2028.

The votes followed a 2025 round of redrawing between censuses. Ballotpedia’s August count of ten states with new 2026 maps included Missouri’s.7 California’s came from Proposition 50, a 2025 vote.1

Missouri’s row reads backwards. A veto referendum is a vote on a law already passed, so a no vote “will leave in place the congressional district boundaries enacted in 2022.”9 Maryland’s amendment declares that nothing in the state constitution “provides applicable criteria” for congressional boundaries.4 Ballotpedia labels Virginia’s count certified, but a judge blocked certification on 22 April and the state Supreme Court called the figures “unofficial results.”313

Courts settled the disputed 2026 maps before November

Virginia’s constitution requires a House of Delegates election between the legislature’s two passages of an amendment, and the first passage came after early voting in 2025 had begun. The court held 4–3 that this made the vote “null and void.”3 Missouri ran its August primary on the 2025 map before its Supreme Court held that the map “has never been the law.”6

Colorado’s Republican Party backed the limit on redrawing, and its Democratic Party opposed it.16

Seats the blocked maps targeted

Virginia’s voided map was expected to move four seats to Democrats, a figure the court took from Democratic legislators and Ballotpedia reached from 2025 gubernatorial returns.313 Missouri’s was aimed at one seat, NPR reported, by ousting Democratic Rep. Emanuel Cleaver in Kansas City.18 Gov. Kehoe’s office said it splits fewer counties.2 Neither map took effect and the projections use different methods, so netting them gives a number no source owns.

The 2028 cycle starts from the in-force maps. Missouri Republicans have floated another redraw,17 and Amendment 6, passed the same day, requires 80% of both chambers to pass laws like those rejected by referendum.9 Whether a new map counts as one is untested. Maryland is the one state where a 2026 vote widened what a legislature may draw, and Gov. Wes Moore has set no timeline.19

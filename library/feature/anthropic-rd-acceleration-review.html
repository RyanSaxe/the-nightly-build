Only the capability index measures what Anthropic's 2x threshold counts

By METR's arithmetic, the private figures behind Anthropic's “less than a factor of 2” come to 1.97, on a redacted indicator the threshold does not name.

5 min read2026-11-07

Three hundredths from the line

On 5 November METR published its review of the automated-R&D section of Anthropic's August 2026 Risk Report.1 The public report says Anthropic's leading indicators, “metrics that we believe could be predictive of later trends” in its capability index, show acceleration “by less than a factor of 2”.2 From the private version's figures METR calculates 1.97, assuming all the observed speed-up came from AI, and says “around 2” describes it better.1

METR agrees that catastrophic risk from the models of the 15 July coverage date was low, “but we think it is plausible that they had crossed their early warning threshold at the time.” Most of the relevant researchers at both organizations lean the other way.1 The published estimates measure four different quantities. Only one, the slope of a capability index, is the threshold's own quantity.

Two baseline years in one

Anthropic's Responsible Scaling Policy (RSP) v3.4 sets two conditions on one route to the threshold. (a) Anthropic observes or expects “double the rate of progress in AI aggregate capabilities” against both the expected rate and the fastest extended rate seen without significant AI help. (b) “it is plausible that this doubling is substantially attributable to the automation of research and/or engineering”.3 Doubling means “as much progress in one year as one would see in two years at baseline”, and the Risk Report says the threshold “functions as a potential early warning”.32

The RSP adds that this “is not the same idea as ‘doubling researchers’ productivity’”. Its changelog rules out a crossing where progress is far faster than it would be without AI tools but the observed rate holds constant.3 A comparison with and without AI speaks to (b). Condition (a) needs the observed rate.

The public Risk Report tests a stricter (b). It concludes that progress remains below “a doubling over pre-AI-assisted rates of progress attributable primarily to AI R&D automation”.2 “Primarily” asks for more than “plausible” and “substantially”.

Each estimate, by what it measures

AECI is “our internal fork of Epoch AI's Epoch Capability Index”, one score fitted across many benchmarks.2 Its slope after a trend break over its slope before is an acceleration in the RSP's sense. Fig. 2 sorts the estimates by what they measure.

Only in lane A is the 2x line condition (a). The 1.97 is in lane B, on an indicator nobody outside can inspect, and METR is uncertain “how these leading indicators relate to downstream capabilities.”1

Lane C is the counterfactual that cannot cross the threshold alone. METR's proposed wording for the report bases the private “slightly less than twice as fast” on “interviews with research leads and informal surveys”, and the private report calls exceeding 2 “plausible”.1 METR's preliminary September estimate, “~1.5X”, came with no stated period.7 In lane D, METR guesses that about 80% of the October acceleration is due to AI, which bears on (b).4

A fit choice moves the estimate from 1.1 to 2.9

Anthropic reads the AECI record as a one-time jump. Its Risk Report attributes Mythos Preview's rise above trend to “specific research progress that … we do not believe was significantly accelerated by AI”, and calls Mythos 5's similar position “weak evidence against a dramatic increase”.2 Anthropic's Claude Opus 5.5 system card prefers the jump “in 99 of 100 resampled fits” and finds that “under either reading the slope has not doubled.”5

METR adds the internal Models 2, 3 and 4, finished from July to October 2026. Fitted through Mythos 5 and tested on them, a permanent slope change has an RMSE of 1.9 against the jump's 3.9, and wins in 81% of bootstrap draws.4 METR's main estimate is 1.9x, a rise from about 15 to 29 index points a year after a break on 7 October 2025. Changing the link function moves it across the line.4

METR finds “broadly compelling” Anthropic's case that its models could not yet replace its research staff, the route METR calls Full Substitution.1 Anthropic's amended S-1 of 2 November says estimates for future models “indicate that we may imminently meet” the RSP's definition, and that it has “in the past not identified” a significant acceleration until after it occurred.8

For 15 July, only estimates in lanes B and C reach 2. On the threshold's own quantity, METR's two figures for that date, 1.5 and 1.9, are both under the line.1 Anthropic's own published ratio rose from 1.53 on 22 September to 1.70 on 7 October.

Reinforcement Learning

MaskablePPO's network never learns which moves are illegal

Five MaskablePPO agents that average 10 moves per episode on the four-disk Tower of Hanoi need 34 to 63 once Stable-Baselines3's evaluate_policy plays them without the mask.

16 min read2026-11-18

Why this matters

In every state of the Tower of Hanoi, three or four of the six moves are illegal, yet the policy network of an off-the-shelf agent such as PPO gives all six a probability. In Q-learning the mask, A(s) written as 1s and 0s, had to reach the choice and the target by hand. MaskablePPO, the PPO of the sb3-contrib package, applies it to the policy itself. Before every training move it asks the environment for the mask and samples from the legal moves alone, which Huang and Ontañón call invalid action masking. Its network never has to learn which moves are illegal, so the agent needs the mask wherever it chooses a move. You will train MaskablePPO on hanoi.py beside a PPO agent that pays for its illegal moves, then find the evaluation calls that leave the mask out without a warning.

Seed 0's trained network rates an illegal first move most probable

We train MaskablePPO on hanoi.py with seed 0 and a budget of 50,000 steps, then read its policy network at the start, where all four disks sit on peg 0. MaskablePPO is the PPO of sb3-contrib, a companion package to Stable-Baselines3, with masking added and nothing else changed.1 With the mask, its greedy agent solves the puzzle from the start in 15 moves, the fewest possible.

The network alone gives each of the six moves a probability, legal or not (Fig. 1). At the start only 0 → 1 and 0 → 2 are legal. The move the network rates most probable, at 0.657, is 1 → 0, which would lift a disk off the empty peg 1.

In training, MaskablePPO never makes that move, because it applies the mask before it samples. It replaces the logit of every illegal move with −108, so the softmax gives those moves probability 0.2 The two legal moves keep their probabilities relative to each other. 0 → 1 had 0.296 and 0 → 2 had 0.002, together 0.298, so with the mask they have 0.994 and 0.006.

In any state, the masked policy is the network's distribution conditioned on a legal move.

After training, the agent's greedy move at the start depends on whether the caller passes the mask. With no mask, predict returns 1 → 0. With the mask, it returns 0 → 1, the first move of the 15-move solution.

MaskablePPO's training depends only on legal moves' logits

hanoi.py, from the lesson on action masking in Q-learning, already has the method MaskablePPO calls. MaskablePPO looks for exactly the name action_masks, and it never reads info["action_mask"].3 Before each training step it calls that method and passes the mask to the network with the observation.4 It finds the method through every wrapper around the environment, here a TimeLimit of 100 moves and a wrapper that counts illegal moves.5 If the environment is vectorized, a container that steps several copies at once, MaskablePPO asks each copy for its own mask.3

MaskablePPO stores each mask with its step and applies the stored mask again when it updates the network.4 At a state where a move is illegal, the update sees that move's logit only after it has become −108, so no gradient reaches the logit from that state. The logit still changes, because the same weights compute the logits of every state. No part of training depends on its value where the move is illegal, so the network never learns which moves are illegal. A later lesson on masking in policy gradients shows why a gradient taken through the masked distribution is still a policy gradient.

An environment without the method stops training at once, since learn raises a ValueError.4 sb3-contrib's ActionMasker wrapper adds the method, given a function that returns the mask for the environment's current state.6 The 2.9.2 documentation says MaskablePPO behaves like PPO unless the environment is wrapped with ActionMasker,1 but the code does so only under learn(..., use_masking=False).

maskable.py trains either agent with the course's discount, 0.9, and every other setting at its default, as in the lesson on training an off-the-shelf agent. learn(50_000) then runs 25 batches of 2,048 steps, 51,200 in all.4 The listing needs hanoi.py in the same folder, and its dependency block adds sb3-contrib 2.9.2, the current release, to agent.py's.7 Its demo trains seed 0 and prints the numbers behind Fig. 1 and Fig. 2 in about a minute.

Without a mask, PPO spends three training moves in ten on no-ops

An agent that ignores the mask needs a rule for what an illegal move does, and the environment sets it. The environment checker steps with random moves that ignore the mask,1 so hanoi.py's step accepts an illegal move, leaves every disk where it is and pays −1, a no-op. SB3's plain PPO learns about illegal moves only from that wasted move. MaskablePPO never triggers the rule: maskable.py counts 0 illegal moves in 51,200 training steps.

compare.py, below, trains both agents with seeds 0 to 4. Each greedy policy then plays the same 100 episodes in a separate evaluation environment, whose seed training never uses, and is scored by its moves per episode, at most 100. Random legal moves need 87.5. The scripted policy, the textbook recursion that moves the largest misplaced disk once every smaller disk is out of its way, needs 9.9.

PPO sends 28 to 32 of every 100 training moves to step as illegal no-ops, and MaskablePPO sends none (Table 1). PPO learns the puzzle anyway. Its greedy agents need 9.9 to 12.9 moves per episode, and MaskablePPO's need 9.9 to 10.0, within 0.1 of the scripted policy.

Legal moves are common in Hanoi. A random move there is legal about half the time, since 240 of the 486 state-move pairs are legal. Huang and Ontañón compared masking with a reward penalty for invalid actions in µRTS, a real-time strategy game.8 A unit to command, picked at random from the 16 cells of the 4×4 map, was valid with probability 0.125, and on that map a penalty of −0.01 matched masking's return of 40. On the larger maps, where that probability fell as low as 0.0034, every penalty they tried ended at 1 or below while masking still reached 40.8

compare.py, complete, and its output

uv run compare.py needs hanoi.py and maskable.py in its folder, and it trains one seed per process. It took about three minutes on the four cores used here.

SB3's evaluate_policy runs the network without its mask

Training is the one place MaskablePPO fetches the mask on its own. predict applies a mask only when the caller passes action_masks,9 and SB3's evaluate_policy, from the lesson on training an off-the-shelf agent, calls predict without one.10 Neither warns. sb3-contrib's own evaluate_policy, in sb3_contrib.common.maskable.evaluation, asks the environment for a fresh mask before every move.11 Its documentation says to use that one, and notes that "use of masks is manual and optional outside of learning".1

Through SB3's evaluate_policy, the five MaskablePPO agents need 34 to 63 moves per episode, against 10 through sb3-contrib's (Table 2). At a state whose most probable move is illegal, the deterministic agent makes the no-op, stays where it is, and chooses the same move again until the time limit cuts the episode. Seed 0's network has 10 such states, the start among them, and its greedy episodes from 33 of the 80 starts end at the limit (Fig. 2).

If these failures come from training that never sampled an illegal move, PPO should not have them, since each illegal move it sampled cost it a wasted move. Its network gives illegal moves 0.02 to 0.03 of its probability, against 0.30 to 0.35 for MaskablePPO's (Table 2), and it needs 9.9 to 12.9 moves per episode with no mask (Table 1). Seeds 5 to 9, from uv run compare.py 5, end the same way.

Problems

1. Run seed 0's agent with no mask and deterministic=False, so that predict samples from the network's six probabilities. Which legal move does it make from a state, and with what probability? How many no-ops does it make first at the start, on average?

Answer

A no-op leaves the state as it was, so the next draw comes from the same six probabilities. The first legal move drawn is a with probability π(a | s) divided by the legal moves' total, which is πmask(a | s) from the equation above. The agent follows the masked policy and makes no-ops on the way. Each draw is illegal with probability q, the illegal moves' total, so the number of no-ops before a legal move has mean q / (1 − q). At the start q = 0.657 + 0.020 + 0.002 + 0.024 = 0.703, which gives about 2.4 no-ops, each paying −1 and using one of the episode's 100 moves.

2. In the lesson on action masking in Q-learning, the estimates of moves the agent never made changed its other estimates. MaskablePPO's illegal logits get no gradient either. Why do they not change its training, and where do they do harm?

Answer

Q-learning's target read each never-updated estimate during training, through its max over all six moves. MaskablePPO reads an illegal logit nowhere in training. It samples from the masked distribution, and its update applies the stored mask, so in both the logit is −108 and its move has probability 0. The illegal logits do harm only where something reads the network without the mask, as SB3's evaluate_policy does.

The takeaway

MaskablePPO calls the environment's action_masks() before every training move, sets each illegal logit to −108, and samples from the legal moves alone. Its update applies the stored masks, so its network never learns which moves are illegal. On the four-disk Tower of Hanoi, five such networks still gave illegal moves about 0.3 of their probability. PPO without a mask learned the puzzle too, while spending about three training moves in ten on no-ops, and its networks gave illegal moves 0.02 to 0.03. Run through SB3's evaluate_policy, which passes no mask, the masked agents needed 34 to 63 moves per episode instead of 10. Every call that chooses a move after training has to pass the mask.
